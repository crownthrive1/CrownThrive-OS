## Crown Credits provider canary

A live Stripe Checkout Session was successfully created against the governed $10 Crown Credits top-up Price and then intentionally expired without payment.

The provider returned live dynamic payment methods including card, Link, ACH, Cash App, Klarna, Afterpay/Clearpay, Amazon Pay, Alipay, and WeChat Pay. No money moved during the canary.

Stablecoin/Crypto was not returned as an available payment method.

## Stablecoin provider canary

A separate live Checkout request explicitly asked Stripe for the Crypto payment method. Stripe rejected the request because Crypto is not activated/available for the platform account.

Independent Payment Method Configuration readback confirmed:

- Platform Crypto `available=false`.
- Connected Standard account Crypto `available=false`.
- The connected account currently has a Crypto display preference enabled on at least one configuration, but provider availability remains false.

The institutional state is therefore:

`PROVIDER_ENABLEMENT_PENDING`

No CrownThrive API, MCP, wallet, checkout, or agent may present stablecoin acceptance as active until Stripe provider readback reports the capability available.

## Stablecoin-to-Crown-Credits architecture

Stablecoin payment is a tender source, not the Crown Credits asset class.

When Stripe stablecoin payments become available:

1. Customer pays through Stripe's Crypto/stablecoin experience.
2. Stripe handles token/network interaction and provider settlement.
3. CrownThrive independently reads back the completed Checkout Session, PaymentIntent, Charge, amounts, currency, product/price identity, tax state, and idempotency state.
4. Crown Credits are issued only after exact readback reconciliation.
5. CHLOM Wallet appends stablecoin tender provenance and settlement evidence to its chain-hashed ledger.
6. Crown Credits remain nontransferable, non-cash-redeemable, non-interest-bearing internal store credits and remain `crypto_asset=false`.
7. CHLOM Wallet does not infer or claim custody of the payer's stablecoins.

## CHLOM Wallet settlement bridge

Verified Crown Credits settlement/reversal state is projected automatically to the CHLOM Wallet append-only ledger.

The bridge distinguishes:

- Fiat payment settlement.
- Stablecoin payment settlement.
- Fiat reversal/refund/dispute evidence.
- Stablecoin reversal/refund evidence.

Provider success never directly manufactures an entitlement, rights grant, public performance claim, economic settlement, or authority receipt.

## Stripe webhook capacity

Stripe rejected creation of an additional live event destination because the platform account is at its provider limit of 16 live webhook/event destinations.

No existing endpoint was deleted merely to create capacity.

Crown Credits production settlement therefore uses verified provider readback reconciliation as authoritative economic truth. Webhooks are an acceleration mechanism when available, not the authority that creates Crown Credits.

## Agent E continuity reconciliation

The API/MCP & Federation Specialist correctly failed closed when its configured Stripe credential alias appeared empty. The configured alias was legacy and no longer represented the production Stripe secret source.

The canonical production alias was reconciled to the verified Vault source. Agent E remains enabled and fail-closed. The rule now fails closed against the canonical source rather than an obsolete alias.

This preserves safety behavior while preventing false production quarantine caused by legacy naming drift.

## Production governance

Production does not mean unrestricted execution.

- ThriveEvergreen remains exact-ECAC gated.
- D3 authority remains human-reserved.
- No self-approval.
- No silent deletion of institutional history.
- No autonomous production rights grant.
- No raw provider secret return.
- No provider success → entitlement inference.
- No stablecoin tender → CHLOM custody inference.
- No stablecoin tender → crypto Crown Credits inference.

The operational objective is production execution inside signed authority with exact readback, append-only evidence, compensation/reversal handling, and fail-closed provider capability boundaries.
