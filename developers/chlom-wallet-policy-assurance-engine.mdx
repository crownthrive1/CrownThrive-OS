The CHLOM Wallet Policy Assurance Engine converts a proposed wallet action into a deterministic, hash-bound decision receipt. It keeps the customer experience simple while enforcing the complex authority, evidence, rights, economic, passkey, provider, chain, pricing, and release boundaries underneath.

```text
Wallet intent
   ↓
CAPS — compile the exact policy and authority ceiling
   ↓
AIR — verify agent, heartbeat, head, authority, and requested effects
   ↓
EAGR — resolve required, missing, stale, and invalid evidence
   ↓
VCL — preserve Money, Rights, Rewards, Impact, Proof, and Identity semantics
   ↓
RIGOR — combine hard denials, holds, and bounded risk
   ↓
DRIFT — create deterministic intent and receipt fingerprints
   ↓
ECAC | HOLD | DENY
```

CHAOS continuously generates deterministic adversarial cases against the same compiled rulepack.

## Proprietary CHLOM layer

External standards and providers remain replaceable. That policy layer can support CrownThrive's own platforms and later become part of controlled WalletKit APIs, private deployments, readiness assessments, and managed services.

## Decision meanings

### ECAC

`ECAC` permits progression only inside the compiled controlled-test corridor. It never authorizes production activation, provider writes, custody, money movement, rights grants, chain broadcast, public pricing, checkout, phase advancement, or merge.

### HOLD

`HOLD` means required evidence, freshness, reconciliation, rollback, identity, or independent review is incomplete. Resolution requires a new exact-snapshot evaluation.

### DENY

`DENY` rejects a request that crossed a semantic or authority boundary. Examples include payment-only entitlement, false Impact settlement, Rewards-as-cash claims, protected evidence on-chain, private passkey material, EntryPoint codehash mismatch, authority escalation, self-approval, custody, money movement, chain broadcast, checkout, phase advancement, or merge.

## AI policy

AI is not the decision authority. The current engine uses deterministic reason templates. A future language model may explain a receipt only as an advisory layer and may not change the disposition, manufacture evidence, create authority, reinterpret rights, or authorize execution. Any retained advisory output must be hash-bound to the deterministic receipt.
