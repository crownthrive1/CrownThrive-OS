# Platform registry

The platform registry defines **what each CrownThrive property is responsible for** and prevents duplicated, conflicting, or unauthorized functions.

<Note>
  This public registry describes operating roles. It does not expose private architecture, credentials, customer data, partner terms, or protected implementation details.
</Note>

## Registry fields

Every platform record should eventually include:

- platform ID and canonical name
- owner and operating entity
- corridor and purpose
- public domains
- current lifecycle state
- inputs and outputs
- upstream and downstream dependencies
- revenue channels
- data classes handled
- governing policies
- approval gates
- accountable operator
- public support route
- change history
- public-safe portfolio route
- internal documentation links
- alias/predecessor/successor relationships
- press-room/media-kit route or archive press disposition
- public `Powered by CrownThrive` / ecosystem relationship evidence where observed
- dated public-copy scan state and correction status

The master human-facing projection is the [Public Brand & Platform Portfolio](/portfolio/public-brand-platform-portfolio). The complete domain lineage remains in the [82-domain register](/knowledge/phase-2-99-workstream-3a-holdings-82-domain-source-seed), and public media facts route through the [CrownThrive Press Room](/press/index).

## Platform lifecycle states

Recommended public lifecycle states:

- `CONCEPT`
- `DEVELOPMENT`
- `CONTROLLED_TEST`
- `PRODUCTION`
- `LIMITED_AVAILABILITY`
- `MAINTENANCE`
- `LEGACY`
- `ARCHIVED`

A platform may be operational in one function and still have other modules in development. Public documentation should state scope rather than applying one broad label to every capability.

## Ownership and dependency rule

A downstream platform receives only the permissions, data, and assets necessary for its function.

Examples:

- Backroad FM may program an approved Virality Music track without receiving source stems or adaptation rights.
- GSO may list a commercial license only after the rights record and listing state are approved.
- CrownLytics may measure conversion without receiving private journal text or complete payment credentials.
- ThrivePush may send an approved campaign without owning the customer’s entitlement record.

## Registry maintenance

The registry should be updated when:

- a platform is created, renamed, merged, deprecated, archived, reserved or reactivated
- a public domain changes role or custody
- an owner or accountable operator changes
- a new data class is introduced
- a new revenue rail is activated
- a dependency changes
- a governance rule changes
- a platform enters or exits production
- a public portfolio page or `Powered by CrownThrive` relationship changes
- a press/media-kit fact, metric, boilerplate or contact changes

A platform leaving production does **not** disappear from the registry. Its history, domains, predecessor/successor links, press disposition and surviving legal/support/rights obligations remain documented.

<Warning>
  Do not resolve platform ambiguity through marketing copy. Resolve it in the registry, then update public copy from the approved record. Public `Powered by` language is corroborating relationship evidence, not runtime or authority proof.
</Warning>
