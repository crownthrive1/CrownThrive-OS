# Convergent Ecosystem

CrownThrive is not governed as a loose portfolio of unrelated websites. The **Convergent Ecosystem** is the permanent institutional model that allows multiple industries, brands, platforms, programs, media properties, and operating services to function as one coordinated body while preserving distinct customer promises and operating responsibilities.

## Institutional purpose

The model exists to prevent fragmentation. Shared identity, rights, data, support, analytics, distribution, rewards, operating procedures, and governance should be consumed by active platforms rather than rebuilt independently.

The public CrownThrive site describes this as an operating system spanning business building, community, media, commerce, education, automation, analytics, advertising, licensing, and governance. Current public descriptions also acknowledge that not every platform is fully integrated or production-ready at the same time. Institutional documentation must therefore separate **vision**, **current capability**, **planned integration**, and **historical implementation**.

## Operating rule

A platform may have its own brand, domain, users, products, and workflows, but it should connect to common institutional services where practical:

```text
Identity → Integration → Rights → Operations → Support → Data → Retention
```

This shared spine is how CrownThrive pursues **One Seat, Multiple Industries** without pretending that one login, one database, or one vendor already powers every property.

## Corridor model

Current planning groups the portfolio into these operating fields:

- Holding, Governance & IP
- Technology & Operating Spine
- Media & Culture
- Beauty, Wellness & Physical
- Commerce & Brands
- Education, Talent & Community
- Growth, Advertising & Distribution
- Hospitality, Travel & Experiences where separately tracked in the portfolio registry

Corridor labels are organizational tools. They can evolve without changing stable asset identity.

## What convergence does not mean

Convergence does not require every platform to share the same codebase, vendor, database, interface, or public brand. Specialized systems remain systems of record for their own functions. CrownThrive IO/MCP, CHLOM, CrownThrive Support, CrownLytics, and the other shared services provide interoperability and governance across those boundaries.

## CIE predecessor gate

Cultural Imprint Engine is the current predecessor packet in the Framework Factory sequence. The public-safe handoff may describe CIE pass evidence, HOLD, correction, or reopen state, but it has no runtime, provider, database, commercial, certification, or voting effect.

The current result is `HOLD_TARGET_FRAMEWORK_NOT_ACCEPTED`. A Convergent implementation packet may not be promoted until all of these are independently evidenced:

- CIE reaches `linked_governed` and governed acceptance;
- Agent D certifies the exact current CIE head;
- current parent/child OIDC, contract-digest, backlink, message, acknowledgement, and rollback evidence is valid;
- Phase 2.99 hard-exit requirements pass;
- Convergent research sources receive a separate governed promotion;
- current-main topology and collision checks pass;
- applicable security, operations, privacy, IP, rights, and TEVV evidence is accepted;
- lawful A/B/C/D/S quorum includes mandatory Agent D and no deny or block.

## Research-only handoff contract

A prepared CIE-to-Convergent envelope is advisory input only. It must not contain person scoring, sensitive-trait inference, credentials, private fingerprints, protected calibration, private evaluation corpora, restricted evidence, or unlicensed content. Unknown or disallowed material remains HOLD rather than being silently normalized or promoted.

No Convergent parent agent, subagent fleet, child repository, API, MCP, database route, customer entitlement, or commercial offer is activated by the current packet. Those surfaces belong to a later, separately accepted factory packet.

## Institutional standard

Every active asset should eventually have a stable ID, owner, canonical URL, lifecycle state, customer, revenue model, support path, data events, rights record, integration map, dependency map, backup/export method, and risk classification.
