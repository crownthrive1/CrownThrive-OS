# Cultural Imprint Engine

The **Cultural Imprint Engine (CIE)** is CrownThrive's cultural-governance framework. It exists to prevent the ecosystem from scaling operationally while losing context, authorship, cultural specificity, narrative continuity, community accountability or long-term stewardship.

Recovered Framework OS doctrine establishes five canonical CIE dimensions:

1. **Identity Fit**
2. **Community Value**
3. **Story Alignment**
4. **Brand Safety**
5. **Legacy Impact**

The current institutional doctrine also requires CIE to govern cultural context and audience, narrative/canon, protected identities and character continuity, imagery/aesthetics/language/representation, source-community attribution, cross-imprint reuse, commercialization boundaries, corrections, retirements and successor descriptions.

## Standardized CIE-100 score

Each canonical dimension contributes 20 points. The deterministic engine begins each dimension at 20 and applies evidence-backed severity deductions. All five dimensions require evidence.

| Dimension | Maximum |
| --- | ---: |
| Identity Fit | 20 |
| Community Value | 20 |
| Story Alignment | 20 |
| Brand Safety | 20 |
| Legacy Impact | 20 |
| **Total** | **100** |

Decision bands:

- **85–100: PASS**
- **70–84: REVIEW**
- **50–69: HOLD**
- **0–49: FAIL**

A hard-block violation overrides the numeric total. Missing evidence produces `HOLD_INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE` rather than an invented score.

The 85-point threshold is intentionally stringent and broadly consistent with CrownThrive's recovered Framework OS decision doctrine, where the highest action band begins around the mid-80-percent range.

## Non-negotiable hard blocks

CIE cannot be averaged around when there is evidence of:

- fabricated community endorsement;
- identity impersonation or deliberate erasure;
- dehumanizing or discriminatory representation;
- knowingly false cultural or canon claims;
- unauthorized rewrite of protected canon or identity;
- exploitative use of sacred, restricted or private material;
- material source-community attribution fraud;
- retaliatory suppression of a documented correction;
- CIE score/evidence manipulation;
- bypass of a required CIE review.

## Code of ethics and boundary

CIE evaluates **artifacts, uses and contexts**, not people.

CIE must not:

- score a person's cultural authenticity;
- infer or rank race, ethnicity, religion or other sensitive personal traits;
- act as a substitute for legal, rights, security, privacy, tax or professional determinations;
- police community membership or identity by stereotype;
- treat cultural governance as unrestricted censorship;
- manufacture community consensus from weak or missing evidence;
- convert a CIE PASS into a rights grant or a CHLOM/license approval.

Every material finding requires evidence, a reason, a correction path and an appeal/escalation route.

## Relationship to CHLOM

CIE and CHLOM are complementary, not duplicates. **CIE answers whether use is culturally and narratively aligned. CHLOM answers whether use is owned, permitted, licensed, eligible, recorded and auditable.** A use can pass one layer and fail the other.

The implementation pallet is [CIE Cultural Governance Pallet](/chlom/cie-cultural-governance-pallet).

## Permanent framework agent

CIE is being institutionalized as `ct.framework-agent.cie`, a permanent foundational framework agent with Agent A as operational parent for routing only. Its vote remains independent of Agent A.

Five domain subagents independently prepare evidence for the canonical dimensions and a sixth remediation/escalation subagent coordinates corrections. All CIE subagents are non-voting.

See [CIE Framework Agent](/automation/cie-framework-agent).

## Ecosystem scan behavior

After governed adoption, CIE becomes a mandatory cross-ecosystem review lane for material changes involving:

- public cultural or community claims;
- canon, narrative or protected-character continuity;
- identity, representation or aesthetics;
- cross-imprint/cross-platform reuse;
- cultural commercialization;
- a new public asset;
- material brand repositioning.

The agent may produce a score, findings, corrections, assigned-agent tasks, documentation deltas, release holds and human escalations. It may not execute a D3 action.

Existing assets are not silently declared compliant. They enter a retroactive scan queue; new/materially changed artifacts become fail-closed under the CIE trigger after the governance-v2 packet is accepted.

## Customer and client assessments

CIE can be productized as a governed assessment service for domains, pages, websites, documents, campaigns, products, media/brand assets, software surfaces, dataset descriptions and other digital artifacts.

The customer report must include:

- CIE score and verdict;
- five-dimension breakdown;
- evidence-backed deductions;
- hard blocks, if any;
- required corrections;
- assigned remediation owners;
- human-review requirements;
- reasons in understandable language.

A CIE Conformance Report is a CrownThrive framework assessment, not legal accreditation or a government certification.

## AI and ML evolution

CIE starts deterministic and explainable. Future ML may assist with pattern detection and finding prioritization only after governed labeled data, privacy review, bias evaluation, TEVV and human override are proven. ML may never determine a person's cultural authenticity.

## Governance v2 implication

Founder direction dated 2026-08-20 authorizes CIE as the first permanent foundational framework voter. CT-ADR-GOV-012 proposes a six-voter pool with a 75% ceiling-rounded quorum: **five affirmative approvals out of six**, with Agent D still mandatory. CIE subagents do not add votes. D3 remains human-reserved.

Future foundational framework agents may permanently expand the voter pool only through explicit founder authorization, a machine contract, independent evaluation suite, parent assignment and governed acceptance. The approval ratio remains 75%, rounded up.
