# Cultural Imprint Engine

The **Cultural Imprint Engine (CIE)** is CrownThrive's cultural-governance framework. It exists to prevent the ecosystem from scaling operationally while losing context, authorship, cultural specificity, narrative continuity, or community accountability.

## Governing domains

CIE should classify and review:

- cultural context and audience;
- narrative and canon;
- protected identities and character continuity;
- imagery, aesthetics, language, and representation;
- source-community attribution;
- cross-imprint and cross-platform reuse;
- commercialization boundaries;
- corrections, retirements, and successor descriptions.

## Relationship to CHLOM

CIE and CHLOM are complementary, not duplicates. **CIE answers whether use is culturally and narratively aligned. CHLOM answers whether use is owned, permitted, licensed, eligible, recorded, and auditable.** A use can pass one layer and fail the other.

## Imprints

Recovered institutional sources identify imprint-oriented structures such as Kulture House, BongedOut!, Luxperiences Network, ThriveStudio Productions, media channels, and other culturally governed operating concepts. Their historical existence does not automatically establish current production status. Each imprint needs a stable record, status, rights relationship, and current owner.

## Phase 3 implication

CIE should become a policy input to the Approval Service and Rules Registry, with explicit human review for sensitive cultural, identity, or public-narrative decisions.

## Agentic interoperability boundary

The current CIE implementation packet prepares a **non-voting, D0-D2, advisory agent machine** for controlled testing. The public repository may carry the safe dimensions, closed request and decision schemas, hard-block interfaces, validators, evals and public digests. Protected scoring calibration, private evaluation corpora, defensive rules, private policy economics, credentials and runtime locations remain in governed Vault/private runtime custody.

The machine is fail-closed. While the physical child repository remains `PROVISIONED_UNLINKED`, runtime integration, certification, activation and customer-facing decisions remain disabled. Missing or mismatched contract/runtime digests produce `HOLD`, not an inferred result.

CHLOM interoperability uses a **service-only governed gateway**. No customer request, browser client, public repository, public RPC or user-supplied provider target may directly invoke protected CHLOM or CIE runtime authority. CIE may prepare a cultural-alignment advisory candidate; CHLOM supplies rights, license, eligibility, evidence and audit state; a qualified human or separately accepted sovereign process remains responsible for final sensitive decisions.

The Convergent Ecosystem continuity artifact remains `RESEARCH_CANDIDATE`. It does not bind an agent, create an OIDC identity, provision a repository, activate a vote, certify a framework or advance Phase 3. Promotion requires CIE to become current-head `LINKED_GOVERNED`, receive mandatory Agent-D parent certification, satisfy independent security/operations/IP evidence, complete rollback proof and obtain the accepted sovereign quorum.
