# Centralized, Partitioned and Decentralized Data Roadmap

CrownThrive requires centralized institutional coherence, partitioned operational security and a credible path to selective decentralization. The objective is not one database or one chain. The objective is one governed institution whose records can be trusted, moved, recovered and progressively distributed.

## Architecture principles

- one authoritative institutional vocabulary and stable-ID graph;
- one authoritative record per aggregate, with explicit cross-domain grants;
- operational stores partitioned by domain, tenant, purpose and classification;
- cross-domain exchange through versioned APIs/events;
- restricted evidence separated from public summaries;
- secrets and keys separated from ordinary application data;
- append-only correction and material event history;
- portable formats and tested export;
- cloud-first operational usefulness;
- decentralization only where it adds verifiability, resilience, interoperability, settlement or governance value.

## Layer 1 — Institutional control plane

Holds or references:

- organizations, people/service actors and roles;
- platforms, brands, imprints, universes, rooms and assets;
- source authority and evidence references;
- versions, status, aliases, predecessors/successors;
- policies, approvals and authority;
- API/MCP/domain/repository/deployment/vendor inventory;
- public claims and metrics definitions;
- data-flow and dependency maps;
- component/pallet/paper/IP registries;
- run packets and Phase 3–5 state.

This layer should not store every customer transaction or private source file.

## Layer 2 — Partitioned operational domains

### Identity domain

Accounts, external subjects, account links, authentication assurance, consent, role assignments, organizations and recovery.

### Rights/CHLOM domain

Assets, claims, evidence references, rights, policies, DLA/licenses, usage, entitlements, revenue rules, cases and remedies.

### Commerce domain

Products/offers/prices, orders, provider references, subscriptions, refunds, disputes, entitlements, downloads and statements.

### Media/content domain

Works, masters, editions, universes, characters, canon, releases, schedules, products and distribution.

### Ministry/education domain

Scripture-content relationships, rooms, studies, curricula, plans, organizations, courses, certificates and private learner/member records.

### Advertising/growth domain

Campaigns, advertisers/publishers, placements, creator deliverables, attribution, affiliates, rewards and messaging.

### MM Suites/phygital domain

Locations, suites/seats, operators, Suite Pros, bookings, products, training, territories, buildout, incidents and impact.

### Support/knowledge domain

Tickets, articles, policies, SOPs, runbooks, source lineage, feedback and search indexes.

### Analytics domain

Normalized events, derived aggregates, approved metrics and model outputs. Raw restricted payloads remain in source domains unless explicitly needed.

## Layer 3 — Restricted evidence and IP vaults

Separate storage for:

- contracts, assignments, licenses and signed agreements;
- private rights/evidence;
- customer/identity verification;
- investigations and legal holds;
- unpublished masters/source files;
- trade secrets, economic models and protected CHLOM internals;
- sensitive journals/reflections;
- incident/security evidence;
- privileged/legal communications where applicable.

Store integrity hashes/references in the control plane; do not duplicate sensitive payloads into broad indexes.

## Layer 4 — Secret and key plane

- API keys/OAuth client secrets;
- database credentials;
- webhook signing secrets;
- encryption/signing keys;
- recovery/break-glass credentials;
- deployment secrets;
- provider tokens.

Use a secret manager/KMS; applications and docs receive references/leases. Access is logged and rotated.

## Layer 5 — Backup and continuity plane

- database PITR/snapshots;
- object-version replication;
- immutable/off-provider institutional exports;
- repository mirrors/tags;
- DNS/domain inventory;
- provider exports;
- restore environments;
- recovery authority and runbooks;
- recurring restoration tests.

## Data-flow contract

Every cross-domain flow identifies:

```yaml
flow_id: ct.data-flow.01J...
source_domain: commerce
destination_domain: chlom
purpose: verify_paid_entitlement
legal_policy_basis: ct.policy.digital-products.v3
classification: restricted
fields:
  - order_id
  - subject_id
  - product_id
  - payment_status
retention: event_reference_only
consent_required: false
event_type: order.payment_confirmed
failure_behavior: hold_entitlement
owner: ct.role.commerce-integration
```

No “sync all data” integrations without field/purpose design.

## Tenant and relationship isolation

Use row-level tenant controls, organization boundaries, object relationships and policy-as-code. A role in one tenant/platform does not automatically grant another. Cross-tenant access requires explicit relationship/grant and DAIL evidence.

## Data retention and deletion

Retention is based on data class, purpose, legal/contractual requirement, security, user rights and historical value. Legal hold overrides deletion. Deletion should propagate to derived stores where required while retaining lawful minimum audit references.

## Cloud-first phase

Use portable managed services where they reduce operational risk: PostgreSQL, versioned object storage, queues/event bus, durable workflows, search, observability, KMS and infrastructure-as-code. Maintain provider abstraction and export.

## Federated/sovereign phase

As CrownThrive matures:

- self-host or multi-cloud critical control services;
- isolate key custody;
- maintain independent registries/exports;
- support organization-managed nodes or data partitions where justified;
- use public/verifiable proofs rather than publishing private data;
- allow certified external verifiers/oracles;
- preserve interoperability across platforms and licensees.

## Selective decentralization candidates

- DID/VC resolution;
- proof/attestation anchoring;
- public license/rights verification;
- external verifier/oracle networks;
- multiparty settlement;
- governance approvals;
- LEX transaction proofs;
- cross-institution credential/entitlement portability.

## What should not be indiscriminately decentralized

PII, passwords, private evidence, contracts, journals, payment credentials, security incidents, confidential economics and material that cannot be lawfully corrected/deleted should not be placed on immutable public ledgers.

## Automation and drift control

Watch schema compatibility, missing events, cross-domain leakage, stale replicas, backup health, storage thresholds, access anomalies, API/provider changes, retention violations and restore readiness. Automatically quarantine or degrade when policy allows; escalate consequential corrections.

## Phase sequence

- **2.98:** discover current stores, vendors, APIs, flows and exports;
- **2.99:** decide private core, IaC, secret manager, tenancy and event topology;
- **3.0:** seed control plane and schemas;
- **3A–D:** implement services and governed flows;
- **4:** federate platform cohorts;
- **5:** selectively distribute proofs, identity, licensing, settlement and governance where validated.
