# Dependency, Backup and Recovery Architecture

CrownThrive must be recoverable without depending on one SaaS vendor, one repository, one account, or one person's memory.

## Dependency register

Every P0/P1 platform and institutional service should record:

- source repository;
- deployment host;
- database/storage;
- DNS/domain registrar;
- authentication provider;
- payment provider;
- email/communications provider;
- analytics provider;
- object/media storage;
- external APIs;
- legal/rightsholder dependencies;
- export method;
- replacement option;
- recovery owner.

## Backup classes

### Institutional knowledge

Canonical source should exist in version control, with periodic portable export of Markdown/MDX, registries and navigation/config manifests.

### Restricted source masters

Contracts, signed documents, rights evidence, unreleased masters and confidential research require encrypted controlled backup separate from public Mintlify.

### Service data

Databases need a defined RPO/RTO, backup cadence, retention period, restore owner and test schedule appropriate to their risk.

### Media and IP assets

Masters, stems, artwork, books, source files, metadata and checksum manifests should not live exclusively inside generation, distribution or publishing vendors.

## Secret recovery

Backups must not become a plaintext secret archive. Maintain an inventory of secret names, owners, rotation process, dependent services and recovery method while credentials themselves remain in an approved secret manager.

## Vendor exit packet

Every critical vendor should eventually have:

- export procedure;
- contractual/data-retention considerations;
- migration destination or adapter plan;
- dependency list;
- DNS/routing cutover plan where relevant;
- credential revocation checklist;
- post-exit validation.

## Restore testing

A backup is not verified until restoration is tested. Phase 3 should schedule recurring synthetic recovery tests for registry data, institutional docs, event/evidence records and selected service databases.

## Current continuity estate — founder Q7 reconciliation

The current continuity estate is multi-source and intentionally heterogeneous. As of August 19, 2026, governed recovery planning recognizes:

- Google Drive / Google Docs institutional masters and historical working records;
- Gmail/email governance, provider, recovery and operational correspondence;
- GitHub repository and archive history;
- local computer/storage copies;
- Verpex individual cPanel server backups and hosted assets;
- phone-held copies where they are the surviving copy of a source artifact;
- physical notebook records containing CrownThrive canons, doctrines and related founder material.

Founder memory may help discover or explain a source, but it is **not a certified backup** and cannot substitute for a governed record.

Physical notebook/canon/doctrine material becomes a governed recovery artifact only after it is digitized or otherwise indexed, source-registered, versioned and linked to the relevant institutional record.

### Supabase backup disposition

The currently connected CrownThrive Supabase project does not have provider-managed automatic backup/PITR coverage under the current free-plan operating state. Until a paid backup/PITR change is separately authorized, GATE-007 requires a governed logical database export and off-site copy rather than pretending provider backups exist.

A Supabase continuity packet must therefore define:

- logical database export method and cadence;
- schema \+ data scope;
- checksum/version metadata;
- off-site destination independent from Supabase;
- restore target and restore instructions;
- a reproducible restore test;
- Storage-object continuity separately, because database backup alone does not restore deleted Storage objects;
- upgrade/PITR decision only through separate cost/authority review.

## Continuity principle

SaaS is replaceable infrastructure. CrownThrive identity, rights, doctrine, evidence and canonical records must remain institution-controlled and portable.
