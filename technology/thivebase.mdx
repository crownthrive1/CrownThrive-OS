# THIVEBASE — Internal Infrastructure Base

**THIVEBASE** is the canonical CrownThrive internal brand for the shared infrastructure base beneath the governed MCP/CHLOM operating stack.

> Naming control: the founder-adjudicated canonical spelling is **THIVEBASE**. Do not silently normalize it to `ThriveBase`, `Thrive Base`, or another spelling without a later ADR.

THIVEBASE is an **internal CrownThrive service identity**, not the name of any one vendor. Supabase is the current primary database/control-plane provider used in the present build, while GitHub, Mintlify and other services support source control, CI, documentation, automation, observability and delivery. Provider succession must not change the THIVEBASE institutional identity.

## Institutional role

THIVEBASE is the base layer for:

- governed MCP/API control-plane state;
- CHLOM/DAIL metadata and evidence references;
- internal platform, provider and integration registries;
- identity/authority references that do not belong in public application data;
- adapter certification and provider-state records;
- rate-limit and request-budget state;
- cost, quota and capacity monitoring;
- partner/vendor/referral-program records;
- infrastructure lifecycle, replacement and exit planning;
- low-cost development and prototype operation before scale justifies paid capacity.

THIVEBASE does **not** replace CrownThrive ID, CrownThrive IO/MCP, CHLOM, CrownLytics or the individual portfolio platforms. It underlays and supports them.

## Current cost doctrine

The current Phase 2.99/early-Phase-3 strategy is **zero-to-low incremental infrastructure cost** wherever that does not compromise security, recovery or operational correctness. Free/community/public-repository allowances may be used for development and bounded production-like validation, but "free" is never treated as a permanent architectural requirement.

A provider must be upgraded, replaced, partitioned or supplemented when capacity, reliability, recovery, compliance, support, latency or unit economics justify it.

## Capacity thresholds

Every bounded provider quota tracked through THIVEBASE uses the same initial utilization policy unless a service-specific policy overrides it:

| Utilization | State | Required behavior |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0–49% | `green` | Continue and record trend |
| 50–69% | `watch` | Increase observation frequency and forecast exhaustion |
| 70–79% | `plan` | Open scaling/reduction/provider-option plan |
| 80–89% | `action_needed` | Execute approved capacity or optimization action before constraint |
| 90–100%+ | `scale_gate` | Treat as service-risk condition; block avoidable growth that worsens the constrained resource |

Thresholds apply independently to database size, storage, egress, cached egress, MAU, Edge Functions, Realtime messages/connections, provider request budgets and any future metered resource.

### Forecasting rule

A raw utilization percentage is insufficient. Monitoring should also calculate trend and predicted exhaustion date where data exists. A rapidly growing resource may enter `plan` or `action_needed` early even below the nominal threshold.

## Data-placement rule

THIVEBASE should keep the authoritative control plane compact.

**Suitable for the control plane:** stable IDs, integration state, policy metadata, authority references, approvals, DAIL metadata/hashes, provider snapshots, quota state, partner records and configuration references.

**Do not make the same database the default warehouse for:** large media, raw evidence, unrestricted logs, high-volume clickstream telemetry, full catalog binaries, unbounded analytics events or vendor credentials.

Large/high-volume data belongs in governed storage/analytics systems with references retained in THIVEBASE.

## Phase integration

### Phase 2 / 2.99 — current

- formalize THIVEBASE identity and boundaries;
- register current providers and capacity baselines;
- establish partner/vendor/referral registry;
- keep CrownThrive IO and Collab writes fail-closed until certified;
- preserve source authority and current-vs-target distinctions.

### Phase 3 — executable institutional core

- make THIVEBASE the governed internal registry/control substrate for production-ready CrownThrive ID, IO/MCP, CHLOM, DAIL, adapter state and policy/evidence references;
- automate sanitized usage snapshots and threshold evaluation;
- establish backup/export, restore and provider-exit tests.

### Phase 4 — federated ecosystem activation

- consume THIVEBASE across the P0/P1 platform adapter estate;
- route capacity, health, partner, cost and integration signals into CrownLytics/Ops surfaces;
- support corridor-specific tenancy/partitioning without fragmenting institutional identity.

### Phase 5+ — scale and commercialization

- support external developer/partner tenants where justified;
- meter governed API/MCP usage;
- enable licensed/managed THIVEBASE deployments without giving customers access to CrownThrive's internal authority or restricted institutional records;
- scale infrastructure based on verified demand and unit economics.

## Monetization boundary

THIVEBASE begins as internal infrastructure. External monetization must package **capability**, not CrownThrive's private data or sovereign authority.

Permitted future product directions include managed infrastructure setup, MCP integration, governed workflow hosting, compliance/rights infrastructure, usage-based APIs, enterprise support, licensed deployment blueprints, implementation services, training/certification and partner/referral revenue. Any external offering requires its own terms, privacy/security boundary, support obligations, pricing model and CHLOM rights record.
