## Characters and the Evidence Room

Virality Music characters connect songs, books, families, rooms, timelines, products, and story worlds. The Evidence Room is a creative dossier and publishing format for organizing competing testimony, canon facts, disputed facts, timelines, exhibits, and corrections. It must never be presented as real-world legal proof merely because it uses evidence language.


Protected canon facts, private contributor details, raw source prompts, private likeness evidence, contracts, disputes, and anti-fraud or security records do not belong in the public character object.

## Evidence Room vocabulary

| Label | Public meaning |
| --- | --- |
| canon fact | a fact accepted inside the exact fictional continuity/version |
| testimony | a character's in-story account, which may be incomplete or unreliable |
| disputed | the fictional record intentionally preserves competing interpretations |
| unknown | canon has not established the answer |
| exhibit | a designed story artifact or public-safe dossier element |
| correction | a versioned change or living-witness correction; prior evidence remains preserved |
| no-likeness | the character has no approved public face; absence is intentional |
| archived edition | a prior evidence-edition state preserved for lineage, not current instruction |

## Image and alt-text standard

Meaningful images must help a nonvisual reader understand why the visual is present.

- **Character portrait:** name the fictional character and the story-relevant visual context; do not infer personality, diagnosis, relationship, or history from appearance alone.
- **Book cover:** identify the title and edition, then describe only meaningful visual elements not already conveyed by adjacent text.
- **Evidence exhibit:** identify the exhibit type and what relationship or sequence it communicates; provide a longer text alternative for dense timelines or diagrams.
- **World artwork:** describe location, action, and mood when those details orient the visitor.
- **Decorative texture:** use empty alt text so assistive technology skips it.
- **No-likeness:** provide a text identity card or intentional no-likeness label; do not create a placeholder face.
- **Sensitive or adult art:** place the audience/content warning before the image and avoid using alt text to surprise the reader with graphic detail.

Suggested pattern:

```text
[Fictional character name] in [meaningful setting or action], from [world or edition].
```

Do not begin every description with “image of,” and do not stuff alt text with marketing copy, filenames, credits, or SEO keywords.

## Rights and identity boundary

- A public portrait does not grant merchandising, training, face-replacement, voice-cloning, adaptation, or endorsement rights.
- A fictional character inspired by culture or place is not permission to appropriate real people, communities, traditions, or marks.
- No-likeness and restricted-likeness states must propagate to product, adaptation, media, and generative workflows.
- Evidence Room PDFs may contain story expression, illustrations, typography, arrangement, and character IP with separate rights layers.
- Corrections preserve prior-version lineage; they do not silently erase the earlier edition.
