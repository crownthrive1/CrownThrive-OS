# CHLOM Adversarial Security Lab

**State:** CONTROLLED TEST / RESTRICTED IMPLEMENTATION.

The CHLOM adversarial lab exists to discover control weaknesses before production promotion without exposing production systems or third parties to uncontrolled testing.

## Teams

- **Red Team** — attempts bounded failures against synthetic fixtures, isolated replicas, policy models and sanitized configuration snapshots.
- **Blue Team** — triages findings and designs containment, patch candidates, regression tests and rollback plans.
- **Purple Team** — independently reproduces findings and verifies patch efficacy. It cannot be the originating Red or Blue identity for the same material decision.

All three teams are non-voting and D2 maximum.

## Hard quarantine

The lab contract requires:

- no external-network attack authority;
- no production-write authority;
- no third-party exploitation;
- no credential exfiltration;
- no destructive testing;
- no public disclosure of unresolved vulnerability detail;
- no originator self-verification;
- versioned findings, patches and rollback digests;
- preserved history rather than silent overwrite.

## Deterministic scoring

Two restricted-implementation/public-contract algorithms support the lab:

- `ct.alg.gen7.sres` — Security Resilience Exposure Score;
- `ct.alg.gen7.pes` — Patch Efficacy Score.

Exact weights, thresholds, exploit fixtures and protected findings remain in Vault. Public surfaces may expose only stable IDs, purpose, lifecycle state, sanitized remediation status and digests.

## First lifecycle canary

The controlled-test lifecycle has been exercised using an intentionally vulnerable **synthetic RBAC fixture**. Red detected the deliberate weakness, Blue produced a candidate repair, and Purple independently verified the repair in the lab. Production was not touched and no external network was used.

That canary proves workflow mechanics only. It is not evidence that CrownThrive production had the seeded weakness.

## Promotion boundary

A lab-accepted patch is not automatically a production patch. Production application still requires the target system's normal authority, change-control, rollback, CI/security and independent-readback gates.
