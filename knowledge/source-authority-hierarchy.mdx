# Institutional Source Authority Hierarchy

CrownThrive has accumulated strategy, implementation, public copy, legal/policy material, technical research, vendor studies, operating plans, chat decisions, and live production evidence across multiple eras. The institutional knowledge base needs an explicit precedence model so agents and people do not treat every source as equally authoritative.

## Default precedence

Unless a newer founder-approved Architecture Decision Record explicitly changes the order, use:

1. **Founder-approved current ADRs, adopted governance decisions, and canonical machine-readable registries.**
2. **Verified production evidence and release records** from the system that actually performed the action or holds the transactional state.
3. **Current adopted contracts, policies, licenses, and approved operating procedures** within their defined scope.
4. **Current platform operational state** confirmed by deployment, database, transaction, entitlement, support, or other system-of-record evidence.
5. **Current dated public CrownThrive statements** treated as management/public claims unless independently verified.
6. **Historical business plans, prospectuses, white papers, playbooks, Help Center content, and technical architecture documents.**
7. **Experimental launch guides, vendor evaluations, market studies, prototypes, and future-state research.**
8. **Conversation history and model memory** as contextual retrieval aids only until material claims are tied back to a governed source.

## Scope matters

Authority is contextual. A Stripe payment record can prove payment state but not copyright ownership. A signed license can establish granted rights but not necessarily product delivery. A website can prove what CrownThrive publicly represented at a time, but not automatically that every claimed capability worked in production.

### Source-internal authority language does not bypass precedence

Some recovered CrownThrive sources describe themselves as an **authoritative operating manual**, **Founder Constitution**, **single source of truth**, **master prospectus**, **finalized** package or similar. That wording is itself evidence of the role the source was intended to play at its effective date. It does **not** automatically move the source above current legal/transactional/operational evidence, later founder adjudication, accepted ADRs or the correction graph.

Examples:

- `S90` CHLOM Grand Prospectus may be a consolidated design authority for its source family without proving current chain/token/DAO/legal state;
- `S91` Founders Handbook is strong doctrine/governance lineage, while its own controlling-version language points to the digitally executed version/latest approved Operating Agreement for enforcement;
- `S97` Enterprise Architecture & Governance Manual strongly supports the intended framework hierarchy and platform standards, while actual councils, delegated authority and deployed controls require separate evidence;
- `S100` Holdings plan is authoritative management planning within its scope but does not prove HoldCo formation, audited financial performance or execution completion.

Machine source records therefore store both `source_declared_authority` and `institutional_effective_authority`. The latter is resolved using source class, claim class, effective date, conflict state and this hierarchy.

## Provider and integration three-layer rule

For vendor-backed platforms, maintain three separate source questions:

1. **Provider capability** — what the vendor's current official documentation says the product/API can do.
2. **CrownThrive account entitlement** — what the verified CrownThrive plan, account, scopes, limits and credentials are permitted to use.
3. **CrownThrive deployed integration** — what the current CrownThrive code/configuration/data/webhooks/tests/monitoring actually implement.

Provider documentation is authoritative for provider capability, not for CrownThrive entitlement or deployment. A CrownThrive account screen is evidence of account state, not proof that the application integration is correct. A public page is evidence of representation/reachability, not proof of the underlying API, source repository, rights, payment or entitlement chain.

The same layered rule applies to source recovery:

- a public deployment proves public output;
- a deployable build artifact supports rollback/recovery evidence;
- only an integrity-verified editable repository/source master proves editable source history.

No agent may collapse these layers to fill a missing field.

## Conflict handling

When two sources disagree:

1. preserve both sources;
2. classify their authority and effective dates;
3. identify the precise disputed field or claim;
4. determine whether one supersedes the other;
5. if the conflict is material and cannot be resolved mechanically, create an ADR/decision item;
6. update the canonical registry only after a supportable decision;
7. preserve the old value in correction/version history.

## Dynamic facts

Metrics, product counts, platform statuses, domains, vendor assignments, plan names, prices, team roles, and public capabilities can change rapidly. Dynamic facts should be retrieved from their current system of record whenever possible instead of copied indefinitely into prose.

## Restricted sources

A source can be authoritative without being public. Public Mintlify pages may describe the existence and governance role of restricted records while keeping confidential contents in controlled storage.

## Phase 3 implication

Every registry object and consequential DAIL event should be able to identify the source or evidence class supporting its state. The Orchestrator should use this hierarchy when retrieving conflicting information rather than resolving conflicts from prompt intuition.
