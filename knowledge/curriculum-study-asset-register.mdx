## Curriculum and Study Asset Register

CrownThrive treats educational content as a versioned institutional asset class. Historical Phase 2.x promotion work remains lineage; current governance operates under Phase 3 and preserves source, rights, cultural/theological review, product, commerce, entitlement and deployment states independently.

## Confirmed source families

KJV Visualized and The Sermon Toolkit source material includes study and product families such as:

- The Abrahamic Covenant at a Glance
- The Tabernacle: Space and Symbol
- Servant Leadership Study
- Melanated TV Watch and Study Sheet
- Bless the House Family Watch Kit
- visual studies and scripture resources
- devotionals and reading plans
- sermon-development tools
- ministry-room and study-room resources
- family, youth, teacher, church, and small-group materials
- printables and digital-license products

CrownThriveU and adjacent education properties add courses, cohorts, certifications, instructor-led programs, training, expert knowledge, and career/community progression assets.

## Minimum asset record

Every curriculum/study asset should eventually include `content_id`, canonical title/aliases, family, edition/version, contributors, sources, audience, content type, rights class, theological/editorial review where applicable, lifecycle, commerce/license state, price/entitlement references, custody/master locations, relationships, revision history and public-claim evidence.

## Reuse rule

Before generating new studies or curriculum, agents must search this register for existing material. Existing work may be revised, bundled, relicensed, reformatted, localized or expanded while preserving lineage.

## Separation of truth classes

A curriculum source can be owned and complete while its storefront product is inactive. A product can be listed while its master is incomplete. A study can be historically valid while a newer edition is canonical. Source acceptance, rights, cultural/theological review, product completion, economic activation, entitlement and production deployment must remain separate.
