# CII and ThriveFund Governance

The **CrownThrive Impact Institute (CII)** and **ThriveFund** represent the ecosystem's impact, capability-building, reinvestment, and milestone-accountability functions. Their legal and tax form must not be inferred from program language.

## Institutional role

CII should govern impact criteria, program standards, evidence, public accountability and learning. ThriveFund is the working capital/reinvestment mechanism for designated pilots, scholarships, grants, integrations, milestone funding and other approved ecosystem objectives.

## Capital-routing standard

Each allocation should identify:

- source of funds;
- program or purpose;
- authority and approval;
- recipient or controlled project;
- milestone conditions;
- permitted use;
- payment schedule;
- reporting/evidence requirements;
- holdback, clawback or correction rules where adopted;
- outcome and impact measures;
- legal/tax classification.

## Proof over narrative

Impact claims require measurable evidence. Public reporting should distinguish dollars committed, dollars disbursed, outputs delivered, people served, outcomes observed and longer-term impact. A program announcement is not equivalent to completed impact.

## Relationship to Holdings and CHLOM

The proposed Holdings layer allocates institutional capital. CHLOM governs applicable rights, permissions, eligibility and evidence. CII/ThriveFund governs designated impact purpose and accountability.

## Legal boundary

Do not imply nonprofit, charitable, grant-making, securities, investment-fund or tax-advantaged status unless formally established and reviewed. The operating system should model the program's actual legal state.

## Phase 3 implication

The Approval, Revenue and DAIL services should support designated allocations, milestones, evidence, holds, corrections and reporting without turning agent recommendations into autonomous disbursement authority.
