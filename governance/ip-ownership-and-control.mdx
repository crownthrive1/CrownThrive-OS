# IP Ownership and Control

CrownThrive treats intellectual property as an institutional asset class rather than an afterthought. **Ownership**, **control**, **commercial-use permission**, **copyrightability**, **license authority**, and **possession of a file** are different questions.

## Protected asset classes

The IP inventory can include:

- trademarks, trade names, logos and identity systems;
- frameworks, methods, operating systems and proprietary terminology;
- source code, custom modifications and technical documentation;
- websites, databases and structured registries where protectable;
- books, articles, curricula and training materials;
- music compositions, lyrics, sound recordings, stems and production assets;
- characters, story worlds, scripts, artwork and visual identities;
- photography, video, podcasts and broadcast programming;
- templates, printables, product designs and creative assets;
- domains, handles and contractual platform rights;
- confidential methods, partner data and trade-secret material;
- licenses received from third parties.

## Chain-of-title record

Each material asset should identify creator/contributor, employer/contractor relationship, assignment or license, source evidence, current controller, restrictions, third-party dependencies, derivative relationships, registrations where applicable and commercialization authority.

## AI-assisted works

AI generation does not remove the need for human-authorship, source, voice, similarity, terms-of-service and rights records. Commercial platform permission and statutory copyright protection are distinct. The Rights Registry should preserve both the human contribution and AI provenance rather than describing every output simply as owned or unowned.

## Confidential IP

Public documentation should explain the existence and purpose of protected systems without publishing CrownThrive's confidential Fingerprint implementation, unreleased source, proprietary policy logic, credentials, private economic calibration or other restricted methods.

## Phase 3 implication

CHLOM's Rights Registry becomes the machine-readable chain-of-title and permission spine. Product, content, platform and licensing systems reference that record rather than maintaining inconsistent ownership text.
