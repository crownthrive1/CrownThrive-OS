## Succession and Generational Continuity

CrownThrive's long-term mission requires the institution to survive changes in leadership, vendors, platforms, credentials, and individual availability. The public framework currently refers to this as **Succession \+ Gen. Lock** and emphasizes stewardship continuity, knowledge transfer, and generational protection.

## Continuity domains

A mature succession system should govern:

- legal/entity ownership and control;
- intellectual-property stewardship;
- domain and infrastructure custody;
- administrator and emergency-access succession;
- financial/account-signing authority;
- key contracts and partner relationships;
- source-code and data custody;
- narrative/canon and cultural stewardship;
- governance/approval authority;
- institutional knowledge and training;
- business-continuity and disaster-recovery plans.

## Knowledge transfer

Critical operating knowledge must exist in controlled systems, not solely in one person's memory. Every P0 system should have an owner, backup owner, current documentation, access-recovery method, source registry, dependencies, and operational runbooks.

## Generational safeguards

Long-term guardrails should be expressed as adopted governance and legal instruments rather than slogans. Software can record restrictions, approvals, beneficiaries, roles, and evidence, but it cannot substitute for estate, trust, corporate, tax, or other professional legal planning.

## Phase 3 implication

Organization, authority, credential-custody and asset registries must support successor relationships, delegated authority, emergency access, revocation, effective dates, and durable audit evidence.
