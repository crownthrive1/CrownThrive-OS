# dS-CaaS Governance Layer

CrownThrive currently publishes **dS-CaaS** as an institutional framework associated with always-on compliance enforcement logic, eligibility and permission-rule automation, and compliance proof/audit discipline.

## Source discipline

The current public material establishes the framework label and these functional themes, but the source set reviewed for this pre–Phase 3 audit does **not** establish a sufficiently authoritative expansion of the acronym or prove that one production service already implements the full concept. The operating system therefore preserves the label without inventing an acronym expansion.

For target architecture, CrownThrive may describe dS-CaaS functionally as a **decentralized compliance/governance-as-a-service profile** only when the wording is clearly marked as target architecture rather than a claim about the acronym's historical expansion or current deployment.

## Current functional role

Within the current architecture, dS-CaaS should be treated as a policy/compliance execution concept spanning:

- eligibility checks;
- permissions and entitlements;
- policy and rule-pack version references;
- jurisdiction and applicability profiles;
- compliance evidence and decision receipts;
- decision traceability;
- holds, exceptions, appeals and escalation;
- audit-ready events;
- continuous drift detection;
- bounded self-healing and rollback.

## Relationship to CHLOM

CHLOM governs rights, rules, roles, revenue, records and remedies. dS-CaaS is a **runtime enforcement profile** consuming those governed records. It is not an independent authority that can silently override ownership, qualified legal review, privacy, security, culture, contracts, human-reserved approvals or effective law.

The intended relationship is:

```text
CHLOM canonical rights/rules/roles/evidence
        ↓
dS-CaaS policy compiler + applicability resolver
        ↓
agent/service/tool gate
        ↓
decision + proof + audit event
        ↓
monitoring / correction / bounded self-healing
        ↓
CHLOM DAIL + remedy/correction lineage
```

## Agent-sovereign enforcement

`CT-ADR-GOV-011` makes CrownThrive's own coded governance layer the sovereign automation authority for repository promotion. The five-agent relay uses a 75% quorum (`4/5`), independent Agent D gatekeeper approval, an `85/100` automatic-merge risk threshold, specialist activation and D0–D3 authority classes.

This is a reference pattern for future dS-CaaS execution: a provider's platform-level feature may supply telemetry or enforcement help, but provider controls do not become CrownThrive's institutional authority merely because they exist.

## Self-checking and self-healing

The target dS-CaaS loop is:

```text
detect
→ classify
→ preserve evidence
→ evaluate authority + applicable policy
→ block unsafe promotion
→ propose bounded repair
→ validate original failing control
→ run full institutional/security suite
→ independent verification
→ quorum where required
→ apply or escalate
→ append audit/correction record
```

Self-healing must never weaken the rule that detected the problem, fabricate evidence, erase history, expose credentials, broaden permissions, rotate production credentials without authority, or convert a D3 action into D2.

## Specialist operating cells

Rule packs activate specialists according to the affected domain:

- **Legal and regulatory:** licensing, contracts, rights, securities, token/crypto classification, money-transmission/KYC/AML, sanctions, privacy, consumer protection, franchise and cross-border rules.
- **Security and privacy:** auth, secrets, dependency/supply-chain risk, cryptography, incident response, least privilege and privacy engineering.
- **Operations/SRE:** providers, deployments, domains, backups, continuity, incident recovery, billing and fulfillment.
- **Blockchain/cryptographic protocol:** chains, smart contracts, ledgers, wallets, custody, bridges, oracles, settlement and cryptographic proofs.
- **AI/ML/LLM TEVV:** model/agent evaluation, prompt/rule integrity, autonomous-action boundaries, drift, provenance and human-review controls.
- **IP and licensing:** chain of title, contributor provenance, license eligibility, voice/likeness, derivative/adaptation rights and developer licensing.
- **Finance/tax/treasury:** economic classification, tax treatment, settlement, royalties, reserves, treasury and accounting evidence.
- **Accessibility and consumer protection:** accessibility, disclosures, marketing/endorsement claims and user-impact controls.
- **Global/localization:** jurisdictional overlays, language, regional operating constraints, sanctions/privacy/tax localization and lawful expansion.

Agents may automate evidence collection, policy evaluation, deterministic repair and recommendations. They do not impersonate counsel, regulators, auditors or other licensed professionals when those roles are legally required.

## Policy chaining and decentralized target architecture

The long-range target is a versioned **policy graph** rather than one monolithic rules file. A decision may chain:

```text
identity + role
→ asset/right
→ jurisdiction
→ consent/privacy
→ product/license
→ economic rule
→ security posture
→ provider/deployment state
→ action eligibility
→ evidence/receipt/remedy
```

Phase 9 may evaluate cryptographic attestations, decentralized identifiers/verifiable credentials, public anchoring, multiparty settlement, blockchain adapters, poly-chain interoperability or tokenized mechanisms **only where a validated problem justifies them**. Private evidence and PII do not become public-chain data by default.

A token, coin, chain, wallet, smart contract, cryptographic proof or decentralized registry appearing in CHLOM research is not evidence of a current CrownThrive cryptocurrency, security, legal tender, registered offering, money-transmission authority, production wallet or compliant public sale.

## Licensing and CrownThrive control

The dS-CaaS/CHLOM implementation may become licensable infrastructure under the Phase 6 developer/IP economy, with CrownThrive-controlled specifications, versioning, certification, API/MCP/SDK access, adapter rules, policy-pack distribution and usage evidence. Public repositories may carry public-safe specifications and validation code; secrets, private evidence, restricted policy internals and protected IP remain in their governed private homes.

External licensees receive only the rights expressly granted by an effective agreement. Open visibility of a specification does not waive CrownThrive intellectual-property, trademark, trade-secret or licensing controls.

## Phase progression

- **Phase 2 / 2.99:** recover definitions, rules, evidence, roles, authority and machine policy; do not claim the decentralized target is live.
- **Phase 3:** implement the executable institutional core: approval/rules/evidence/DAIL services, agent controls, policy decision records and bounded automation.
- **Phase 4:** federate certified adapters and distinct platform systems without collapsing authority.
- **Phase 5:** prove governed economic lanes.
- **Phase 6:** activate controlled external licensing/developer access where rights and economics are ready.
- **Phases 7–8:** extend controls into phygital/regional operations and Holdings/capital structures only with their own gates.
- **Phase 9:** evaluate/activate advanced decentralized, cryptographic, blockchain and interoperable capabilities only after legal/security/privacy/recovery gates.
- **Phase 10:** preserve policy, source, keys, recovery, succession, localization and provider-exit continuity across generations and jurisdictions.

## Phase 3 treatment

Phase 3 should not create a service named dS-CaaS merely because the framework exists. First define authoritative terminology, intended scope, policy inputs/outputs, human-review boundaries, specialist activation, evidence model and failure semantics. The initial Approval Service + Rules Registry + DAIL may implement much of the behavior while preserving stable conceptual identity.
