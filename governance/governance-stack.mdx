## Governance Stack

CrownThrive governance is layered because no single framework can answer every institutional question.

## Core layers

| Layer | Governing question |
| --- | --- |
| Founder / Managing Member authority | Who holds final institutional authority under the current operating structure? |
| Proposed Holdings / Portfolio Council | Which assets are funded, prioritized, merged, licensed, reserved, acquired, or sunset? |
| Cultural Imprint Engine | Is the use culturally, narratively, aesthetically, and contextually aligned? |
| CHLOM | Who owns or controls it, what is permitted, under what terms, and what evidence exists? |
| Master Narrative Ledger | What is the approved current description, terminology, canon, and historical lineage? |
| CII / ThriveFund governance | What impact criteria, designated allocations, evidence, and public accountability apply? |
| InfoSec / Privacy | Is access, data handling, signing, security, retention, and incident behavior acceptable? |
| Support / Knowledge governance | What source and procedure should people and agents rely on? |
| Operating Review | What decisions are required based on current execution, incidents, revenue, renewals, content, support and risk? |

## Decision rights

Every material decision should identify:

- decision owner;
- required reviewers;
- evidence;
- affected assets and actors;
- policy/rights basis;
- effective date;
- reversible versus irreversible nature;
- implementation owner;
- communication and documentation requirements.

## Governance is executable

Phase 3 should not reduce governance to a page of principles. Approval rules, authority, policies, rights, lifecycle states and audit events must become machine-addressable while preserving human decision authority for material actions.

## Conflict rule

If two governance layers conflict, execution pauses. A technically allowed action may still fail cultural, rights, privacy, legal, security, portfolio, or financial review.
