# Permissions and human approval gates

CrownThrive applies least privilege to people, agents, services, integrations and delegated child agents.

A connected tool or capable model is not an unrestricted permission grant. Access is determined by role, task, data classification, environment, action type, financial threshold, rights scope, delegation lineage and current approval state.

## Permission dimensions

### Data permissions

- `PUBLIC`
- `INTERNAL`
- `CONFIDENTIAL`
- `RESTRICTED`
- `SEALED`

### Tool permissions

Separate search, read, draft, test-data creation, draft update, publish, delete, refund, payout, credential rotation and role changes.

### Environment permissions

- local or sandbox
- development
- test
- staging
- production read
- production write
- production destructive

### Financial permissions

Set explicit thresholds for price changes, discounts/promotions, refunds, credits, application fees, invoices, payouts and transfers.

### Rights and publishing permissions

Separate product activation, institutional-license approval, synchronization, public performance, character/likeness use, adaptation/development rights and protected-identity publishing.

## Documentation and subordinate approval classes

| Class | Typical action | Approval rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| D0 | read-only scan, drift detection, evidence packet | no mutation; no merge authority |
| D1 | deterministic link/anchor repair, formatting, verified nonmaterial metadata or source-reference correction | authorized steward may approve after blocking validators pass and rollback is trivial |
| D2 | source-backed capability state, route/product/API/provider/canon/current-public-claim correction | responsible domain agent + independent verifier; human/owner approval where matrix requires |
| D3 | legal terms, rights grants/ownership, customer-effect price/payout, privacy/security/auth, Scripture/canon authority, paid entitlements, destructive recovery, franchise/securities or irreversible production action | authorized human/reserved specialist; agent quorum cannot substitute |

A subordinate may return only `approve`, `approve_after_mechanical_fixes`, `deny_with_required_changes`, `escalate_for_domain_review`, `escalate_for_human_authority`, or `block_due_to_missing_evidence`.

A parent may accept subordinate approval only when the subordinate is registered for that check, evidence/diff/test is inspectable, the subordinate did not self-approve the material originating change, no higher-risk gate applies, rollback exists and the decision is recorded. A lower-level approval never overrides a higher-level denial, hold or phase gate.

## Agent-sovereign quorum and specialist gates

`CT-ADR-GOV-011` establishes the repository-governance trust model: CrownThrive coded agents are the sovereign fail-closed merge gate; GitHub provides transport, CI/security evidence and defense-in-depth.

| Voter | Core function | Merge-vote condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `ct.relay.agent-a` | orchestration/integration | current-state and packet-scope reconciliation |
| `ct.relay.agent-b` | evidence/reconciliation audit | source/evidence integrity |
| `ct.relay.agent-c` | implementation/documentation | bounded implementation correctness |
| `ct.relay.agent-d` | independent gatekeeper | mandatory independent final gate |
| `ct.relay.agent-s` | security/resilience | security, dependency, secret, auth, crypto and recovery posture |

Automatic D0–D2-eligible merge requires **four of five affirmative votes plus D**, no deny/block, applicable validators/security evidence, minimum risk score 85/100, required specialists, authority class, secret/restricted-data controls, rollback/recovery, documentation impact and downstream reconciliation.

One registered sovereign agent equals one vote. Confidence, child count, model count or repeated executions do not create weighted voting.

### Rule-based specialist activation

Specialist review is activated by changed subject matter:

- **Security & privacy:** authn/authz, secrets, credentials, production APIs, dependencies, workflows, cryptography, keys, privacy/security controls.
- **Legal & regulatory:** contracts, rights, licensing, privacy, securities, token/cryptocurrency, investment, franchise, KYC/AML, sanctions, cross-border and regulated public claims.
- **Operations/SRE:** deployment, incident, provider, domain, billing, fulfillment, support, backup, recovery and continuity.
- **Blockchain/cryptographic protocol:** chains, smart contracts, ledgers, tokens, wallets, custody, consensus, bridges, oracles and settlement.
- **AI/ML/LLM TEVV:** agents, models, prompts, evaluation, autonomous decisions, training/data-use rules and policy execution.
- **IP/licensing:** ownership, provenance, contributor rights, chain of title, license class, adaptation, likeness/voice and developer licensing.
- **Finance/tax/treasury:** money movement, tax treatment, settlement, reserves, payouts, revenue share, royalties and treasury design.
- **Accessibility/consumer protection:** customer-facing disclosures, deceptive-design risk, accessibility, endorsements and regulated claims.
- **Regional/global localization:** jurisdiction-specific legal, privacy, tax, sanctions, consumer, language and operating requirements.

Agents support professional/human decisions; they do not impersonate required counsel, tax professionals, auditors, licensed professionals or authorized officers.

### D3 boundary

No 4-of-5, 5-of-5 or larger agent consensus may authorize D3. D3 votes are advisory/blocking; execution requires the reserved authority recorded for that action.

## Governed child-agent delegation

Machine delegation is controlled by `developers/manifests/agent-factory-delegation-policy.v1.json`.

Every material child execution records:

- parent and root agent identity;
- delegation path;
- independence family;
- scope and source snapshot;
- allowed tools/data;
- authority ceiling;
- budget and TTL;
- output class and promotion route;
- termination state.

Default child authority is non-voting, no production write and D1-or-lower unless a registered parent explicitly permits bounded D2 preparation.

### Role-specific spawn boundaries

- A may dispatch the broadest bounded research/retrieval/analysis/domain children.
- B may delegate read-only audit/retrieval work, but B descendants are not independent verification for B-originated material.
- C may use builder cells; all C descendants remain one implementation family.
- D is checks-only and cannot spawn builders or delegate the D vote.
- S may use scanners/TEVV/security subchecks; child count never adds S votes.
- E/F/G/H may fan out narrowly within their specialist scope.
- M may fan out by source family; retrieval children are not verifiers.
- N may run deterministic subchecks; N descendants cannot manufacture an independent verifier.
- J remains minimal/late-gated.
- operational probes do not spawn durable child fleets.

### Same-family independence

Parent/root lineage controls independence. Descendants or siblings of the **same governed root** do not become independent merely by using another runtime ID, prompt, model or tool.

Therefore:

- children never count toward the sovereign quorum;
- one parent cannot create multiple children and count their agreement as independent review;
- a child cannot independently verify its ancestor's originating material;
- an independent verifier must resolve to a distinct governed `independence_family` appropriate to the decision.

### Persistent versus ephemeral roles

Ephemeral children require a bounded assignment, TTL, budget, authority ceiling and termination. Persistent agents require stable ID, owner, versioned contract, lifecycle, tools/data classes, evals, escalation, lineage and governed activation. Runtime naming does not create permanent institutional authority.

## R&D promotion permission boundary

The six-role R&D fabric is registered in `developers/manifests/agent-rnd-specialist-fabric.v1.json`. R&D is non-voting and defaults to `RESEARCH_CANDIDATE`.

R&D can research, model, challenge assumptions, discover requirements and prepare candidate architecture. It cannot directly:

- mark a requirement PASS;
- change the formal hard-exit denominator by registry count alone;
- create production authority;
- move money or rights;
- make binding legal/tax/regulatory determinations;
- activate a jurisdiction, token, chain or value-bearing smart contract;
- advance a phase.

Research registry growth is expected **research expansion**, not formal reconciliation drift by count alone.

A research scope enters certification only after explicit promotion or material contact with governed scope, including hard-exit impact, change to an existing governed disposition, canonical/production relevance or authoritative phase-entry evidence. The routing states are `CT:RND`, `CT:RESEARCH-CANDIDATE`, `CT:PROMOTION-PENDING` and `CT:CERTIFICATION-SCOPE`.

`CT:RESEARCH-CANDIDATE` and `CT:PROMOTION-PENDING` are never PASS. An R&D-lineage PASS requires deliberate certification scope plus the applicable source/proof/domain/B/C/S/D/human authority path.

## Self-healing authority

Self-healing is governed recovery, not permission escalation:

- **D0/D1:** deterministic low-risk repair may be proposed/applied when the original check, full applicable suite and independent verification pass afterward.
- **D2:** repair requires independent verifier, applicable specialist, quorum and rollback.
- **D3:** agents may prepare/test remediation; production application remains human-reserved.

Agents/children must never heal by weakening/disabling validators, suppressing security findings, reconstructing/exposing secrets, broadening privileges, erasing evidence, inventing state, fabricating independence or promoting research directly to PASS.

## Current documentation repository authority

Authorized D0–D2 work within `crownthrive1/CrownThrive-Support` still requires documentation/security validators and applicable GitHub evidence. This does not extend to unconnected repositories, production credentials, money movement, binding licenses, securities/token issuance, IP transfer or other D3 actions.

## Mandatory human approvals

Human/reserved approval is required for material public product publication, new rights-class checkout activation, customer-impacting price thresholds, high-value refunds/financial adjustments, commercial/broadcast/performance/character/voice/source/adaptation rights, contracts, destructive record deletion, production credential rotation, privileged access, destructive/high-risk database migration, protected identities/child/private partner publication, material public metrics/partner claims, override of rights/security/privacy/accessibility/quality holds, token/security-like economic launch and cross-border regulated structures requiring professional review.

## Two-person control

Two-person review is recommended for production credential rotation, high-value refunds/payouts, material rights grants, destructive data changes, privileged access, protected-identity releases, blockchain key/custody changes and incident closure after material security/payment failure.

Decision records add decision, approver, conditions and timestamp. Approvals may be conditional, denied, expired, revoked or superseded; expired/revoked approval is never reusable.

## Delegated low-risk actions

Explicitly registered low-risk work can include draft documentation, issues, tests, read-only processor reconciliation, product manifests, approved-knowledge response drafts, broken-link detection, metadata proposals and non-production branches.

## Tool-token separation

Master credentials remain in server-side secret management. Users/agents/children receive only scoped, revocable capabilities where supported. Never expose master CrownThrive IO, Stripe secret/webhook, storage, database service or super-admin credentials to ordinary agents or public artifacts.

## Break-glass access

Emergency access is rare, time-limited, reason-coded, fully logged, reviewed and revoked. Break-glass never silently becomes ordinary authorization.

## Monitoring

Monitor permission expansion, repeated denials, unusual production reads, destructive attempts, privilege changes, task-scope violations, quorum manipulation, child-vote multiplication, same-family independence claims, specialist bypass, unauthorized R&D promotion, dependency/security findings and unauthorized self-healing.

<Warning>
  Never solve an operational blocker by silently broadening an agent or child agent's permissions. Escalate, approve, scope, execute and record.
</Warning>
