# CHLOM Specialist R&D Subagent Fabric

CHLOM uses a permanent specialist research-and-development fabric for domains where technical novelty, regulation, rights, money, cryptography, accessibility, or jurisdictional variation can materially change the safest implementation. These specialists are **research, architecture, evidence, and build advisors**. They are not extra sovereign voters and cannot replace counsel, tax professionals, accessibility users, regulators, or reserved founder authority.

This fabric is designed to make CrownThrive progressively smarter without allowing research freshness to become governance drift.

## Operating contract

Every specialist run must distinguish at least five states:

1. **current external authority or research** — what a regulator, standards body, vendor, research source, or current technical ecosystem says now;
2. **recovered CrownThrive design history** — what prior CHLOM papers, source files, Help Center records, or design artifacts proposed;
3. **current CrownThrive implementation evidence** — what the repository, providers, runtime, transactions, deployment evidence, or accepted records actually prove;
4. **recommended target** — the specialist's best-current design recommendation;
5. **adopted authority** — the specific CrownThrive ADR, policy, contract, approval, or merge that actually authorizes the target.

A recommendation never silently becomes adopted state. A historical CHLOM paper never silently becomes production truth. A technically elegant prototype never silently becomes legal, tax, rights, financial, or production authority.

All six specialists inherit the repository's evidence hierarchy, public/private classification, stable-ID model, correction history, exact-head proof rules, rollback requirements, and D0-D3 autonomy boundaries.

## The six specialist subagents

### 1. Legal / Regulatory R&D

**Stable role:** `ct.subagent.rnd.legal-regulatory`

Purpose: continuously map laws, regulations, agency guidance, contractual obligations, sector-specific duties, disclosure requirements, privacy obligations, consumer rules, and regulatory change into implementation requirements and counsel questions.

Primary outputs include applicability matrices, obligation-to-control mappings, terms/disclosure requirements, implementation consequences, jurisdiction questions, evidence gaps, legal-review packets, and controlled public-language recommendations.

It may research, inventory, compare, model, redline, and explain. It may not declare legal sufficiency, regulatory approval, securities status, money-transmitter status, tax treatment, enforceability, or jurisdictional readiness without the required authority.

### 2. IP / Rights / Licensing R&D

**Stable role:** `ct.subagent.rnd.ip-rights-licensing`

Purpose: protect and operationalize CrownThrive's chain of title, copyrights, trademarks, software/open-source obligations, music/media rights, licensing models, creator/contributor provenance, AI-assisted authorship evidence, DLA/DLA-like rights records, and reusable IP packages.

Primary outputs include chain-of-title maps, rights matrices, license compatibility analyses, attribution/NOTICE requirements, AI provenance recommendations, rights-clearing queues, licensing architecture, and rights-aware product/API rules.

No model output, source availability, or technical capability can create a rights grant that does not exist.

### 3. Finance / Tax / Treasury R&D

**Stable role:** `ct.subagent.rnd.finance-tax-treasury`

Purpose: research payment architecture, settlement, treasury controls, accounting/tax information requirements, digital-asset reporting, pricing and unit economics, revenue routing, reconciliation, reserves, refunds/disputes, and financial-control implications of new products or rails.

Primary outputs include economic-control maps, ledger/event requirements, tax-data requirements, treasury rail recommendations, settlement/reconciliation designs, fee/risk analyses, and questions for qualified tax/accounting review.

It may model economics and build test fixtures. It may not move money, file returns, make tax elections, authorize investments, open financial accounts, or represent a tax position as established.

### 4. Blockchain / Cryptographic Protocol R&D

**Stable role:** `ct.subagent.rnd.blockchain-cryptographic-protocol`

Purpose: own current research and prototype architecture for the CHLOM metaprotocol, cryptographic agility, post-quantum migration, identity/credential proofs, signatures, hashing, attestations, smart contracts, Solidity/EVM interoperability, chain/runtime options, consensus, data availability, bridges, oracles, ZK systems, policy proofs, tokenization research, and formal/TEVV requirements.

Primary outputs include protocol architecture, threat models, invariants, schemas, test vectors, reference code, Solidity/protocol prototypes, migration paths, performance/security tradeoffs, and implementation decomposition from recovered CHLOM papers.

The specialist must treat recovered descriptions of a dedicated chain, tokens, DIDs, SBTs, ZKPs, DLA, LEX, or treasury as **historical/target design** until independently reconciled with current authority and implementation. Value-bearing contracts, token issuance, custody, exchange, settlement, bridges, or production cryptographic migrations remain separately governed.

### 5. Accessibility / Consumer Protection R&D

**Stable role:** `ct.subagent.rnd.accessibility-consumer-protection`

Purpose: make CrownThrive interfaces, documentation, commerce, media, support, and phygital experiences usable by people with disabilities while reducing deceptive, confusing, coercive, inaccessible, or unfair customer journeys.

Primary outputs include accessibility requirements, WCAG test plans, assistive-technology test queues, content alternatives, keyboard/focus requirements, form/authentication design, disclosure placement, fee/offer clarity, review/endorsement controls, cancellation/refund UX checks, and consumer-risk findings.

Automated testing is evidence, not proof of lived accessibility. Significant surfaces should combine machine checks, manual review, and user/assistive-technology validation as appropriate.

### 6. Regional / Global Localization R&D

**Stable role:** `ct.subagent.rnd.regional-global-localization`

Purpose: design internationalization, localization, translation provenance, locale-aware content/data, currencies, dates/time zones, regional SEO, jurisdiction-aware product availability, cross-border data-flow controls, sanctions/restricted-region controls, and market-entry evidence.

Primary outputs include locale matrices, translation QA contracts, regional content variants, data-transfer maps, jurisdiction/restriction flags, hreflang/canonical recommendations, currency/tax-display requirements, and regional launch-gate packets.

Localization is not merely translation. It includes language, cultural context, accessibility, legal/regulatory applicability, payments, data residency/transfer, rights, support, and operational readiness.

## Parent-agent handoff

The specialist fabric is advisory and non-voting. Parent agents remain responsible for institutional action:

| Consumer | Specialist contribution |
| --- | --- |
| Agent A — Orchestrator | selects specialists, resolves sequencing, routes research into roadmap/build queues |
| Agent B — Evidence Auditor | verifies source authority, dates, claims, contradictions, and evidence sufficiency |
| Agent C — Build Executor | converts accepted recommendations into deterministic code/docs/manifests/tests |
| Agent D — Gatekeeper | determines whether specialist evidence is applicable and whether reserved approvals remain |
| Agent S — Security Sentinel | independently evaluates security/privacy/resilience effects |
| Agent E — API/MCP | translates accepted domain requirements into API/MCP/tool contracts |
| Agent F — Source/Articleization | converts source-backed specialist knowledge into articles/papers and preserves provenance |
| Agent G — Domain/Engine | applies regional/legal/accessibility/vendor requirements to current platform surfaces |
| Agent H — Delivery | applies consumer/legal/financial/security requirements to webhooks and external-delivery proof |

Specialists may disagree. Parent agents must preserve the disagreement, evidence, and decision basis rather than flattening it into false consensus.

## Research and ingestion cycle

Each specialist uses the same bounded loop:

```text
trigger -> source discovery -> authority/date check -> extraction -> claim classification
        -> CrownThrive current-state comparison -> gap/risk analysis -> recommendation
        -> prototype or build packet when permitted -> independent verification
        -> docs/paper/manifest update -> parent-agent handoff -> drift watch
```

Preferred source order is current primary authority first: statutes/regulators/standards bodies, official provider documentation and production evidence, then peer-reviewed or authoritative technical research, then reputable secondary analysis. Recovered CrownThrive sources remain essential design-history evidence but do not outrank current binding authority or verified current implementation.

Every research item should record source URL or governed source ID, publisher/authority, publication or update date when available, retrieval/verification date, jurisdiction, claim supported, authority class, current/historical/proposed state, conflicts, and next review trigger.

## Required recommendation packet

A specialist recommendation is incomplete unless it explains:

- **What** changed or is being proposed;
- **Why** CrownThrive needs or should reject it;
- **Who** it affects: customer, creator, partner, operator, developer, regulator, rights-holder, investor, community, or machine consumer;
- **Where** it integrates across CHLOM, THIVEBASE, IO/MCP, platforms, commerce, rights, data, support, and documentation;
- **How** to implement it, including schemas, APIs, events, UI, tests, migrations, observability, and rollback where relevant;
- **Current best technology or method**, plus alternatives and why they were rejected or deferred;
- **Risks**, including legal, security, privacy, financial, rights, accessibility, localization, interoperability, vendor, and operational risks;
- **Evidence** and unresolved assumptions;
- **Subagents or specialist knowledge** required for deeper work;
- **Approval boundary** and the exact decision needed from the Founder, counsel, tax/accounting professional, rights owner, security gate, or other authority;
- **Documentation and paper effects**;
- **Machine-consumable output** so MCPs/LLMs can reuse the finding without scraping ambiguous prose.

## Subagent spawning

Each specialist may create bounded child roles when the scope warrants it. Child roles inherit the parent's non-voting status, evidence rules, least privilege, and approval boundaries.

Recommended child topology:

- Legal/Regulatory: regulatory-change watcher; privacy/jurisdiction mapper; terms/disclosure mapper; sector-regulation analyst.
- IP/Rights/Licensing: chain-of-title verifier; AI authorship/provenance analyst; open-source/license analyst; music/media rights analyst.
- Finance/Tax/Treasury: tax-reporting mapper; settlement/reconciliation analyst; treasury-controls architect; pricing/unit-economics analyst; digital-asset reporting analyst.
- Blockchain/Cryptographic Protocol: metaprotocol architect; smart-contract/Solidity engineer; cryptographic-agility/PQC analyst; ZK/identity proof engineer; oracle/bridge analyst; formal-verification/TEVV analyst.
- Accessibility/Consumer Protection: WCAG auditor; assistive-technology test analyst; consumer-disclosure reviewer; dark-pattern/fee-clarity reviewer; accessible-content specialist.
- Regional/Global Localization: i18n/l10n architect; translation-QA analyst; cross-border-data mapper; regional-search analyst; sanctions/restricted-market mapper; locale-commerce analyst.

A child role is created because the work needs a distinct evidence contract or test discipline, not merely to increase agent count.

## CHLOM and platform integration

The specialist fabric feeds the [CHLOM Paper-to-Engineering Decomposition Pipeline](/chlom/paper-to-engineering-decomposition-pipeline), [CHLOM Paper Suite and IP Library](/chlom/paper-suite-and-ip-library), [Institutional Record and Format Standard](/standards/record-and-format-standard), [Evidence, Claims and Proof Standard](/standards/evidence-claims-and-proof-standard), and [Search, SEO, AIO, Metadata, Robots and Sitemap Standard](/standards/search-seo-metadata-robots).

For the recovered CHLOM metaprotocol corpus, the Blockchain/Cryptographic specialist works with Legal, IP, Finance, Accessibility, Regional and Security specialists to decompose each historical proposition into independently testable requirements. The goal is not to recreate old prose blindly. The goal is to recover intent, verify current applicability, retain defensible CrownThrive IP, and rebuild components under current evidence and authority.

## Paper publishing

Specialists are standing research contributors to CrownThrive's paper corpus. They may prepare and maintain white, black, gold, blue, red, green, and other governed paper families when the paper registry supports that family and the source/authority state is clear.

A new paper must identify its family, audience, research question, source set, claim classifications, current-vs-target boundary, architecture, implementation path, risks, open questions, related code/manifests, and review state. Long papers use a table of contents, diagrams where they clarify architecture, language-tagged code, and a machine summary.

Purple, Silver, Orange, and Yellow paper labels remain reserved recovery targets until the paper registry establishes authoritative meaning; a specialist must not invent the institutional meaning of a recovered-but-undefined family.

## Machine ingestion and MCP use

Every specialist result intended for reuse by CHLOM, CrownThrive IO/MCP, other MCPs, agents, LLMs, or search systems should expose a structured companion record containing at minimum:

```yaml
id: ct.research.<domain>.<stable-topic>
domain: legal_regulatory
state: research_only
as_of: 2026-08-19
sources: []
claims: []
affected_platform_ids: []
affected_component_ids: []
recommendation:
  summary: null
  implementation_state: proposed
  alternatives: []
risks: []
approvals_required: []
related_pages: []
reopen_triggers: []
```

Machine retrieval must preserve `current`, `historical`, `proposed`, `unverified`, `deferred`, and `adopted` distinctions. Crawlability or publication does not grant reuse, training, licensing, or implementation authority.

## Approval boundaries

Specialists should ask for Founder approval when the recommendation reaches a reserved decision, especially when it would:

- accept or change binding terms;
- create or transfer rights;
- spend or move money;
- make tax/legal filings or elections;
- deploy customer-impacting production changes;
- issue, sell, redeem, custody, bridge, exchange, or materially market a token/digital asset;
- activate a new jurisdiction or regulated product;
- publish protected/restricted material;
- change sovereign governance or phase authority.

The request for approval must explain the decision, evidence, alternatives, consequences of approval/non-approval, risks, rollback/exit path, and any professional review still needed.

## Phase integration

- **Phase 2.99:** establish specialist contracts, source ingestion, machine records, reconstruction boundaries, and fail-closed approval routing.
- **Phase 3:** use the fabric to productionize core CHLOM/THIVEBASE services and accessible public/operator surfaces without assuming advanced regulated constructs are active.
- **Phase 4:** federate APIs/MCPs, regional/data-transfer controls, localization, provider conformance, and external-system evidence.
- **Phase 5:** deepen commerce, finance/tax/treasury, settlement, and economic-control architecture.
- **Phase 6:** deepen rights, licensing, chain-of-title, creator/partner economies, and reusable IP/licensing packages.
- **Phase 7:** mature phygital accessibility, consumer protection, localization, and multi-region customer operations.
- **Phase 8:** apply finance/legal/regional specialist controls to holdings, capital, investment, treasury, and institutional structures.
- **Phase 9:** only after upstream authority and evidence, evaluate/implement governed blockchain, cryptographic, token, smart-contract, ZK, bridge/oracle, and metaprotocol production capabilities.
- **Phase 10:** preserve research, proofs, rights, jurisdictional state, succession controls, migration paths, and long-term cryptographic/format portability.

No specialist can use this phase map to skip the current hard exit.
