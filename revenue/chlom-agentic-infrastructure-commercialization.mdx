# CHLOM & Agentic Infrastructure Commercialization

CrownThrive is not limited to selling individual AI agents. The commercial opportunity is the ability to help institutions **deploy, govern, verify, recover, audit and scale AI labor and machine-executed policy without transferring uncontrolled authority to models or vendors**.

The offer catalog is machine-readable at `developers/manifests/chlom-agentic-commercial-offer-catalog.v1.json`. Every offer inherits the [IP Disclosure & Commercialization Gate](/governance/ip-disclosure-and-commercialization-gate).

## Commercial doctrine

CrownThrive should generally retain ownership/control of the core institutional architecture and monetize through:

- software/service access;
- implementation and migration services;
- enterprise and reference-architecture licenses;
- SDK/API/MCP/A2A licenses;
- capability-pallet and agent-pack licenses;
- support/update subscriptions;
- training and CrownThrive credentials;
- conformance/certification services when the standards program is mature;
- OEM/white-label licenses;
- marketplace/partner economics;
- managed governance and dS-CaaS operations.

Customers receive the capability, authorized implementation rights and support appropriate to the offer. They do not receive CrownThrive sovereign authority, private production identities, credentials, restricted evidence or the trade-secret kernel merely because they bought a package.

## Offer-state model

Every offer progresses independently:

```text
concept
→ candidate
→ packaged
→ rights_cleared
→ pricing_authorized
→ fulfillment_certified
→ checkout_staged
→ live
```

Current Phase 2.99 work can aggressively build and package **candidate** offers. It cannot infer a public price, package license or live checkout from market opportunity alone.

## Product family 1 — CHLOM Enterprise License

**Buyer:** enterprises, institutions and platform operators.

**Value:** an organization licenses an approved CHLOM implementation profile covering agent governance, policy, evidence, rights, approvals, workflow and institutional continuity.

**Potential commercial forms:** annual enterprise license, deployment license, named-environment license, site/organization license, support/update subscription and implementation services.

**Protected boundary:** public doctrine and licensed implementation may be delivered; CrownThrive-specific sovereign authority and trade-secret kernel remain excluded.

**Earliest revenue phase:** Phase 5, with deeper developer licensing in Phase 6.

## Product family 2 — Governed Reference Architecture License

**Buyer:** architects, engineering teams, consultancies and institutions that want to implement internally.

**Value:** sanitized reference architecture, schemas, governance patterns, diagrams, deployment requirements and conformance checklists.

This is one of the easiest assets to package early because it can be derived from the institutional documentation while retaining private kernel separation.

**Earliest revenue phase:** Phase 5.

## Product family 3 — Agent Governance Runtime

**Buyer:** enterprises operating internal or customer-facing agents.

**Value:** managed control plane for agent identities, roles, delegation, authority ceilings, approvals, evidence, policy decisions, audit history, failure handling and recovery.

**Commercial forms:** SaaS subscription, private deployment, managed instance, enterprise support and implementation.

**Protected boundary:** the service exposes governed interfaces and reports; internal scoring/calibration, private policy implementation and security controls can remain trade-secret controlled.

**Build:** Phase 3 onward. **Revenue activation:** Phase 5 onward. **Federated enterprise scale:** Phase 12.

## Product family 4 — Agent Factory SDK

**Buyer:** developers, enterprise engineering teams and qualified implementation partners.

**Value:** reusable developer contracts for parent/child agent delegation, child authority ceilings, budgets, TTL, independence lineage, termination, evidence and promotion routes.

**Package:** SDK, schemas, examples, validators, test fixtures and documentation.

Potentially novel technical mechanisms must pass the patent/trade-secret disclosure gate before detailed public release.

**Build:** Phase 3. **Developer monetization:** Phase 6.

## Product family 5 — CHLOM Capability Pallets

A capability pallet packages one governed institutional function behind a stable contract.

Initial commercial candidates include:

- Evidence Reconciliation Pallet;
- Phase Gate Pallet;
- API/MCP Certification Pallet;
- Credential Continuity Pallet;
- Webhook Integrity Pallet;
- Vendor/Engine Watch Pallet;
- Documentation Impact Pallet;
- Rights/Authority Pallet;
- Delegation Lineage Pallet.

Each pallet can have independent versioning, license, implementation profile, test suite, support lifecycle and conformance state.

**Earliest revenue phase:** Phase 5; developer licensing matures in Phase 6.

## Product family 6 — Institutional Agent Packs

These are sanitized organizational roles derived from the CrownThrive agent system, not unrestricted copies of internal operational authority.

Candidate packs:

- Orchestrator/Chief-of-Staff Pack;
- Evidence Auditor Pack;
- Build/Documentation Executor Pack;
- Independent Gatekeeper Pack;
- Security/Resilience Pack;
- API/MCP Specialist Pack;
- Source/Knowledge Reconciliation Pack;
- Vendor/Domain Certification Pack;
- Webhook/Delivery Certification Pack;
- Continuity/Recovery Pack;
- R&D Specialist Packs.

A commercial pack can include role contracts, example prompts, schemas, evals, negative tests, runbooks and extension points. CrownThrive may withhold internal master prompts or configurations when they embody protected operating know-how.

**Earliest revenue phase:** Phase 5.

## Product family 7 — CHLOM Conformance & Certification

**Buyer:** vendors, enterprise implementers, developers and implementation partners.

**Value:** independent verification that an implementation satisfies an adopted CHLOM standard/profile.

Potential revenue includes certification assessment, retesting, remediation, surveillance/renewal, training and implementation-partner qualification.

This is **not active today**. A future certification or certification-mark program requires mature standards, non-self-certification rules, legal/brand review and independent decision integrity.

**Build/standards maturity:** Phase 6 onward. **Primary commercialization:** Phase 13.

## Product family 8 — Implementation & Migration Services

**Buyer:** institutions that want CrownThrive to design or install the governance architecture.

Services can include:

- institutional discovery and requirements mapping;
- existing-agent inventory and risk classification;
- policy/authority model design;
- API/MCP/A2A integration;
- evidence/DAIL architecture;
- migration and rollout planning;
- validation and acceptance;
- operator/developer enablement.

This service line can later attach to every software/license product as a high-value professional-services layer.

**Earliest revenue phase:** Phase 5; institutional replication scales in Phase 11.

## Product family 9 — Managed Governance / dS-CaaS

CrownThrive operates the control function for the customer instead of merely licensing components.

Managed services can include:

- continuous policy/evidence reconciliation;
- agent and tool-governance monitoring;
- exception/approval packets;
- provider and integration drift;
- rights/license state maintenance;
- documentation and change-control reconciliation;
- compliance/control evidence preparation;
- incident and recovery governance.

**Build:** Phase 3. **Revenue:** Phase 5. **Advanced autonomous operations:** Phase 14.

## Product family 10 — CrownThriveU Agentic Governance Training

Training tracks can eventually include:

- Governed Agent Operator;
- Agentic Systems Developer;
- Evidence & Reconciliation Auditor;
- Security/TEVV Reviewer;
- CHLOM Implementation Architect;
- Rights/Licensing Operator;
- Enterprise Governance Lead.

CrownThrive credentials must remain clearly CrownThrive-issued credentials unless a separate accreditation, licensure or professional-recognition process establishes something stronger.

**Earliest revenue phase:** Phase 5.

## Product family 11 — OEM / White-Label Licensing

Approved vendors may embed selected CHLOM capabilities behind their own product interface under an OEM agreement.

Commercial terms may eventually include minimum commitments, per-tenant or usage pricing, support, update rights, attribution/co-branding, audit/conformance requirements and restrictions on reverse engineering or redistribution where legally appropriate.

**Earliest revenue phase:** Phase 6; enterprise federation scale in Phase 12.

## Product family 12 — MCP / A2A Governance Infrastructure

This packages CrownThrive governance profiles around agent-to-tool and agent-to-agent connectivity.

Potential components:

- tool authority and data-class profiles;
- delegation/identity mappings;
- evidence and audit requirements;
- side-effect classes;
- conformance validators;
- adapter libraries;
- policy/approval hooks.

Public interoperability profiles can coexist with licensed implementation components and protected optimization/security mechanisms.

**Build:** Phase 3. **Developer revenue:** Phase 6. **Federation:** Phase 12.

## Product family 13 — Qualified Implementer & Developer Marketplace

As external implementers mature, CrownThrive can monetize a governed ecosystem of qualified developers, consultants, pallets, agents and service providers.

Potential economics include implementation referral/marketplace fees, qualified-partner subscriptions, conformance/certification fees, support contracts, approved pallet/agent listings and attributable implementation revenue.

This is later-stage and must preserve tax, contractor, marketplace, consumer, antitrust and jurisdictional review where applicable.

**Build:** Phase 6. **Scale:** Phases 11–13.

## Product family 14 — Enterprise Support & Update Subscription

Recurring support can be attached to licensed reference architectures, SDKs, pallets, agent packs, OEM components and enterprise deployments.

Potential scope:

- version updates;
- compatibility advisories;
- implementation support;
- migration guidance;
- security/standards notices;
- support SLAs by tier after capacity and economics are modeled.

**Earliest revenue phase:** Phase 5.

## What stays protected

Do not package unrestricted versions of:

- CrownThrive sovereign authority configuration;
- private production identities or credentials;
- raw restricted policy bundles;
- private economic calibration;
- internal security findings;
- protected adversarial/evaluation corpora;
- private DAIL/evidence bodies;
- customer/partner or personal records;
- private break-glass controls;
- patent candidates before disclosure strategy is dispositioned;
- trade-secret-controlled implementation detail.

## Pricing architecture

Pricing is a separate governed object from the offer itself. The current offer catalog deliberately carries `price_status: not_authorized`.

When pricing work opens, each offer should model:

- buyer and willingness-to-pay hypothesis;
- COGS/model/provider/runtime cost;
- implementation/support burden;
- expected gross margin;
- one-time vs recurring vs usage components;
- enterprise minimums;
- partner/channel economics;
- implementation fees;
- update/support terms;
- tax/accounting treatment;
- refund/cancellation/renewal terms;
- comparable market alternatives.

No agent should populate live Stripe prices from a speculative pricing hypothesis.

## Commerce and Stripe boundary

Stripe is a commerce rail, not product authority.

A future live offer requires the exact offer version, package/license terms, authorized price, fulfillment/provisioning behavior, webhook/entitlement proof, support/refund/dispute terms and IP gate disposition. Product/Price IDs are execution records after governance, not proof the offer is ready.
