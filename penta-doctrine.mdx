# PENTA Doctrine

**PENTA** is CrownThrive's five-stage institutional execution doctrine:

> **Discover → Govern → Execute → Verify → Preserve**

The word **penta** derives from the Greek _pénte_, meaning **five**. CrownThrive uses that meaning deliberately. PENTA is not merely a naming prefix; it is the shared operating grammar for institutional systems, software services, agents, runtimes, media engines, publishing systems, continuity layers, factories, federation components, and future participants.

Every PENTA participant should be understandable by a human, addressable by software, governed by CHLOM, observable through ThriveBase, and able to prove what it did.

## The five stages

### 1. Discover

A PENTA participant first establishes what exists and what is relevant: providers, endpoints, repositories, assets, records, identities, schemas, contracts, capabilities, dependencies, versions, queues, routes, participants, health state, and institutional context. Discovery must distinguish evidence from assumption. A discovered endpoint is not automatically a certified write surface.

Representative components include **PentaDiscover**, **PentaSearch**, **PentaQuery**, **PentaFetch**, **PentaGet**, **PentaList**, **PentaRead**, **PentaObserve**, and **PentaResolve**.

### 2. Govern

Governance determines what may happen, under which authority, with what risk ceiling, and with what evidence requirements. CrownThrive's governance layer is anchored by **CHLOM** and its capability, vault, certification, continuity, and evidence controls.

Naming never manufactures authority. The existence of **PentaDelete** does not create universal deletion. It can execute only an exact operation with the required certification and authority.

Representative components include **PentaAuth**, **PentaVault**, **PentaSign**, **PentaCertify**, **PentaValidate**, **PentaBind**, and **PentaLock**.

### 3. Execute

Execution performs an approved action through the correct runtime, provider-native adapter, internal service, workflow, queue, compiler, or deployment lane. Execution should be bounded, observable, and idempotent where practical.

Representative components include **PentaRoute**, **PentaTun**, **PentaPost**, **PentaPut**, **PentaPatch**, **PentaDelete**, **PentaCreate**, **PentaUpdate**, **PentaUpsert**, **PentaQueue**, **PentaDispatch**, **PentaCompile**, **PentaGenerate**, **PentaDeploy**, and **PentaRelease**.

### 4. Verify

No meaningful execution is complete merely because a request was sent. Verification uses read-after-write, response validation, checksums, receipts, provider readback, canaries, tests, heartbeats, reconciliation, signatures, and independent proof surfaces.

Representative components include **PentaBeata**, **PentaTest**, **PentaAudit**, **PentaReconcile**, **PentaSnapshot**, and **PentaObserve**.

### 5. Preserve

Preservation retains institutional truth for continuity, auditability, reproducibility, succession, provenance, future automation, version history, and long-horizon stewardship. It preserves what happened, what authorized it, what version executed it, and enough context for another participant to continue later.

Representative systems include **PentaGeneration**, **PentaBooks**, **PentaMedia**, **PentaStudios**, **Penta Federation**, CHLOM archive/evidence systems, and the CrownThrive continuity fabric.

## The PENTA contract

A PENTA participant should have a canonical identity, defined role, discoverability, governed authority, execution surface when executable, verification surface, preserved version/evidence state, interoperability, human legibility, and machine legibility.

## Extensible by design

PENTA is a family, not a closed list. Future participants may be added whenever a genuinely distinct institutional responsibility emerges. New names should extend the grammar rather than duplicate an existing role.

Every new participant should answer: What does it discover? What governs it? What does it execute? How is it verified? What does it preserve? What does it depend on? What risk class can it operate autonomously? Which operations require additional certification or human authority?

This intentionally leaves room for future CrownThrive systems, ecosystem participants, developer extensions, licensed implementations, additional providers, acquired technologies, and new corridors.

## Current institutional PENTA systems

- **PentaMedia** — media asset operations, bindings, maintenance, and continuity.
- **PentaBooks** — governed manuscript, edition, QA, packaging, and publication lifecycle.
- **PentaGeneration** — seven-generation continuity, handoff, horizons, proof, and succession.
- **PentaStudios** — studio/media-production assets, providers, routes, MCP, and control surfaces.
- **Penta Federation** — cross-system bindings, events, proofs, and federation state.
- **PentaFramework Factory** — governed framework/software generation.
- **Penta Control** — PENTA control-plane surface.
- **Penta MCP** — machine/tool interoperability surface.
- **PentaRoute** — governed routing and execution family.

## Relationship to CrownThrive architecture

PENTA does not replace CrownThrive's institutional layers. It gives them a common execution grammar.

- **CrownThrive** remains the ecosystem/institutional umbrella.
- **CHLOM** remains governance, capability, rights, policy, vault, and evidence authority.
- **ThriveBase** remains the principal execution/evidence/data control plane.
- **Phase 3** is the autonomous self-discovery, qualification, bounded-write certification, and convergence era.
- **PentaFramework Factory** and the CrownThrive Software Factory manufacture or extend executable components.
- **Penta Federation** carries identity, bindings, events, proofs, and cross-system continuity.
- **PentaDocs** is the institutional knowledge layer and should explain the architecture for both humans and machines.

## Canonical rule

> **If CrownThrive cannot discover it, govern it, execute it, verify it, and preserve it, the institutional loop is incomplete.**
