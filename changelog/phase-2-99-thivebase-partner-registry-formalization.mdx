# Phase 2.99 — THIVEBASE and Partner Registry Formalization

## Founder adjudication

CrownThrive now recognizes **THIVEBASE** as the canonical internal brand for the shared infrastructure base beneath CrownThrive ID, IO/MCP, CHLOM/DAIL and the broader institutional control plane.

Canonical spelling is **THIVEBASE**. This is a new Phase 2.99 founder-adjudicated identity and must not be inserted retroactively into the dated S100/S103 source row counts.

Stable ID:

```text
ct.platform.internal.thivebase
```

## Architecture decision

THIVEBASE is provider-independent. Current implementation providers such as Supabase, GitHub and Mintlify remain replaceable operating inputs rather than CrownThrive institutional identity.

THIVEBASE owns the internal operating concern for:

- governed control-plane data;
- MCP/CHLOM operational metadata;
- provider/integration state;
- capacity/cost/quota monitoring;
- partner/vendor/referral records;
- evidence pointers and continuity state;
- future metering for external infrastructure products.

It does not replace CrownThrive ID, CrownThrive IO/MCP, CHLOM, DAIL, CrownLytics or application-specific data systems.

## Capacity policy adopted

Initial default utilization states:

```text
0–49%   GREEN
50–69%  WATCH
70–79%  PLAN
80–89%  ACTION NEEDED
90%+    SCALE GATE
```

A forecasted exhaustion date, provider incident, recovery limitation, security issue or renewal risk can escalate a resource before its nominal percentage threshold.

The current Supabase Free-plan baseline is recorded as a dated 2026-08-19 provider snapshot from official Supabase pricing/billing documentation. Provider limits are mutable and require re-verification before future scale decisions.

## Partner/vendor/referral registry adopted

The dedicated Partner, Vendor and Referral Registry is established as an overlay on the engine/service registry.

All 85 historical S100 engine/service rows are automatically inside the partner/vendor review universe, and newer providers are appended separately without modifying the historical source count.

The registry adds:

- relationship/plan/license state;
- costs and renewal data;
- capacity/rate limits;
- API/webhook/OAuth/MCP capability;
- security/privacy/data classification;
- support/SLA and provider-exit state;
- referral/affiliate/partner-program research and verification;
- payout/attribution terms;
- disclosures and restrictions;
- referral revenue and conversion evidence.

No vendor is represented as having an affiliate/referral program until current provider evidence verifies it.

## Phase inheritance

### Phase 2 / 2.99

Formalize THIVEBASE, partner coverage, current provider baselines, safe thresholds and monetization rules. Phase 3 remains blocked pending the full Phase 2.99 hard exit.

### Phase 3

Productionize THIVEBASE as the compact internal registry/control substrate; connect sanitized provider usage/capacity readings; automate threshold evaluation; establish backup/restore/provider-exit evidence.

### Phase 4

Federate THIVEBASE state across P0/P1 adapters and route capacity/cost/partner signals into CrownLytics/Ops; activate approved partner catalogs and cross-platform referral attribution.

### Phase 5+

Enable governed external THIVEBASE capabilities: managed MCP/integration infrastructure, usage-based APIs, compliance/rights infrastructure, licensed deployment blueprints, enterprise support, partner marketplace and developer-economy offerings.

## Commercialization doctrine

THIVEBASE begins as an internal cost-efficiency and governance asset. External monetization sells **governed capability and implementation**, not CrownThrive's internal data, credentials or sovereign decision authority.

Candidate revenue lanes:

1. managed MCP/API integrations;
2. compliance/governance infrastructure as a service;
3. rights/licensing/DAIL infrastructure packages;
4. usage-based developer/API access;
5. setup, migration and managed operations;
6. enterprise support and white-label/managed deployment;
7. training/certification through CrownThriveU;
8. templates, implementation packs and licensed reference architecture;
9. approved partner/referral commissions;
10. partner/developer marketplace listing, sponsorship or transaction economics where appropriate.

Every externalized lane requires pricing, terms, privacy/security boundaries, support obligations, CHLOM rights records and measurable unit economics before production sale.
