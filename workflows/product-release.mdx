# Product release workflow

This workflow applies to new or materially revised first-party products.

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    A[Register source] --> B[Complete content]
    B --> C[Culture and canon review]
    C --> D[Rights and provenance review]
    D --> E[Produce files and previews]
    E --> F[Quality and accessibility]
    F --> G[Assign commerce rail]
    G --> H[Configure checkout, reader, or license]
    H --> I[Test fulfillment and policy]
    I --> J[Human release approval]
    J --> K[Publish and reconcile]
    K --> L[Monitor and improve]
```

## Entry criteria

The workflow begins when:

- a governed concept or source exists
- an accountable owner is assigned
- the intended audience and product type are known
- the requested release scope is defined

## Step 1 — Register the source

**Owner:** Publishing Agent or product operator

Create or update:

- work and edition IDs
- canonical source
- version
- universe and category
- contributors
- initial rights and provenance status
- intended formats
- related products and prior editions

**Stop if:** the source cannot be identified, the edition conflicts with an existing canonical edition, or ownership is materially unclear.

## Step 2 — Complete the content

Confirm that the product contains the substantive work promised by its title and description.

For a printable or workbook, verify page count, prompts, instructions, answer or usage structure where applicable, personal-use notice, and actual value.

For a book, verify manuscript completeness, front matter, contents, narrative or instructional body, appendices, and edition notice.

## Step 3 — Culture and canon review

**Owner:** Rights and Governance Agent with designated human reviewer

Review:

- cultural alignment
- identity and portrait mapping
- chronology and continuity
- protected identities
- child and family boundaries
- representation and terminology
- relationship to prior editions

## Step 4 — Rights and provenance review

Confirm:

- ownership and contributors
- artwork and illustration provenance
- music, lyrics, voice, likeness, and source use
- AI tool and human-authorship records where applicable
- permitted personal, institutional, performance, commercial, or derivative scope
- restrictions and holds

Assign the approved rights class.

## Step 5 — Produce release assets

**Owner:** Publishing Agent

Generate the required release package:

- final PDF, EPUB, image, audio, video, or bundle
- US Letter and A4 where assigned
- cover and preview images
- product thumbnail
- product manifest
- checksum
- metadata
- accessibility features
- version and change log

## Step 6 — Quality and accessibility

**Owner:** QA and Security Agent

Test:

- file opens and renders correctly
- text is readable
- pages and navigation are complete
- links and QR codes resolve
- no placeholder or clipped content remains
- color contrast and form labels meet requirements
- child-focused materials remain separated from mature content
- no secrets, private data, or protected assets are exposed

## Step 7 — Assign the commerce rail

**Owner:** Commerce Agent

Choose:

- Stripe Direct
- Good Shit Only
- Go Flipbooks
- subscription or membership entitlement
- service or partnership workflow
- free controlled resource

Document why the selected rail matches the product and rights class.

## Step 8 — Configure commerce and fulfillment

Depending on the rail, create and verify:

- product and price mapping
- checkout or listing
- signed-webhook handling
- entitlement
- protected delivery
- hosted-reader route
- license agreement or rights listing
- credit price where eligible
- refund, dispute, expiration, and support behavior

## Step 9 — Test

Minimum direct-commerce tests:

- successful checkout
- webhook verification
- duplicate webhook
- closed browser before success page
- entitlement creation
- protected delivery
- expired link
- download limit
- repurchase
- refund or dispute state
- missing asset and support path

Test all public product pages, search, sitemap, metadata, and mobile layouts.

## Step 10 — Human release approval

Approval evidence should include:

- source and version
- canon approval
- rights approval
- QA report
- commerce and fulfillment test
- public copy
- pricing
- affected platforms
- rollback or deactivation plan

## Step 11 — Publish and reconcile

Update every affected system in one controlled release:

- product registry
- public store and product page
- Stripe or assigned commerce rail
- entitlement mapping
- search and sitemap
- publishing hub and related world pages
- Go Flipbooks or GSO status
- analytics and event instrumentation
- PentaDocs changelog

## Step 12 — Monitor

Track:

- checkout success and failure
- entitlement mismatches
- downloads and support demand
- refunds and disputes
- conversion
- broken links and file issues
- public-claim drift
- customer feedback

## Completion evidence

A product release is complete only when:

- the product exists
- its governing records agree
- the assigned transaction or access path works
- required approvals exist
- public status matches production state
- monitoring and correction paths are active

<Warning>
  Do not publish a batch of product cards first and plan to create the actual products later.
</Warning>
