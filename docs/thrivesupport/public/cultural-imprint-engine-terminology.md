# Cultural Imprint Engine™ Terminology and Ecosystem Language

CrownThrive uses specific names for different layers of its ecosystem. These terms should not be substituted for one another in support articles, platform copy, implementation records, or operator documentation.

## Cultural Imprint Engine™ (CIE)

The **Cultural Imprint Engine™** is CrownThrive’s cultural governance system. It governs how the institution maintains narrative, aesthetic, editorial, identity, and cultural integrity across corridors, platforms, brands, and media. It also helps prevent drift as new channels and ventures are added.

The phrase **“Cultural Impact Engine” is deprecated terminology drift**. When found in recoverable documentation, it should be corrected to **Cultural Imprint Engine™** unless the text is intentionally documenting an old historical label.

## Convergent Ecosystem

The **Convergent Ecosystem** is the larger operating field that connects CrownThrive brands, platforms, infrastructure, commerce, media, community, technology, and governance. A platform can have its own purpose while still participating in shared CrownThrive rails.

## Thrive Flywheel™

The **Thrive Flywheel™** describes CrownThrive’s compounding participation and growth architecture: activity in one part of the ecosystem can create discovery, participation, distribution, retention, measurement, rewards, or opportunity elsewhere when the relevant integrations and permissions exist.

## CHLOM™

**CHLOM™** is CrownThrive’s governance and authority fabric for rights, licensing, compliance, overrides, interoperability, and controlled automation. It should not be described as a generic marketing program or ordinary membership feature.

## Seat Model™ and gateways

CrownThrive uses seat/gateway concepts to organize identity, participation, role design, onboarding, and routing across the ecosystem. Individual properties may still use distinct account systems or provider-backed authentication while those integrations continue evolving.

## Support writing rule

When writing or updating Help Center content:

1. Use the canonical current term.
2. Preserve the exact product/brand name when known.
3. Do not imply every platform has identical maturity, access, or integration status.
4. Do not expose private governance mechanics in a public article.
5. If an old article conflicts with a current approved policy or institutional source, flag and reconcile the conflict rather than copying it forward.
