# Account Sign-In, Access, and Recovery

CrownThrive operates across multiple connected properties. Some use a shared ecosystem identity or gateway, while others may still use their own email/password or third-party sign-in flow. If you cannot sign in, first identify the exact property you are trying to access.

## 1. Confirm the correct CrownThrive property

Check the page address and product name. An account that works on one CrownThrive property may not automatically authenticate another property when that property uses a separate underlying account system.

## 2. Use the recovery option shown by that property

Use **Forgot password**, **Reset password**, **Sign in with Google/Facebook**, or the recovery option displayed on the affected site. Use only links presented on an official CrownThrive property or a provider page reached from that property.

If a recovery email is expected:

- Verify the email address was entered correctly.
- Check spam, junk, promotions, and filtered folders.
- Search for the property name as well as “CrownThrive.”
- Avoid repeatedly requesting new codes if the site warns that prior codes will be invalidated.

## 3. Rule out a browser/session problem

If your credentials are accepted but the page loops, returns you to sign-in, or loads incorrectly:

1. Open the site in a private/incognito window.
2. Disable extensions that may block cookies or scripts for a single test.
3. Clear site-specific cookies/cache for the affected property rather than deleting unrelated browser data.
4. Try another supported browser or device.
5. Confirm your device date and time are correct when authentication codes are involved.

## 4. If your account is locked or access changed

Do not create multiple replacement accounts unless the product specifically instructs you to. Contact support with:

- the property or URL;
- your account email/username;
- the approximate time access stopped working;
- the exact error message;
- whether password reset was attempted;
- whether you still control the email address or sign-in provider associated with the account.

For ownership, role, or identity changes, support may require verification through an approved workflow before access is changed.

## 5. If you suspect unauthorized access

Treat the issue as a security incident. Change the affected password, secure the email account used for recovery, revoke active sessions or exposed tokens where the product provides that option, and contact ThriveSupport with the relevant timestamps and evidence.

## Never send credentials to support

Do not send your password, one-time code, recovery code, API key, private key, full payment-card number, or bank credentials. Support may ask you to verify control of an account, but verification should not require disclosure of reusable secret credentials in a normal ticket or chat.
