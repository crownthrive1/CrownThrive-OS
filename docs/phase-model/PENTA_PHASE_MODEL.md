# CrownThrive PENTA Institutional Phase Model

**Effective date:** 2026-08-26  
**Status:** Canonical Phase 3 institutional model  
**Doctrine:** **Discover → Govern → Execute → Verify → Preserve**  
**Current institutional phase:** **Phase 3 — Execute**  
**Current OS release family:** **CrownThrive OS 3.x**

## Why the phase model is being refactored

CrownThrive accumulated a long sequence of useful but increasingly granular transition labels while the institution was recovering, converging, documenting, certifying providers, rebuilding CHLOM, expanding CIE, establishing ThriveBase, creating factories, and moving toward autonomous operations. Labels such as Phase 2.5, 2.7, 2.8, 2.9, 2.95, 2.97, 2.98, 2.99, pre-entry, convergence waves, sprint numbers, and similar shorthand served a practical purpose at the time: they let the institution distinguish intermediate work while the target architecture was still being discovered.

That same granularity is now counterproductive as an institution-wide roadmap. It creates several failure modes: the meaning of a phase can become dependent on a specific chat or workstream; a decimal phase can be mistaken for a maturity guarantee; older documents can look current merely because their number sounds later; platform teams can invent parallel timelines; and institutional direction can fragment into dozens of small transition labels rather than one durable lifecycle.

PENTA resolves that problem by aligning the institutional phase model to the same five-stage doctrine that now governs execution architecture:

> **Phase 1 — Discover**  
> **Phase 2 — Govern**  
> **Phase 3 — Execute**  
> **Phase 4 — Verify**  
> **Phase 5 — Preserve**

This model is intentionally simple enough to survive future technologies, providers, brands, product lines, personnel changes, acquisitions, generations, and architecture refactors. Component versions, maturity states, waves, releases, milestones, and sprint identifiers may continue to exist, but they are not new institutional phases.

## The five phases

### Phase 1 — Discover

Phase 1 answers the institutional question: **What exists, what matters, and what is actually true?**

Discovery includes identifying CrownThrive's platforms, brands, corridors, legal entities, intellectual property, providers, websites, repositories, data stores, people, participants, workflows, historical records, contracts, APIs, MCP surfaces, risks, dependencies, opportunities, duplicated systems, missing systems, and sources of institutional truth.

The output of Phase 1 is not authority. It is an evidence-backed map from which authority can be governed. Discovery should separate observation from assumption, historical fact from current fact, provider capability from configured capability, and desired architecture from verified architecture.

CrownThrive has completed the institution-level discovery generation sufficiently to move beyond it as the current phase. Discovery remains a permanent operating function through components such as PentaDiscover, PentaSearch, PentaQuery, PentaFetch, PentaRead, PentaObserve, CrownLytics, reconciliation agents, source registries, PentaDocs, and repository/provider scans.

### Phase 2 — Govern

Phase 2 answers: **Who or what has authority, under what rules, and with what evidence?**

This is where CrownThrive established and refined the governance architecture around CHLOM, CIE boundaries, rights and licensing, roles, vault custody, DAIL/evidence, capability ceilings, provider certification, security, version lineage, source-of-truth hierarchy, archive rules, interoperability contracts, and the difference between a discovered capability and a permitted operation.

All historical decimal Phase 2 labels are now consolidated into this single institutional generation. They remain valuable historical evidence, but they are no longer active planning layers. Their substantive achievements are inherited by current systems when an active record explicitly incorporates them.

Phase 2 remains permanently present as a function through CHLOM, PentaAuth, PentaVault, PentaSign, PentaCertify, PentaValidate, licensing controls, credential registries, governance workflows, D0-D3 risk controls, human-reserved authority, and bounded-write certification.

### Phase 3 — Execute

Phase 3 answers: **Can CrownThrive perform the institution's work through durable, governed software rather than depending on a person or a chat to push every action?**

This is the current generation.

Phase 3 is the execution era of CrownThrive. The institution is converting knowledge, provider access, governance, products, content systems, commerce, media, documentation, publishing, factories, APIs, MCPs, schedulers, evidence, continuity, and operational workflows into executable software that can discover work, route it, perform bounded actions, verify results, preserve receipts, and continue operating across sessions and personnel changes.

The Phase 3 execution architecture centers on PENTA and includes PentaRoute, PentaTun, PentaBeata, the wider Penta primitive family, PentaFactory, CrownThrive Software Factory, Penta Federation, PentaMedia, PentaBooks, PentaGeneration, PentaStudios, PentaDocs, ThriveBase, CHLOM, CIE, CrownThrive IO, PentaGreen, repository federation, provider-native adapters, queues, internal schedulers, proof surfaces, deployment lanes, commerce controls, and self-discovery/certification loops.

Phase 3 is not complete simply because software exists. Its completion standard is institutional rather than cosmetic: priority CrownThrive functions must be able to execute durably through governed software; each critical capability must have a known owner and source of truth; provider writes must be bounded to certified operations; failures must produce evidence and recovery work instead of disappearing; automation must not manufacture D3 or destructive authority; and enough execution proof must exist to support the transition into an assurance-first Phase 4.

### Phase 4 — Verify

Phase 4 answers: **Can CrownThrive continuously prove that what it believes is operating is actually operating, that results are correct, and that the institution can detect silent failure or drift?**

Phase 4 is not merely more testing. It is the refactoring of CrownThrive into an assurance-first institution.

Phase 4 will expand independent readback, cross-provider verification, conformance testing, reproducibility, fault injection, red-team and adversarial testing, recovery drills, provider-exit exercises, financial/economic reconciliation, content and rights integrity checks, security attestations, evidence hashing, release provenance, cross-repository state proofs, data-quality assurance, performance/service-level measurement, and certification renewal.

Where Phase 3 asks whether an action can be executed, Phase 4 asks whether its correctness, authority, effect, and continuity can be proven without trusting the same component that performed it.

Phase 4 completion requires material institutional claims and critical operations to be continuously provable, reproducible, independently checkable, and resistant to silent drift. It should be possible to distinguish a healthy-looking system from a genuinely certified system using machine-readable evidence rather than interpretation.

### Phase 5 — Preserve

Phase 5 answers: **Can the institution survive change without losing itself?**

Preservation is broader than backups. Phase 5 is the deliberate conversion of CrownThrive into durable institutional memory and continuity: archives, succession, PentaGeneration, seven-generation stewardship, provider portability, repository portability, rights custody, data migration, asset provenance, executable runbooks, recovery packages, knowledge inheritance, version history, durable identities, licensing continuity, dependency replacement, succession of human authority, and preservation of the Cultural Imprint.

Phase 5 treats continuity as a first-class product requirement. CrownThrive should be able to change a provider, replace a runtime, lose access to a platform, onboard a new operator, transfer stewardship to a later generation, or reconstruct a subsystem without losing the institutional meaning, rights, evidence, state, or operating knowledge required to continue.

Phase 5 completion means CrownThrive can survive personnel, provider, platform, repository, and generation transitions without losing institutional identity, authority, evidence, or operational continuity.

## PENTA is cyclical even though the institutional phases are sequential

The institution moves through five primary generations, but every operational object may run the five-stage PENTA loop repeatedly.

For example, a provider integration can be discovered during Phase 3, governed through CHLOM, executed through PentaRoute, verified through readback, and preserved through receipts—all while the institution remains in Phase 3. The institutional phase describes the dominant institutional objective, not a prohibition on using the other four functions.

This distinction prevents the phase model from becoming a waterfall process.

## Retirement of decimal institutional phases

Effective with this model, CrownThrive will not create new decimal institutional phases such as `3.1`, `3.7`, `3.99`, `4.5`, or similar labels as institution-wide generations.

Existing decimal Phase 2 labels are **retired as current roadmap taxonomy** and retained as historical aliases. They may still appear in release notes, filenames, branches, old articles, migration names, evidence, commits, provider snapshots, or archive records because those records must not be rewritten to manufacture a cleaner history.

The canonical historical mapping is:

- Phase 1 and recovery/discovery work → **Phase 1 — Discover**.
- Phase 2 and Phase 2.5/2.7/2.8/2.9/2.95/2.97/2.98/2.99/pre-entry convergence → **Phase 2 — Govern** historical generation.
- Current institutional generation → **Phase 3 — Execute**.
- Future assurance generation → **Phase 4 — Verify**.
- Future continuity generation → **Phase 5 — Preserve**.

## What replaces decimal phases

The institution still needs precision. It should use the right dimension instead of inventing another phase:

- **OS release:** `3.x`, `4.x`, `5.x`.
- **Component contract/schema/API version:** v1, v2, v3, etc.
- **Maturity state:** candidate, controlled test, active, production, write-verified, reserve, historical, superseded, retired.
- **Risk/authority class:** D0-D3 and applicable agent/autonomy ceilings.
- **Wave/sprint/cohort:** temporary delivery grouping, not institutional generation.
- **Milestone:** evidence-backed deliverable or capability goal.
- **Certification state:** read-verified, write-verified, deployment-verified, rollback-verified, etc.

This separation makes the roadmap easier to reason about and prevents a higher phase number from being confused with a stronger provider or legal claim.

## Phase transition governance

No single chat, branch, document, agent, workflow, or provider can independently declare an institutional phase transition.

A transition requires a canonical OS record, supporting evidence, archive/current-state reconciliation, a version/release decision, updated PentaDocs projection, component impacts identified, and a defined successor baseline. Reserved governance remains reserved.

The transition to Phase 4 should occur only when the institution has enough stable execution infrastructure that verification can become the dominant refactoring objective. The transition to Phase 5 should occur only when assurance is mature enough that preservation and succession can become the dominant objective without merely preserving unverified systems.

## The long-term baseline

CrownThrive's future roadmap is therefore intentionally clear:

**Discover what is real. Govern what is allowed. Execute what is authorized. Verify what happened. Preserve what must outlive us.**

That is the PENTA institutional lifecycle, the CrownThrive phase model, and the baseline against which future architecture should be evaluated.
