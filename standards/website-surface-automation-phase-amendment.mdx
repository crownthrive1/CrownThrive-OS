# Website Surface Automation Phase Amendment

This standard is an additive phase-control amendment. It does not weaken the [Ten-Phase Institutional Program Charter](/standards/ten-phase-institutional-program-charter), the [Phase 2.99 Plan](/changelog/phase-2-99-plan), or the [Phase 3 Readiness Gate](/technology/phase-3-readiness-gate). Where this amendment adds a website-specific predicate, that predicate is inherited by the applicable phase.

## Universal website invariant

A website that renders is not necessarily integrated. A governed CrownThrive website surface requires:

- stable surface and platform identity;
- explicit provider/project/version state;
- governed content/claim sources;
- governed Figma/Canva or other asset-source relationships;
- contact/CTA/form/link expectations;
- data-purpose and consent boundaries;
- observable check/event evidence;
- remediation/update state;
- recovery/provider-exit path;
- provider mutation authority appropriate to risk.

Unknown provider/project/write state fails closed.

## Phase 2.99 — registration and control-plane seed

Phase 2.99 website-surface readiness requires:

- `website_surfaces` registry;
- creative asset routes;
- contact/CTA interaction definitions;
- append-oriented check evidence;
- site-update/remediation queue;
- Website Surface & Interaction Agent identity and queue classes;
- dedicated Website Surface MCP contract;
- direct MCP deployment state separated from central federation state;
- contact monitoring that does not submit live customer forms;
- explicit provider-write certification state;
- Figma/Canva asset routes that default to no automatic production sync;
- claim/count/phase differences routed to namespace/evidence reconciliation rather than automatic correction.

Phase 2.99 may use autonomous D0 read checks and D1 plan generation. External website mutation is not authorized by this amendment.

## Phase 3 — executable website contract

Phase 3 must make surface, interaction, claim, asset-route, consent, event, remediation, and provider-adapter contracts executable.

Before any provider-side autonomous repair is eligible, at least one bounded provider adapter must prove exact project identity, least-privilege authorization, allowed mutation surface, rollback, read-before-write, read-after-write, idempotency/concurrency, audit, and recovery.

Direct Website Surface MCP deployment and central Institutional MCP federation are separate certification predicates.

## Phase 4 — federated activation

Website cohorts may federate through common:

- CrownThrive ID/account linking where applicable;
- entitlement and commerce handoffs;
- support routing;
- consent/purpose boundaries;
- CrownLytics/CrownPulse signals;
- Figma component/pattern inheritance;
- Canva downstream creative-production routing;
- event and correction propagation.

A shared design or provider must not collapse platform identity, audience, data ownership, rights, or system-of-record responsibility.

## Phase 5 — measured market operation

Website operations add measurable evidence for:

- contact/CTA conversion;
- form availability and delivery reliability;
- interaction error rate;
- accessibility defects;
- creative asset reuse and drift;
- update/remediation latency;
- support burden;
- revenue/entitlement handoff integrity where applicable;
- public-claim freshness.

A better-looking surface is not sufficient Phase 5 evidence without measurable customer/operational outcome.

## Phase 6 — licensed/developer website economy

Mature website/design templates, components, adapters, API/MCP tools, and implementation patterns may enter developer/licensing programs only after chain-of-title, permitted asset use, developer terms, version/deprecation, support, security, and portability are accepted.

Canva templates and Figma components remain governed derivatives/assets; external access does not grant CHLOM/CIE institutional authority.

## Phase 7 — phygital surfaces

The same website interaction contract extends to:

- ThriveKiosks;
- TapStations;
- MM Suites interfaces;
- event displays/check-in;
- public/shared devices;
- location-specific AdLuxe/commerce/reward interactions.

Additional physical safety, session privacy, accessibility, device identity, health, and rollback controls apply.

## Phase 8 — portfolio custody

Holdings-scale website governance requires:

- complete surface/project/provider inventory;
- asset ownership/custody;
- provider contracts and exports;
- domain and deployment relationships;
- design-system and creative-source lineage;
- cost/renewal/support evidence;
- provider-exit and reconstruction procedures.

Brand/project/site variants are not automatically separate IP/economic assets.

## Phase 9 — advanced provenance and interoperability

Advanced cryptographic provenance, attestations, or decentralized interaction proofs may be added only when a validated operational problem justifies them. No private contact payload or unnecessary PII may be immutably published.

## Phase 10 — continuity

Continuity artifacts must preserve:

- surface/project/provider IDs;
- current routes and domain relationships;
- contact/interaction definitions;
- creative source/version lineage;
- agent/MCP contracts;
- provider exports;
- rollback/rebuild instructions;
- credential recovery references;
- correction history;
- vendor-exit procedure.

The institution must be able to reconstruct a governed website surface without undocumented founder/operator memory.

## Absolute no-go conditions

- provider/project rendering treated as write integration;
- MCP tool registration treated as central dispatch proof;
- health monitoring submits real customer forms without a separate approved test packet;
- operational contact is converted into marketing consent;
- Figma/Canva output is pushed to a live site without registered source/version/destination/rights/accessibility state;
- an unverified design token is promoted through site automation;
- public count/phase/price/metric differences are auto-corrected without namespace/evidence reconciliation;
- a provider-side website write occurs without rollback and read-after-write evidence;
- an agent self-approves a consequential public-site mutation.
