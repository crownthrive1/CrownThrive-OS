## Twenty-phase impact horizon

The named and adopted roadmap on this page remains authoritative for **Phases 1–10**. A later founder directive requires all material documentation and institutionalization work to preserve downstream awareness through **Phase 20**. Because current recovered evidence does not yet define Phases 11–20, those later slots are governed as `reserved_definition_required` impact placeholders rather than invented phase names. See [Twenty-Phase Horizon Reconciliation](/standards/twenty-phase-horizon-reconciliation) and the [Documentation Reconciliation & Continuity Framework](/standards/documentation-reconciliation-continuity-framework).

## Why the roadmap expanded

The earlier roadmap correctly grouped executable core, ecosystem activation and external scale, but continued Phase 2 recovery has shown that CrownThrive needs more independently governed gates between revenue proof, licensing/developer expansion, physical/phygital operations, holdings/capital scale, advanced CHLOM interoperability and generational continuity.

Expanding the roadmap reduces ambiguity rather than adding bureaucracy. It gives each materially different risk domain its own entry gate, checkpoints, evidence requirements, rollback path and exit gate while preserving one continuous institutional history.

## Fluid-roadmap rule

This roadmap is **versioned and fluid**. After every material pass, patch, source recovery, correction, adjudication, provider change, architecture decision, legal/economic clarification, platform-state change, validation failure or implementation finding, the responsible run must reassess every downstream phase that depends on the changed fact.

A downstream phase is updated when its:

- objectives;
- dependencies;
- entry or exit criteria;
- hard checkpoints;
- acceptance tests;
- required artifacts;
- authority/approval boundaries;
- risk assumptions;
- automation responsibilities;
- sequencing;
- migration/deprecation rules;
- rollback/recovery requirements;
- communication obligations;
- or handoff state

has materially changed.

No future phase may remain stale merely because it was authored earlier. Prior versions remain preserved and the change reason is recorded.

## Universal phase contract

Every top-level phase and every material subphase/pass/workstream must define:

1. purpose and institutional outcome;
2. scope and explicit non-goals;
3. source/evidence baseline and current-state assumptions;
4. dependencies and stable IDs;
5. hard entry gate;
6. internal hard checkpoints;
7. completion criteria;
8. hard exit gate;
9. validation/evaluation requirements;
10. authority and approval requirements;
11. rollback, recovery and continuity path;
12. downstream phase impact;
13. documentation-impact and changelog obligations;
14. communications/notification obligations;
15. exact next-state handoff.

A gate may change only through a governed version/decision record. A later phase cannot silently waive an earlier unresolved requirement.

## Phase 1 — Institutional Mapping

## Mission

Establish the macro CrownThrive institutional map: ecosystem, Convergent Ecosystem relationships, corridors/lanes, Cultural Imprint Engine, Thrive Flywheel, brands, frameworks, terminology, source families, ownership concepts and initial governance standards.

## Hard exit

The institution has a navigable macro model with stable naming/relationship rules and enough source lineage to begin forensic recovery without treating memory or marketing language as current truth.

## Phase 2 — Institutional Recovery, Reconciliation & Documentation

## Mission

Recover, reconcile, adjudicate and institutionalize CrownThrive knowledge, Help Center content, CHLOM, MM Suites, portfolio identities, rights/IP, legal/policy material, platform/vendor/domain state, architecture, metrics, aliases, contradictions and operating rules.

Phase 2 includes the already-established decimal subphases and workstreams through **Phase 2.99**. Those identifiers remain historically stable.

## Hard entry

Phase 1 macro map and source-governance rules exist.

## Hard checkpoints

- source/evidence hierarchy and contradiction ledger;
- stable identity and alias/successor model;
- platform-by-platform extraction;
- Help Center/articleization recovery and disposition;
- private-core/repository/data architecture decisions;
- governance-as-code enforcement: institutional validators run before merge, the effective `main` branch/ruleset makes the designated governance status check mandatory, bypass authority is explicit, and post-merge `main` revalidation remains enabled as defense-in-depth;
- machine schemas and seed records;
- retroactive reconciliation of earlier Phase 2 passes;
- hard Phase 3 readiness evaluation.

## Hard exit

Phase 2.99 acceptance criteria are satisfied: machine seeds are referentially valid; P0/P1 knowledge is substantive or explicitly unresolved; source/current/historical states cannot silently promote; repository/private/public boundaries are implementation-ready; **CrownThrive-OS's effective `main` policy is fail-closed so the institutional governance validation check is a required merge condition rather than monitoring only**; post-merge `main` revalidation remains enabled; required hard gates have evidence; and the Phase 3 entry packet is exact.

## Phase 3 — Executable Institutional Core

## Mission

Turn accepted institutional meaning into executable services and machine contracts: identity, authority, evidence, DAIL, rights, policy, licensing states, economics, remedies, developer/API/MCP contracts, schemas, events and agent controls.

## Hard entry

Phase 2 hard exit and Phase 3 readiness gate both pass. Production secrets/private evidence have controlled homes. No unresolved source state can be interpreted as permission. The connected CrownThrive-OS repository must also have verified fail-closed merge governance on `main`: the institutional validation status check is mandatory before merge, the bypass model is explicitly governed, and canonical `main` remains independently revalidated after merge.

## Internal program

Phase 3 may retain granular subphases such as bootstrap, trust core, rights/policy core, economic/remedy core, developer/identity/integration core and bounded advanced-research laboratories where still valid. Their historical `3A–3E` naming can remain as subphase lineage while future revisions use explicit stable IDs.

## Hard exit

At least two materially different platform pilots can complete an auditable governed lifecycle across identity, authority, evidence, rules, rights/offer, agreement/payment where applicable, entitlement, usage/economics, correction/remedy and controlled API/MCP access with tested recovery.

## Phase 4 — Federated Ecosystem Activation

## Mission

Connect specialized CrownThrive properties through the common institutional spine without flattening their distinct roles, data stores, rights, audiences or operating models.

## Hard entry

Phase 3 core services are production-capable for the selected cohort and each participating platform has an accepted capability/data/rights/legal/support/adapter/rollback profile.

## Hard checkpoints

- progressive CrownThrive ID/account linking;
- certified adapters/MCPs;
- cross-platform entitlements/events;
- CrownLytics/CrownPulse measurement;
- support/legal/policy enforcement;
- permissioned communications and attribution;
- cohort-specific reliability/privacy/security tests;
- MM Suites and other phygital handoffs only when their own legal/operating gates permit.

## Hard exit

Multiple cohorts operate through the shared spine with measured reliability and reconciled identity, entitlement, attribution, support and recovery. A new platform can enter through a repeatable certification process.

## Phase 5 — Revenue & Market Activation

Prove sustainable monetization across the ecosystem before broad external licensing or capital-scale expansion. Convert technical integration into measurable customer value, conversion, retention and unit-economics evidence.

## Hard entry

Phase 4 has demonstrated reliable governed cross-platform operation for the revenue paths being activated. Current product, price, fulfillment, rights, refund/support and measurement records exist.

## Hard checkpoints

- advertising inventory and campaign economics;
- media/publishing/music licensing revenue;
- affiliate/ambassador/creator attribution and payout reconciliation;
- education/services/consulting revenue;
- retention/rewards and cross-platform flywheel conversion;
- current unit economics, cohort performance and support cost;
- no historical economic schedule acting as a current default.

## Hard exit

Selected revenue engines are repeatable, attributable, supportable and reconciled with evidence. CrownThrive can distinguish promising experiments from validated economic lanes.

## Hard entry

External licensees/developers/partners can operate under controlled contracts with verifiable rights, access, usage, economics, support, deprecation and remedies.

## Phase 7 — Physical, Phygital & Regional Expansion

## Mission

Scale place-based and device-mediated CrownThrive operations: MM Suites, ThriveKiosks, TapStations, AdLuxe Places, events, physical service locations, wholesale and regional operating models.

## Hard entry

Relevant digital spine, commercial model, legal/insurance/safety package, operator training, device/location security and support systems are accepted. A prospectus, domain, hardware purchase or concept does not satisfy entry.

## Hard checkpoints

- MM Suites pilot and Suite Pro operating evidence;
- location/device identity and health;

- local/regional agreements, insurance and jurisdictional review;
- wholesale/inventory/provider continuity;
- repeatable operator onboarding and support.

## Hard exit

Selected physical/phygital programs demonstrate safe, supportable, measurable replication with clear regional authority, economics and recovery paths.

## Phase 8 — Holdings, Capital & Portfolio Scale

## Mission

Mature portfolio stewardship, capital allocation and risk separation using proven operating evidence rather than historical diagrams: Holdings structure, IP stewardship, intercompany arrangements, SPVs/subsidiaries where justified, acquisitions/licensing/sales, treasury controls and portfolio lifecycle decisions.

## Hard entry

Revenue and operational evidence justify structural complexity. Current legal/accounting/tax advice and entity records support each activated structure.

## Hard checkpoints

- asset/portfolio valuation evidence and lifecycle state;
- HoldCo/entity/IP custody decisions;
- capital-allocation and treasury controls;
- intercompany/license/service agreements;
- risk isolation and insurance;
- acquisition/divestiture/licensing criteria;
- investor/partner reporting without unsupported projections.

## Hard exit

Portfolio structure and capital flows match current legal records and operating reality, with auditable stewardship, risk separation and succession-ready ownership/custody records.

## Phase 9 — Advanced CHLOM & Interoperable Infrastructure

## Mission

Activate advanced provenance, verifiable credentials, cryptographic proofs, external attestations/oracles, programmable settlement and decentralized/interoperable components only where conventional infrastructure has demonstrated a real limitation.

## Hard entry

A validated problem, threat model, legal/privacy/security posture, recovery model and measurable advantage exist. Experimental protocols do not inherit production authority from research.

## Hard checkpoints

- DID/VC or equivalent identity proofs where justified;
- privacy-preserving/proof systems;
- cryptographic provenance and public anchoring boundaries;
- oracle/attestation governance;
- multiparty settlement/governance where warranted;
- interoperability/bridge threat modeling;
- key custody, recovery and reversibility;
- no private evidence/PII placed immutably in public systems.

## Hard exit

Each activated advanced capability proves operational value, security, legal/privacy fitness, portability and recovery without becoming an unnecessary dependency for the core institution.

## Phase 10 — Generational Continuity, Sovereign Scale & Institutional Permanence

## Mission

Make CrownThrive resilient beyond any one founder, vendor, cloud, model, repository, provider or generation while preserving cultural mission, IP stewardship and community accountability.

## Hard entry

Core operations, revenue, licensing, portfolio governance and continuity mechanisms are mature enough to be transferred and independently exercised.

## Hard checkpoints

- founder/key-person dependency inventory and reduction;
- succession, delegation and stewardship transfer;
- independent backups, restore drills and provider exits;
- durable institutional archives and cultural records;
- cross-border/multilingual/legal localization;
- regional/global licensing governance;
- independent key custody and disaster recovery;
- long-horizon funding/maintenance model;
- recurring proof that the institution can operate through leadership/vendor transition.

## Hard exit

CrownThrive can maintain governance, rights, records, revenue, support, continuity and cultural stewardship across leadership and technology transitions without undocumented founder knowledge as a critical dependency.

## Downstream-change propagation contract

Every material run asks:

```text
What changed now?
→ which assumptions did it invalidate or refine?
→ which current gates change?
→ which future phases depend on those assumptions?
→ update those future phases in the same governed documentation-impact cycle
→ preserve prior versions
→ record why and when
→ leave exact next-state handoff
```

A material change that affects a future phase but leaves its roadmap stale is a documentation-governance failure.

## Automation doctrine

The roadmap is designed for **hybrid automation with minimal human interaction**.

- D0 and explicitly permitted D1 work should become deterministic, self-checking and automatically recoverable.
- Agents may draft, reconcile, map, regenerate, validate, detect drift, open/fix documentation deltas and merge within authority.
- D2/D3 decisions stay behind the adopted human/specialist authority matrix.
- No authoring agent self-approves a material D2/D3 conclusion.
- A blocked packet does not freeze the institution; the run records the blocker, updates affected gates/future phases and continues the next independent safe packet.
- Every successful material pass leaves machine-readable next state, changelog and communication evidence.

## Permanent rules
