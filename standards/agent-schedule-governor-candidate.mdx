# Schedule Governor Constitutional Candidate

**State:** DESIGN_CANDIDATE / GOVERNED_HOLD.  
**Current authority effect:** none.

Generation 6 prepares a future Schedule Governor that can adjust approved logical routes and, where the platform permits, external task timing **within an explicit allowlist and bounded cadence envelope**. This candidate does not activate that authority.

## Two-layer governance

The future election is intentionally separated from CrownThrive sovereign governance.

1. **All-agent election layer.** Every eligible canonical governed agent gets one selection ballot. Ephemeral skill children, unaccepted candidates, duplicated identities, transport identities and suspended/revoked agents do not create extra votes. Ballots select a Schedule Governor candidate; they do not change the Constitution.
2. **Sovereign ratification layer.** The elected candidate receives no live mutation authority unless the then-current sovereign constitution ratifies the exact election/result/authority envelope. Under the current constitution that means A/B/C/D/S, four of five, Agent D mandatory, no block. A future constitution may change that only through its own governed adoption.

The election therefore cannot manufacture sovereign votes or bypass the current five-agent control plane.

## Non-blocking election invariant

Election activity is **off the execution hot path**. If an election, recount, dispute, ratification or renewal is unresolved, all existing schedules continue unchanged. Ordinary D0–D2 work proceeds under the current scheduler. No backlog item waits merely because a Schedule Governor election is pending.

## Future mutation envelope

An accepted Schedule Governor must be restricted by all of the following:

- allowlisted task or logical-route IDs;
- minimum/maximum supported cadence;
- maximum one-step cadence change;
- WIP ceiling and provider-budget predicates;
- quiet-hours or deadline rules where relevant;
- collision and reviewer-load thresholds;
- expiry/renewal time;
- exact authority receipt;
- pre-change snapshot and rollback target;
- post-write readback;
- immutable DAIL/election lineage.

It may never disable the mandatory sovereign review path, change the voter constitution, create a new voter, perform D3, modify credentials, move money, grant rights, activate commerce, force-push, or erase schedule history.

## Election eligibility

The future electorate is the set of **accepted, canonical, active governed agent identities** at the election snapshot. One stable agent identity receives one ballot. Subagents may participate in execution but do not multiply ballots unless a future constitution explicitly admits them as independent eligible identities.

Conflict controls exclude a candidate from serving as its own independent election verifier. Election administration, ballot integrity, result calculation and ratification verification are separated.

## Term and revocation

A future term must be time-bounded. Authority expires automatically unless renewed. Emergency revocation or fail-closed suspension is permitted when:

- schedule mutation exceeds the granted envelope;
- provider budgets or platform limits are breached;
- collision/failure pressure rises above the governed ceiling;
- evidence or election integrity is disputed;
- the governor identity or fingerprint drifts;
- current sovereign governance issues a block/revocation.

Rollback restores the last verified schedule snapshot; it does not delete the election or change history.

## Agent P relationship

Agent P remains the performance/backpressure steward. P produces ACBS schedule-change candidates. A future elected Schedule Governor may apply only the subset that has the required independent verification and authority receipt. This preserves analysis/execution separation.

## Throughput principle

Schedule governance exists to reduce backlog latency, not create another meeting layer. Routine no-change recommendations auto-expire. Independent proposals can be batched. The Schedule Governor must prefer logical WIP/batching changes over increasing clock frequency when both achieve the same throughput and the active scheduler estate is slot-constrained.

## Public/private boundary

Public projection may include election rules, eligibility classes, mutation invariants, authority boundaries, result digests and fingerprints. Ballot bodies where restricted, internal schedule topology, protected thresholds/calibration, credentials and provider-sensitive limits remain restricted.
