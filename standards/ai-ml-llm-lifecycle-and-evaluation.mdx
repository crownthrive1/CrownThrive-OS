# AI, ML and LLM Lifecycle and Evaluation Standard

CrownThrive uses AI to expand capability, reduce repetitive work and improve discovery, production, support, analysis and governance. AI remains one component inside the institutional operating system—not the owner of CrownThrive truth, rights or authority.

## System categories

- deterministic automation;
- rules/policy engine;
- statistical/predictive ML;
- anomaly/risk detection;
- retrieval/search;
- generative text/image/audio/video;
- tool-using agent;
- multi-agent workflow;
- human-assisted decision support;
- privacy-preserving/federated/cryptographic model research.

Every system declares its category. “AI-powered” alone is not a system description.

## Lifecycle

`idea → use_case_review → data_review → provider/model_review → prototype → evaluation → controlled_pilot → approved_production → monitored → changed → suspended → retired`

## Data governance

Record training, fine-tuning, retrieval and evaluation data separately. For each dataset capture ownership/license, collection purpose, consent, provenance, demographic/context limits, sensitive classes, retention, transformations, splits, quality, known bias/gaps, version and approved uses.

Do not treat public availability as permission to train, commercialize or infer sensitive characteristics. CrownThrive-owned content remains subject to its rights profile even when publicly viewable.

## Provider and model review

Before production use evaluate:

- provider identity and service terms;
- data retention and training use;
- enterprise/workspace controls;
- region and residency;
- model/version stability;
- security/privacy evidence;
- output/IP provisions;
- moderation and policy limits;
- cost, latency and quotas;
- API/version/deprecation behavior;
- export/portability and fallback;
- incident/support path.

Provider or model changes are versioned releases and may require re-evaluation.

## Prompt and retrieval governance

System prompts, policies, examples, tools and retrieval profiles are versioned institutional artifacts. They have owners, audiences, classifications, tests and release states.

Retrieved files, pages, messages and user content are untrusted data. Defend against prompt injection, malicious links, data exfiltration, tool misuse and instruction collisions. A retrieved source may inform content; it cannot grant authority.

## Evaluation dimensions

Applicable evaluations include:

- factuality and source faithfulness;
- rights/provenance handling;
- policy and role compliance;
- privacy/secrets leakage;
- prompt injection/tool abuse;
- cross-tenant isolation;
- harmful bias and cultural flattening;
- canon consistency;
- Scripture/source boundary;
- legal/financial/medical overclaim;
- robustness to ambiguous or conflicting sources;
- refusal/hold behavior;
- structured-output validity;
- action success and rollback;
- hallucinated claims, IDs, links or routes;
- cost, latency and reliability;
- accessibility and audience fit.

## Evaluation sets

Maintain synthetic fixtures, historical incidents and corrections, adversarial prompts, stale/conflicting sources, unauthorized-user tests, restricted-data canaries, known rights traps, platform-specific scenarios and regression examples. Restricted evaluation data remains private.

## Release threshold

A model score alone does not approve a workflow. Approval considers severity-weighted failure, business impact, human-review coverage, rollback, observability, vendor risk and silent-harm risk.

## Monitoring

Track model/provider/prompt/tool version, request/correlation IDs, classification, tool calls/approvals, latency/cost, refusal/hold rate, correction/escalation rate, error rates, drift, incidents, feedback/appeals and downstream outcomes. Do not log raw restricted inputs/outputs by default.

## Human review

Human review is required where policy specifies it, especially for publication, rights/legal claims, high-impact eligibility, money, safety, security, ministry/theological materials, private journals, regulated categories and novel conditions.

Reviewers receive source and evidence context and can correct, reject, hold or escalate. Human approval is recorded; it does not erase model responsibility or prior versions.

## Generative creative systems

For music, images, writing, video and other creative outputs record model/provider/version, inputs/uploads, generation date/account/tier where relevant, human contribution, edits/arrangement, source assets, voice/likeness, similarity/clearance state, rights claims and output/master locations.

Do not imitate living artists as the production standard or imply endorsement or identity.

## Predictive and risk systems

Scores must be explainable enough for the use, versioned, monitored and appealable where they affect people. They do not independently establish fraud, guilt, compliance, creditworthiness, professional fitness or legal status.

## Agentic systems

Agents require scoped tools, no self-approval, idempotency, independent verification, a kill switch, budget, failure mode, DAIL events and fallback to manual or read-only operation.

## Change and retirement

Re-evaluate material model/provider/prompt/data/tool/policy changes. Retirement includes disabling credentials/tools, preserving required evidence, migrating workflows, updating docs and measuring unresolved dependencies.

## External standards posture

CrownThrive may align controls with current NIST AI risk-management guidance and relevant security/privacy standards. Alignment is not a certification unless independently established.

## Phase 3 deliverables

Seed AI-use-case, model/provider, prompt-policy, dataset, evaluation, agent and incident schemas. Prove at least one low- or medium-risk workflow end to end before expanding autonomy.
