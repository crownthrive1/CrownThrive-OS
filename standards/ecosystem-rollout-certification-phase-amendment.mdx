# Ecosystem Rollout and Certification Phase Amendment

## Status and authority

This amendment records the implementation requirements created by GitHub issues `#178` and `#179` and the THIVEBASE program `ct.program.ecosystem-rollout-certification.v1`.

It does **not** waive the Phase 2.99 hard exit, the full-documentation-estate gate, or the current roadmap authority. Phase 3 remains blocked. Prospective Phase 11–20 requirements remain subordinate to the roadmap-v2 adoption process tracked by the parent institutional roadmap.

## Permanent rollout gates

Every platform rollout must preserve independent state for identity, evidence, provider mapping, authentication, read/write capability, design/brand provenance, interaction/accessibility, rights, credit commerce, fulfillment, reversals, observability/DAIL, recovery/provider exit, public documentation/claims and lifecycle/release.

A later phase may consume a proven earlier result. It may not reclassify an earlier HOLD as PASS by assumption.

## Phase 2.99 — rollout registration and proof

Phase 2.99 requires:

- an issue-backed rollout/certification program and credit-migration program;
- a machine cohort registry;
- explicit stable-ID state with `identity_pending` for unresolved platforms;
- the sixteen-dimension certification matrix;
- a credit-migration queue for every platform with current or prospective commerce impact;
- asset-release certification separate from payment-rail maturity;
- append-only canary evidence;
- explicit legacy-payment/webhook retention state;
- provider/account/project and credential boundaries;
- rights/legal/tax/security/accessibility/regional specialist routing;
- exact recovery/provider-exit references;
- documentation/DAIL and rollback impact.

Phase 2.99 may package, test and prepare monetizable assets. A package remains non-live when rights, license terms, legal/tax, fulfillment, security or authority gates remain open.

## Phase 3 — executable core

Phase 3 must make accepted rollout contracts executable:

- stable platform identity contracts;
- certification-dimension schemas;
- entitlement and `CTL-*` license contracts;
- Store Credits ledger and reversal contracts;
- bounded provider-adapter contracts;
- DAIL evidence contracts;
- customer/account authorization boundaries;
- independent verification and rollback.

At least one accepted pilot cohort must prove the applicable lifecycle before wider federation.

## Phase 4 — federated ecosystem activation

Phase 4 federates certified identity, entitlement, consent, events, design, contact, analytics and commerce contracts. Platform-specific rights, economic and policy distinctions remain explicit.

A federated wallet does not imply that every platform has the same offer, refund, rights, age, jurisdiction or settlement rules.

## Phase 5 — revenue and market activation

Phase 5 requires attributable and supportable revenue evidence:

- funded credits and provider settlement reconciliation;
- purchase and entitlement correctness;
- deliverability/access;
- refunds/disputes/reversals;
- revenue recognition/economic owner rules;
- conversion and support telemetry;
- anomaly and fraud controls;
- documented customer remedies.

Revenue activation cannot precede the applicable fulfillment and legal/economic gates.

## Phase 6 — licensing, IP and developer economy

Phase 6 externalizes mature, rights-cleared CrownThrive capabilities such as:

- runbooks;
- developer kits;
- APIs/MCP access;
- governed design-system assets;
- website/contact automation templates;
- implementation services;
- conformance tooling.

Each release requires versioned rights/license terms, support boundaries, provenance, delivery and remedies. Trade-secret/restricted kernels remain separate from public/licensed projections.

## Phase 7 — physical, phygital and regional expansion

Phase 7 may extend Store Credits and certified interactions to appropriate MM Suites, kiosk, event, location and device experiences only after consumer, accessibility, regional and payment/settlement classification review.

Store Credits do not become a general payment instrument merely because a kiosk can read the balance.

## Phase 8 — Holdings and portfolio scale

Phase 8 applies:

- provider concentration/custody controls;
- IP and asset custody;
- platform-level revenue attribution;
- legal/entity/economic separation;
- cross-platform operational risk;
- provider replacement and export/restore readiness.

## Phase 9 — advanced interoperability

Phase 9 may add cryptographic provenance, credentials or other advanced interoperability only for a validated control problem and after security/legal/rights/economic review.

Store Credits remain a closed-loop accounting mechanism unless separately reclassified through governed legal/economic authority. Machine-readable value is not token/crypto authority.

## Phase 10 — continuity

Phase 10 requires reconstruction evidence for:

- rollout cohort and certification state;
- credit balances from append-only events;
- orders, entitlements and license lineage;
- asset versions and hashes;
- canary history;
- provider/project/account mappings;
- legacy-rail retirement history;
- policies, agent contracts and restore procedures.

## Prospective Phase 11 — institutional replication

Package the accepted rollout/certification framework for repeatable deployments without flattening local authority, rights, jurisdiction or culture.

## Prospective Phase 12 — enterprise federation

Support multi-organization rollout/certification with explicit authority, identity, data, contract and settlement boundaries.

## Prospective Phase 13 — standards and certification

Formalize CrownThrive/CHLOM conformance profiles, qualified implementer requirements, certification evidence and customer-facing assurance only after lower-phase operational proof.

## Prospective Phase 14 — autonomous operations

Expand agentic rollout, remediation and self-healing only where evidence, rollback, authority ceilings, anomaly handling and human escalation have been demonstrated.

## Prospective Phase 15 — knowledge and model sovereignty

Make rollout/certification memory, evals, evidence retrieval and agent contracts portable across model/provider changes.

## Prospective Phase 16 — treasury and settlement

Apply accounting, treasury, settlement and reconciliation controls to payment-provider funding and Store Credits while preserving their closed-loop/non-transferable boundary unless a separately governed classification changes it.

## Prospective Phase 17 — global IP exchange

Scale rights/licensing exchange and creator/developer participation under jurisdiction-aware provenance, chain-of-title and remedies.

## Prospective Phase 18 — civic and institutional partnerships

Define rollout/certification profiles for civic, educational, cultural and institutional partners with public-interest and accountability controls.

## Prospective Phase 19 — intergenerational oversight

Introduce independent long-horizon review, succession and cultural/institutional stewardship for certification standards and commerce policy.

## Prospective Phase 20 — perpetual institutional continuity

Prove that rollout state, credits, rights, licenses, evidence, assets and recovery remain reconstructable through provider, model, cloud, repository, leadership and generation changes.

## Fail-closed conditions

The rollout program must HOLD rather than promote when any applicable condition is true:

- unresolved stable identity;
- contradictory current evidence;
- provider capability represented as deployed integration;
- missing credential/auth boundary;
- provider write represented from read success;
- missing rights/chain-of-title state;
- Store Credits legal/tax gate not accepted;
- package/license terms still marked draft/review-required;
- private delivery or reversal behavior unproven where applicable;
- production legacy rail scheduled for retirement before replacement proof;
- public claims ahead of evidence;
- Phase 3 entry asserted before the Phase 2.99 hard exit.
