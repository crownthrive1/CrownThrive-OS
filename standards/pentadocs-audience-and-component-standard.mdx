PentaDocs is CrownThrive's institutional knowledge layer. Mintlify is a rendering, navigation, search, and hosting provider. A provider component can improve comprehension, but no component creates institutional authority or evidence.

## Outcome

Every substantive PentaDocs page must help one primary audience answer a defined set of questions, see the exact current-state boundary, find the governing record, and know what to do next. Pages may serve secondary readers, but they must not combine incompatible public, operator, developer, legal, restricted, and historical instructions into one undifferentiated body.

  <Card title="Human outcome" icon="person">

## Non-negotiable page rules

1. Assign one `primary_audience` and one `page_type`.
2. State the editorial `content_state` without collapsing runtime, evidence, legal, rights, economic, deployment, or provider-write state.
3. Separate current, target, historical, superseded, candidate, and unresolved material.
4. Date and source every material current-state, metric, production, certification, price, legal-status, registration, rights, provider, or availability claim.
5. Use public-safe evidence references instead of restricted evidence bodies.
6. Preserve stable article identity and correction/supersession lineage where available.
7. Use components for meaning, sequence, comparison, navigation, or emphasis—not decoration.
8. Include meaningful root-relative links to the governing and next-action records.
9. Preserve mobile readability, accessible media, plain language, and non-color status cues.
10. Treat validation, source acceptance, projection deployment, runtime verification, and certification as separate states.

## Required frontmatter

Version 1.0.0 pages require four audience/profile fields in addition to the existing `title` and `description`, for six required fields total:

```yaml
standard_version: "1.0.0"
primary_audience: "executive"
page_type: "doctrine"
content_state: "current_with_holds"
```

The six required fields are:

- `title`;
- `description`;
- `standard_version`;
- `primary_audience`;
- `page_type`;
- `content_state`.

An `article_id`, verification date, evidence level, source references, owner, and version remain strongly recommended and should resolve through the governed article manifest rather than being invented during a formatting migration.

### Primary audience values

`executive`, `public`, `operator`, `developer`, `rights_support`, or `historical`.

### Page type values

`orientation`, `doctrine`, `registry`, `reference`, `status`, `workflow`, `runbook`, `standard`, `support`, `how_to`, `policy`, `legal`, `developer`, `changelog`, `historical_record`, or `redirect`.

### Content state values

| State | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `current` | The page represents current accepted meaning within its exact scope. |
| `current_with_holds` | Current meaning is documented, but named capability, evidence, provider, legal, rights, economic, or release predicates remain open. |
| `candidate` | Proposed source awaiting applicable acceptance or promotion. |
| `historical` | Dated context with no present implementation authority. |
| `superseded` | Replaced by an identified successor and retained for lineage. |
| `unresolved` | Source, owner, evidence, contradiction, or disposition is not yet resolved. |

`content_state` describes the page's editorial posture. It does not replace the independent states in the [Institutional Record and Format Standard](/standards/record-and-format-standard).

## Audience orientation

Every governed non-redirect version 1.0.0 page must begin with a bounded audience-orientation block. Managed blocks use only the escaped, normalized values already present in `title`, `description`, `primary_audience`, `page_type`, and `content_state`, plus two selected governing-link records. They do not invent page claims, owners, dates, evidence, or operational state.

```mdx
{/* pentadocs:audience-orientation:v2 */}
<Info>
  **Audience:** [Plain-language audience] (`public`).

  **This page:** [Escaped frontmatter title] — [Escaped frontmatter description]

  **Documentation state:** `unresolved` for page type `reference`. Documentation state is editorial posture only; it does not certify runtime, deployment, legal, rights, financial, provider, production, release, security, or operational status.

  **Unresolved reason:** no explicit page-level editorial acceptance state was declared in the migrated source.

  **Evidence needed:** an accepted governing source or accountable-owner attestation.

  **Review role:** PentaDocs governance.

  **Review trigger:** next material edit or source reconciliation.

  **Related guidance:** [Documentation source governance](/standards/documentation-source-of-truth-and-autonomous-governance) and the [Institutional record standard](/standards/record-and-format-standard).
</Info>
{/* /pentadocs:audience-orientation:v2 */}
```

The opening marker must immediately precede one top-level `Info`, `Note`, or `Warning` callout, and the closing marker must immediately follow that callout. The block must:

- contain the exact bold labels `Audience:`, `This page:`, and `Documentation state:`;
- identify the reader, the page-specific title and description, the page type, and the editorial content state;
- include the exact boundary: **Documentation state is editorial posture only; it does not certify runtime, deployment, legal, rights, financial, provider, production, release, security, or operational status.**;
- contain exactly two selected links in `managed_v2` mode, or at least two links in `custom` mode; every counted link must be distinct, existing, root-relative, non-self, and not a redirect source;
- escape MDX- and Markdown-active characters from source scalars before rendering; and
- avoid restricted information and unsupported state language.

Historical, superseded, changelog, and historical-record pages must use `Warning` and additionally include the exact sentence: **This page is dated context, not current operating authority.**

An `unresolved` block must also include these four exact labels and governed fallback statements:

- **Unresolved reason:** no explicit page-level editorial acceptance state was declared in the migrated source.
- **Evidence needed:** an accepted governing source or accountable-owner attestation.
- **Review role:** PentaDocs governance.
- **Review trigger:** next material edit or source reconciliation.

### Managed, custom, and redirect modes

The generated page-profile manifest records exactly one orientation mode for every governed route:

| Mode | Contract |
| --- | --- |
| `managed_v2` | The renderer owns the exact bounded block and selects exactly two links. Its input and rendered-byte SHA-256 hashes must match the current deterministic projection. |
| `custom` | Tailored prose and useful additional links are preserved through an explicit page-profile override. The same labels, exact state boundary, historical sentence when applicable, and a minimum of two distinct existing non-self, non-redirect links remain mandatory. |
| `redirect_exempt` | A typed redirect has no orientation block and remains subject to the redirect contract. |

Orientation mode is generated profile metadata. Authors must not add `orientation_mode` to page frontmatter. An exact known generated-v1 block may be replaced by the v2 renderer. A non-matching custom block must never be silently overwritten; it must be registered and validated as `custom`.

### Deterministic inputs, hashes, and corpus metrics

The input hash is the SHA-256 digest of canonical UTF-8 JSON containing the normalized decoded `title`, `description`, `primary_audience`, `page_type`, `content_state`, and the ordered labels and targets of two selected links. Managed mode has exactly those two links. Custom mode may retain additional valid links in the rendered block while the first two deterministic valid links form the input-hash link records. The route is excluded so duplicate page-specific inputs remain observable as collisions. The render hash covers the exact bounded orientation bytes, including any additional custom links. Unicode is normalized to NFC, whitespace is collapsed for scalar inputs, and escaping is applied once from decoded source characters.

The profile manifest and validator must fail closed unless all of these corpus invariants hold:

- `page_specific_orientation_rate` is `1.0` for governed non-redirect pages;
- `generic_v1_page_count` is `0`;
- `self_link_page_count` is `0`;
- `invalid_link_page_count` is `0`;
- `input_collision_groups` and `input_collision_excess` are `0`; and
- `render_collision_groups` and `render_collision_excess` are `0`.

Validation must also prove hash drift when any managed input changes, idempotence on a second migration run, safe escaping of MDX-active input, exact-v1-only replacement, custom-orientation retention, and preservation of the normalized substantive body outside the bounded orientation block.

## Current-state and evidence boundary

The page must make the real-life picture visible: what is current, what is proven, what is bounded, what remains held, and what is historical.

For each material claim, record or link to:

- exact subject and scope;
- state dimension being described;
- as-of date or effective period;
- source or evidence reference;
- applicable evidence class;
- accountable owner or reviewing role;
- unresolved predicates and next review where applicable.

Use the [Evidence, Claims and Proof Standard](/standards/evidence-claims-and-proof-standard):

`E0_unverified → E1_source_exists → E2_owner_attested → E3_operational → E4_transactional → E5_independent → E6_reconciled`.

<Warning>
  A route, interface, component, repository merge, generated file, successful request, provider feature, payment surface, or documentation page proves only its exact observed event or representation. It does not independently prove production, certification, legal sufficiency, rights clearance, entitlement, settlement, or provider-wide authority.
</Warning>

### Required state language

| Label | Required meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `CURRENT — VERIFIED AS OF [date]` | Current claim with exact scope and evidence reference. |
| `TARGET` | Intended architecture or outcome; not current-state proof. |
| `HISTORICAL` | Dated context only; no implementation authority. |
| `HOLD` | A required predicate is unresolved; name the prohibited action. |
| `BLOCKED` | Name the dependency, owner, evidence needed, and next review. |
| `FAILED` | State the attempted assertion and missing/negative readback; never issue PASS. |
| `PASS` | Name the exact tested assertion, environment, timestamp, and evidence. |
| `DEGRADED` | Identify the working and failed scope, workaround, and escalation. |
| `CERTIFIED` | Use only with an applicable authorized certification record. |

## Audience archetypes

### Executive

Required questions:

- Why does this matter strategically?
- What decision, outcome, value, impact, or risk is involved?
- What is the current verified state?
- How does it connect to CHLOM, CIE, the Thrive Flywheel, corridors, platforms, and continuity?
- Who owns the decision, what is blocked, and what comes next?

Required sections:

1. Executive outcome
2. Current state and evidence
3. Strategic fit
4. Authority and accountable owner
5. Metrics and impact
6. Risks, blockers, and dependencies
7. Decision or next milestone
8. Continuity and related records

Preferred components: `Columns`, `Card`, state tables, compact Mermaid, `Info`, and `Warning`.

Escalation language: `DECISION REQUIRED — D3 human-reserved` or `HOLD — [predicate]; [action] remains prohibited`.

### Public

Required questions:

- What is this and how does it help me?
- Is it available, and am I eligible?
- What should I do and expect?
- What payment, privacy, rights, accessibility, or support conditions apply?

Required sections:

1. What this helps you do
2. Availability and eligibility
3. How it works
4. Options and expected outcome
5. Privacy, payment, rights, and accessibility
6. Troubleshooting
7. Get help

Preferred components: `Columns`, `Card`, `Steps`, `Tabs`, exact comparison tables, accessible media, and restrained callouts.

Use `AVAILABLE NOW`, `PILOT`, `WAITLIST`, `PLANNED`, or `UNAVAILABLE` only when supported. Do not expose unexplained internal authority codes or invent availability, prices, fulfillment promises, or SLAs.

### Operator

Required questions:

- When is this workflow used and who may run it?
- What preconditions, inputs, permissions, backups, and stop conditions apply?
- What steps, readbacks, evidence, rollback, and escalation are required?

Required sections:

1. Objective and scope
2. Authority and preconditions
3. Inputs and protected-data boundary
4. Procedure
5. Verification and readback
6. Failure and rollback
7. Evidence and closure
8. Escalation

Preferred components: `Steps`, task lists, exact tables, `CodeGroup`, branching Mermaid, `Warning`, and `Danger`.

Escalation language: `STOP — do not continue`, `FAILED — no PASS issued`, and `ROLLBACK REQUIRED — [trigger and target]`.

### Developer

Required questions:

- What contract, version, environment, authentication, and base URL apply?
- What is the smallest safe example?
- What parameters, responses, errors, limits, retries, events, and compatibility rules apply?
- What is documented, sandbox-tested, controlled-test, read-verified, or write-verified?

Required sections:

1. Quickstart
2. Contract and current-state matrix
3. Authentication and permissions
4. Environments and versions
5. Request, parameters, and response
6. Errors, limits, retries, and idempotency
7. Webhooks and events
8. Testing and certification boundary
9. Security, compatibility, and support

Preferred components: `CodeGroup`, `ParamField`, `ResponseField`, `RequestExample`, `ResponseExample`, `Expandable` for nested API fields, `Tabs`, `Steps`, and event-flow Mermaid.

Required distinction: `documented_contract`, `sandbox_verified`, `controlled_test`, `production_read_verified`, and `production_write_verified` are not interchangeable.

### Rights and support

Required questions:

- What right, license, complaint, policy, or support issue is covered?
- What governing instrument, evidence, intake, process, authority, privacy, remedy, or review applies?
- When is qualified legal, security, privacy, finance, cultural, or rights review required?

Required sections:

1. Plain-language summary
2. Scope and applicability
3. Current evidence state
4. Permitted and prohibited activity
5. Intake requirements
6. Review process
7. Authority and decision boundary
8. Evidence and privacy handling
9. Outcome, remedy, response, or appeal
10. Contact and escalation

Preferred components: `Warning`, `Danger`, `Info`, issue-type `Tabs`, `Steps`, exact tables, and machine-schema code only where needed.

Required language: **Public visibility is not a license.** A documentation page does not replace a signed agreement, controlling policy, applicable law, or qualified review.

### Historical

Required questions:

- What source is this, when was it effective, and how is it classified?
- What did it historically propose?
- What superseded it, what remains latent, and what does it not prove?
- What current source and approvals would be required before reuse?

Required sections:

1. Historical-context warning
2. Source identity and classification
3. Historical summary
4. Historical architecture or timeline
5. Then-versus-now comparison
6. Current successor
7. Latent concepts
8. Explicit non-authority boundary
9. Reactivation requirements
10. Provenance and restrictions

Preferred components: `Warning`, historical `Badge`, comparison tables, Then/Now `Tabs`, lineage Mermaid, and source-detail disclosure only when public-safe.

Required language includes `context_mode: historical_only`, `implementation_authority: none`, and `production_proof: false`. Follow the [Historical Context Boundary](/knowledge/historical-context-boundary).

## Mintlify component semantics

The provider behavior below follows Mintlify's official component documentation. CrownThrive adds governance rules without redefining the provider component.

| Component | Provider-supported purpose | CrownThrive rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Columns` | Responsive grid of one to four columns | Use instead of `CardGroup`. Prefer two columns for substantive mobile content; three or four only for short cards. |
| `Card` | Distinct navigation or grouped information | Give every linked card a real destination and descriptive title. A card is not proof. |
| `Steps` / `Step` | Sequential actions or events | Use only when order matters. Do not turn historical designs into executable instructions. |
| `Tabs` / `Tab` | Switchable options, versions, platforms, or languages | Do not hide required warnings or use tabs as access control. |
| `CodeGroup` | Multiple titled code examples | Examples must match a governed contract and use placeholders for secrets. |
| `ParamField` / `ResponseField` | API or SDK request/response fields | Use exact names, types, defaults, required/deprecated state, and contract version. |
| `Expandable` | Nested request/response object properties | Do not use as a generic FAQ substitute. |
| `RequestExample` / `ResponseExample` | Desktop sidebar examples that become inline on mobile | Each fenced block needs a title; examples remain non-authorizing evidence. |
| `Note` | Supporting nuance | Do not bury a stop condition in a note. |
| `Info` | Important context | Use for orientation and bounded explanatory state. |
| `Tip` | Optional helpful practice | Never use for a mandatory control. |
| `Check` | Confirmed item or positive checkpoint | State what evidence supports the check. |
| `Warning` | Material limitation, hold, or caution | Name the condition and prohibited action. |
| `Danger` | Irreversible, destructive, legal, financial, privacy, or security risk | Use sparingly and include escalation/rollback. |
| `Badge` | Inline status, version, or metadata label | Pair color with text; a badge never certifies itself. |
| `Update` | Dated release note, changelog, or product update | Do not use as a generic current-state box; it can generate RSS entries. |
| `Frame` | Styled visual/media container with optional caption | Require alt text and a useful caption when context is not obvious. |
| `Mermaid` | Rendered relationship or sequence diagram | Use only when the relationship is easier to understand visually. |
| `Visibility` | Conditional human-web versus agent-Markdown content | Never treat as authentication, authorization, redaction, or privacy control. Both variants must be public-safe. |
| `Prompt` | Copyable/openable pre-built AI prompt | Publish only intentional public prompts with no secrets, protected system prompts, or unauthorized actions. |
| `Icon` | Inline decorative or emphasis icon | Do not use icons as the only meaning carrier. |
| `Color` | Color/design token display | Restrict to brand/design reference; never encode status by color alone. |
| `Tree` | File or hierarchy representation | Show only real, public-safe structure and label target versus current structure. |
| Image/video/embed | Media | Require rights/provenance, alt text, caption or transcript, and a stable public-safe source. |

## Accessibility and responsive behavior

- The frontmatter `title` supplies the single H1; the body starts at H2 and continues in order.
- Write link text that explains the destination.
- Give images meaningful alt text; decorative images use empty alt text.
- Provide captions/transcripts for material video or audio.
- Never rely on color, icon, placement, or animation alone.
- Keep tables focused; use prose or stacked sections when a wide table becomes unusable on mobile.
- Prefer `Columns cols={2}` for substantial content and verify its collapsed mobile order.
- Keep code examples horizontally manageable and explain required inputs outside the code.
- Preserve plain-text meaning for Markdown/agent projection.

## Visibility and public/private boundaries

`Visibility for="humans"` and `Visibility for="agents"` control provider rendering between the web UI and Markdown output. They do not secure content. Hidden navigation, `noindex`, tabs, accordions, CSS, comments, and direct-link obscurity are also not security controls.

Restricted, internal, strategic, confidential, privileged, machine-only, credential, personal, payment, legal-strategy, protected evidence, or unreleased source material must remain outside the public MDX source.

## Redirect profile

A `page_type: redirect` profile is exempt from audience-orientation, substantive-body, and H1 richness requirements only when it includes:

```yaml
page_type: "redirect"
content_state: "superseded"
deprecated: true
noindex: true
redirect_to: "/root-relative-canonical-route"
```

The destination must be root-relative, canonical, public-safe, and non-circular. Redirect chains and external redirect targets fail the profile.

## Validation is not certification

| State | What it proves | What it does not prove |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Source parse | JSON/MDX/frontmatter can be read | Meaning, evidence, deployment, or adoption |
| Structural validation | Required metadata, marker, sections, and component rules conform | Runtime behavior, rights, legal sufficiency, or production |
| Link validation | Referenced source routes resolve in the checked tree | External service availability or current provider behavior |
| Source acceptance | Governed source revision was accepted | Mintlify deployment, indexing, or anonymous availability |
| Projection readback | A specific rendered page was retrieved | Underlying system production or legal/economic authority |
| Runtime verification | A scoped operation/readback was observed | Provider-wide authority or institutional certification |
| Certification | An authorized certification record covers an exact scope | Unrelated capabilities, future state, or perpetual validity |

No author, formatter, component, test, validator, workflow, provider, or agent may self-certify a consequential claim merely because it generated or validated the source.

## Enforcement and migration

New or materially changed pages must meet version 1.0.0 immediately once the standard is accepted and the applicable merge gate is active. Existing pages should be classified and upgraded in registered waves:

1. public legal, rights, payment, pricing, current-state, production, and developer-write pages;
2. operator runbooks and workflows;
3. CHLOM and platform registries;
4. public/community/support guidance;
5. doctrine, portfolio, and executive pages;
6. historical, superseded, and long-tail records.

A migration must never invent `article_id` values, owners, evidence, current-state claims, or certification merely to satisfy a formatter. Missing data becomes `unresolved` or `current_with_holds` with a governed delta.

## Definition of done

A page is format-complete when:

- the six required frontmatter fields validate;
- its primary audience and page type are accurate;
- the orientation marker/callout passes unless it is a typed redirect;
- required archetype questions and sections are answered;
- current, target, historical, held, and unresolved states are explicit;
- material claims have appropriate source/evidence boundaries;
- components follow their semantic purpose;
- public/private and accessibility checks pass;
- governing and next-action links resolve;
- validation, source acceptance, projection, runtime, and certification remain distinct;
- correction, supersession, redirect, or escalation is preserved where applicable.

Format completion is documentation evidence only. It never manufactures broader CrownThrive authority.
