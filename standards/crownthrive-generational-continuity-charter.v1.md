# CrownThrive Seven-Generation Continuity Charter v1

## Purpose

This charter establishes a long-horizon continuity rule for CrownThrive, CHLOM, the CrownThrive Operating System, the Cultural Imprint Engine, Pentafabric, and associated institutional assets. It is designed to preserve mission, provenance, rights, stewardship, maintainability, and orderly succession across seven planning generations while leaving room for lawful human governance, new team members, users, and future institutional forms.

## Seven-generation planning horizon

The seven-generation horizon is a planning and stewardship commitment, not an irrevocable transfer of legal ownership. Each generation inherits an evidence-backed institutional state, operating constraints, rights records, maintenance obligations, and the authority actually granted to it at the time.

The planning model is:

1. Generation 1 — Founder and originating architecture.
2. Generation 2 — Continuity and institutionalization.
3. Generation 3 — Expansion and professional stewardship.
4. Generation 4 — Distributed leadership and ecosystem maturity.
5. Generation 5 — Long-horizon resilience and succession.
6. Generation 6 — Intergenerational modernization and preservation.
7. Generation 7 — Re-adjudication of mission, rights, and stewardship under then-current law and evidence.

## Human lineage and institutional succession

Founder intent is preserved as historical and governance evidence. It does not allow an automated system to manufacture legal authority or bypass applicable law.

The institutional record must distinguish:

- founder-originated intent;
- current lawful ownership;
- current authorized officers or stewards;
- delegated team authority;
- successor authority;
- beneficiary or family lineage evidence where lawfully applicable;
- user and community participation rights;
- contractor and contributor rights;
- third-party rights;
- reserved founder decisions;
- expired, revoked, or superseded authority.

Succession records must be effective-dated, attributable, reviewable, and reversible where legally and operationally possible.

## Team and user architecture

Continuity does not mean founder-only control. The system must make room for employees, contractors, advisors, operators, developers, creators, partners, users, community members, trustees, successors, and future institutional roles.

Every consequential role should have:

- a stable identity;
- explicit permissions;
- separation of duties;
- an authority ceiling;
- a defined scope;
- evidence of appointment or delegation;
- expiry or review conditions where appropriate;
- an audit trail;
- a succession path.

## Generational lock

The generational lock is a preservation mechanism for mission, provenance, rights, architectural lineage, and institutional memory. It is not a permanent freeze.

The lock requires that consequential changes preserve or explicitly adjudicate:

- canonical source lineage;
- intellectual-property provenance;
- rights and licensing state;
- DAIL evidence lineage;
- CHLOM contracts and conditions;
- Pentafabric layer ownership and interfaces;
- recovery and restoration capability;
- security boundaries;
- economic authority;
- human succession evidence;
- public/private classification.

## No autonomous succession

Agents may maintain succession records, detect gaps, prepare candidate packets, and request review. Agents may not appoint themselves, transfer ownership, rewrite founder lineage, appoint a successor, dissolve CrownThrive, or create a new legal entity without separately authorized human and legal processes.

## Continuity tests

At each institutional release gate, the system should test whether a qualified successor could reconstruct the architecture, understand the rights state, locate protected evidence through authorized paths, restore required services, operate the public-safe documentation, and identify unresolved obligations.

A system that only its current builders understand is not considered generationally maintainable.
