# Platform Extraction and Next-Run Preparation Standard

Every platform review must leave a versioned institutional package—not a loose summary, screenshot set or chat narrative. The package must let a future human or agent understand, operate, integrate, migrate, protect and improve the platform without rediscovering its foundations.

## 1. Extraction identity

Record:

- extraction/run ID;
- platform stable ID and canonical name;
- aliases, historical names and successor/predecessor;
- accountable owner and reviewers;
- release version and source snapshot date;
- intended audience of the package;
- public/private/restricted projections;
- current confidence/evidence state;
- extraction completeness percentage by domain—not one vague total.

## 2. Institutional identity and portfolio role

Capture:

- legal owner/operator and control basis;
- asset type: platform, brand, imprint, engine, framework, program, channel, room, service or vendor-backed implementation;
- corridor/lane and Thrive Flywheel role;
- Cultural Imprint Engine profile;
- CHLOM capability pallets/six functions;
- primary acquisition, activation, retention, revenue, distribution and impact roles;
- P0/P1/P2 portfolio priority;
- lifecycle, implementation, legal and evidence states;
- current public approved wording.

## 3. Domains, URLs and search state

Inventory all:

- registered and historical domains/subdomains;
- canonical host and alternates;
- public routes and route classes;
- account, admin, API, webhook, callback, media, download and status hosts;
- redirects and inbound-link dependencies;
- DNS/registrar/TLS/CDN/WAF ownership;
- robots, sitemap indexes and structured-data profiles;
- analytics/search-console connections where authorized;
- stale/duplicate/dead routes and migration candidates.

## 4. Technology and deployment

Capture:

- product/vendor/engine/framework and version;
- hosting/cloud/account/project/region;
- source repositories, branches, tags and deployment workflow;
- runtime/languages/packages;
- databases, object storage, queues, workflows, search, caches and CDN;
- themes/plugins/custom code for WordPress or hosted tools;
- environments and URLs;
- infrastructure-as-code or manual configuration;
- feature flags and migrations;
- observability, health and status;
- backup/restore and vendor exit;
- known unsupported/end-of-life dependencies;
- cost, renewal and license ownership.

## 5. Identity, roles and access

Inventory:

- auth providers and login methods;
- current account/identity model;
- CrownThrive ID/SSO readiness and account-link fields;
- organization/team/tenant relationships;
- roles, permissions, delegated authority and relationship checks;
- MFA and recovery;
- service accounts, bots and API clients;
- admin/super-admin/break-glass access;
- onboarding, verification, suspension, appeal, offboarding and deletion;
- current access-review gaps.

## 6. Audiences and role surfaces

For each audience/role, list available or required:

- public experience;
- visitor/member/customer account;
- creator/contributor;
- seller/licensor/licensee;
- affiliate/ambassador;
- advertiser/publisher;
- developer/partner;
- instructor/mentor/church/school;
- Suite Pro/location/operator;
- support/moderator/reviewer;
- admin/super-admin;
- founder/portfolio/governance;
- machine/API/MCP.

Map portals, dashboards, navigation, permissions, workflows, shared widgets and platform-specific interfaces.

## 7. Products, services and commerce

Capture every product/service family and its state:

- work/product/SKU/edition/offer IDs;
- public title and internal version;
- source/master/files;
- pricing and currency;
- free/paid/subscription/service/license;
- Stripe/provider Product/Price/account references;
- checkout route and environment;
- webhook events and endpoint;
- order/subscription records;
- entitlement/fulfillment/download limits;
- tax, promo, trial, cancellation, refund and dispute;
- certificate/license terms;
- support and reconciliation;
- historical price versus active offer;
- unsupported physical or legacy offers;
- revenue, commission, royalty and allocation schedules.

Test the complete customer lifecycle. Button presence is not checkout readiness.

## 8. Content, IP and canon package

### Media/music/story platforms

Extract imprints, universes/worlds, series, characters, canon, chronology, works, lyrics, compositions, masters/stems, books, editions, portraits/no-likeness identities, evidence/story formats, radio/programs, rooms, products, source bibles, audience/content controls, adaptations and participatory rights.

### Ministry/education platforms

Extract Scripture/content boundaries, rooms, studies, curricula, courses, devotionals, reading plans, journals/reflections, people/places/events/themes, products/editions, memberships, church/teacher/pastor/student roles, licenses, minors/privacy and review standards.

### Brand/commerce/place platforms

Extract brand/imprint, product formulas/designs/templates, suppliers, wholesale, service standards, locations/territories, professional roles, training, customer consent, marketplace/booking, rewards/referrals and licensing/readiness.

Every asset receives chain-of-title/provenance, source location, rights state, classification, current version and related platform/product/license IDs.

## 9. Data and events

Inventory:

- data entities and source-of-truth owner;
- fields and classification;
- tenant/organization partition;
- collection purpose and consent;
- retention/deletion/legal hold;
- cross-domain flows;
- analytics/derived data;
- event names/schemas;
- personally sensitive data;
- exports/imports;
- data-quality issues;
- backup/restore;
- current privacy/security risks.

No generic “sync” record is sufficient; define fields, purpose, trigger, authority and failure behavior.

## 10. API, webhook, feed, export and MCP inventory

Collect and verify:

- official docs and source contracts;
- base URLs by environment;
- REST/GraphQL/RPC/feed/export/database interfaces;
- endpoints, methods, schemas and errors;
- authentication/OAuth/OIDC/scopes;
- SDKs/packages/Postman collections;
- rate/cost limits;
- pagination/filtering;
- idempotency/concurrency;
- webhook endpoint, signatures, events, retry/replay/dead-letter;
- health/version;
- platform MCP opportunities and existing tools;
- vendor adapters and replacement path;
- test evidence and support status.

Unknown interfaces remain `unverified`. Do not guess private URLs or claim APIs from marketing language.

## 11. Legal Depot and policy dependencies

Map applicable:

- master terms/privacy/cookies/communications;
- platform addendum;
- role/program terms;
- commerce/refunds/subscriptions;
- creator/content/AI/provenance;
- affiliate/advertising;
- developer/API/MCP;
- rights/licensing/DMCA;
- minors/education/ministry;
- MM Suites/place/operator;
- CII/impact;
- employment/contractor/vendor;
- support/SLA/security;
- migration/sunset;
- jurisdiction/regulated category;
- historical supersession and acceptance evidence.

Identify claims requiring current legal/filing/registration evidence.

## 12. Search, metadata and discovery

Audit:

- titles/descriptions/canonicals;
- structured data;
- robots and sitemaps;
- duplicate/thin/orphan pages;
- internal links/navigation;
- accessibility/alt/captions/transcripts;
- stale metrics/pricing/status;
- index leakage of private/token routes;
- programmatic SEO quality;
- public API/feed/AI-discovery outputs;
- canonical ownership across CrownThrive platforms;
- performance and search health.

Create a versioned platform SEO profile and automated fleet checks.

## 13. Support, operations and continuity

Capture:

- support channels and ticket/provider;
- SLA/tier/escalation;
- help articles/SOPs/runbooks;
- moderation/abuse;
- incident and status communications;
- payment/rights/security runbooks;
- operating cadence;
- owner/on-call;
- backup/restore and recovery objectives;
- domain/provider failure path;
- vendor exit;
- data/customer portability;
- succession/offboarding;
- current single points of failure.

## 14. Claims, metrics and analytics

List every material public/management claim with source, definition, date, method and evidence level. Separate records, members, users, visitors, listings, products, active SKUs, revenue, GMV, projections, plays, releases, licenses and impact.

## 15. Cross-platform handoffs

For every integration/handoff identify:

- source and destination platform;
- purpose and user value;
- trigger/event;
- fields/data class;
- rights/consent/policy;
- identity and entitlement handling;
- API/MCP/adapter;
- owner;
- failure/retry/reconciliation;
- analytics return signal;
- lifecycle/version.

## 16. Cleanup and technical debt

Inventory duplicate routes, stale credentials, old webhooks, unused branches, deprecated plugins, unsupported APIs, dead vendors, bridge scripts, abandoned products, outdated claims and source duplicates. Apply the Legacy Cleanup standard; do not delete before dependency/evidence review.

## 17. Risk and gap ledger

Classify gaps by:

- rights/legal;
- security/privacy;
- identity/access;
- payment/commerce;
- data/integration;
- support/continuity;
- content/canon/culture;
- accessibility/search;
- technical/dependency;
- economic/financial;
- evidence/claim;
- organizational capacity.

Record severity, owner, mitigation, blocker status, accepted/deferred state and next review.

## 18. Next-run packet

Every extraction ends with:

- exact branch/commit/deployment and release version;
- completed/changed/deferred records;
- source set and snapshot dates;
- tests and evidence;
- unresolved conflicts and decisions;
- API/domain/vendor deltas;
- migration/cleanup plan;
- rollback/recovery notes;
- next workstreams in dependency order;
- required tools/permissions by reference;
- no-repeat list;
- inherited platform and phase state.

## Definition of complete extraction

“Complete” means every domain above is either populated with evidence or explicitly marked `uninspected`, `not_applicable`, `source_not_located`, `blocked` or `deferred` with owner/reason. Empty cells are not completeness.
