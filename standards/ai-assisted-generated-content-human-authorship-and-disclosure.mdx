# AI-Assisted and Generated Content: Human Authorship, Rights and Disclosure Standard

<Warning>
  This is an institutional governance standard, not legal advice. AI copyright, publicity, privacy, consumer-protection, contract and platform rules continue to evolve. Valuable, disputed, exclusive, synthetic-person, registration and cross-border uses require qualified current review.
</Warning>

## Core doctrine

CrownThrive does **not** use the phrase “AI-generated” or “AI-assisted” as a substitute for a rights analysis.

Four independent questions must remain separate:

1. **Provider permission** — may the output be used commercially under the applicable provider terms and account facts?
2. **Copyrightability** — what human-authored expression, if any, qualifies for copyright protection under applicable law?
3. **Ownership/control** — who controls each composition, master, text, image, code, performance, voice, likeness, mark and source input?
4. **Downstream license** — what exact rights may CrownThrive grant to a buyer, partner, platform or model developer?

The institutional phrase is:

> **Commercial permission, copyright, ownership, and customer permission are not synonyms.**

## Current U.S. Copyright Office alignment

The U.S. Copyright Office's 2025 Part 2 AI report states that generative-AI outputs can be copyright-protected only where a human author determined sufficient expressive elements. Human-authored material perceptible in the output, creative arrangement, or human modifications may qualify; **mere provision of prompts does not by itself supply sufficient human authorship**. AI assistance or inclusion of AI-generated material within a larger human-authored work does not automatically bar copyrightability.

The Office's 2023 registration guidance also requires applicants to disclose AI-generated content in works submitted for registration and to claim only the human-authored material they are entitled to claim.

Institutional consequence: CrownThrive must preserve **work-level human-contribution evidence**, not rely on blanket statements such as “human-directed,” “we paid for the tool,” or “the platform says commercial use is allowed.”

## Content/provenance classes

Every material AI-assisted asset should use one of these working classifications:

- `HUMAN_AUTHORED_NO_GENERATIVE_AI`
- `AI_ASSISTED_HUMAN_AUTHORED`
- `MIXED_HUMAN_AND_AI_MATERIAL`
- `PREDOMINANTLY_AI_GENERATED_WITH_HUMAN_SELECTION_EDITING`
- `AI_GENERATED_PROVIDER_COMMERCIAL_PERMISSION`
- `SYNTHETIC_VOICE_OR_LIKENESS`
- `AI_TRAINING_OR_DATASET_DERIVATIVE`
- `RIGHTS_REVIEW_REQUIRED`
- `RIGHTS_HOLD`

These are evidence/workflow classes, not government legal determinations.

## Human Contribution Record

Each commercially significant work should record the human creator, task, date, source material, iterations, rejected options, selections and final contribution.

Possible protectable or rights-relevant human contributions include, where facts support them:

### Music and audio

- original lyrics and lyric revisions;
- original melody/composition;
- human performance or recorded instrumental material;
- harmony/arrangement decisions;
- structure and sequencing;
- section replacement and comping;
- stem selection/editing;
- timing, phrasing and production decisions;
- mix/master contributions;
- narrative, character and scene authorship;
- metadata/credits and final release selection.

### Written/publishing work

- original text;
- outline/structure;
- rewriting and substantive edits;
- selection/coordination/arrangement;
- diagrams, examples and worksheets;
- characters, canon and story-world expression;
- human fact-checking/editorial decisions.

### Visual/design work

- original sketches/source art;
- composition/layout;
- human selection and arrangement;
- retouching/compositing;
- typography and graphic system;
- original logos/illustrations;
- human alterations of generated source material.

### Software/technical work

- architecture;
- original code;
- test design;
- system integration;
- debugging/refactoring;
- selection/arrangement/modification of generated code;
- documentation and implementation choices.

A prompt log can help explain process, but prompts alone are not treated as sufficient proof of copyright authorship.

## Suno-specific current provider overlay

For Virality Music and other CrownThrive projects using Suno, preserve a dated provider record rather than assuming one permanent rule.

Current Suno Terms state, subject to compliance, that Pro/Premier paid-tier subscribers receive an assignment of Suno's right/title/interest in Suno-owned outputs generated from the subscriber's submissions **during the paid subscription term**. Suno simultaneously states that it does not warrant that copyright will vest in any output.

Current Suno Terms also restrict free/Basic-tier outputs to lawful internal, personal and noncommercial use with attribution. A later subscription should not be treated as retroactively curing rights for earlier free-tier generations unless the applicable provider record expressly supports that conclusion.

Required Suno evidence for material assets:

- generation date/time;
- account and paid/free tier at generation;
- applicable terms version/date;
- output/project ID where available;
- uploaded source inputs and proof of authority;
- human-authored lyrics/source material;
- subsequent human edits/arrangements;
- public/private/remix settings if rights-relevant;
- final master lineage.

**Never state “we own the copyright because we paid Suno.”** Provider contractual commercial rights and statutory copyright protection are separate inquiries.

## Inputs, samples and reference authority

A tool accepting an upload does not mean the user had the right to provide it.

Every uploaded lyric, audio clip, stem, performance, voice, image, document, codebase, dataset, sample, loop or reference file should have:

- source/origin;
- creator/owner;
- governing license/agreement;
- permitted derivative/use rights;
- upload/model-training permission;
- commercial/distribution permission;
- attribution;
- territory/term;
- proof location;
- decision: `cleared`, `restricted`, `replace`, `remove`, or `review_required`.

Public availability and ordinary purchase do not automatically authorize model training, derivative generation or commercial redistribution.

## Similarity and imitation control

Do not use provider screening as final clearance. Before high-value release/licensing, review material similarity, samples, interpolations, recognizable source recordings, identifiable voices/personas and third-party marks.

Creative direction should describe behavior—tempo, harmony, rhythm, instrumentation, vocal register, room, era, dynamics and emotional function—rather than instructing a tool to clone a living artist or identifiable person's protected performance/persona.

## Voice, likeness and digital replicas

A performance release is not automatically a synthetic-voice or model license.

Separate:

- underlying written/composed work;
- human performance;
- captured master;
- name and likeness;
- promotional rights;
- voice transformation;
- synthetic voice generation;
- reusable voice model/profile;
- fine-tuning/training/dataset use;
- derivative performance;
- compensation/royalty;
- term and revocation.

No public stream, book, character profile, recording or ordinary product purchase grants permission to clone, fine-tune, fingerprint, dataset, synthesize or generate new performances from a human voice by implication.

Do not market a synthetic/composite/generated voice as a named human performance unless the representation is true and authorized.

## Copyright registration workflow

For a registration candidate:

1. identify work type and publication status;
2. inventory human authors and claimant basis;
3. identify AI-generated/excluded material;
4. preserve human drafts/source recordings/edit history;
5. prepare an accurate AI disclosure/exclusion description;
6. claim only the qualifying human-authored material supported by evidence;
7. preserve application/deposit/case/certificate/correction records;
8. link the registration record back to the exact asset/version.

A CrownThrive internal declaration or provider commercial-use permission is not a Copyright Office determination.

## Licensing AI-assisted assets

Use qualified grant language such as:

> CrownThrive grants the rights expressly stated in this license **to the extent CrownThrive owns or controls those rights**, subject to identified provider, contributor and third-party restrictions.

Do not guarantee:

- copyright protection for purely AI-generated elements;
- uniqueness/non-similarity of model output;
- absence of all third-party claims;
- registrability of a work or mark;
- exclusivity beyond what the evidence and contract actually provide.

License human-authored/protectable elements, contractual provider permissions and CrownThrive-controlled arrangement/editorial/master/brand/canon rights as separate layers where necessary.

## AI training/data/model permissions

Default state for public CrownThrive content is **no implied training permission**.

Training, scraping, dataset creation, embeddings extraction for model development, fine-tuning, model evaluation using protected/private materials, reusable voice models, style/persona emulation and derivative datasets require a specifically authorized agreement.

A public webpage, robots accessibility, API response, purchase, download or reader entitlement is not itself such an agreement.

Allowed training/data use must name:

- source assets;
- model/project;
- permitted purpose;
- commercial/research status;
- retention;
- redistribution;
- derived-data ownership/use;
- security;
- deletion/destruction;
- audit/reporting;
- territory/term;
- downstream model/output restrictions;
- compensation;
- opt-out/termination where applicable.

## Public disclosure standard

Internal provenance is always required for governed assets. Public disclosure must be accurate and proportionate.

Disclose AI assistance/generated material when required by:

- applicable law/regulator;
- registration process;
- platform/DSP/provider rule;
- buyer/license contract;
- material consumer understanding;
- voice/likeness/synthetic-person context;
- CrownThrive's own product-policy promise.

Do not falsely market generated material as a named human's performance or imply a human process that did not occur.

## Advertising, reviews and testimonials

CrownThrive will not use AI to fabricate customer reviews, testimonials, celebrity endorsements, sales proof or social proof.

The FTC's consumer-review rule prohibits fake/false reviews and testimonials, including reviews attributed to nonexistent AI-generated people. Material relationships between an advertiser and endorser must be clearly disclosed. CrownThrive agents must therefore:

- publish only genuine attributable reviews/testimonials with permission;
- disclose employee, affiliate, sponsor, creator or other material connections;
- never condition compensation on a required positive sentiment;
- never present company-controlled review content as independent;
- label sponsored/paid/native advertising clearly;
- never fabricate AI performance, earnings, placement or customer-success claims.

## Business and earnings claims involving AI

No CrownThrive AI product, course, studio service, automation or incubator offer may promise guaranteed revenue, passive income, funding, valuation, rankings, sales or business outcomes unless a specific substantiated representation is legally supportable.

Use actual documented results with defined periods/methodology. Simulation, forecast and scenario outputs must be labeled as such.

## Confidentiality and trade secrets

AI systems do not override private-source boundaries. Do not place trade-secret prompt grammars, model credentials, private reference libraries, client data, unreleased masters, restricted canon, license-key pools, private rights files or privileged material into public prompts/models unless the approved provider/data-use profile permits it.

Separate public teaching layers from licensed tools and confidential implementation depth.

## Agent rules

Agents may help draft, classify, compare, generate, transform and test content. They may not autonomously:

- declare legal ownership where evidence is unresolved;
- certify copyrightability;
- execute exclusive/high-value rights grants;
- authorize synthetic use of a real person's voice/likeness;
- remove a rights hold without authority;
- fabricate citations/proof/testimonials;
- expose private source/provenance records;
- convert provider marketing into legal clearance.

## Release gate

An AI-assisted asset becomes `DISTRIBUTION_APPROVED` or `LICENSE_ELIGIBLE` only after the required combination of:

- source/input authority;
- provider terms/account timing;
- human-contribution record;
- version/master lineage;
- voice/likeness consent;
- sample/similarity review;
- contributor agreements;
- metadata;
- audience/safety review;
- copyright/registration disclosure decision;
- product/license scope;
- final rights approval.

Precision is the operating advantage: **document the human contribution, license the exact use, and never promise more rights than the evidence supports.**
