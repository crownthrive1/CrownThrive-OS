# Engine, Domain and Vendor Registry

CrownThrive's asset base includes a large number of purchased software products, hosted services, source-code licenses, plugins, APIs, domains, and vendor relationships. These are **operating inputs**, not the institution itself.

The August 2026 management registry records exactly **85 mapped engine/service rows and 82 domain rows** in its S100 source scope. Those are independently defined dated inventory universes, not interchangeable counts and not current-production totals. The exact source-row recoveries now live in the [Holdings 85-Row Engine & Service Seed](/knowledge/phase-2-99-workstream-3a-holdings-85-engine-service-source-seed) and [Holdings 82-Row Domain Seed](/knowledge/phase-2-99-workstream-3a-holdings-82-domain-source-seed). Later-discovered engines/providers/domains remain separate records rather than being forced into those source counts.

## THIVEBASE and partner overlay

**THIVEBASE** is now the internal infrastructure brand responsible for carrying governed provider, capacity, cost, integration and partner-registry state as the machine-readable operating substrate matures.

The [Partner, Vendor and Referral Registry](/portfolio/partner-vendor-referral-registry) is an overlay on this registry. Every `S100-ENG-001` through `S100-ENG-085` engine/service row is in partner/vendor review scope, and newer infrastructure providers are added as supplemental records without rewriting the historical S100 count.

This adds commercial fields such as verified partner/referral-program status, payout/attribution terms, disclosure obligations and referral revenue while preserving technical/vendor independence.

## Engine record

Each engine or external service should track:

- stable engine ID;
- vendor and product name;
- CrownThrive assignment(s);
- current implementation role;
- license type and purchase evidence;
- version/build;
- support/renewal status and date;
- hosting/deployment location;
- source-code custody and modifications;
- data stored or processed;
- API/integration capability;
- export/backup method;
- security and privacy classification;
- replacement candidate and migration notes;
- current, legacy, planned, or retired status;
- related partner/vendor record;
- verified referral/affiliate program state where applicable;
- referral economics and disclosure requirements when verified.

## Domain record

Every domain should track registrar, DNS authority, renewal date, auto-renew state, canonical platform mapping, redirects, email dependencies, TLS, deployment target, historical assignments, and sunset/disposition state.

## Vendor-independence doctrine

The CrownThrive service identity survives implementation changes. If one booking engine, community platform, ad server, link tool, flipbook system, analytics vendor or THIVEBASE infrastructure provider is replaced, the institutional platform ID remains stable and the ledger records the succession.

Referral revenue does not weaken this doctrine. CrownThrive must retain a documented exit/replacement path even when a vendor partner relationship is profitable.

## Commercial-license review

Purchased script or SaaS licenses require review before CrownThrive exposes functionality commercially to end users. The registry should retain evidence and current vendor terms rather than assuming an old purchase permits every future business model.

Provider referral/affiliate programs require the same current verification. Program existence, eligibility, payout terms, sub-affiliate rights and disclosure rules must be verified before CrownThrive monetizes recommendations.

## Continuity

Critical source, configuration, content, data and documentation require independent backup/export. No vendor's dashboard is the sole system of institutional memory.

THIVEBASE capacity monitoring should also track quota consumption, projected exhaustion, renewal risk and cost growth so provider constraints are visible before they become incidents.
