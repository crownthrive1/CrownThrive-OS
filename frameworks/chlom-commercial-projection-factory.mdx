## CHLOM Commercial Projection Factory

## Institutional relationship

`crownthrive1/CrownThrive-OS` is the **public canonical governance parent** for the factory-managed CHLOM commercial overlay. It carries CrownThrive-controlled public-safe product policy, documentation, manifests, contracts, factory controls, validators, release rules, and public/private classification boundaries.

`crownthrive/chlom-protocol` is the **public commercial child**. It exists for product discovery, technical evaluation, integration documentation, public registries, commercial licensing, machine-access terms, public developer contracts, verification artifacts, support routing, and commercial acquisition funnels.

Both repositories are public. Restricted institutional truth, protected evidence bodies, private contracts, credentials, private economic logic, protected Fingerprint derivation, and confidential implementation material stay outside public Git.

The child may not manufacture parent authority or expose vault-bounded material.

## Pentafabric placement

The projection factory sits primarily in **Layer 4 — Execution & Interoperability** of CrownThrive Pentafabric™. It projects public-safe outputs derived from Layers 1–3 and can hand verified commercial candidates to **Layer 5 — Economy & Distribution** through PentaGreen / ECAC.

The factory does not itself create economic authority.

## Factory flow

`governed sources → canonical parent → public-safe source package → projection validator → deterministic build → independent verification → child candidate branch → child pull request → optional PentaGreen handoff`

The factory follows the PentaFactory Execution Builder doctrine: a builder materializes candidate artifacts, but a build receipt is not certification and the builder cannot certify its own output.

## CrownThrive OS paint

Every generated projection carries or resolves:

- product and repository-family identity;
- exact upstream parent commit SHA;
- projection schema version;
- CrownThrive Pentafabric architecture identity;
- CrownThrive ownership and commercial-license boundary;
- canonical CHLOM terminology;
- public/private security classification;
- support, licensing, and developer routing.

## Canonical terminology

DAIL means **Decentralized Autonomous Information Ledger**.

DLA means **Dynamic Licensing Asset**.

Historical uses of DAL and historical uses of DLA as “Decentralized Licensing Authority” remain recoverable lineage, but are not current canonical product meanings. Current licensing-authority responsibilities are expressed as **Licensing Stewardship / Issuer Authority**.

## Commercial posture

The child is designated a monetized commercial repository. That designation creates a commercial product and acquisition surface; it does not by itself assert that checkout, a token, a mainnet, public APIs, a payment rail, certification, entitlement, or a specific price is live.

Commercial capability classes include:

- Public / Community
- Developer / Integration
- Business / Commercial
- Enterprise / Institutional
- Ecosystem / Partner
- Research / Education
- OEM / Embedded / White-label when expressly licensed

Prices, entitlements, credentials, and activation claims resolve from separately governed current commercial state rather than being invented in repository documentation.

## Managed public package

The managed child overlay can include:

- README and repository rights notice;
- commercial licensing notice;
- machine-access terms;
- support and voluntary-contribution funnel;
- Pentafabric architecture and topology;
- commercial product and licensing pathways;
- agent and factory documentation;
- DAIL and repository-boundary contracts;
- exact upstream provenance.

Unmanaged child registries, schemas, tests, historical research, and other validated public content are preserved.

## Security boundary

The projection is allowlist-only. It must fail closed on credential-shaped material and may not export raw secrets, private contracts, customer records, protected-person data, private unit economics, confidential source evidence, private prompts/scoring, unreleased implementation assets, or Fingerprint private derivation details.

## Continuous operation

The factory is designed for event-driven execution when managed sources change and scheduled continuity runs to detect drift. When the bounded `CHLOM_PUBLIC_REPO_TOKEN` credential is present, a changed public-safe package may be pushed to a child candidate branch and opened as a pull request. The workflow does not force-push, self-merge, or delete unmanaged child content.

Specialized CHLOM agents may prepare research, identity, compliance, documentation, commercial, and mesh-reliability candidates for the factory. Candidate preparation does not equal production activation.
