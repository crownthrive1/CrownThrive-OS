# Provider Outage and Capacity Recovery Runbook

Use this runbook when a CrownThrive service is unavailable, degraded, read-only, capacity-constrained, restarted, rate-limited, or otherwise impaired by a provider or infrastructure dependency.

The objective is not merely to make the interface load again. The objective is to prove restoration, reconcile dependent state, preserve evidence, and make the next failure cheaper and safer.

## Trigger conditions

Open or attach to an incident when one or more of these conditions is observed:

- native heartbeat becomes stale or fails;
- provider reports outage/degradation;
- application or database becomes unreachable;
- storage, request, MAU, egress, quota, or other included capacity approaches or reaches a limit;
- service enters read-only or protective mode;
- provider-plan or add-on change requires restart/reconfiguration;
- credential or authorization stops working;
- webhook/job/queue backlog materially accumulates;
- provider billing/cost-control behavior threatens availability; or
- dependent CrownThrive platforms fail a synthetic canary.

## Authority and roles

| Function | Minimum responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Incident owner | owns severity, scope, current state and handoff |
| Infrastructure/security agent | diagnoses provider, capacity, access and security state |
| Recovery/rollback agent | preserves rollback and degraded-mode options |
| Platform agents | record `impact` or `no_impact` and platform-specific evidence |
| Evidence auditor | verifies evidence completeness and separation of claims |
| Documentation sentinel/steward | records documentation impact and public-safe projection |
| Founder/finance/security authority | approves reserved spend, contractual, high-risk security or irreversible decisions |

No role may infer permission from technical access.

## 1. Detect and open the incident

Create a stable incident ID/key and record:

- detected timestamp in UTC;
- affected service IDs;
- initial severity;
- reporter/source class;
- known customer impact;
- provider state;
- most recent native heartbeat;
- current capacity/usage evidence where relevant;
- active deployments/configuration versions; and
- known credential or authorization changes.

If exact root cause is unknown, keep it unknown. Do not convert correlation into cause.

## 2. Contain safely

Choose the lowest-risk containment consistent with impact:

- pause risky writes;
- switch to approved read-only/degraded mode;
- quarantine broken jobs or replay sources;
- stop nonessential scheduled load;
- preserve queues rather than dropping work;
- prevent duplicate payment/entitlement/provider actions;
- protect credentials and evidence; and
- retain a tested rollback target.

Do not change money destinations, rights, legal terms, security policy, or public claims to conceal a failure.

## 3. Capture provider and capacity evidence

Record provider evidence separately from CrownThrive evidence:

- provider control-plane/API state;
- plan/capacity state;
- quota or retention limits implicated;
- cost-control/spend-cap state when continuity-relevant;
- add-on or plan-change timing;
- provider incident/support reference where available; and
- account evidence classification.

Exact account billing, payment, tax, credential, or customer details stay restricted.

Provider capability and provider plan state do not prove CrownThrive runtime recovery.

## 4. Execute the approved mitigation

Examples include:

- provider-plan/capacity adjustment under authorized scope;
- service restart;
- storage cleanup only when retention/legal/rights rules permit;
- traffic throttling or queue batching;
- restoring from a verified recovery point;
- switching to an approved fallback provider/path;
- rotating or reissuing a credential when required; or
- rolling back a deployment/configuration change.

Every mitigation records actor, authority, timestamp, affected records, expected effect, rollback/compensation, and verification plan.

## 5. Require a fresh native heartbeat

A provider dashboard that says `healthy` is insufficient.

The recovery gate requires a CrownThrive-native heartbeat generated after the mitigation/restart threshold. At minimum verify:

- heartbeat timestamp is newer than the gate timestamp;
- database/service check is successful;
- project/runtime state is expected;
- heartbeat evidence resolves to the intended service/environment; and
- the heartbeat is not merely a replayed/stale record.

If the heartbeat is absent, remain in `RECOVERY_MONITORING`.

## 6. Re-run dependent canaries

After the native heartbeat, test representative dependencies according to the service:

- database read/write within bounded test scope;
- auth/service identity;
- storage read/write;
- API/MCP read path;
- scheduled job/queue flow;
- webhook delivery/replay;
- application health;
- payment/entitlement canary where applicable;
- backups/export path; and
- cross-platform event/analytics handoff.

A P0 recovery should not be declared from a single green check when dependent systems remain unreconciled.

## 7. Credential gate

If the incident involves a new or rotated provider credential, keep the credential workflow fail-closed:

```text
fresh heartbeat missing
→ credential intake HOLD

fresh heartbeat passes + secret not yet supplied
→ ready to receive secret through restricted channel

secret supplied
→ encrypt/store in approved Vault
→ record stable credential ID + Vault reference + fingerprint
→ bounded runtime canary
→ custody/readback verification
→ verified active
```

Never treat an app ID, project ID, public client ID, token label, or screenshot reference as the secret itself.

Never place the secret value in GitHub, Mintlify, chat-visible documentation, logs, screenshots, tickets, or analytics.

## 8. Reconcile the incident

Before `resolved`, reconcile:

- affected platform states;
- queued/retried jobs;
- duplicate or missed provider events;
- data written during degraded mode;
- payments/entitlements/rights transitions where relevant;
- monitoring state;
- credential custody;
- backup/restore evidence;
- customer/support impact; and
- documentation/changelog impact.

Use compensating actions rather than deleting inconvenient history.

## 9. Monitoring window

Move to `monitoring` after service restoration. During the monitoring window:

- watch fresh heartbeats;
- inspect error and saturation trends;
- compare capacity against thresholds;
- validate scheduled jobs;
- watch dependent services; and
- preserve any recurrence as a linked event rather than silently resetting the incident clock.

## 10. After-action review

Every material incident must answer:

- what happened;
- what was affected;
- what evidence established cause and recovery;
- what control failed or was missing;
- what prevented worse impact;
- whether capacity thresholds were visible early enough;
- whether backup/degraded/fallback paths worked;
- whether communications were accurate;
- what should be automated;
- what test should be added; and
- what reusable lesson should enter the capability factory.

## 11. Convert lessons into institutional capability

A validated recurring pattern may become:

- alert/threshold rule;
- synthetic canary;
- status-control template;
- incident schema;
- runbook;
- governed agent skill;
- recovery drill;
- provider-exit checklist;
- evaluation fixture;
- managed service; or
- licensed public-safe template/package.

Commercialization remains a separate governed state. A lesson being useful does not automatically make it releasable, licensable, legally sufficient, or safe to expose.

## Completion gate

Do not close the incident until:

- service is restored or an accepted degraded state is explicit;
- a fresh native heartbeat exists;
- required dependent checks pass or are explicitly held/deferred;
- impacted agents/platforms have recorded their disposition;
- credential custody is correct where applicable;
- reconciliation is complete;
- after-action evidence exists; and
- documentation/lesson/factory impacts are recorded.
