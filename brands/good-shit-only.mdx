# Good Shit Only — Buy the Product, Know the Rights

Good Shit Only, commonly shortened to **GSO**, is CrownThrive's broad commerce and rights-routing surface for digital products, books, printables, templates, creative assets, selected services, bundles, hosted-reader offers, approved physical/POD goods and standardized rights offers where those offers have passed the applicable release gates.

## What this helps you do

- Discover CrownThrive products and approved brand collections.
- Purchase personal-use digital products and bundles.
- Access hosted or protected delivery experiences when included in an offer.
- Understand when a product includes a license and when it does not.
- Route complex commercial-rights requests into review instead of pretending every right is self-serve.

## Availability and eligibility

GSO has a current institutional commerce role, but the exact current marketplace engine, database, API and complete transaction-state verification remain open in the registry. This public guide therefore explains **how the marketplace is governed** without claiming that every historical product or route is currently purchasable.

## The most important rule

<Warning>
  A product listing does **not** automatically grant commercial rights. Personal access, download access, hosted-reader access, resale, reproduction, adaptation, manufacturing, source files, exclusivity and AI/data/model rights are different permissions.
</Warning>

## Offer types

### Personal digital access

Books, printables, journals, templates, guides, readers and bundles where the customer receives the access described in the current offer.

### Creator and professional resources

Editable or production-oriented resources can have broader usage rights, but only when the offer explicitly says so.

### Physical and print-on-demand goods

Physical products require a verified production or fulfillment model, shipping terms and refund/support route. A digital image of a product is not proof that inventory exists.

### Standardized licenses

Self-serve licensing can apply only when the asset is rights-cleared and the license class is predefined. The offer should identify scope, territory, term and any quantity/seat/location limits that matter.

### Reviewed rights transactions

Adaptation, exclusivity, characters/universes, manufacturing, source masters, broad institutional use, AI/data/model/voice rights and high-value or unclear chain-of-title requests should go through reviewed handling.

## How it works

<Steps>
  <Step title="Read the offer, not just the product image">
    Confirm what is being sold: a file, reader access, physical product, service, bundle or license.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check the price and terms">
    Use the current offer. A Stripe Price or old marketing screenshot is not a promise that checkout and fulfillment still work.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Complete checkout">
    Payment should create the appropriate order record and, where applicable, entitlement or license record.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Use only the rights you received">
    If the transaction granted personal access only, do not treat it as resale, reproduction or adaptation permission.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Use support for delivery or rights questions">
    Open a case when fulfillment, entitlement, refund or license scope is unclear.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Payment, order, entitlement and license are different

A successful payment is only one part of the transaction. The governed flow separates:

`payment → order → access entitlement → license (only if granted) → delivery → usage/support/refund evidence`

That separation protects customers and creators when a payment is refunded, disputed or tied to a specific license term.

## Pricing

GSO does not use one universal subscription price for everything because the catalog can include very different product and license classes. Each live offer should identify:

- Current product/edition/SKU.
- Current price and currency.
- Whether taxes or shipping apply.
- Whether the item is digital, physical, service-based or licensed.
- Delivery or fulfillment method.
- Refund/support rules.
- Rights included with the purchase.

Do not use a price from a historical product registry or another CrownThrive storefront as a current GSO quote.

## Seller and brand participation

A seller or participating brand needs authority to list the asset, an applicable agreement, a valid payout/tax profile where required, current product/source records, rights documentation, claims compliance and a support/fulfillment owner. Marketplace participation does not transfer the seller's IP to GSO or CrownThrive unless a separate agreement expressly does so.

## Privacy, payments and rights

GSO should never expose protected source files or private rights evidence merely because a customer bought a consumer product. Keep payment data with the approved processor, use protected delivery for restricted files and preserve order/license evidence when a refund or dispute changes the active entitlement.

## Troubleshooting

**I paid but cannot access the product.** Use support with the order/payment reference. Payment success and entitlement delivery are separate states and need to be reconciled.

**I bought a digital product. Can I resell it?** Not unless the current offer includes resale rights.

**I want to adapt a book or character.** That is a rights transaction, not a standard consumer download. Use the reviewed licensing route.

**A product card exists but checkout is unavailable.** A listing can outlive a verified offer. Use only current active checkout paths.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does GSO create licenses just because something is listed?

No. CHLOM remains the rights and authority layer. GSO can present approved offers; it does not manufacture rights.

### Can physical products appear on GSO?

Yes, where production, fulfillment, shipping, quality and support are verified.

### Can creators sell through GSO?

The institutional model supports approved seller/brand participation, subject to current onboarding and rights requirements.

### Is a refund the same as deleting the order history?

No. Refund, entitlement and license state can change, but historical evidence should remain available for reconciliation.
