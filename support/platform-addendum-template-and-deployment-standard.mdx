# Platform Addendum Template and Deployment Standard

## Why addenda exist

CrownThrive should not duplicate an entire Terms/Privacy/Licensing stack on every platform. A platform addendum inherits approved institutional policies and states only what is unique to that platform, role or transaction.

An addendum cannot silently override a higher-authority signed agreement or create rights the platform does not control.

## Required sections

### 1. Platform identity and scope

- platform name/stable ID;
- operator/entity;
- service purpose;
- current lifecycle state;
- related brands/imprints/subsystems;
- services expressly excluded from the addendum.

### 2. Eligibility and roles

Identify visitor, account user, member, creator, provider, seller, licensee, advertiser, developer, operator, administrator or other roles. Do not give one role another role's permissions by implication.

### 3. Accounts, identity and authorization

State login/identity model, organization authority, role changes, delegated administration, suspension and account recovery. If CrownThrive ID/SSO is not universally deployed, do not represent it as such.

### 4. Platform-specific data

Identify material data classes, visibility, connected services, processors, cross-platform handoffs, retention, export/delete behavior, analytics, cookies and any sensitive/minor data.

The addendum should point to the effective Privacy Policy and only add platform-specific differences.

### 5. User or partner content

Define:

- content type;
- ownership representation;
- service-operation license;
- public/private visibility;
- moderation;
- distribution destinations;
- deletion/retention;
- promotional reuse;
- derivative/licensing options;
- AI/model-use state;
- copyright/likeness/voice/source representations.

### 6. Products, fees and billing

If applicable:

- product/service classes;
- one-time/subscription/usage fees;
- renewal/cancellation;
- taxes;
- refunds;
- chargebacks;
- payout/split/commission schedule references;
- escrow/hold/reserve if applicable;
- payment provider.

Do not duplicate volatile prices in legal prose when a versioned economic schedule/order can control them.

### 7. Rights and licensing

Reference the Master Licensing and Rights Architecture and state platform-specific license families, restricted rights, chain-of-title prerequisites, source-file access, public performance, adaptation, manufacturing, sublicensing, AI training, voice/likeness and verification rules.

### 8. AI and automation

State:

- AI features actually used;
- providers/model families where disclosure is appropriate;
- human-review responsibility;
- source-input authority;
- inference/evaluation/training state;
- automated decision/support boundaries;
- content provenance and disclosure requirements;
- prohibited synthetic voice/likeness behavior;
- correction/appeal route.

### 9. Community/moderation or marketplace rules

If the platform includes UGC, providers, sellers, reviews, comments or listings, define moderation, prohibited conduct, ranking/featured-placement disclosures, complaint process, provider independence and no-guarantee boundaries.

### 10. Advertising/affiliate/sponsorship

Define paid placement, material connection, commission/attribution, claims responsibility, prohibited categories, data/measurement and endorsement disclosure requirements.

### 11. Third-party services and vendors

Identify meaningful provider categories and explain that third-party terms may apply. Vendor use does not transfer CrownThrive ownership of its institutional platform identity, nor does CrownThrive own third-party rights merely because a provider is integrated.

### 12. Support, incidents and availability

State support route, service-level posture if any, scheduled/unscheduled outages, data/backup expectations, abuse/security reporting and no-guarantee boundaries.

### 13. Suspension, termination and sunset

Define:

- user/account suspension;
- content removal;
- rights holds;
- outstanding payments/refunds;
- export window;
- deletion/retention;
- successor migration;
- redirects;
- continuing licenses/obligations;
- archived acceptance evidence.

A platform can sunset while contractual/record obligations continue.

### 14. Incorporated documents and precedence

List exact policy IDs/versions or version-resolution rules. Use the standard precedence model:

1. specifically executed agreement/order/license where applicable;
2. applicable platform/role/transaction addendum;
3. Master Terms;
4. incorporated policies/schedules to the extent applicable.

Conflicts are adjudicated rather than silently selecting favorable text.

### 15. Contact and notices

Provide the actual support/legal/privacy/rights routes applicable to the platform. Do not send pre-assent users to a login-gated page as the sole location for operative terms.

## Optional modules

Add only when applicable:

- minors/parental consent;
- health/wellness;
- church/ministry;
- school/student;
- event recording;
- creator release;
- advertiser/publisher;
- API/developer;
- seller/provider;
- rewards/credits;
- physical location/operator;
- franchise/regional legal gate;
- impact/contributions;
- international/cross-border restrictions;
- union/guild/collective obligations.

## Deployment QA

Before `effective` state:

- current platform state verified;
- old addenda captured/hashed;
- links/canonicals tested;
- privacy/data mapping current;
- license scope current;
- provider references current;
- AI/model state current;
- prices/economic schedules referenced correctly;
- user roles resolved;
- required disclosure placement tested;
- acceptance evidence path tested;
- archive/rollback ready;
- approval/counsel state recorded.

## Automation boundary

Agents may instantiate this template from the platform registry and flag missing sections. They may not mark a legal addendum `effective`, invent legal review, or silently broaden rights during automatic generation.
