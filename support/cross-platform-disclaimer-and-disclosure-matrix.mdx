# Cross-Platform Disclaimer and Disclosure Matrix

<Warning>
  This matrix defines institutional coverage and drafting requirements. It does not substitute for jurisdiction-specific legal review, professional advice, required statutory notices, contract terms or regulator-mandated disclosures.
</Warning>

## Purpose

Disclaimers are not decorative footer language. They must prevent a reasonable user from confusing:

- information with professional advice;
- access with a license;
- platform permission with copyright protection;
- estimates with guaranteed outcomes;
- fictional/dramatized material with factual allegations;
- ministry education with pastoral/clinical crisis care;
- wellness content with medical diagnosis or treatment;
- sponsorship with independent editorial content;
- provider listings with CrownThrive endorsement;
- a prototype, roadmap or prospectus with current deployment;
- internal impact allocation with charity/investment claims;
- AI assistance with human identity or certainty.

The disclosure must appear **where the misunderstanding could occur**, not only inside a distant Legal Center.

## Universal CrownThrive disclaimer stack

Every platform/product selects the applicable layers below.

### 1. General information / no professional advice

Use when content discusses business, legal, financial, tax, cybersecurity, health, ministry/pastoral, investment, franchise or other professional matters.

Core concept:

> CrownThrive materials are provided for informational, educational, operational or creative purposes and do not constitute individualized legal, tax, financial, investment, medical, mental-health, pastoral or other licensed professional advice unless a separately identified qualified professional is expressly engaged for that purpose.

Avoid implying that a disclaimer makes otherwise regulated conduct permissible.

### 2. Licensing / access does not grant rights

Use on products, readers, downloads, media, catalogs, characters, worlds, APIs, templates and source assets.

Core concept:

> Viewing, streaming, purchasing, downloading, receiving API output or accessing a CrownThrive resource does not grant rights beyond the exact license, order, agreement or written authorization that applies to that use.

A purchase receipt, public webpage or platform availability is not a sync, broadcast, adaptation, manufacturing, model-training, trademark, franchise or enterprise license.

### 3. Copyright / trademark / registration status

Use wherever marks, copyright, patent/provisional or other IP status could be misunderstood.

- `TM` may indicate a claimed source identifier where appropriate; it does not prove registration.
- `®` is used only for marks actually registered for the applicable goods/services.
- a copyright notice does not prove every element is copyrightable or registered;
- company/domain/social-handle ownership is not trademark registration;
- “patent-pending,” “registered,” “filed,” “exclusive” and similar status terms require exact evidence.

### 4. AI-assisted/generated content

Use where users encounter AI-generated/assisted music, writing, art, video, code, analysis, recommendations or automation.

Disclose as appropriate that:

- AI may assist generation, editing, analysis or production;
- outputs may contain errors, omissions, non-unique material or unintended similarities;
- human review and work-level provenance govern publication/licensing;
- provider commercial-use terms do not guarantee statutory copyright;
- synthetic voice/likeness use requires separate authority;
- no public access grants model-training, scraping, cloning or dataset rights by implication.

Do not use a generic AI disclaimer to hide a materially synthetic real-person representation.

### 5. User-provided source authority

Use on uploads, submissions, creator tools, sermon intake, media submissions, custom services and AI inputs.

Core concept:

> By submitting material, the user represents that they have the authority required for CrownThrive and its approved processors to receive and use it for the stated service. Submission does not transfer ownership except as an applicable agreement expressly states.

Require more specific releases/licenses for voices, likenesses, minors, confidential data, third-party music/art, training data and regulated information.

### 6. Third-party providers / no affiliation by reference

When CrownThrive references external tools, stores, AI providers, platforms, payment processors, distributors or media services:

- third-party marks belong to their owners;
- reference does not imply sponsorship or endorsement unless a real relationship exists;
- provider features, prices, terms and availability can change;
- CrownThrive's rights/grants cannot exceed upstream provider restrictions.

### 7. Estimates, projections and business outcomes

Use on business plans, incubator materials, AI tools, valuation, market scenarios, revenue models, performance projections and economic examples.

Core concept:

> Forecasts, scenarios, estimates, benchmarks and modeled economics are planning tools—not guarantees of revenue, funding, profit, valuation, ranking, customer acquisition, licensing success or investment return.

Never convert scenario assumptions into testimonials or actual results.

### 8. No securities / franchise / regulated offer by implication

Use on Holdings, MM Suites, CHLOM, ThriveFund/CII, licensing and prospectus-adjacent material where a reader could infer a regulated offer.

Current institutional documents do not create a security, investment fund, franchise, franchise offer, charitable deduction, ownership interest, territory grant or regulated financial product merely by describing a possible architecture. Any such relationship requires the specific legally authorized package and evidence.

## AI and automated decision disclosures

When an AI/agent makes or assists a recommendation affecting access, moderation, pricing, licensing, support, eligibility or workflow:

- identify AI/automation use where material;
- explain the decision class and human-review route where appropriate;
- do not claim human review occurred if it did not;
- provide correction/appeal paths for consequential errors;
- keep restricted journal, health, legal, financial or children/minor data outside advertising and ordinary analytics;
- never present the AI as a licensed attorney, clinician, physician, pastor, financial adviser or other credentialed professional.

## Advertising, sponsorship, affiliates and endorsements

FTC-aligned rule: paid or material relationships must be disclosed clearly and conspicuously in language users understand.

Use context-appropriate labels such as:

- `Ad`;
- `Advertisement`;
- `Sponsored`;
- `Paid partnership`;
- `Affiliate link — CrownThrive may earn a commission`.

Do not rely on ambiguous labels or a disclosure that appears only after the user must click “more.” Platform-provided disclosure tools may not always be sufficient by themselves.

Never:

- generate fake reviews/testimonials;
- use AI-created nonexistent reviewers;
- pay for a required positive sentiment;
- hide employee/founder/affiliate relationships;
- present CrownThrive-controlled review content as independent;
- suppress legitimate negative feedback in a way that materially distorts the consumer picture.

## Media, fiction, testimony and evidence labels

Use explicit content classes where confusion is possible:

- `Fiction / dramatization`;
- `Inspired by / composite` when truthful;
- `Personal testimony`;
- `Attributed claim`;
- `Allegation — not established as fact`;
- `Commentary / opinion`;
- `Historical/documentary source`;
- `Evidence record` only when the evidence class and source are defined.

Fictional Evidence Room or dossier design must not visually imply court/government/legal proof when it is a narrative device.

## Ministry and faith disclosures

KJV Visualized and Sermon Toolkit must distinguish:

- exact Scripture/source text;
- CrownThrive commentary;
- history/context;
- denominational/traditional interpretation;
- devotional application;
- testimony;
- visual reconstruction;
- fictional/dramatized narrative;
- AI-assisted explanation.

AI is not represented as a pastor, clergy member, emergency service or clinician. Private prayer/reflection records are not used in advertising or ordinary analytics. Crisis or clinical situations require appropriate human/emergency resources, not automated ministry claims.

## Beauty and wellness

Where CrownThrive platforms publish hair, skin, wellness, grooming or product information:

- do not imply medical diagnosis/treatment absent lawful substantiation and product status;
- distinguish cosmetic/grooming information from medical advice;
- show ingredient, allergen, safety, patch-test, usage or manufacturer information where applicable;
- identify whether CrownThrive, a Suite Pro, marketplace seller or third party supplies the product/service;
- do not guarantee individual results;
- professional credentials/insurance/availability must be current when represented.

## Education, challenges, expertise and community authority

Course completion, quiz/challenge scores, leaderboard rank, badges, rewards or CrownThrive certificates do not imply a government license, accredited degree, professional board certification, employment, ownership, equity, CHLOM permission, corporate authority or guaranteed advancement unless that exact later state is independently verified and stated.

A CrownConsults provider, ThrivePeer mentor, CrownThriveU instructor, ThriveAlumni officer/committee member or Commons moderator must have a separately defined and evidenced scope. Platform eligibility, community trust or CrownThrive training does not replace current jurisdictional licensure where regulated.

Historical civic/governance language in ThriveAlumni sources remains historical unless re-adopted through current governing authority. CrownThrive Commons social roles remain local to that community layer and do not become institutional identity or consent by implication. Digital Business Academy remains unresolved against CrownThriveU until an adopted adjudication states otherwise.

## Marketplace and directory neutrality

A listing, ranking, badge, directory presence or payment capability does not by itself mean CrownThrive guarantees a provider's quality, legality, insurance, identity, availability, work product or regulatory compliance. Where CrownThrive performs verification, describe exactly what was verified and when.

## Commerce disclosure panel

Before checkout, display the information material to the purchase:

- seller/licensor;
- exact product/edition/service;
- format and delivery method;
- access vs download vs hosted reader;
- price/tax/recurrence;
- applicable license class and excluded rights;
- refund/cancellation rules;
- compatibility/accessibility where material;
- support route;
- subscription renewal/cancellation where applicable;
- physical/digital distinction;
- jurisdiction/age restrictions where applicable.

Do not hide material purchase terms behind post-payment delivery.

## API and developer disclaimer

Public API docs should state:

- version/environment;
- authentication/scopes;
- quotas/rate limits;
- deprecation policy;
- data classification;
- third-party/provider dependencies;
- rights in returned content;
- no scraping/training rights unless expressly granted;
- absence/presence of SLA;
- support/security reporting;
- consequential-action approval boundaries.

Documentation availability is not a guarantee that a user's plan/key has access to every endpoint.

## Placement rules

Use the closest effective disclosure location:

- price/license exclusions next to the offer;
- sponsorship disclosure adjacent to sponsored content;
- AI/synthetic-person disclosure at the media/content experience where material;
- health/ministry/professional boundary at the advice/tool interaction;
- earnings/forecast qualifier next to the projection;
- hosted-reader/download distinction beside product format;
- age/content notice before mature content;
- affiliate disclosure before or near actionable affiliate links.

Footer-only disclosure is insufficient where the user could act before seeing it.

## Final rule

Use disclaimers to **clarify the real boundary**, not to excuse an unsupported claim. If a statement is misleading, unsubstantiated or legally unavailable, fix the statement or product architecture rather than attempting to disclaim it away.
