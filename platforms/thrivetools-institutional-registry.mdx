## Source reconciliation

Recovered CrownThrive platform/domain material preserves all three labels — `ThriveTools`, `ThriveTools SEO`, and `ThriveTools OPT`. A separate master-link manifest places `thrivetools.io` in the primary-domain set and explicitly places `seo.thrivetools.io` and `optimize.thrivetools.io` in the subdomain set.

That evidence resolves the prior Phase 2.99 parent/child ambiguity without rewriting the historical source vocabulary:

```text
thrivetools.io
├── seo.thrivetools.io       → ThriveTools SEO
└── optimize.thrivetools.io  → ThriveTools OPT
```

The historical S100 82-domain source universe remains unchanged. Its `S100-DOM-057` record is the root `thrivetools.io` record. Child hosts recovered from a separate subdomain manifest are attached as descendant deployment/domain edges rather than retroactively inflating the 82-row source count.

## ThriveTools SEO current boundary

The source and domain manifests establish the child identity and `seo.thrivetools.io` host relationship. They do **not**, by themselves, certify the current SEO application version, API contract, authenticated runtime, provider identity, deployment SHA, data model or write authority. Those fields remain `verification_open` until current operational evidence is recovered.

## Integration contract

Both children inherit CrownThrive-wide controls from the ThriveTools parent while retaining capability-specific records for:

- domain/deployment identity;
- provider and engine lineage;
- API/MCP endpoints and scopes;
- admin/operator/customer roles;
- data classes and retention;
- CrownLytics events;
- legal/privacy/license coverage;
- CHLOM rights/provenance where proprietary modifications or distributable outputs exist;
- heartbeat, health and release evidence;
- monetization and entitlement state when activated.

Neither child may silently inherit a capability, API, pricing state or certification from the other.
