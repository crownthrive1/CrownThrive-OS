## Verified public product role

Current ThriveMaps copy positions it for SaaS founders, creators, agencies, membership communities and product teams to:

- collect feature requests, bug reports and improvement ideas;
- organize/search feedback;
- let users vote;
- use clear request statuses;
- publish public roadmaps;
- publish changelogs;
- support commenting/discussions;
- notify users about changes;
- manage multiple products/brands/boards;
- moderate and merge duplicates;
- keep boards public or private;
- export reports;
- use standalone/public URLs;
- use payment-gateway integration according to plan copy.

## Lane discipline

ThriveMaps is the **direction layer**, not the execution or support layer.

```text
community / customers / partners

ThriveMaps

        ↓ approved direction

execution and delivery
        ↓
release proof
        ↓
ThriveMaps changelog
        ↓
CrownPulse / ThrivePush
amplification
        ↓
CrownLytics
adoption/performance evidence
```

### Explicit boundaries

- ThriveSupport owns support problems, troubleshooting, account and billing help.
- Collab Portal/OpsOasis own project execution, tasks, approvals and operational work.
- CrownLytics owns deep measurement and adoption analytics.
- Cliques/community systems own community graph/discussion beyond feedback-specific threads.

## Feedback truth model

Votes are a signal, not an automatic product commitment.

Each feedback item should preserve:

```yaml
feedback_id:
board_id:
product_id:
submitter_actor_id:
category:
request_type:
content_version:
created_at:
moderation_state:
duplicate_of:
vote_count:
status:
public_status_note:
internal_execution_ref:
changelog_ref:
```

Roadmap publication must distinguish `planned` from guaranteed delivery.

## Status model

Current public materials use a ladder including:

- New;
- Under Review;
- Planned;
- In Progress;
- Shipped;
- Not Now;
- Closed.

Status changes should be append-only events with actor, timestamp and rationale rather than destructive overwrites.

## Public/private boards

Current public material states boards can be public or private.

Institutional rule: `private board` must be backed by real authorization. Private board content may include product strategy, partner requests or internal priorities and cannot rely on hidden navigation or non-indexing alone.

## Pricing and entitlement surface

Current public site advertises Starter, Growth, Business, Agency and Enterprise tiers with monthly/annual prices and feature lists.

Classification: `public-commercial-offer` pending checkout/fulfillment verification.

Canonical entitlement records must separate:

- product/price ID;
- billing interval;
- feature/board/product limits;
- team/user limits;
- export rights;
- private-board rights;
- notification rights;
- payment-gateway integration rights;
- effective date;
- grandfathering/migration state.

Public pricing should not become the sole machine authorization source.

## Notifications and lifecycle

Current public material says users can receive notifications when an idea status changes, an update posts or an item ships.

Phase 2.98 must verify actual channels/provider, consent, suppression, template versioning, retries, event sourcing and unsubscribe behavior before federation with ThrivePush/CrownPulse.

## Moderation and integrity

Required governance:

- spam/abuse controls;
- duplicate merging with lineage;
- moderation event history;
- no vote manipulation;
- role-based admin actions;
- content/reporting policy;
- rate limiting;
- transparent status-note history;
- export and deletion behavior;
- user/account identity mapping.

## SEO and public roadmap controls

Public board, roadmap and changelog pages can create substantial indexable content. The SEO fleet must control:

- canonical URLs;
- thin/duplicate feedback pages;
- user-generated spam;
- pagination;
- private-board exclusion;
- structured data where appropriate;
- stale/closed-item lifecycle;
- redirects after merges;
- generated titles/descriptions;
- noindex policy for low-value or sensitive surfaces.
