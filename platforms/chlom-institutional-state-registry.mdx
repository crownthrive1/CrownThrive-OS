## Why this registry exists

CHLOM has accumulated multiple generations of public, research and target-architecture language. The institutional problem is not that those eras exist; the problem is allowing agents, developers, sales copy or legal surfaces to flatten them into one current-state claim.

This registry therefore separates:

1. current institutional governance and licensing doctrine;
2. source-defined cloud/API implementation contracts;
3. historical and research architecture;
4. public legacy claims still reachable;
5. future decentralized or cryptographic modules;
6. legal/IP filing claims that require independent evidence.

## Decentralization roadmap

CHLOM should not decentralize every datastore or workflow merely because decentralization is part of its historical vision.

Approved staging:

```text
central institutional authority / stable IDs / policies
        ↓
partitioned operational services and evidence stores
        ↓
federated adapters / portable interfaces
        ↓
selective cryptographic proofs, attestations or decentralized settlement
        ↓
greater decentralization only where rights, resilience, portability or trust measurably improve
```

Business meaning and stable IDs must survive infrastructure replacement.

## Agent authority

Agents may:

- retrieve approved current rights/policy records;
- evaluate low-risk deterministic rules;
- prepare license drafts;
- flag conflicts or missing evidence;
- create review cases;
- produce audit summaries;
- route work to authorized reviewers.

Agents may not independently:

- invent ownership;
- clear disputed rights;
- declare patent/registration status without evidence;
- execute irreversible licensing/settlement outside delegated authority;
- expose trade-secret source logic;
- convert a research module into current product claims;
- waive legal/cultural/security review gates.
