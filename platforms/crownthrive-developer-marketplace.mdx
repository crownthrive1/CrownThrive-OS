## Closed-loop credit preview

The marketplace is staged around Crown Credits under owner-approved internal policy v2 (`ct-marketplace-living-policy-2026-08-20-v2`, SHA-256 `27ab6873e8d11658a69273896fa37978704a085cb6fafbc90a7556a82719ed34`). One US dollar is represented as 100 credits. Paid credits do not expire or incur dormancy or service fees. Credits are first-party, closed-loop, nontransferable, noncash, non-crypto, non-interest-bearing, and unusable with third-party sellers; wallet balances and aggregate daily loads are capped at 199,999 credits. The policy is not external legal, tax, rights, accessibility, or payment-eligibility certification.

Stripe is the funding rail for hosted top-up Checkout. Supabase Auth binds the customer identity; the CrownThrive application ledger is the balance authority; Supabase owns product eligibility, entitlements, licenses, and protected delivery.

## Transaction authority

A client redirect never creates credits. The signed-event webhook and bounded reconciler retrieve Checkout, PaymentIntent, Charge, and Price evidence server-side. They verify mode, payment state, immutable creation snapshot, explicit `exclusive` tax behavior, amount, currency, quantity, bound Product/Price, customer identity, and metadata before persisting provider evidence and the credit entry in one transaction.

A credit purchase is a second, atomic transaction. It locks the account, requires a reviewed eligible jurisdiction and approved immutable offer-to-release binding, debits credits once, and snapshots that same binding, asset version and hash, operative license version, and terms version into the order, entitlement, and license grant. Private delivery authorizes that exact entitlement-bound asset at request time and uses a short-lived signed URL.

Refund and dispute handling uses compensating ledger entries. Historical ledger entries are preserved rather than edited.

## Pricing and publication policy

The internal price bands use a 400-credit floor. Reference examples include 400–700 for a printable, 800–3,900 for an ebook, 2,900–4,900 for a runbook, 4,900–7,900 for a playbook, 9,900–29,900 for a toolkit, and 7,900–14,900 for an API/MCP pack. An exact offer needs three relevant comparables from two independent sources and cannot activate from a band alone. Bundle discounts are capped at 15%, do not stack, and cannot reduce a licensed product to zero.

License multipliers are SOLO 1×, PRO 2×, TEAM 4×/10 seats, EDU\_IMPACT 2.5×/30 seats, and ENTERPRISE 10×/100 seats. OEM/white-label is disabled until Phase 20. Every publication requires eight gates, two independent reviewers, 95/100 internal editorial/design/usability/accessibility-evidence thresholds, and 100/100 executable validation. EPUB 3.3, EPUB Accessibility 1.1, and applicable WCAG 2.2 AA are unasserted targets, not automatic certifications.
