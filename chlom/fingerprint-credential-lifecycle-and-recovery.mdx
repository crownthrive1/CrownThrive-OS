# Fingerprint Identity — Credential Lifecycle, Revocation and Recovery

Historical CHLOM sources treat the **Fingerprint ID** as the durable identity anchor linking an actor to credentials, roles, licenses, reputation, governance participation and compliance evidence. They also explore DIDs, Verifiable Credentials, Soulbound Tokens and zero-knowledge proofs as representations and proof mechanisms around that identity.

The modern requirement is broader than any one blockchain identity format: **provider-independent identity continuity with revocable, attributable credentials and privacy-preserving proof options**.

## Fingerprint ID

The historical Fingerprint concept is not simply a device fingerprint or biometric identifier. Across the Grand Prospectus and Technical Blueprint it functions as a cryptographic/stable identity anchor joining:

- actor identity;
- credential references;
- licenses and DLA relationships;
- role/reputation state;
- compliance history;
- evidence/attestations;
- governance participation;
- economic routing where authorized.

Modern CHLOM should preserve the stable identity semantics without requiring historical biometric/device implementation assumptions.

## DID lineage

Historical sources describe DIDs as self-controlled cryptographic identifiers capable of supporting public keys, service endpoints, credential references and pseudonymous interaction.

Current design principle:

```text
stable institutional identity
      != one auth provider
      != one wallet address
      != one DID method
      != one email address
```

Authentication identifiers can rotate while the institutional identity remains continuous.

## Issuance lifecycle

```text
claim / application
      |
      v
source evidence collected
      |
      v
issuer authority verified
      |
      v
policy / qualification review
      |
 +----+------------------+
 |                       |
PASS                    HOLD / DENY
 |                       |
 v                       v
issue credential      no active credential
 |
 v
ACTIVE
```

No credential becomes authoritative merely because a provider returned `verified` or because a token was minted. Issuer authority and source evidence remain separate.

## Historical SBT representation

Historical CHLOM proposes non-transferable SBTs for KYC status, professional qualifications, age/eligibility, regulator/issuer roles and other credentials.

The durable invariant is **non-transferable credential meaning**, not the token itself.

```text
credential meaning
      |
      +--> database credential
      +--> signed VC
      +--> SBT representation
      +--> future proof adapter
```

A future SBT can mirror an institutional credential, but it cannot expand its authority.

## Revocation and expiration

The whitepaper explicitly recognizes that a credential may need to expire or be revoked even if the original token/issuance history is immutable.

```text
ACTIVE
  |
  +--> EXPIRED -----> renewal review
  |
  +--> SUSPENDED ---> investigation / hold
  |
  +--> REVOKED -----> cannot satisfy proof
  |
  +--> REPLACED ----> successor credential
```

The historical record remains. Current validity changes.

Recovery must not silently create a new institutional person when only authentication changes.

## Credential portability

Historical CHLOM imagines credentials reusable across contexts. Modern portability requires explicit scope and relying-party rules.

A credential issued for one purpose does not automatically authorize every other purpose.

```text
credential exists
   != credential current
   != issuer trusted for this purpose
   != subject eligible for this action
   != role/delegation authority
```

## Identity versus role versus authority

This separation is mandatory:

```text
IDENTITY: who is this actor?
CREDENTIAL: what claim has an issuer verified?
ROLE: what institutional function is assigned?
DELEGATION: what authority was granted?
APPROVAL: what specific action was approved?
EXECUTION: what side effect may occur?
```

A course certificate, SBT, professional credential or KYC result cannot silently become corporate/legal authority.

## ZK proof relationship

Credentials may act as private witnesses for a zero-knowledge proof.

```text
restricted credential
       |
       v
local / trusted prover
       |
       v
proof: required statement is true
       |
       v
verifier checks proof + current revocation state
       |
       v
PASS / HOLD / DENY
```

Proof validity does not bypass credential freshness or revocation.

## Privacy boundary

Public identity projections should disclose only what is necessary. Private personal data, evidence bodies, verification documents and sensitive attributes remain protected.

```text
PRIVATE

          |
          | minimum necessary projection
          v
PUBLIC-SAFE
stable pseudonymous ID + allowed status
```

## Provider evidence

External KYC, identity, certification or registry providers may supply evidence. They remain replaceable execution/evidence sources.


## Failure conditions

HOLD or DENY when:

- issuer authority cannot be proven;
- credential is expired/revoked;
- identity linkage is ambiguous;
- a key rotation cannot be authenticated;
- provider result conflicts with authoritative evidence;
- a credential is used outside its allowed scope;
- a relying party requests restricted attributes without authorization;
- two identities appear to represent the same actor and duplicate resolution is incomplete.
