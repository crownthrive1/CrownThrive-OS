# CHLOM revenue, splits, reversals, and remedies model

> **State:** Phase 2 / 2.99 public-safe doctrine. This page defines a candidate institutional model. It does not recognize revenue for accounting or tax purposes, authorize a split or payout, set a price, activate Stripe, create a settlement obligation, decide a dispute, grant rights, issue an entitlement, or open Phase 3.

CHLOM treats **payment observation, revenue classification, economic allocation, payable status, settlement, reversal, and remedy as separate governed facts**. A payment-provider event can establish that the provider reported an event. It does not independently establish rights, license acceptance, entitlement, fulfillment, earned revenue, the identity of every beneficiary, or authority to move money.

This separation lets CrownThrive preserve one correction-safe economic record without allowing a checkout event, old commission table, dashboard number, or ledger entry to silently create legal or financial authority.

## Core distinctions

| Concept | Meaning | Does not prove |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Payment observation | A provider or governed ledger reported a payment-related event | Revenue recognition, rights, delivery, or payout authority |
| Gross transaction amount | The amount bound to an exact transaction before governed adjustments | Amount earned or distributable |
| Revenue classification | The governed accounting or economic classification applied to an amount | Tax treatment, legal conclusion, or cash availability |
| Economic schedule | The exact versioned rules for allocations, commissions, royalties, reserves, fees, and remedies | That the schedule was accepted or effective for a transaction |
| Allocation or split | A calculated attribution of an amount under one accepted schedule | A payable, transfer, or completed payout |
| Accrual | An amount recorded as potentially earned under defined conditions | That all conditions have matured |
| Payable | An authorized obligation eligible for settlement after holds and prerequisites | That money has been sent or received |
| Settlement | Evidence that an authorized obligation was discharged through the approved rail | Finality against later refund, dispute, correction, or recovery |
| Reversal | A compensating economic event that reduces, cancels, or reclassifies a prior event | Erasure of the original event |
| Remedy | A governed response such as refund, cure, suspension, revocation, restoration, offset, or appeal | Automatic legal sufficiency or universal applicability |

Cash received is not automatically recognized revenue. A calculated share is not automatically payable. A provider transfer is not automatically a correct settlement. Every conclusion must remain within the scope of its evidence and authority.

## Economic schedule identity

Pricing, commissions, royalties, referral compensation, service fees, reserves, revenue shares, and remedies require an exact **economic schedule version**. Historical schedules remain evidence of what was proposed or applied in their effective period; they do not become current terms through repetition.

A governed schedule should identify:

- stable schedule ID and version;
- issuing authority and applicable entity;
- effective start and end;
- covered asset, offer, release, service, corridor, or agreement;
- currency or governed closed-loop denomination;
- gross-amount definition;
- discounts, taxes, provider fees, reserves, costs, and exclusions;
- eligible parties and verified institutional identities;
- allocation formula, basis, rounding, minimums, caps, and negative-balance treatment;
- earning, vesting, approval, hold, and payout conditions;
- refund, partial-refund, dispute, cancellation, fraud, chargeback, and correction rules;
- attribution window and evidence requirements where a referral is involved;
- payout rail and separately authorized financial thresholds;
- rights, license, terms, policy, and jurisdiction references;
- supersession, correction, appeal, recovery, and rollback rules.

A missing, stale, contradictory, unaccepted, or inapplicable schedule keeps the affected calculation on **HOLD/UNKNOWN**. Agents must not fill gaps with an old prospectus, a marketplace convention, an industry average, a provider default, or an inferred founder preference.

## Allocation lifecycle

A public-safe candidate lifecycle is:

1. **observed** — a bounded source reported an event;
2. **classified** — the event was assigned a governed transaction and revenue class;
3. **bound** — the exact rights, license, release, offer, parties, and economic schedule were identified;
4. **calculated** — the allocation was deterministically computed;
5. **reviewed** — required financial, rights, tax, fraud, and authority gates were evaluated;
6. **accrued** — a conditional economic amount was recorded;
7. **payable** — all maturity, hold, identity, and approval conditions were satisfied;
8. **settled** — the approved rail produced provider and institutional readback;
9. **reconciled** — provider, application, treasury, entitlement, and DAIL evidence agree;
10. **corrected or remedied** — later events are linked through append-only compensation rather than silent mutation.

A transaction may stop at any state. **DEFERRED, BLOCKED, NOT_REVIEWED, NOT_INDEPENDENTLY_VERIFIED, and UNKNOWN are not PASS** and do not mature an amount into a payable.

## Exact transaction binding

Every economic record should bind to the smallest exact governed unit that supports the claim. Depending on the corridor, that may include:

- transaction, order, invoice, credit top-up, or adjustment ID;
- stable buyer or institutional subject ID;
- exact offer, SKU, asset, release, and manifest digest;
- exact license, terms, rights, price, tax-decision, and economic-schedule versions;
- provider event and object references without exposing secrets;
- gross amount, currency or credits, and event time;
- discount, fee, tax, reserve, refund, and dispute components;
- allocation inputs, formula version, rounding rule, and output;
- accrual, payable, settlement, and reconciliation state;
- authority receipt and independent-review references;
- entitlement, delivery, revocation, and remedy links;
- DAIL event, predecessor, supersession, correction, and rollback references.

“Latest” must not select an economic schedule, product release, rights state, or license for a completed transaction. A later version cannot silently rewrite the terms or economics that governed an earlier event.

## Stripe and payment-provider boundary

For the governed CrownThrive credit-store design, **Stripe is a funding rail, not the wallet, rights, entitlement, revenue-allocation, or license authority**. Supabase Auth identifies the application subject, and the governed application ledger remains the balance authority. A successful provider event does not by itself grant credits, issue a license, deliver an asset, activate an offer, recognize revenue, or authorize a split.

Provider delivery should be processed from verified, bounded, replay-safe events and reconciled with canonical readback. Stripe recommends webhook-driven fulfillment because customers may not return to a success page, and it requires fulfillment logic to be safe when called more than once. CrownThrive therefore requires signature verification, deterministic idempotency, duplicate-event handling, asynchronous-success and failure handling, bounded retries, and reconciliation before dependent state can advance.

The current owner-approved marketplace control model is first-party and closed-loop. Third-party sellers, payouts, transfers, cash-out, Connect settlement, auto-reload, and recurring credit subscriptions remain disabled. Any future change to that boundary requires a separately accepted policy, legal, tax, payments, identity, fraud, security, and treasury packet; documentation cannot activate it.

## Closed-loop credits

Credits are not cash, cryptocurrency, interest-bearing value, or transferable property. A funding payment and a credit-ledger entry are separate events:

- provider payment evidence shows what Stripe reported;
- the application ledger records the governed credit effect;
- an order records the exact offer and release selected;
- a license acceptance records the exact permissions accepted;
- an entitlement records the bounded access granted;
- delivery records protected access to that immutable release.

A reversal of funding must be reconciled through append-only compensating ledger entries. It must not edit history, silently delete an order, or produce a negative customer balance without the policy-authorized hold, remedy, and review path.

## Refunds, disputes, reversals, and corrections

A remedy event must preserve the original event and explain what changed. At minimum, the remedy record should identify:

- original transaction and affected economic records;
- remedy class and reason;
- full or partial amount;
- provider event status and effective time;
- affected credits, entitlement, license, delivery, allocations, accruals, payables, and settlements;
- policy, agreement, rights, and schedule versions;
- decision authority and required specialist review;
- customer notice or appeal path where applicable;
- compensating entries and reconciliation status;
- unresolved residual amount, reserve, hold, or recovery action;
- DAIL correction and supersession references.

### Refund

A refund changes the transaction's economic state but does not erase the original payment or the evidence that fulfillment may have occurred. Full and partial refunds require separate rules for credit restoration or removal, entitlement continuation or revocation, already-consumed value, allocations, fees, and any previously settled amount.

### Dispute

A dispute begins as a contingent provider and institutional state. It should place affected dependent amounts on the applicable hold without being represented as a final loss. A won, withdrawn, or lost dispute creates a new state and new compensating entries. The system must preserve the provider outcome, internal decision, evidence, and any restoration or recovery action.

### Reversal and clawback

A reversal reduces or cancels a prior economic effect. A clawback is a governed recovery remedy against an amount previously accrued, made payable, or settled. Neither should be inferred from a provider event alone. The applicable agreement, schedule, law, financial authority, notice, limits, and appeal conditions must be identified.

### Correction

A correction addresses an inaccurate classification, party, schedule, amount, formula, or state. Corrections are versioned and append-only. The prior value, corrected value, reason, evidence, actor, authority, affected systems, effective time, migration, rollback, and public-notice effect must remain inspectable.

## Affiliate, referral, royalty, and contributor economics

Attribution evidence and compensation authority are different. A click, code, referral, lead, content contribution, rights record, or sale may support an attribution claim within its evidence scope; it does not establish a commission, royalty, ownership interest, employment relationship, partnership, or payable.

Before an allocation can mature, the governed record should resolve:

- eligible program and participant identity;
- accepted agreement and current schedule;
- qualifying event and attribution method;
- rights and content-use permission;
- returns, disputes, cancellations, fraud, and attribution windows;
- duplicate or conflicting attribution;
- tax, sanctions, KYC, or other specialist requirements where applicable;
- reserve, maturity, minimum, cap, offset, and payout conditions;
- correction, suspension, removal, appeal, and recovery rules.

Historical Crown Affiliates, Ambassador, Partner Circle, creator, contributor, or royalty economics remain historical until a current accepted schedule and operating packet deliberately revive them. Recovery of a source or old percentage does not reactivate compensation.

## Accounting, tax, and legal boundary

This doctrine does not decide when revenue is recognized under GAAP or another accounting standard, determine nexus or taxability, select a tax code, establish withholding, decide contractor or employee status, create a fiduciary duty, or authorize a payout. Those conclusions require the applicable entity, jurisdiction, contract, transaction facts, qualified specialist, and acceptance record.

Public reporting must distinguish at least:

- transaction volume;
- cash collected;
- credits funded;
- orders placed;
- revenue classified;
- revenue recognized by the authorized accounting process;
- allocations calculated;
- accruals recorded;
- payables approved;
- settlements completed;
- refunds and disputes;
- net reconciled outcome.

A marketplace listing, provider dashboard, checkout session, credit balance, invoice, or projected split must not be presented as verified revenue.

## DAIL event model

DAIL preserves the economic evidence graph. Events may record or reference:

- payment observed, failed, or expired;
- credits funded or held;
- order proposed or accepted;
- license accepted;
- entitlement issued, suspended, revoked, or restored;
- fulfillment completed or failed;
- allocation calculated;
- accrual created;
- payable approved;
- settlement observed and reconciled;
- refund created or completed;
- dispute opened, won, lost, or withdrawn;
- reversal, clawback, offset, or reserve applied;
- correction, supersession, appeal, or restoration issued.

DAIL is an event and lineage architecture in the current baseline. A Fingerprint ID or later cryptographic anchor can establish integrity or linkage within its scope; it does not establish rights, accounting correctness, identity control, settlement finality, or legal sufficiency.

## Reconciliation invariants

A revenue-and-remedy path cannot be certified unless:

- provider events are authenticated and replay-safe;
- provider and application identifiers map to one exact governed transaction;
- amount and currency or credits match the authorized offer;
- the applicable release, license, rights, price, tax-decision, and schedule versions are exact;
- duplicate events do not duplicate credits, orders, entitlements, allocations, or settlements;
- refunds and disputes generate correct compensating effects;
- negative, timeout, partial-success, and unknown-outcome paths fail closed;
- settlement evidence is separate from calculated allocation;
- customer-private and provider-restricted data remain protected;
- rollback and recovery are tested;
- independent reviewers confirm required rights, security, accessibility, financial, tax, legal, and governance scopes;
- public claims match the reconciled current state.

Provider configuration, synthetic fixtures, a passing unit test, or a successful UI redirect can prove only its bounded technical fact. It cannot substitute for external delivery or end-to-end reconciliation when those are the acceptance criteria.

## Public projection and #131 boundary

Public-safe documentation may describe concepts, lifecycle states, interfaces, reasons, invariants, evidence classes, public provider guidance, candidate/HOLD boundaries, accepted policy names and versions, public-safe digests, and sanitized aggregate outcomes.

It must not expose customer, partner, payee, or provider-private records; credentials or signing secrets; private identifiers or restricted fingerprints; private economic schedules or negotiations; fraud thresholds or defensive scoring; protected algorithms or proprietary test corpora; restricted runtime DDL, evidence bodies, or key-management design; unresolved-rights material; or non-public legal advice.

## What this model does not establish

This model does not:

- approve a price, split, commission, royalty, reserve, refund, tax, or payout;
- revive a historical affiliate, creator, contributor, Partner Circle, or licensing schedule;
- authorize Stripe Connect, third-party sellers, transfers, cash-out, or subscriptions;
- recognize revenue or establish accounting treatment;
- prove ownership, rights clearance, license acceptance, entitlement, delivery, or settlement;
- grant a sovereign vote, repository certification, framework activation, or Phase 3;
- convert a typed name, DID candidate, Fingerprint ID, provider object, or ledger row into cryptographic or legal authority.
