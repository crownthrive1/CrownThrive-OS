# Sovereign Industry Licensing Blueprint Framework

The Grand Prospectus separately reserves an **Industry Blueprints** section spanning media, wellness, finance, education and related sectors.

The complete original body of every sovereign-industry study is not present in the current source corpus. This page therefore reconstructs the **shared blueprint pattern** supported by the recovered titles, master whitepaper use cases, DLA/LEX architecture and current CHLOM rights/rules/roles/revenue/records/remedies model.

## Meaning of sovereign-industry licensing

In current CHLOM usage, **sovereign** should not imply that CrownThrive supersedes government law or possesses governmental sovereignty. The useful design concept is an industry-specific licensing domain with its own structured rights, roles, policies, evidence and remedies while remaining subordinate to applicable law and legitimate external authority.

```text
APPLICABLE LAW / EXTERNAL AUTHORITY
               |
               v
      CHLOM INDUSTRY PROFILE
               |
   +-----------+-----------+
   |     |     |     |     |
 Rights Rules Roles Revenue Records Remedies
```

## Shared sector blueprint

Every industry profile should answer nine questions.

### 1. What is the governed object?

Examples:

- song/composition/master;
- film/program/episode;
- property/title/lease right;
- curriculum/course/certification;
- game/simulation asset or operator permission;
- healthcare credential/consent/service authorization;
- financial product/service permission;
- government/public-sector permit or supplier credential;
- sports media/merchandise/player-related right;
- literary work/edition/translation/adaptation right.

### 2. Who controls source rights or authority?

The blueprint must distinguish:

- owner/rightsholder;
- authorized licensor;
- regulator or statutory issuer;
- professional credential issuer;
- platform/operator;
- participant/licensee;
- delegated reviewer.

CHLOM does not invent external authority.

### 3. What rights or permissions exist?

```text
SOURCE AUTHORITY
      |
      v
RIGHTS / PERMISSION INVENTORY
      |
      +--> exclusive / nonexclusive
      +--> territory
      +--> media / field of use
      +--> term
      +--> transfer
      +--> sublicense
      +--> derivative use
      +--> economic obligations
```

### 4. What rules apply?

Rules may come from:

- law/regulation;
- contract;
- platform policy;
- professional standards;
- industry standard;
- safety requirements;
- rights-holder requirements;
- CrownThrive institutional policy.

Each source needs authority/effective-period metadata.

### 5. What evidence is required?

Potential evidence classes include:

- identity/organization;
- chain of title;
- professional license;
- training;
- insurance;
- consent;
- audit report;
- financial qualification;
- sanctions screening;
- content provenance;
- safety inspection;
- external registry status.

### 6. What economic schedule applies?

The blueprint may reference:

- license fee;
- subscription;
- royalty;
- commission;
- revenue split;
- reserve;
- refund/reversal;
- tax/withholding treatment;
- reporting obligation.

Historical source examples do not become current prices/rates automatically.

### 7. What lifecycle states exist?

```text
CANDIDATE
   |
   v
EVIDENCE / RIGHTS REVIEW
   |
   v
ELIGIBILITY / POLICY REVIEW
   |
   v
APPROVED
   |
   v
ACTIVE
   |
+--+----+-------+--------+
|       |       |        |
v       v       v        v
RENEW SUSPEND REVOKE EXPIRE
```

### 8. What remedies exist?

Possible remedies include:

- correction;
- suspension;
- revocation;
- takedown;
- refund/reversal;
- access restriction;
- remediation plan;
- re-training;
- appeal;
- external referral where required.

### 9. What is public versus restricted?

Each sector must define what can appear in a public verification surface without leaking personal, financial, medical, legal, security or proprietary information.

## Music

Confirmed source family: sovereign music licensing study plus whitepaper/LEX examples.

Governed objects may include composition, sound recording/master, artwork, performance, synchronization, mechanical/reproduction, distribution and derivative/remix rights.

Key CHLOM questions:

```text
who controls composition?
who controls master?
which territories?
which media?
which term?
which derivative rights?
which royalty/split schedule?
which credits/attribution?
```

A streaming or distribution event does not manufacture rights ownership.

## Film and streaming

Potential governed layers include:

- underlying script/book rights;
- production ownership;
- performer releases;
- music synchronization;
- territory/window rights;
- streaming/platform license;
- syndication/FAST/broadcast rights;
- promotional clip rights;
- derivative/remake rights.

```text
source rights
  -> production package
  -> territory/window license
  -> platform entitlement
  -> usage/performance records
  -> economic reconciliation
```

## Real estate

Historical whitepapers discuss property/title tokenization and controlled transfer concepts. Current CHLOM should treat statutory title, deed/recording and licensed professional requirements as external authority domains.

CHLOM may govern evidence, workflow, permissions, contractual rights and audit trails but does not replace a government land registry merely by issuing a token.

Possible objects:

- ownership evidence;
- lease;
- management authority;
- booking/use license;
- franchise/location rights;
- inspection/insurance attestations.

## Education

Potential objects include:

- curriculum rights;
- instructor authorization;
- course delivery license;
- certification program;
- institutional partnership;
- content reproduction;

- continuing-education completion evidence.

Key separation:

```text
course certificate
   != professional license
   != academic accreditation
   != government authorization
```

## Gaming and simulation

Historical sources discuss operator licenses, age/eligibility and programmable rules.

Possible controls include:

- operator authorization;
- age/identity eligibility;
- jurisdiction/geofencing;
- asset-use rights;
- tournament/event rights;
- creator/mod rights;
- anti-fraud controls;
- payout/award conditions.

If gambling or regulated wagering is involved, external legal/regulatory authority remains mandatory.

## Healthcare

The whitepaper explicitly explores healthcare/pharma credential and privacy use cases, including professional credentials, patient consent and privacy-preserving proof concepts.

Current boundary:

```text
CHLOM may verify evidence / permissions / workflow
CHLOM does not create a medical license
CHLOM does not replace required clinical/legal authority
```

Potential governed objects:

- professional credential evidence;
- consent authorization;
- data-access permission;
- training completion;
- facility/vendor qualification;
- research/data-use agreement.

## Finance and capital markets

Historical sources contemplate broker/dealer-type licensing, KYC/AML, accreditation and institutional finance.

Modern controls should distinguish:

- identity/KYC evidence;
- sanctions screening;
- professional/entity registration;
- product eligibility;
- accredited/qualified status where lawfully relevant;
- transaction policy;
- custody/provider evidence;
- settlement/reconciliation.

A CHLOM credential does not replace SEC/FINRA/state/other required authorization.

## Government and public sector

Possible CHLOM uses include:

- permits;
- certifications;
- procurement rights;
- public grant conditions;
- document/evidence integrity;
- training/clearance status;
- inter-agency proofs.

Government authority must originate from the actual authorized body.
