## Identity, Fingerprint, DID, SBT and Zero-Knowledge Lineage

Identity is foundational to CHLOM because rights, licensing, authority, compliance, evidence and remedies all depend on knowing which actor or organization a decision applies to.

Historical papers explored **Fingerprint IDs, DIDs, verifiable credentials, soulbound tokens and zero-knowledge proofs**. Current CHLOM preserves those semantics without claiming that a public DID/SBT/ZK stack is production-ready.

## Fingerprint ID — historical anchor

The Grand Prospectus and Technical Blueprint treat the **CHLOM Fingerprint ID** as a cryptographic identity anchor joining identity, credentials, roles, licenses, rights, reputation, governance participation, evidence, financial routing, overrides and disputes.

Historical pseudocode also explores sybil/duplicate-identity detection and device/behavior signals. A Fingerprint ID is therefore an **identity architecture concept**, not proof that biometric/device fingerprinting or a specific historical algorithm is live.

## DID — Decentralized Identifier

Historical design uses DIDs so individuals/organizations can control identity keys and link credentials without exposing all private data on-chain.

A DID-oriented implementation may support key rotation, multiple verification methods, service endpoints, credential references, recovery, pseudonymous public identity and selective proof of real-world qualifications.

Current CrownThrive identity may use stable institutional IDs and conventional infrastructure while preserving future DID/VC adapter compatibility.

## VC — Verifiable Credential

VCs represent signed, issuer-attributable claims that can be checked independently. Their authority derives from issuer, evidence, scope and current validity — not merely from credential format.

## SBT — Soulbound Token

Historical CHLOM uses non-transferable SBTs for KYC status, professional certifications, age/eligibility, issuer/regulator roles, training/qualification and compliance history.

The invariant is **non-transferable credential authority**, not the token representation. A modern implementation may use a database credential, signed VC, entitlement record or future SBT if revocation, issuer authority, expiry, evidence and privacy requirements are preserved.

## Credential lifecycle

```text
claim
→ evidence
→ issuer review
→ credential issuance
→ active
→ expiry / suspension / revocation
→ renewal / correction / restoration
```

Historical papers sometimes describe SBTs as permanent while also discussing revocation registries. Current architecture treats credential **validity state** as independently controllable even if issuance history remains immutable.

## ZK / ZKP — Zero-Knowledge Proofs

CHLOM's historical privacy promise is that a person or organization should be able to prove a compliance fact without revealing unnecessary source data.

Examples include proving age threshold, license possession, KYC completion, accredited status, residency/citizenship eligibility, solvency, transaction compliance, audit success, source-of-funds or other policy conditions.

## ZKX — historical proof layer

The Genesis Volume refers to a **ZKX** layer/module for proof verification, including circuits, verification keys, batching/recursion and links between proofs, DAL attestations and DLA enforcement.

Current status: ZKX is historical/advanced research unless a modern implementation package explicitly adopts the label.

## Selective disclosure and encrypted evidence

The Grand Prospectus describes encrypted data vaults and selective disclosure: public records contain minimum metadata, sensitive documents remain encrypted/off-chain, hashes/commitments prove integrity, access is limited and a proof can satisfy a rule without exposing the whole record.

This survives directly into modern public/private/restricted evidence classification.

## Identity is not authority

Holding a credential, badge, SBT, certificate or DID does not automatically create CrownThrive institutional authority.

Separate records are required for identity, role, credential, professional license, capability, delegation, approval and execution authority. A course certificate cannot silently become a professional license or corporate office.

## External identity providers

CHLOM may integrate with government ID, KYC providers, professional registries, enterprise SSO, passkeys or other identity systems. Provider evidence remains separate from institutional truth:

```text
provider says verified
→ evidence received
→ authority/source evaluated
→ CHLOM credential/eligibility decision
→ DAIL receipt
```

## Current implementation status

- stable institutional identity: actively being built/governed;
- DID/VC compatibility: target/adapter architecture;
- public SBT credential system: historical/target unless specifically evidenced;
- production ZK verification: advanced target/laboratory;
- Fingerprint semantics: preserved historical/core design;
- privacy/evidence classification: current doctrine.
