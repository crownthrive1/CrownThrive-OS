## DID role

DID or another stable provider-independent identity identifier represents the public/interoperable identity anchor. It can bind key history, service endpoints and credential subjects without exposing private Fingerprint state.

## Fingerprint role

The CHLOM Fingerprint layer is private internal assurance used for identity resolution, deduplication, risk/continuity or other source-defined assurance functions. Its exact features, private identifiers and heuristics are not part of the public DID document and are not published through ZK receipts.

```text
public DID != private Fingerprint ID
```

## Credential / SBT role

Historical CHLOM research uses SBT/soulbound-token concepts alongside verifiable credentials for non-transferable identity/role/compliance claims. Current architecture should represent the institutional claim independently of any specific blockchain token container.

A current credential can therefore have:

- stable credential ID/schema;
- issuer and subject;
- claim set;
- issue/expiry/revocation;
- evidence refs;
- optional external/token alias;
- proof/ZK compatibility;
- DAIL lineage.

A historical SBT representation is an alias/implementation option, not the credential's institutional identity.

## ZKP role

ZK proofs allow a subject to prove selected credential/identity predicates without disclosing the full credential or private identity evidence. Example proof families are institutionalized separately: age, geography, identity attributes, financial eligibility and proof-of-license.


Credential issuance does not grant unrelated action authority.

## Proof flow

```text
policy requests predicate
 -> ZKX selects accepted circuit/version
 -> subject constructs proof from eligible credential/witness
 -> verifier checks proof + circuit/key registry
 -> revocation/currentness checked
 -> public predicate result enters policy engine
 -> authority gateway remains separate
```

## Recovery and key rotation

Identity recovery or DID key rotation changes current control without deleting historical credential/proof events. The system must decide whether credentials survive recovery, require reissuance, or need additional proof depending on credential schema and issuer policy.

Private Fingerprint assurance may assist internal recovery/resolution, but its details remain protected.

## Revocation

Credential/SBT revocation and DID key revocation are separate. A valid key cannot resurrect a revoked credential; a valid credential proof may be unusable if the controlling identity/key context is compromised or superseded under policy.

## Anti-transfer semantics

Historical SBT research focuses on non-transferable credentials. Current architecture should enforce subject binding and issuer/revocation rules at the institutional layer rather than relying solely on a token's transfer restriction.

## Privacy architecture

- public DID exposes minimum interoperable identity data;
- credential body is access-controlled;
- private Fingerprint is restricted;
- ZK proof reveals only approved predicate/public inputs;
- DAIL stores safe receipts/references rather than raw private evidence;
- public widgets consume audience-scoped projections;
- proof/correlation metadata is reviewed for deanonymization risk.

## Provider/blockchain neutrality

DID methods, credential libraries, wallets, blockchain/SBT implementations and ZK proof systems are replaceable implementation layers. Stable CHLOM identity, credential, proof and evidence IDs remain canonical.

## Threat model

- public/private identity correlation leakage;
- private Fingerprint exposure;
- DID key compromise;
- credential cloning/transfer despite non-transferable intent;
- revoked credential proof reuse;
- proof correlation across services;
- provider wallet becoming identity truth;
- wrong-subject credential binding;
- recovery flow allowing credential takeover;
- source/issuer spoofing.
