## Purpose

A ZK identity/attribute proof can establish selected predicates about an identity or credential relationship without exposing the full identity record. Candidate predicates include:

```text
subject controls credential C
credential issuer belongs to accepted issuer set
credential contains attribute satisfying predicate P
credential subject matches proof subject commitment
credential is current and not revoked
subject possesses required role/qualification attribute
```

The proof can reduce unnecessary disclosure while preserving source and revocation checks.

## Identity proof is not one concept

CHLOM distinguishes:

- authentication/control of a DID/key;
- identity verification;
- possession of a credential;
- attribute proof;
- role/authorization proof;
- uniqueness/deduplication assurance;
- organization identity;
- account/provider alias binding.

A proof of one does not automatically prove another.

## Credential possession predicate

A generic proof can show that the prover possesses a credential matching an accepted schema/issuer and bound to the subject commitment while revealing only selected attributes or predicate results.

```text
credential_valid
AND issuer_accepted
AND subject_binding_valid
AND not_revoked
AND requested_attribute_predicate = true
```

## DID/key-control relationship

A cryptographic proof of key control can demonstrate control of a DID-associated key under the applicable DID/key history. It does not by itself prove legal identity or that the key has not been shared/compromised.

## Attribute minimization

Instead of revealing full values, a circuit can prove:

- membership in an allowed qualification class;
- possession of an active credential type;
- role eligibility;
- threshold/range conditions;
- issuer membership;
- validity/currentness.

Public policy should request only the minimum necessary predicate.

## Fingerprint boundary

The protected CHLOM Fingerprint layer can contribute internal identity-resolution assurance, but its device/state/behavioral heuristics, deduplication rules and private identifiers are not public circuit inputs/outputs unless a separately approved privacy design creates a safe commitment interface. Public DID and proof identifiers remain distinct from private Fingerprint state.

## Revocation and recovery

Identity proofs must consider credential/key revocation and identity recovery. A proof generated before a key/credential revocation may become unusable for current policy. Recovery should not erase historical proof receipts; it updates current key/identity validity.

## Role versus authority

A proof that a subject has a role/credential can satisfy one eligibility input. It does not prove that the subject has authority for a particular action. ALG-002 still resolves the exact action scope and delegation.

## Circuit governance

Each identity proof circuit defines credential schemas, accepted issuer registry, public attributes/predicates, key/DID binding rules, revocation mechanism, context/nonce binding, proof-system version and security review.

Protected circuit implementation/witness logic remains in restricted custody where classified.

## Privacy threats

Correlation across proofs, stable public identifiers, overly specific attribute combinations, timing metadata and verifier challenges can deanonymize subjects even if the core proof is zero-knowledge. Privacy review must consider metadata, not only witness secrecy.

## DAIL evidence

Public-safe receipts can record proof/circuit/version, predicate type, issuer/source class, result, currentness/revocation checks, context and evaluated time. Do not store full credential or private witness.

## Failure states

Invalid proof, wrong subject binding, unacceptable issuer, revoked/expired credential, retired circuit, stale revocation data, key rotation mismatch, context replay, role proof used as authority, private Fingerprint leakage.

## Tests

Test valid credential possession, wrong issuer, revoked credential, recovered DID/key, selective attribute proof, cross-tenant/context replay, correlation-minimization, retired circuit and public payload privacy scan.
