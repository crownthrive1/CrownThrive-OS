## Historical purpose

In the DLA context, the source family frames TLaaS as a way for organizations and platforms to consume CHLOM licensing capabilities without building the entire licensing infrastructure themselves.

Historical functions include:

- applicant/licensee onboarding;
- identity and credential verification;
- licensing eligibility checks;
- issuance of programmable or tokenized licenses;
- license metadata and term management;
- renewal, suspension and revocation;
- transfer and sublicensing where permitted;
- LEX integration;
- royalty/fee handling;
- API/service access;
- audit/event records.

## Scope

Historical TLaaS/DLA scope can be grouped into six domains.

### 1. Rights and authority

A license service cannot create rights that the source holder or steward does not control.

```text
requested license rights
    <= issuer authority ceiling
    <= licensable source rights
    <= source rights
```

### 2. Identity and eligibility

Source-era CHLOM connects DIDs, Fingerprint ID, credentials/SBT research and ZK proofs to licensing decisions.

Current interpretation:

```text
verified identity != licensing authority
credential valid != license approved automatically
```

### 3. License lifecycle

```text
candidate
 -> review
 -> approved/active
 -> renew/amend/transfer
 -> hold/suspend/revoke/expire/terminate
```

Historical token representation is optional in the current model. The durable current object is the Dynamic Licensing Asset.

### 4. Compliance

Source-era TLaaS invokes compliance/risk logic before issuance or transfer. Current CHLOM normalizes this through versioned policy/rules and approval services.

### 5. Marketplace and entitlement

LEX may provide discovery/transaction capability, but a licensing service does not imply a public marketplace.

```text
license exists != LEX-listed
LEX purchase != rights source
payment success != entitlement automatically
```

### 6. Economic routing

Historical sources discuss fees, token payments, royalties, marketplace economics, staking/bonds and treasury flows. These are research-era economic concepts unless separately adopted.

A generic historical service model can be expressed as:

```text
service revenue
  = issuance/renewal fees
  + verification/API usage fees
```


## Relationship to Trust Layer as a Service

Trust Layer as a Service is preserved separately. It packages verification/assurance functions such as identity, credential, evidence, attestation and policy-result verification.

```text
Tokenized Licensing as a Service
        !=
Trust Layer as a Service
```

They may interoperate but are not synonyms.

## Relationship to current DLA

Historical DLA referred to **Decentralized Licensing Authority**. Current CHLOM uses DLA for **Dynamic Licensing Asset** and moves issuer/administrative power into Licensing Stewardship / Issuer Authority.

## Failure modes

- source rights unresolved -> HOLD;
- issuer authority exceeds source scope -> DENY;
- stale credential -> HOLD;
- token/provider write succeeds but canonical DLA write fails -> no institutional license;
- duplicated request -> idempotent replay, not second canonical license;
- marketplace payment succeeds before license/entitlement finalization -> reconcile/compensate;
- historical economic term presented as current -> reject and resolve adopted schedule;
