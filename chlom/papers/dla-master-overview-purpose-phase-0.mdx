## Purpose of this reconstructed record

## Historical problem statement

Related source documents frame the licensing problem as a combination of:

- slow/manual eligibility checks;
- fragmented licensing records;
- fraud or counterfeit credentials;
- unclear transfer and sublicensing rights;
- disconnected identity/compliance evidence;
- costly lifecycle administration;
- weak auditability;
- inconsistent enforcement across systems.

The historical DLA was designed as a single authority-oriented answer to those problems.

## Historical purpose

```text
DEFINE LICENSABLE RULES
        |
VERIFY APPLICANT / HOLDER
        |
CHECK ELIGIBILITY / COMPLIANCE
        |
ISSUE OR DENY LICENSE
        |
TRACK LIFECYCLE
        |
VERIFY USE / TRANSFER
        |
SUSPEND / REVOKE / EXPIRE WHEN AUTHORIZED
```

## Historical scope

Source-era DLA discussions include licenses, permits, certifications, titles and other governed permissions. The concept was industry-agnostic and intended to support different rule profiles rather than one universal license schema.

## Historical design principles

### Programmatic issuance

Eligibility conditions should be evaluable and consistently enforced.

### Tamper-evident records

License state should be independently verifiable rather than dependent on easily altered documents.

### Identity linkage

Licenses should resolve to the correct person/entity/role using identity and credential evidence.

### Lifecycle awareness

A license is not merely issued; it can renew, expire, suspend, revoke, transfer or terminate according to governing terms.

### Hierarchical authority

Master/sub-license relationships can be represented, with child authority constrained by parent/source authority.

### Auditability

Material decisions and state changes should generate reviewable evidence.

## Current conceptual decomposition

Historical DLA combined authority, issuance and license-state semantics. Current CHLOM intentionally splits them:

```text

    |
    v
LICENSING STEWARDSHIP / ISSUER AUTHORITY
    |
    v
DYNAMIC LICENSING ASSET
    |
    +--> Rules Registry
    +--> Approval Service
    +--> LEX eligibility
    +--> entitlement
    +--> lifecycle/remedies
    |

```

```text
authority record = who may act
```

## Safety and authority constraints

Current CHLOM adds stricter authority semantics than historical prose sometimes implied:

```text
automation may execute within authority
automation may not manufacture authority
```

and:

```text
issued_rights <= issuer_authority <= source_rights
```
