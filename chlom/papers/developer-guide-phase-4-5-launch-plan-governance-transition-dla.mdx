## Transition dimensions

### Operational control

Move from narrow trusted operators toward broader operator participation only after identity, permissions, monitoring, incident response and slashing/remedy policies are evidenced.

### Policy/governance control

Move from founder/institutional control to additional governed participants only through an explicit constitutional/authority migration. Historical DAO/token concepts are research lineage, not assumed current policy.

### Issuer authority

Decentralizing infrastructure does not decentralize source rights.

```text
more validators/operators
   !=
more licensing rights
```

Licensing Stewardship remains source-rights constrained.

### Data/oracle control

Broader oracle participation requires source identity, evidence quality, freshness, conflict resolution and revocation/penalty controls.

### Upgrade control

Runtime/contract upgrade power should become more distributed only when emergency recovery, timelock, testing and audit mechanisms remain sufficient.

## Historical-to-current DLA transition

The historical roadmap assumed DLA as the Decentralized Licensing Authority. Current CHLOM has already made a semantic architecture change:

```text
historical authority monolith
        |
        v
Licensing Stewardship / Issuer Authority
        +
Dynamic Licensing Asset
        +
Rules Registry / Approval Service
        +
DAIL evidence
```

Any future decentralization must operate on these separated authority boundaries.

## Launch gates

A current DLA-related production promotion should require evidence for:

- source-rights and issuer-authority model;
- state-machine correctness;
- policy versioning;
- evidence/oracle freshness;
- identity/key management;
- DAIL durability;
- incident/rollback/compensation paths;
- security review;
- integration reconciliation;
- documentation/current-main alignment;
- explicit human/governed production authorization.
