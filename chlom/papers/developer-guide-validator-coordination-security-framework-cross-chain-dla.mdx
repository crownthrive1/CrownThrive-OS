## Developer objective

Cross-chain licensing operations require independent verification of source state, finality and claim integrity. Historical CHLOM research uses validator/oracle concepts for this assurance. Current reconstruction treats validators as a possible assurance role, not as licensing authorities.

## Core separation

```text
validator validates evidence/state
        !=
validator owns source rights
        !=
validator may issue any license
```

A validator can prove or attest what it is authorized to observe; it cannot expand a Dynamic Licensing Asset or issuer authority.

## Coordination topology

```text
SOURCE NETWORK OBSERVATION
        |
 independent observers/validators
   /      |       \
  v       v        v
signed observations / proofs
        |
aggregation / quorum / conflict check
        |
bridge claim candidate
        |
Rules + Approval
        |
PASS | HOLD | DENY
        |
DAIL evidence
```

## Validator identity

Each validator/operator must resolve to an institutional service identity with:

- stable ID;
- signing-key references;
- allowed observation classes;
- network scope;
- active/suspended/revoked status;
- evidence of current authorization;
- rotation/recovery path.

## Quorum semantics

A quorum should be profile-specific rather than globally assumed.

```text
accepted_claim
IFF
minimum eligible validators satisfied
AND required confidence/finality satisfied
AND no blocking conflict exists
```

Exact thresholds remain policy-controlled and may be restricted.

## Source independence

Where meaningful, validator observations should avoid all resolving to a single unaudited provider. Apparent multi-party consensus over one upstream source is not independent corroboration.

## Finality requirements

Cross-chain operations need an explicit source-finality policy. A recent transaction may be observed but not yet final enough for a high-impact licensing or entitlement decision.

## Key security

Public architecture requires:

- separate signing identities per role/environment;
- key rotation and revocation;
- no secrets in public documentation;
- replay-resistant signed messages;
- domain/network separation;
- least privilege;
- auditable signer provenance.

## Conflict handling

```text
validator set agrees -> continue policy evaluation
material disagreement -> HOLD
invalid signer -> discard + record
stale signer/observation -> discard or HOLD
```

A majority is not automatically authoritative if the majority is ineligible or observing stale/invalid evidence.

## Misbehavior and remedies

Historical CHLOM research includes staking/slashing ideas. Current-safe handling is broader:

- suspend validator authorization;
- reject affected observations;
- open investigation/case;
- rotate/revoke credentials;
- correct contaminated projections;
- apply contractual/economic remedies only where an adopted agreement authorizes them.

Historical token slashing remains research lineage unless explicitly activated elsewhere.

## Availability and Byzantine assumptions

Design for unavailable, delayed, equivocal or compromised validators. No single validator should become an unbounded cross-chain authority merely because others are offline.
