# Founder Adjudication and After-the-Fact Correction

CHLOM reconstruction uses a **publish-with-lineage, correct-without-erasure** model.

The Help Center recovery must not stall merely because every historical contradiction has not yet been personally adjudicated. At the same time, reconstructed text must never silently become irreversible institutional truth.

## Operating rule

A source-grounded article may be reconstructed and published when the available evidence is sufficient to explain:

- the historical concept;
- the current interpretation;
- unresolved conflicts;
- implementation state; and
- the source basis used for reconstruction.

Publication does **not** waive the Founder's reserved authority to later adjudicate canonical names, acronyms, platform/framework identity, predecessor/successor relationships, historical-versus-current meaning, economic schedules, strategic disposition, implementation priority, or public/private classification.

Founder adjudication changes the **effective institutional interpretation**. It does not erase the source history that preceded it.

## Article states

Every recovered or reconstructed article should resolve to one of these states:

- `reconstructed_source_grounded` — body rebuilt from identified source families;
- `reconstructed_with_conflict` — body rebuilt while a material contradiction remains explicit;
- `founder_adjudication_pending` — a founder decision is needed for canonical resolution;
- `founder_adjudicated` — a dated founder decision establishes the current institutional meaning;
- `historical_preserved` — retained as an older architecture, claim, acronym, or operating period;
- `superseded_currently` — preserved but no longer the current default;
- `restricted_source_only` — source exists but operative detail is not suitable for public documentation;
- `source_not_located` — title/reference exists but adequate source content has not yet been recovered.

## Required reconstruction envelope

Each reconstructed article should identify, where applicable:

1. **Current meaning** — present institutional interpretation.
2. **Historical meaning** — what earlier papers or Help Center eras said.
3. **Source basis** — exact source families used.
4. **Implementation status** — historical, specified, prototype, controlled test, pilot, production, or another governed state.
5. **Known conflicts** — terminology, status, economic, identity, or authority conflicts.
6. **Founder adjudication status** — whether a later founder ruling may change current interpretation.
7. **Correction behavior** — how later rulings propagate.

## Correction pattern

```text
historical source
    ↓
reconstructed article vN
    ↓
founder adjudication
    ↓
correction / supersession record
    ↓
article vN+1
    ↓
terminology + contradiction + alias + source + changelog propagation
```

The prior article version and source lineage remain institutional history.

## Known examples reserved for after-the-fact adjudication

- **AIE** — historical **AI Intelligence Engine** versus later **Anomaly Intelligence Engine** reuse.
- **ADE** — historical **Autonomous Decision Engine** versus later **Attribution & Distribution Engine** reuse.
- **DAL** — used in different source eras for **Decentralized Attestation Ledger**, **Decentralized Adjudication Layer**, and other legacy meanings; current event/audit architecture uses DAIL.
- **DLA** — historical **Decentralized Licensing Authority** versus current **Dynamic Licensing Asset**.
- **TLaaS** — **Tokenized Licensing as a Service** in one source family and **Trust Layer as a Service** in another.
- **XENThrive** — historical commerce/brand lineage rather than a current primary CHLOM platform unless separately reactivated.

These conflicts are preserved now and can be adjudicated later without blocking recovery.

## What reconstruction may not invent

No article may manufacture legal ownership, patent status, token/securities classification, current prices or royalty percentages, active provider integrations, production deployment, credentials, current authority from historical governance metaphors, or current platform status merely because a legacy article existed.

If source evidence does not support the operative claim, the article must say so.

## Propagation requirement

A founder adjudication affecting a canonical term or system must propagate to materially affected records, including:

- terminology and acronym lineage;
- legacy/alias/successor register;
- contradiction ledger;
- source register interpretation metadata;
- platform/framework registries;
- CHLOM module/component catalogs;
- affected Help Center articles;
- changelog;
- machine manifests and validators where the term is machine-significant.

## Continuity rule

New source discovery, a revived platform, a newly recovered paper, or a founder ruling does not restart the recovery program. It enters the same append-only correction and propagation system.

The objective is institutional memory that can improve continuously without losing where it came from.
