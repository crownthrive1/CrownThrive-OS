# CHLOM Fingerprint ID and attestation trust model

> **Current state:** Phase 2 / 2.99 architecture and bounded trust-backbone work. This page does not represent DID/VC production activation, public-chain identity, biometric processing, credential issuance, or Phase-3 entry.

CHLOM separates **identity**, **record integrity**, **human attestation**, **cryptographic control**, **event lineage**, and **external anchoring** so that one proof type is not mistaken for another.

## Six distinct trust objects

| Object | Purpose | What it does not prove by itself |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Stable institutional subject ID** | Gives a durable CrownThrive identity to a person, organization, platform, framework, domain, asset, work, license, agent, paper, schema or governed record. | Current deployment, ownership, authority or cryptographic control. |
| **Fingerprint ID** | Produces a deterministic digest over a versioned canonical record. | Biometrics, identity control, rights ownership, certification or production state. |
| **DID binding** | Provides a standards-based decentralized-identifier binding when an approved method and key-custody model are adopted. | Authority merely because a DID string exists. |
| **Credential / attestation** | Records a claim made by an identified signer or issuer about a subject. | The truth of every underlying claim without source and authority evidence. |
| **DAIL event hash** | Links append-oriented events, corrections and evidence lineage. | The legal or business meaning of the event without the governing record. |
| **External anchor** | Optionally records an independently verifiable receipt in a later approved anchoring system. | CrownThrive authority, ownership or correctness merely because an external network recorded a digest. |

These objects may reference each other, but they remain separately versioned and separately governed.

## Fingerprint ID is not biometric fingerprinting

A CHLOM **Fingerprint ID** is a deterministic record digest. It is not a biological fingerprint, face template, voiceprint, iris scan or other biometric identifier.

Raw biometric templates, private keys, signing seeds, recovery phrases and secret credentials do not belong in ordinary public documentation or ordinary application tables.

## Stable identity comes before canonical subject binding

Source-row fingerprinting may preserve the integrity of a recovered source record before CrownThrive has resolved what durable institutional object that row represents.

That distinction is intentional:

```text
source record
  -> deterministic source fingerprint
  -> reconciliation of object type and identity
  -> stable institutional subject binding
  -> later rights / deployment / credential relationships
```

A source fingerprint therefore preserves **what record was seen**. It does not silently turn the record into an active platform, deployed service, owned asset, certified framework or cleared right.

## Typed-name founder and executive attestations

A founder or executive override must identify the actual human signer before it can be institutionalized.

When the signer supplies their name, CHLOM may preserve that name as a `typed_name_attestation` associated with the versioned statement or Fingerprint ID.

A typed-name attestation means:

- the named human is the declared signer of the institutional statement;
- the statement can be versioned and fingerprinted;
- the attestation can be linked to its source and authority scope;
- corrections or superseding decisions can preserve lineage.

A typed name is **not cryptographic nonrepudiation**. It must not be represented as a public-key signature, DID proof or verifiable credential unless that separate evidence actually exists.

## DID and verifiable-credential boundary

DID/VC and key-backed signing remain separately governed later work.

The intended progression is:

```text
stable subject identity
  -> deterministic record integrity
  -> typed-name / institutional attestation where applicable
  -> approved key-backed signing pilot
  -> DID method and controller binding
  -> credential issuer / verifier prototypes
  -> later governed federation
```

A candidate DID record does not create authority, rights or a sovereign vote. Controller binding, verification methods, key references, proof suites, issuer/verifier policy, revocation/status handling and custody controls must be independently established before stronger cryptographic claims are made.

## Provider independence

The initial CHLOM identity registry may use centralized operational infrastructure, but CHLOM identity is designed to remain portable across providers.

Stable IDs, canonicalization profiles, exported records and governed proofs should survive vendor replacement. No external chain, hosted database or third-party identity provider becomes the sole source of CrownThrive institutional authority merely because it stores or anchors a record.

## Public-chain and advanced cryptography boundary

This trust model does not require a token, DAO or public blockchain.

Rust/FRAME pallets, Solidity/EVM anchoring, zero-knowledge/selective-disclosure systems, oracle/anchor networks and public-chain experiments remain later-phase R&D unless independently promoted through the applicable legal, security, privacy, rights and phase gates.

## Public versus protected implementation

This page is a **public-safe doctrine and interface projection** under the CHLOM/agentic IP-disclosure gate.

Public documentation may explain the trust objects, lifecycle, semantics and separation rules. It should not reproduce CrownThrive-specific key-management design, protected security controls, private DAIL bodies, credential material, proprietary calibration, private evidence or restricted runtime implementation.

## Operating invariants

1. A hash proves integrity relative to the canonicalized input; it does not prove that every underlying claim is true.
2. Source fingerprinting does not prove deployment, ownership, rights or certification.
3. Stable institutional identity is separate from the record fingerprint of any particular version.
4. A typed name is an institutional attestation, not cryptographic nonrepudiation.
5. DID/VC authority must come from accepted governance, controller/key evidence and policy—not from identifier syntax.
6. External anchors are supplementary evidence, never the sole source of CrownThrive authority.
7. Private keys, signing seeds, recovery phrases and raw biometric data remain outside public documentation.
8. Historical or research architecture remains historical or research until deliberately promoted.

## Phase boundary

During Phase 2 / 2.99, CHLOM may document and test deterministic fingerprint contracts, restricted registries, source-row integrity, attestation semantics and public-safe architecture.

Phase 3 is the intended pilot boundary for internal Fingerprint ID services, DAIL evidence binding, key-backed founder/operator signing and DID/VC issuer-verifier prototypes. Later federation and advanced anchoring remain downstream gated work.

This documentation therefore describes **what the trust model means and how its evidence classes stay separate**. It does not claim that later cryptographic or decentralized capabilities are currently active.
