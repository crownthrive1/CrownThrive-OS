## Recovered statement

The Gold Paper summarizes this guarantee as:

```text
BFT liveness => bounded finality for valid enforcement
```

within the historical blockchain/validator model.

## Meaning

If the network satisfies the source's Byzantine fault and liveness assumptions and an enforcement action is validly authorized/submitted, the modeled system eventually finalizes the corresponding state transition within the protocol's bounded-finality expectations.

## Assumptions

Historical BFT validator assumptions, network progress, valid transaction formation, correct governance/enforcement authority, no permanent censorship beyond modeled fault tolerance, and functioning consensus/finality components.

## Current mapping

Current CHLOM is cloud-first. Its practical analogue is bounded execution/reconciliation under queue/job/database/provider reliability, not a claim that a live BFT network currently enforces CHLOM decisions. Future decentralized deployments may remap the theorem to an actual consensus implementation.

## Limitation

Liveness says an authorized valid action eventually processes under assumptions; it does not say every requested action should be authorized, nor that a provider or blockchain is legally entitled to enforce it.

## Verification targets

Historical/future: consensus liveness tests, censorship/failure injection and state-finality checks. Current cloud-first: job retry/idempotency, dead-letter handling, timelock execution, provider reconciliation and operational SLA evidence.

## Production claim boundary

Do not claim formal bounded enforcement for a current service unless its exact architecture has been mapped and verified against an adapted theorem/model.
