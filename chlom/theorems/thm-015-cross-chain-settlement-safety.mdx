## Recovered statement

The Gold Paper states the historical bridge safety guarantee at a high level as: verified light-client/finality proofs can lift the modeled conservation and finalized-state guarantees across a cross-chain settlement boundary.

## Meaning

A destination system should create the mirrored settlement effect only after independently verifying the source chain's accepted/finalized evidence under the modeled light-client proof rules, rather than trusting a centralized relay's assertion.

## Assumptions

Correct source and destination state models, correct light-client implementation, valid finality proof, replay protection, unambiguous message/settlement identity, rate/limit controls where applicable, and no source-chain finality violation beyond the model assumptions.

## Protected boundary

Full proof details, bridge implementation, kill-switch/limit calibration and future deployment design remain restricted/future-gated.

## Current mapping

Current CHLOM interoperability is provider/system-agnostic claim import and reconciliation. Remote finality or provider confirmation remains external evidence and does not automatically create local rights/authority. Future blockchain adapters can use THM-015 as a design lineage target only after implementation and audit.

## Verification targets

Replay protection, duplicate mint/settlement prevention, source reorg/finality failure simulation, delayed message, wrong source, light-client bug assumptions, rate-limit behavior, emergency halt and end-to-end conservation.

## Production claim boundary

No public claim of formally safe cross-chain CHLOM settlement should be made until an actual bridge/light-client implementation is formally mapped, audited and certified.
