## Recovered statement

The restricted source gives the historical deterrence condition:

```text
S >= G / (p * s)
```

where the source uses stake/deposit `S`, maximum gain from misconduct `G`, detection probability `p`, and slash fraction/severity `s`.

## Meaning

Under the source's simplified expected-value model, bonded value must be sufficiently large relative to the potential benefit of misconduct and the probability/severity of punishment so that cheating has non-positive or negative expected economic value.

## Protected boundary

Exact production calibration methodology, role-specific gain estimates, detection-stack modeling, validator/AI co-training effects and operational slashing parameters are restricted. This public record preserves theorem identity and conceptual relationship only.

## Limitations

Human/organizational behavior is not reducible to one expected-value equation. `G`, `p` and `s` can be difficult to estimate, misconduct can have non-financial motives, detection is not independent, and legal/due-process constraints can limit economic penalties.

## Current mapping

Current cloud-first CHLOM can use the concept for designing provider bonds, assurance deposits, rate limits, fraud controls or other incentives in simulation. It must not present the formula as a live stake/slash requirement unless explicitly adopted and legally/technically activated.

## Verification targets

Sensitivity analysis over `G`, `p`, `s`; adversarial scenarios; false-positive/appeal effects; correlated attacks; role-specific bounds; and future mechanized/economic simulation tied to actual implementation.
