## Recovered statement

The Gold Paper summarizes the historical guarantee as:

```text
finalized events are immutable
```

under its ledger/finality assumptions.

## Meaning

Once an event is finalized under the modeled ledger, later parties cannot silently alter the historical record without violating the source's integrity/finality assumptions.

## Modern DAIL interpretation

Current CHLOM preserves the principle through append-only event/evidence architecture: corrections, reversals, revocations and founder adjudications are new events that supersede or reinterpret prior records rather than deleting them.

## Important limitation

An immutable record can still contain an incorrect assertion. Immutability preserves history; it does not guarantee factual truth. Source authority, evidence quality and correction mechanisms remain mandatory.

## Current implementation boundary

Cloud database immutability requires technical controls—append-only policies, audit logs, permissions, backups, integrity digests and correction chains. The historical blockchain theorem does not automatically prove those controls exist in the deployed cloud stack.

## Verification targets

Attempted mutation tests, permission bypass, correction-chain replay, backup/restore integrity, event digest verification, index rebuild and historical readback after supersession.
