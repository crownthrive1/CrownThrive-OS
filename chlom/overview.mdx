## CHLOM Overview

CHLOM is CrownThrive's governance spine for intellectual property, licensing, attribution, evidence, revenue allocation, corrections, remedies, automation boundaries, and institutional continuity.

Its current operating definition is **Compliance Hybrid Licensing and Ownership Model**.

CHLOM provides durable policy meaning across CrownThrive's changing brands, products, providers, agents, repositories, databases, chains, and deployment environments. It governs relationships around the ecosystem; it does not replace the brands or platforms it governs.

## Six canonical functions

CHLOM is organized around six functions:

1. **Rights** — who owns or controls an asset and what permissions exist.
2. **Rules** — conditions, restrictions, jurisdictions, windows, caps, and policies.
3. **Roles** — actors, authorities, responsibilities, and least-privilege permissions.
4. **Revenue** — fees, splits, royalties, commissions, reserves, and reconciliation.
5. **Records** — sources, evidence, approvals, events, versions, receipts, and corrections.
6. **Remedies** — holds, suspensions, revocations, corrections, disputes, refunds, appeals, and restoration.

## Current implementation posture

The current institutional direction is a **cloud-first, API-first, event-driven, multi-tenant, human-governed core** with bounded automation and durable evidence.

Current operating components include:

- stable identity and registry records;
- source and evidence hierarchy;
- rights, ownership, licensing, and attribution state;
- authority classes and human-reserved decisions;
- structured rules and release gates;
- DAIL event lineage and terminal receipts;
- Penta/MCP/API routing and bounded execution;
- provider/readback evidence;
- correction, hold, supersession, rollback, and continuity controls;
- public-safe documentation and private institutional evidence separation.

Historical CHLOM papers describe a broader decentralized architecture including programmable licenses, decentralized identity, zero-knowledge verification, AI compliance, treasury logic, public licensing exchange, token models, and chain-based settlement. Those designs remain target architecture and research unless a current provider/runtime release and applicable professional review prove otherwise.

## Current DAIL operating architecture

DAIL is CHLOM's canonical append-only institutional evidence spine. The current governing architecture is the **Cursor-Controlled DAIL Capacity Mesh**:

```text
One canonical CHLOM DAIL chain
        ↓
Machine / Human / Hybrid / Cursor projections
        ↓
DAIL Cursor coordination
        ↓
500K Continuity Lock
        ↓
Head / Continuity / Archive routing
        ↓
PentaBalancer admission + PentaGas budgeting
        ↓
MeshAssist and approved bounded capacity donors
        ↓
One canonical writer + terminal evidence readback
```

The four projections are bounded views over one canonical event identity:

- **Machine** records automated runtime and execution evidence.
- **Human** records explicit Founder, reviewer, professional, or governance evidence.
- **Hybrid** records handoffs, reconciliations, and machine-human transitions.
- **Cursor** coordinates high-water marks, route/deferred processing, verification, manifests, and terminal receipts.

Cursor is not another ledger. PentaBalancer is admission control, not authority. PentaGas is capacity accounting, not authority. MeshAssist and approved donors contribute bounded execution capacity without creating a second writer or direct cross-project institutional truth.

The continuity lock protects current work while preserving history:

```text
Activate at exact pending routes ≥ 500,000
Release at exact pending routes ≤ 450,000
While locked: 70% Head / 30% Continuity / 0% Archive
```

Archive records are deferred, not erased or declared complete. Retired predecessor drain clocks remain preserved as history and must not be automatically restored.

See [DAIL Cursor-Controlled Capacity Mesh](/chlom/dail-cursor-controlled-capacity-mesh).

## Target metaprotocol architecture

CHLOM may evolve into a CrownThrive-owned institutional metaprotocol that carries durable policy meaning across changing vendors, models, repositories, chains, and deployment environments. The target is context-aware and adaptive, not sentient or self-authorizing.

A mature CHLOM control plane can compose:

- canonical identity and stable IDs;
- source and evidence authority and provenance;
- rights and chain-of-title state;
- policy and rule graphs and jurisdiction profiles;
- roles, delegation, and quorum;
- consent, privacy, and data-use constraints;
- licenses, offers, entitlements, and obligations;
- revenue, royalty, commission, and reserve rules;
- remedies, holds, disputes, and restoration;
- AI/ML/LLM agent and tool permissions and evaluation state;
- cryptographic proofs and attestations where justified;
- adapters to conventional and decentralized infrastructure;
- event and decision receipts and DAIL lineage;
- self-checking, bounded self-healing, rollback, and continuity.

The durable contract is meaning and authority; implementations may change beneath it.

## Agent-governed execution

The repository-governance reference implementation uses eligible relay agents, deterministic quorum, independent approval, risk thresholds, specialist endorsements, and D0–D3 authority classes.

That pattern can generalize into CHLOM policy execution, but not every action is votable. Legal terms, ownership and rights grants, securities or token issuance, privileged access, production credential rotation, money movement, and destructive or irreversible actions remain D3 human/reserved authority even if every agent votes yes.

Agents and automation may:

- collect evidence;
- evaluate deterministic policy;
- produce recommendations and candidate artifacts;
- execute bounded D0–D2 actions when independently authorized;
- reconcile provider and system readback;
- identify drift and prepare repairs;
- route unresolved decisions to accountable humans or professionals.

They may not transform documentation, consensus, capacity, or model confidence into authority.

## AI, ML, and LLM governance

CHLOM governs models and agents as fallible, versioned operators rather than autonomous principals. Machine records should resolve:

- model, provider, version, and evaluation profile;
- task purpose and allowed tools or data classes;
- prompt, rule, and policy versions;
- decision and action authority class;
- confidence or uncertainty and supporting evidence where meaningful;
- independent verification requirements;
- security, privacy, and rights constraints;
- rollback and incident state;
- outcome feedback and drift or correction history.

Agents may learn from approved evidence and update bounded operational state when policy allows. They cannot rewrite accepted institutional truth, law, rights, or authority merely because a model predicts a better answer.

## Self-checking and self-healing

CHLOM self-healing is a governed correction loop:

```text
observe
→ detect drift or failure
→ preserve evidence
→ classify risk and authority
→ block unsafe action
→ generate bounded repair
→ rerun the original failed control
→ run institutional and security validation
→ independent verification
→ quorum if eligible
→ apply or escalate
→ append correction and remedy lineage
```

A system has not healed if it merely hid the finding, disabled the test, widened privileges, erased evidence, or fabricated a passing state.

## Security and resilience

Security is a CHLOM rule domain, not a post-build checklist. Applicable security services consume deterministic validators and provider evidence such as code analysis, dependency review, configuration review, credential scanning, runtime telemetry, access logs, and incident readback.

Critical or high findings block automatic promotion until repaired and revalidated or explicitly escalated through the required authority path.

Production credentials, signing material, restricted evidence, private customer/member data, and protected policy internals remain outside public documentation. Credential rotation, custody changes, and privileged-access changes require separate authorization and evidence.

## dS-CaaS and compliance-service model

The [dS-CaaS Governance Layer](/governance/ds-caas) is the target runtime enforcement profile for CHLOM rules. It can expose policy evaluation, eligibility, permission decisions, compliance receipts, holds, appeals, audit evidence, and bounded self-healing as governed services.

This supports a future compliance and governance-as-a-service licensing lane without making CrownThrive responsible for replacing each licensee's lawyers, regulators, auditors, accountants, tax professionals, or other required specialists.

## Evidence, records, and PentaDocs

DAIL preserves material event lineage. PentaDocs reconciles and distributes the human and machine documentation around that lineage.

The source hierarchy for mutable claims is:

1. current provider or runtime readback;
2. canonical DAIL event and terminal receipt;
3. governed database ledger or registry;
4. accepted repository source and exact commit;
5. governed Drive source and phase record;
6. public documentation after provider readback;
7. historical documents as lineage unless reaffirmed.

See [PentaDocs](/pentas/canonical/penta-docs) and [PentaDocs Cross-Surface Documentation Contract](/standards/pentadocs-cross-surface-documentation-contract).

## Blockchain, cryptography, and poly-chain interoperability

Advanced phases may evaluate blockchain and decentralized infrastructure where a validated use case shows measurable advantage. Candidate capabilities include:

- cryptographic provenance and timestamp anchoring;
- DID or verifiable-credential qualification proofs;
- privacy-preserving proofs;
- smart-contract adapters for explicitly authorized settlement or license logic;
- oracles and attestations with source and dispute controls;
- multi-party governance or settlement;
- multi-chain or poly-chain adapters where portability reduces concentration risk;
- recovery, migration, revocation, and chain-exit mechanisms.

CrownThrive should not make a public chain the only copy of institutional truth, private evidence, or personal data. A chain receipt can prove an event, hash, or attestation without making restricted evidence public.

### Token, cryptocurrency, and NFT boundary

CHLOM token, coin, treasury, NFT, crypto-economic, and public-exchange concepts remain research or candidate lanes until actual authorization and provider deployment evidence exists.

Before any live token, cryptocurrency, transferable NFT edition, public sale, or economic instrument is activated, the program requires the applicable legal classification, securities analysis, money-transmission and BSA/AML review, sanctions controls, tax/accounting design, jurisdiction-specific review, custody and key-management threat modeling, smart-contract and bridge security review, consumer disclosures, market-integrity controls, rights and license clarity, and tested incident and recovery procedures.

A metadata package, artwork, content hash, or contract source is not an NFT deployment. An on-chain claim requires an actual chain, contract, token ID, transaction receipt, token URI readback, ownership/provenance evidence, and applicable rights and authority.

## CrownThrive IP, licensing, and developer economy

CHLOM is intended to be built, governed, versioned, and maintained as CrownThrive institutional IP. GitHub may host public-safe documentation, schemas, policy validators, and reference implementations. Private core services, secrets, evidence, proprietary rules, and restricted implementation details remain separately controlled.

Future licensing may include CHLOM APIs, SDKs, MCP surfaces, certified adapters, policy packs, governance services, implementation guides, and enterprise operating packages under explicit agreements. Visibility of source or documentation does not itself grant trademark, patent, copyright, trade-secret, commercial-use, redistribution, or sublicensing rights.

- **Phase 3:** executable institutional core—identity, evidence, rules, rights, approvals, DAIL, services, and bounded agents.
- **Phase 4:** federated ecosystem adapters and shared institutional spine.
- **Phase 5:** validated revenue and market execution.
- **Phase 6:** licensing, IP, and developer economy.
- **Phase 7:** physical, phygital, and regional controls.
- **Phase 8:** Holdings, capital, and portfolio stewardship.
- **Phase 9:** advanced CHLOM, cryptographic, and interoperable or decentralized infrastructure.
- **Phase 10:** generational continuity, sovereign scale, provider independence, regional and global localization, and succession.

No phase is entered by ambition alone. Exit from one phase and entry into the next remain separate evidence gates.

## Governing doctrine

- Evidence is private by default.
- Corrections append; they do not silently erase history.
- Public claims must be supportable.
- Human or professional approval remains mandatory for D3 legal, rights, compliance, security, money-movement, securities, token, credential, and irreversible actions.
- A concept appearing in a historical paper does not make it production-ready.
- Stable identifiers and lineage survive implementation changes.
- GitHub and external providers supply infrastructure and evidence; they do not silently become CrownThrive's sovereign authority.
- Self-healing must repair and revalidate rather than weaken the governing check.
- Global expansion carries jurisdiction-specific profiles; no universal rule silently overrides local law.
- PentaBalancer admission, PentaGas availability, Cursor coordination, model output, or documentation confidence does not create authority.
- Exactly one canonical DAIL writer is preserved unless a future architecture release explicitly proves and authorizes another model.

CHLOM should be useful, auditable, and governable before it becomes decentralized—and remain recoverable, portable, culturally aligned, and human-accountable after it does.
