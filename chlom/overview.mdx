# CHLOM Overview

CHLOM is CrownThrive's governance spine for intellectual property, licensing, attribution, evidence, revenue allocation, corrections and remedies.

Its current operating definition is **Compliance Hybrid Licensing and Ownership Model**.

## Six canonical functions

CHLOM is organized around six functions:

1. **Rights** — who owns or controls an asset and what permissions exist.
2. **Rules** — conditions, restrictions, jurisdictions, windows, caps and policies.
3. **Roles** — actors, authorities, responsibilities and least-privilege permissions.
4. **Revenue** — fees, splits, royalties, commissions, reserves and reconciliation.
5. **Records** — sources, evidence, approvals, events, versions, receipts and corrections.
6. **Remedies** — holds, suspensions, revocations, corrections, disputes, refunds, appeals and restoration.

## Current implementation posture

Phase 2 and Phase 2.5 establish CHLOM as a **cloud-first, API-first, event-driven, multi-tenant, human-governed core**.

Historical CHLOM papers describe a broader decentralized architecture including programmable licenses, decentralized identity, zero-knowledge verification, AI compliance, treasury logic, public licensing exchange, token models and chain-based settlement. Those designs remain target architecture and research. They are not evidence that those systems are live today.

`CT-ADR-GOV-011` adds an executable governance direction without changing that current-state discipline: CrownThrive's own coded agents, validators, specialist gates and reserved human authority form the sovereign automation control plane. GitHub, model providers, cloud vendors, blockchains and other external systems provide tools/evidence; they do not silently become institutional authority.

## Target metaprotocol architecture

CHLOM may evolve into a CrownThrive-owned **institutional metaprotocol** that carries durable policy meaning across changing vendors, models, repositories, chains and deployment environments. The target is context-aware and adaptive, not sentient or self-authorizing.

A mature CHLOM control plane can compose:

- canonical identity and stable IDs;
- source/evidence authority and provenance;
- rights and chain-of-title state;
- policy/rule graphs and jurisdiction profiles;
- roles, delegation and quorum;
- consent/privacy and data-use constraints;
- licenses, offers, entitlements and obligations;
- revenue/royalty/commission rules;
- remedies, holds, disputes and restoration;
- AI/ML/LLM agent/tool permissions and evaluation state;
- cryptographic proofs/attestations where justified;
- adapters to conventional and decentralized infrastructure;
- event/decision receipts and DAIL audit lineage;
- self-checking, bounded self-healing, rollback and continuity.

The durable contract is meaning and authority; implementation may change underneath it.

## Agent-governed execution

The current repository-governance reference implementation uses five eligible relay agents and a deterministic **75% quorum = 4 of 5**, plus mandatory independent Agent D approval, an `85/100` automatic-merge risk threshold, specialist endorsements and D0–D3 authority classes.

That pattern is intended to generalize into CHLOM policy execution, but not every action is votable. Legal terms, ownership/rights grants, securities/token issuance, privileged access, production credential rotation, money movement and destructive/irreversible actions remain D3 human/reserved authority even if every agent votes yes.

## AI / ML / LLM governance

CHLOM's AI layer should govern models and agents as fallible, versioned operators rather than autonomous principals. Machine records should eventually include:

- model/provider/version and evaluation profile;
- task purpose and allowed tools/data classes;
- prompt/rule/policy versions;
- decision/action authority class;
- confidence/uncertainty and evidence references where meaningful;
- independent verification requirements;
- security/privacy/rights constraints;
- rollback and incident state;
- outcome feedback and drift/correction history.

Agents may learn from approved evidence and update bounded operational state when policy allows, but they cannot rewrite accepted institutional truth, law, rights or authority merely because a model predicts a better answer.

## Self-checking and self-healing

CHLOM self-healing is a governed correction loop:

```text
observe
→ detect drift/failure
→ preserve evidence
→ classify risk/authority
→ block unsafe action
→ generate bounded repair
→ run original failed control
→ run institutional + security validation
→ independent verification
→ quorum if eligible
→ apply or escalate
→ append correction/remedy lineage
```

A system has not healed if it merely hid the finding, disabled the test, widened privileges, erased evidence or fabricated a passing state.

## Security and resilience

Security is a CHLOM rule domain, not a post-build checklist. The Security & Resilience Sentinel and future security services consume deterministic validators plus applicable provider evidence such as code analysis, dependency review and secret scanning. Critical/high findings block automatic promotion until repaired/revalidated or escalated.

Production credentials, keys, restricted evidence and protected policy internals remain outside public repositories. Credential rotation, custody changes and privileged-access changes remain separately authorized.

## dS-CaaS and compliance-service model

The [dS-CaaS Governance Layer](/governance/ds-caas) is the target runtime enforcement profile for CHLOM rules. It can expose policy evaluation, eligibility, permission decisions, compliance receipts, holds, appeals, audit evidence and bounded self-healing as governed services.

This supports a future compliance/governance-as-a-service licensing lane without making CrownThrive responsible for replacing each licensee's lawyers, regulators, auditors or other required professionals.

## Blockchain, cryptography and poly-chain interoperability

Phase 9 may evaluate blockchain and decentralized infrastructure where a validated use case shows measurable advantage. Candidate capabilities include:

- cryptographic provenance and timestamp anchoring;
- DID/VC-style identity or qualification proofs;
- privacy-preserving proofs;
- smart-contract adapters for explicitly authorized settlement/license logic;
- oracles/attestations with source and dispute controls;
- multi-party governance/settlement;
- multiple-chain or **poly-chain** adapters where portability reduces concentration risk;
- recovery, migration and revocation mechanisms.

CrownThrive should not make a public chain the only copy of institutional truth, private evidence or personal data. A chain receipt can prove an event/hash/attestation without making restricted evidence public.

### Token / cryptocurrency boundary

CHLOM token, coin, treasury, crypto-economic and public exchange concepts remain **research targets, not activated production claims**. Before any live token/cryptocurrency/public-sale/transferable economic instrument is activated, the program requires qualified legal classification, securities analysis, money-transmission/BSA-AML review, sanctions controls, tax/accounting design, jurisdiction-specific review, custody/key-management threat modeling, smart-contract/bridge security review, consumer disclosures/market-integrity controls and tested incident/recovery procedures.

Until those gates pass, no CrownThrive artifact should call a research token legal tender, a security, a non-security, registered, compliant, production-ready or publicly available merely by design intent.

## Specialist teams

CHLOM governance activates specialist cells as needed:

- legal/regulatory;
- security/privacy;
- operations/SRE;
- blockchain/cryptographic protocol;
- AI/ML/LLM TEVV;
- IP/rights/licensing;
- finance/tax/treasury;
- accessibility/consumer protection;

Agents handle repeatable evidence and policy tasks; reserved professionals/humans handle the decisions their authority requires.

## CrownThrive IP, licensing and developer economy

CHLOM is intended to be built, governed, versioned and maintained as CrownThrive institutional IP. GitHub may host public-safe documentation, schemas, policy validators and reference implementations. Private core services, secrets, evidence, proprietary rules and restricted implementation details remain separately controlled.

Phase 6 may license CHLOM APIs, SDKs, MCP surfaces, certified adapters, policy packs and governance services under explicit agreements. Visibility of source or documentation does not itself grant trademark, patent, copyright, trade-secret, commercial-use, redistribution or sublicensing rights.

## Institutional role

CHLOM does not replace CrownThrive brands. It governs rights and operating relationships around them.

```text
CrownThrive Holdings / stewardship
        ↓
CrownThrive operating ecosystem
        ↓
CHLOM rights + rules + roles + records + remedies
        ↓
dS-CaaS policy execution / CrownThrive IO / APIs / MCP
        ↓
Platforms, products, services, licenses, agents, evidence and revenue events
```

## Roadmap relationship

- **Phase 2 / 2.99:** recover, reconcile, define and machine-govern CHLOM meaning; no premature decentralized activation.
- **Phase 3:** executable institutional core—identity, evidence, rules, rights, approvals, DAIL, services and bounded agents.
- **Phase 4:** federated ecosystem adapters and shared institutional spine.
- **Phase 5:** validated revenue/market execution.
- **Phase 6:** licensing, IP and developer economy.
- **Phase 7:** physical/phygital/regional controls.
- **Phase 8:** Holdings/capital/portfolio stewardship.
- **Phase 9:** advanced CHLOM, cryptographic and interoperable/decentralized infrastructure.
- **Phase 10:** generational continuity, sovereign scale, provider independence, regional/global localization and succession.

No phase is entered by ambition alone. Hard exit of the current phase and hard entry of the next remain separate requirements.

## Governing doctrine

- Evidence is private by default.
- Corrections append; they do not silently erase history.
- Public claims must be supportable.
- Human approval remains mandatory for D3 legal, rights, compliance, security, money movement, securities/token issuance and irreversible actions.
- A concept appearing in a historical paper does not make it production-ready.
- Stable identifiers and lineage survive implementation changes.
- GitHub/provider features are evidence and infrastructure, not CrownThrive's institutional sovereign authority.
- Self-healing must repair and revalidate, never weaken the governing check.
- Global expansion carries jurisdiction-specific policy profiles; no universal rule silently overrides local law.

CHLOM should be useful, auditable and governable before it becomes decentralized—and remain recoverable, portable and human-accountable after it does.
