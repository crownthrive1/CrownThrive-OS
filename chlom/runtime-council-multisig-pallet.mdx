## Vote/signature semantics

Historical chain designs may bind votes to cryptographic signatures. Current CHLOM should separate:

```text
institutional vote/approval
       |
       +--> actor identity
       +--> authority/delegation
       +--> decision intent
       +--> cryptographic signature evidence
```

A cryptographic signature validates control of a key; it does not independently establish the signer's institutional authority.

## Quorum and threshold math

Let:

```text
E = eligible non-recused decision weight
P = participating decision weight
Y = approving weight
```

Then a rule may specify:

```text
quorum_ratio = P / E
approval_ratio = Y / P
```

Actual thresholds are versioned policy parameters. Historical examples must not be silently adopted.

## Conflict and recusal

Before counting an approval:

```text
member active?
authority valid?
conflict disclosed/resolved?
recusal required?
proposal version current?
```

Recused members should be handled according to the adopted quorum rule; no universal denominator adjustment is invented here.

## Emergency actions

Historical sources contemplated fast council intervention. The current model requires emergency authority to be:

- explicitly pre-authorized;
- narrow in object/action scope;
- time-boxed;
- independently evidenced;
- reversible or compensable where possible;
- automatically reviewed/sunset;
- recorded in DAIL.

```text
EMERGENCY CONDITION
       |
       v
PRE-AUTHORIZED EMERGENCY PROFILE?
       |
       +-- no --> HOLD / escalate
       |
       v
THRESHOLD APPROVAL
       |
       v
TEMPORARY ACTION
       |
AUTO-SUNSET / REVIEW
```

## Timelocks

Approved high-impact actions may still require a timelock. At execution time, the system must revalidate:

- proposal still current;
- approvals not revoked/expired where policy requires;
- target object unchanged or compatible;
- no superseding hold;
- execution authority current.

## Multisig execution

A provider/native multisig can execute a bounded instruction after CHLOM authorization. It is an execution adapter, not the canonical policy engine.

```text
CHLOM APPROVAL
   -> execution envelope
   -> multisig/provider signatures
   -> provider execution
   -> readback
   -> DAIL
```

## Failure modes

### Insufficient quorum

`HOLD_QUORUM_NOT_MET`

### Threshold not met

`DENY_NOT_APPROVED` or remain open according to rule.

### Member authority expired

Vote excluded or proposal held according to policy.

### Conflict discovered after vote

Recalculate validity under adopted rules; preserve original receipt and issue correction.

### Key compromised

Suspend key/member signing ability, preserve prior decisions, rotate/revoke key and reevaluate pending actions.

### Target changed during timelock

HOLD and require re-approval if semantic target/version changed.

## Threat model

- compromised signer;
- collusion;
- hidden member substitution;
- stale authority snapshot;
- quorum gaming;
- conflict concealment;
- emergency-power permanence;
- vote replay across proposal versions;
- provider multisig state diverging from institutional membership;
- self-approval of authority expansion.

## Test vectors

1. threshold \+ quorum satisfied -\> approved;
2. threshold satisfied but quorum not met -\> HOLD;
3. recused member attempts vote -\> rejected/excluded;
4. expired membership -\> vote invalid;
5. proposal body changes -\> old signatures not reusable;
6. compromised key revoked -\> pending execution held;
7. emergency action expires -\> automatic sunset/review;
8. provider multisig execution fails -\> canonical approval remains but action unsettled;
9. membership/key rotation -\> stable institutional identity retained;
10. authority expansion proposed by same body -\> requires separately authorized constitutional/reserved path.
