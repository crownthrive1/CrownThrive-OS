## Recovered architectural idea

The term **Dual DAO** indicates a governance architecture with two distinct decision bodies or decision channels whose interaction is meant to reduce unilateral governance capture and create checks between constituencies or authority classes.

The exact historical chamber composition is not established by the currently located source body. The safe architectural abstraction is:

```text
PROPOSAL
   |
   +--> GOVERNANCE BODY / CHAMBER A
   |
   +--> GOVERNANCE BODY / CHAMBER B
   |
   v
CROSS-BODY DECISION RULE
   |
   +--> APPROVED CANDIDATE
   +--> HOLD / CONFLICT
   +--> REJECTED
   |
   v
TIMELock / RESERVED AUTHORITY / EXECUTION GATE
```

## Why bicameral governance matters

A dual-body design can be used to separate different governance interests—for example technical review from stakeholder approval, or broad community sentiment from constrained institutional authority. CHLOM's broader governance lineage repeatedly distinguishes algorithmic voting from reserved human/corporate authority.

The institutional rule remains:

```text
DAO vote != legal/corporate authority unless explicitly granted
```

## Membership and eligibility

Each body would require its own explicit eligibility and conflict rules. A user holding membership in both bodies cannot automatically bypass independence requirements. Technical expertise, stakeholder status, token holdings, platform role or founder status are distinct dimensions and must not be conflated.

## Historical token-governance boundary

Historical CHLOM governance/token research includes CHM/governance-token ideas, staking and voting thresholds. Those materials are preserved separately and are not current governance parameters. A future Dual DAO could theoretically use token-weighted mechanisms, but that would require explicit legal/economic/technical activation and is not assumed here.

## Founder/reserved authority

Current CrownThrive governance recognizes founder/reserved-authority classes for specified high-consequence actions. A Dual DAO cannot self-amend away reserved authority merely because both chambers vote to do so unless the governing legal/institutional framework explicitly allows it.

## Proposal lifecycle

A current-safe lifecycle would include:

```text
DRAFT
 -> VALIDATED
 -> BODY_A_REVIEW
 -> BODY_B_REVIEW
 -> CROSS_BODY_RECONCILIATION
 -> APPROVED_CANDIDATE / HOLD / REJECTED
 -> TIMELock
 -> AUTHORITY_REVALIDATION
 -> ENACTED / EXPIRED / REVOKED
```

## Conflict handling

If the bodies disagree and no adopted cross-body rule resolves the disagreement, the result is HOLD/NOT APPROVED rather than an AI-generated compromise that creates authority.

## Evidence and transparency

Each vote/review/decision should record participant eligibility snapshot, weights if any, recusals, quorum calculation, decision result, source authority and DAIL references. Public views can expose safe governance history without revealing restricted participant data.

## Threat model

Research/design risks include:

- capture of one or both bodies;
- Sybil or delegated-vote concentration;
- voter conflict of interest;
- ambiguous chamber precedence;
- double-counting the same authority in both bodies;
- token/economic concentration;
- rushed emergency bypass;
- governance deadlock;
- self-amendment beyond legal authority;
- stale eligibility snapshots.

## Verification targets
