## Source boundary

This article preserves that research family without claiming that a particular cryptocurrency rail, token, exchange, wallet or payment architecture is currently live.

## Research problem

CHLOM historically explored how a licensing/compliance system could support multiple economic rails while preserving rights, attribution, treasury, fees, settlements and auditability.

The core research question is:

```text
How can one governed economic obligation be executed through different payment rails
without allowing the rail to become the source of rights, authority or accounting truth?
```

That question remains relevant even when current implementation is cloud-first and primarily uses conventional payment providers.

## Historical fiat rail concerns

A fiat payment path can involve:

- card/ACH/bank or provider authorization/capture;
- invoicing and receivables;
- currency/region constraints;
- processor/provider fees;
- refunds, disputes and reversals;
- payout timing;
- tax/withholding evidence;
- settlement reconciliation;
- provider-specific account/customer aliases.

The institutional record must separate expected CHLOM obligation from provider execution.

## Rights-before-payment invariant

```text
payment cannot manufacture rights
```

A payer cannot acquire a license scope the licensor does not control. A payment success cannot override DLA revocation, missing issuer authority, licensee ineligibility or policy DENY.

## Rail neutrality

Canonical CHLOM transaction, agreement, entitlement, obligation, royalty and settlement IDs should be independent of the payment rail. Provider/transaction hashes, payment intent IDs, bank references or future chain transaction hashes attach as aliases/evidence.

## Research risks

- rail/provider becomes mistaken for canonical accounting;
- volatility/conversion mismatch;
- duplicate execution across chains/providers;
- chargeback/refund asymmetry;
- irreversible external transfer versus reversible institutional remedy;
- lost/compromised keys;
- bridge/oracle compromise;
- jurisdictional/regulatory exposure;
- inaccurate public claims about token or yield activation;
- inconsistent settlement across fiat and crypto rails.

## Test/simulation families

A future rail-neutral simulator should test:

1. same obligation settled through two different approved rail adapters;
2. duplicate provider/chain event;
3. price/conversion rate staleness;
4. refund after allocation;
5. provider fee difference;
6. token/fiat unit mismatch;
7. chain/provider outage;
8. reorg/reversal where applicable;
9. wallet/provider migration;
10. rights revoked before external transfer finalizes;
11. failed reconciliation;
12. provider success with institutional entitlement HOLD.
