## Research problem

The research family addresses how disputes about rights, licenses, transactions, evidence, obligations or governance could be resolved through a structured, evidence-backed and potentially distributed process while preserving party identity/notice, neutral qualified review, evidence custody, interim holds, applicable rule/agreement versions, reasoned outcomes, remedies, appeals and bounded enforcement authority.

## Smart versus decentralized

**Smart Arbitration** can describe machine-assisted or rule-assisted dispute workflows: evidence organization, deadline scheduling, issue classification, policy/contract retrieval, calculations and execution orchestration.

**Decentralized Arbitration** suggests distributing review/decision responsibility across multiple independent participants or governance mechanisms.

Neither label grants an AI, smart contract or validator legal/adjudicative authority without a valid agreement and applicable authority.

## Reviewer / arbitrator assignment

A reviewer/arbitrator must satisfy domain qualification, authority, independence and conflict policy. Assignment may consider skill/load only after hard eligibility constraints. Self-assignment or party-controlled selection that violates independence routes to HOLD.

## AI assistance boundary

CHLOM AI/ML may help with evidence retrieval/organization, timeline extraction, contract lookup, issue classification, inconsistency detection, governed calculations/simulation and draft reason codes. AI does not obtain adjudicative authority by confidence or capability.

## Interim safeguards

Cases may trigger temporary holds or scoped suspensions only when the governing policy allows them. Interim measures should be evidence-backed, reversible where possible, scope/TTL bounded and separately authorized from final disposition.

## Remedy separation

Decision, remedy authorization, external/provider execution and reconciliation are separate. Refund, reinstatement, suspension, DLA amendment, payout adjustment or access changes each require their own execution receipt and DAIL lineage.

## Appeals and corrections

Appeals do not delete the original decision. A later outcome supersedes, remands or affirms according to the governing rules. Factual/procedural corrections append under DAIL/WF-003.

## Legal boundary

This documentation does not create a binding arbitration agreement, legal venue, waiver, arbitrator appointment or enforceable award. Those require actual legal terms and applicable authority. A software review must not be marketed as independent legal arbitration unless that is actually established.

## Future decentralization questions

A future distributed arbitration design would need explicit source-grounded/adopted answers for eligibility, conflict rules, selection, quorum, evidence privacy, incentives/bonds if any, collusion/Sybil resistance, legal enforceability, finality/appeal, emergency authority, jurisdiction and retention/disclosure.

## Threat model

Bias/conflict, bribery/collusion, evidence tampering, party impersonation, hidden private evidence, algorithmic overreach, deadline manipulation, retaliatory holds, false finality, unenforceable outcomes, reviewer credential expiry and cross-tenant leakage.

## Tests

Test conflicted reviewer, insufficient quorum, missing/late evidence, interim hold, appeal/remand, reversed remedy, AI recommendation rejected by authority, confidential evidence, cross-jurisdiction HOLD and provider execution mismatch.
