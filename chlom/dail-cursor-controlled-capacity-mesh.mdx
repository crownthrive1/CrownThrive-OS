## Executive contract

CrownThrive DAIL is one append-only institutional evidence chain. The current production architecture scales execution capacity around that chain without creating competing ledgers or independent writers.

```text
One canonical CHLOM DAIL chain
        ↓
Machine / Human / Hybrid / Cursor projections
        ↓
DAIL Cursor coordination
        ↓
500K Continuity Lock
        ↓
Head / Continuity / Archive route selection
        ↓
PentaBalancer admission + PentaGas budgeting
        ↓
MeshAssist and approved bounded capacity donors
        ↓
One canonical writer + terminal evidence readback
```

The architecture separates **truth**, **coordination**, **capacity**, and **authority**:

- **DAIL** preserves canonical event identity and append-only institutional history.
- **Cursor** coordinates bounded projection, routing, deferred work, verification, high-water marks, and terminal receipts.
- **PentaBalancer** admits, constrains, staggers, or defers execution according to system pressure.
- **PentaGas** quotes and settles internal execution capacity. It does not grant legal, financial, licensing, publication, or archive-bypass authority.
- **CHLOM and the applicable policy/approval trace** remain the authority layer.
- **MeshAssist and capacity donors** contribute bounded execution capacity; they do not become independent writers or institutional principals.

## One canonical chain, four operating projections

The Four-Part Cursor model uses one source event identity with four bounded views:

| Projection | Purpose | Non-authority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Machine | Automated runtime, provider, scheduler, test, and execution evidence | Does not manufacture approval, ownership, or provider truth. |
| Human | Explicit Founder, reviewer, professional, or governance evidence | Does not erase or rewrite machine history. |
| Hybrid | Handoffs, reconciliations, cross-system decisions, and machine-human transitions | Does not silently elevate either participant's authority. |
| Cursor | Input binding, rail positions, route/deferred processing, verification, and terminal receipts | Is a coordinator and projection layer, not another ledger. |

Rail membership does not replace the canonical event ID, sequence, payload hash, predecessor hash, or event hash.

## Current scheduler topology

The governing runtime is centered on the Cursor and supporting bounded controls:

| Job | Cadence | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `ct-dail-cursor-control-v1` | Every minute | Canonical Cursor coordinator through PentaBalancer. |
| `ct-dail-continuity-exact-reconcile-v1` | Every five minutes | Exact pending count, lock hysteresis, route-fence state, and node-load reconciliation. |
| `ct-dail-cursor-mesh-assist-v1` | Every 30 seconds | Elastic subordinate route assistance when PentaBalancer admits it. |
| `ct-dail-oracle-capacity-mesh-v1` | Every 20 seconds | Bounded capacity-mesh admission and donor coordination. |
| `ct-dail-route-backlog-snapshot-v1` | Every five minutes | Bounded backlog observation and recovery-profile evidence. |

The following predecessor clocks remain retired and must not be automatically restored:

- `chlom-dail-compact-catchup-v4`
- `ct-dail-serial-lane-drain-v4`

Their source and historical evidence remain preserved for lineage and rollback analysis. Retirement does not authorize deletion of historical DAIL events or queued work.

## 500K Continuity Lock

The continuity policy protects current institutional work while preserving deeper history.

### Bands

| Band | Definition | Allocation while locked |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Head | Newest 50,000 blocks and current routed work | 70% |
| Continuity | Remaining admissible work inside the newest 500,000 | 30% |
| Archive | Overflow older than the active continuity fence | 0% |

Archive work is deferred, not deleted, skipped, or declared complete.

### Hysteresis

```text
Activate lock when exact pending routes ≥ 500,000
Release lock when exact pending routes ≤ 450,000
Otherwise preserve the current lock state
```

The 50,000-record hysteresis band prevents repeated lock/unlock oscillation around one threshold.

### Two distinct 70/30 rules

The system may contain two separate 70/30 policies. They must not be conflated:

1. **Continuity selection:** 70% Head / 30% Continuity / 0% Archive while locked.
2. **Donor capacity allocation:** an approved donor may make 70% of its discretionary capacity available to Cursor while retaining 30% for its own governed public or staging responsibilities.

The first governs which routes are selected. The second governs how much donor compute is available.

## MeshAssist and capacity donors

MeshAssist is subordinate to Cursor and PentaBalancer. An approved donor contributes bounded execution capacity through a signed, replay-protected manifest or bridge. The private canonical DAIL fabric retains final write authority.

A compliant donor path requires:

- explicit donor identity and operating alias;
- approved capacity allocation and hard ceilings;
- signed request/response or equivalent authenticated bridge evidence;
- replay protection and correlation IDs;
- PentaBalancer admission;
- PentaGas quote, reservation, and settlement where applicable;
- exact route IDs and bounded work scope;
- terminal readback against the canonical DAIL event;
- no direct cross-project database mutation;
- no second DAIL chain or writer;
- no rights, licensing, payment, token, or governance authority created by capacity availability.

Deferrals are valid operating outcomes. A MeshAssist or donor attempt may be deferred by pressure, session use, lease state, route eligibility, or a busy canonical writer without being misclassified as a failed institutional completion.

## PentaGas boundary

PentaGas is internal execution accounting. It can describe expected and actual capacity use by node, depth, pressure, or risk. It cannot authorize:

- archive-fence bypass;
- legal or licensing decisions;
- payment, settlement, treasury, or token issuance;
- credential access;
- publication or storefront activation;
- D3 or human-reserved decisions;
- a second canonical writer.

Archive bypass, where ever permitted, requires an eligible operation class plus a concrete authority trace. Available gas alone is never authority.

## Reliability substrate

The current architecture inherits the reliability controls established before Cursor consolidation:

- idempotent append-or-queue submission;
- collision detection for reused idempotency keys;
- persistent retry accounting;
- retry backoff and active-lease protection;
- cross-partition deferred-event eligibility;
- truthful rollback counters;
- explicit dead-letter state;
- canonical event/hash readback;
- fixed-window arrivals and deliveries;
- preservation of historical queue and event rows during rollback.

Those controls are the substrate. The Cursor-Controlled Capacity Mesh is the governing scheduler and coordination architecture above them.

## Hard ceilings and bounded execution

Hard ceilings are architecture contracts, not utilization targets. The current design preserves bounded serial work under one canonical writer, including ceilings for virtual slices, rail projection, route delivery, deferred events, and verification. Any ceiling change requires a separately governed architecture release, canary, pressure analysis, and terminal readback.

Under pressure, the system constrains or defers work. It must not manufacture a partial institutional PASS.

## Operating acceptance criteria

DAIL may be treated as normal production infrastructure when the following remain true through a meaningful operating window:

- exactly one canonical writer;
- no chain-link, payload-hash, or event-hash errors;
- no retired drain clock reactivation;
- no scheduler/controller mismatch;
- no persistent lock waiter or idle-in-transaction buildup;
- no false terminal failure caused only by DAIL contention;
- current Head latency remains healthy;
- Continuity progresses while the lock is active;
- Archive remains preserved and fenced;
- verification and rail lag are bounded and recovering;
- producer amplification remains below sustainable delivery capacity;
- donor throughput exceeds donor-generated evidence overhead;
- all material completion claims include an observation boundary.

A successful route batch does not prove backlog completion. A falling short-window count does not prove sustained convergence. Acceptance requires exact counts and trend evidence over a defined period.

## Change control and rollback

A DAIL architecture change must:

1. Preserve exact predecessor source, scheduler state, policy state, and restoration instructions.
2. Declare whether it changes truth, coordination, capacity, authority, or only observability.
3. Prove that no second canonical writer or ledger was introduced.
4. Run bounded canaries and exact readback.
5. Update PentaDocs, CHLOM release tracking, DAIL phase ledgers, master IP/asset records where applicable, and the public-safe repository source.
6. Record deployment state separately from documentation state.
7. Append correction or supersession lineage rather than silently replacing history.

Rollback must not reset canonical DAIL events, queued routes, sequence history, or evidence hashes. Restore one coherent controller generation at a time.
