# SCHEMA-002 — Dynamic Licensing Asset Record

## Schema identity

```yaml
schema_id: SCHEMA-002
name: Dynamic Licensing Asset Record
classification: reconstructed_machine_contract
version: 1.0.0
related_algorithm: ALG-003; ALG-004; ALG-005
```

## Purpose

SCHEMA-002 defines the canonical CHLOM record for a **Dynamic Licensing Asset (DLA)**. It separates the stable institutional license/permission identity from provider contracts, storefront listing IDs, payment records or historical Decentralized Licensing Authority terminology.

## Rights-source requirement

A DLA must bind to source rights or a valid parent chain. `source_right_refs` cannot be replaced by a marketplace listing, payment receipt or administrative assertion. Child/sublicensed rights must satisfy FORM-009 and ALG-004/005.

## Scope representation

Rights are multidimensional. Media/channel, territory, term, exclusivity, derivative rights, transferability, sublicensing and quantitative limits are explicit fields or structured subobjects. Unknown scope is not represented as global/perpetual/unlimited.

## Issuer and licensee

Issuer and licensee use canonical CHLOM identity IDs. Provider/customer IDs can appear only in aliases. Issuer identity and issuer authority are separate; activation requires current authority over the exact rights being granted.

## Lifecycle

Status is event-backed. `ACTIVE` is not a permanent Boolean: revocation, suspension, expiry, amendment and supersession create DAIL/lifecycle events. Historical versions remain readable.

## Policy and credentials

A DLA references the exact Compliance Pack and credential requirement versions applicable to activation/use. When currentness matters, downstream validity evaluation rechecks evidence rather than trusting an old activation snapshot indefinitely.

## Economic schedule

Commercial terms reference a versioned economic schedule. Payment evidence and settlement records are separate objects. A provider charge or checkout state cannot directly mutate the DLA into `ACTIVE` without the governing transition.

## Provider aliases

Examples can include external contract IDs, marketplace offer IDs or payment metadata. Aliases aid reconciliation but never become the canonical DLA key.

## Validation rules

- stable DLA/version/tenant IDs;
- asset/source-right references resolve;
- issuer/licensee identities resolve;
- child scope does not exceed parents/source;
- terms and territories are syntactically/semantically valid;
- policy/approval/evidence requirements resolve;
- status transitions follow lifecycle rules;
- no secret/confidential body is embedded in public projection.

## Tests

Test direct source grant, parent/child license, attempted overscope, expired term, explicit unlimited cap, unknown cap, issuer authority revoked, economic schedule changed, provider alias changed and superseded version.

## Versioning

DLA versions are immutable snapshots plus lifecycle events. Material scope/party/economic changes create a new version or explicit amendment object under adopted policy.
