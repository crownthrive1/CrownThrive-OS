## ACE, AIE and ADE — Engine Lineage and Current Mapping

CHLOM's AI/compliance/decision architecture evolved across multiple source eras. ACE, AIE and ADE must therefore be interpreted by **source + effective period**, not by acronym alone.

## ACE — Adaptive Compliance Engine

The strongest recovered historical expansion is **Adaptive Compliance Engine**. Historical responsibilities include policy evaluation, pre-transaction compliance checks, consuming risk intelligence, anomaly response, quarantine/pause triggers, contract screening, reporting and coordination with identity, evidence, DLA and LEX.

### Current mapping

ACE maps to the modern policy/risk evaluation family. Missing authority must fail closed; unresolved rights cannot promote; AI confidence cannot override a hard block; consequential action inherits D0–D3 authority; and decision evidence is preserved in DAIL.

## AIE — AI Intelligence Engine

The **CHLOM Genesis Volume** explicitly defines **AIE = AI Intelligence Engine**.

Historical responsibilities include risk scoring, fraud-pattern recognition, anomaly detection, predictive analysis, regulatory/news text analysis, privacy-aware learning concepts, model updates and policy-simulation support.

AIE historically feeds ACE: AIE produces intelligence; ACE applies that intelligence inside compliance evaluation.

## AIE — Anomaly Intelligence Engine

Later CrownThrive/CHLOM documentation reuses **AIE = Anomaly Intelligence Engine** for a narrower monitoring/anomaly capability.

```yaml
historical_expansion: AI Intelligence Engine
later_expansion: Anomaly Intelligence Engine
relationship: unresolved_reuse
founder_adjudication: available
```

A future founder decision may retain both with namespaced IDs, rename one family, designate predecessor/successor semantics, or make Anomaly Intelligence a child of the broader AI Intelligence family.

## ADE — Autonomous Decision Engine

The Genesis Volume explicitly uses **ADE = Autonomous Decision Engine**.

Historical role: act on eligible outcomes after rules, intelligence, governance and authority inputs are satisfied — for example applying an approved policy change, suspending a license after a valid trigger, or executing a bounded enforcement action.

### Modern authority correction

Current CHLOM does **not** interpret Autonomous Decision Engine as unrestricted autonomy. Execution remains inside accepted authority classes, approval requirements, separation of duties, human-reserved D3 boundaries, evidence freshness, rollback/compensation requirements and independent verification where required.

## ADE — Attribution & Distribution Engine

Later documentation reuses **ADE = Attribution & Distribution Engine** for revenue attribution, split calculations, obligations and distribution/settlement planning.

Modern mapping includes revenue allocation, royalty/commission calculation, attribution records, split statements, settlement plans, reversals/corrections and impact/ThriveFund obligations where applicable.

Provider payment success does not by itself make a distribution institutionally valid; current economic authority must reconcile the action.

```yaml
historical_expansion: Autonomous Decision Engine
later_expansion: Attribution & Distribution Engine
relationship: unresolved_reuse
founder_adjudication: available
```

## Historical engine interaction

```text
external/on-chain data
        ↓
AIE — intelligence / risk / prediction
        ↓
ACE — rules + compliance evaluation
        ↓
DAL/DAIL — evidence / attestation / event lineage
        ↓
ADE — eligible action / distribution depending on source era
        ↓
DLA / LEX / Treasury / governance state
```

## Modern service decomposition

Modern CHLOM decomposes these families into Policy/Rules Service, Decision Gateway, Anomaly/Monitoring Service, Evidence/DAIL Service, Approval/Authority Service, Rights/DLA Service, Revenue Allocation Service, Cases/Remedies Service, External Evidence/Oracle Gateway and governed AI tools.

## AI governance records

Every consequential model/agent should eventually carry model/provider/version, purpose, allowed data, rule/policy version, tools, autonomy/decision ceiling, evaluation profile, limitations, security/privacy constraints, drift status, rollback/fallback, evidence references and a final-authority flag.

For consequential CHLOM use, `ai_final_authority` remains false unless a separately adopted rule defines a narrow eligible automated decision.
