## Rights Ledger and Evidence Model

CHLOM separates **the claim**, **the evidence supporting the claim**, and **the permission to expose that evidence**.

## Rights Ledger

A governed rights record should identify, where applicable:

- stable asset ID
- title / canonical name
- owner or controlling party
- contributor or creator roles
- source of authority
- rights categories
- territories
- channels
- term
- exclusivity
- restrictions
- derivative permissions
- commercial-use status
- institutional-use status
- public-display status
- AI-training status
- AI-voice status
- remix / stem / sampling status
- adaptation status
- governing agreement reference
- reviewer
- effective date
- superseded record
- correction history

## Evidence references

Evidence is private by default.

A public record may say that supporting evidence exists without reproducing restricted source material. Evidence references should preserve enough metadata to audit the decision:

- evidence ID
- source type
- source title
- classification
- custodian
- received or created date
- hash or integrity marker where appropriate
- governing claim
- review state
- access policy

## Provenance for AI-assisted creative work

For AI-assisted music and media, the rights ledger may include:

- generator and model/version
- generation date
- account/subscription tier at generation
- human-authored lyrics
- human arrangement/editing
- human production contribution
- source audio
- voice provenance
- synthetic vocal status
- similarity-review status
- distributor
- DSP disclosure
- copyright-claim status
- PRO status
- ISRC
- master/stem archive reference

The existence of AI assistance does not, by itself, resolve copyright, contract, publicity, voice, or platform questions.

## Correction doctrine

A correction must preserve lineage.

Do not silently replace:

```text
old attribution → new attribution
```

Instead record:

```text
original record
→ correction event
→ corrected record
→ affected downstream objects
→ remediation status
```

This allows CrownThrive to prove what was known, when it was known, what changed, and how affected products or licenses were reconciled.
