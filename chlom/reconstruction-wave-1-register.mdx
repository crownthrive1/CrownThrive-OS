# CHLOM Reconstruction Wave 1 Register

Wave 1 converts the first recovered CHLOM source corpus into durable Mintlify articles while establishing the continuity and founder-adjudication rules that later waves will inherit.

## Program objective

The full recovery target remains the **795-title Help Center census**. A recovered title alone is not considered a recovered body. Each title moves through source discovery, reconstruction, conflict marking, adjudication where needed, documentation QA, and continuity registration.

Wave 1 focuses on the foundational CHLOM paper and terminology layer because later articles depend on these definitions.

## Continuity contract for later waves

Each later wave must:

1. identify the target article/title set;
2. find authoritative source bodies;
3. preserve source wording/meaning before modernization;
4. classify historical versus current claims;
5. mark contradictions rather than normalize them silently;
6. write a substantive body, not a placeholder;
7. add founder-adjudication status where material;
8. link related source and current-state pages;
9. update the wave register and changelog;
10. retain superseded/history records instead of deleting them.

## Full-program accounting

The 795-title census remains the denominator. The reconstruction program should track at minimum:

```yaml
recovered_titles_total: 795
body_reconstruction_required: tracked_per_article
source_grounded_bodies: increment_each_wave
conflicted_bodies: explicit
founder_adjudication_pending: explicit
restricted_only: explicit
```

A future title/body count should only increase when the article body has actually been reconstructed or recovered, not when a title merely appears in navigation.

## Founder review mode

The Founder may review these pages after publication and issue decisions in plain language. A founder decision is then converted into a dated adjudication/correction record and propagated through all affected pages and machine records.

This allows the recovery to move at speed without sacrificing institutional truth.
