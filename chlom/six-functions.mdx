# Rights, Rules, Roles, Revenue, Records, Remedies

Every CHLOM object, workflow, and decision should map to one or more of six canonical functions.

## Rights

Rights answer: **Who controls what?**

Typical records include:

- asset ownership or control
- license grants
- territories and channels
- duration and renewal
- derivative permissions
- remix, sampling, stem, voice, character, adaptation, merchandise, and AI-use permissions
- exclusivity or non-exclusivity
- assignment and transfer restrictions

A public product page is not itself proof of a rights grant. The governing agreement or approved rights record controls.

## Rules

Rules answer: **Under what conditions may the right be exercised?**

Examples include:

- audience or seat limits
- unit caps
- geography
- term
- approved media
- prohibited uses
- attribution requirements
- refund or revocation conditions
- age, privacy, safety, or platform restrictions

## Roles

Roles answer: **Who may decide, approve, execute, inspect, or correct?**

CHLOM separates authority from technical capability. An automated tool may be able to perform an action without being authorized to decide that the action should occur.

Representative roles include founder/governing owner, licensing steward, rights reviewer, product owner, finance reviewer, security reviewer, publisher, operator, support staff, specialist agent, and auditor.

## Revenue

Revenue answers: **What economic obligations result from the transaction?**

Records may include:

- purchase price
- licensing fee
- platform fee
- royalty split
- commission
- reserve
- refund
- chargeback
- payout state
- reconciliation state

Revenue logic must remain distinguishable from legal rights. Payment proves a payment event; it does not automatically define the complete legal permission.

## Records

Records answer: **What happened, what source supports it, who approved it, and what changed?**

CHLOM records should preserve:

- source
- actor
- timestamp
- object version
- approval
- evidence reference
- event
- correction history
- public/private classification

## Remedies

Remedies answer: **What happens when something is wrong, disputed, expired, revoked, misattributed, or noncompliant?**

Remedies include:

- hold
- suspension
- revocation
- correction
- refund
- dispute
- appeal
- reinstatement
- replacement delivery
- public correction
- downstream reconciliation

## Design rule

A complete CHLOM workflow should be explainable without relying on implementation-specific technology.

The six functions are the business meaning. APIs, databases, smart contracts, wallets, or future chain modules are implementation layers beneath that meaning.
