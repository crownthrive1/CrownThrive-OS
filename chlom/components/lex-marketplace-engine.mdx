## Purpose

The LEX Marketplace Engine is the provider-neutral application/service component that orchestrates marketplace behavior around CHLOM licensing objects. It is distinct from the broader LEX architecture, the historical LEX runtime pallet, a storefront/provider implementation, the DLA itself and a payment provider.

Its job is to project eligible rights-bearing offers into searchable marketplace experiences and coordinate transaction state without allowing marketplace activity to manufacture rights.

## Core invariant

```text
marketplace listing != rights
marketplace purchase != DLA validity
provider payment != entitlement
```

Every marketplace effect resolves back to canonical asset, source-right, DLA, policy, agreement and economic records.

## Major capabilities

### Listing orchestration

Create/update governed marketplace projections only after listing eligibility passes. A listing binds to exact asset/DLA version, offered scope, licensor authority, economic schedule, policy state and effective window.

### Discovery and filtering

Project approved listing metadata into searchable indexes by asset/category, right/use type, territory, term, channel, economic form, licensor/brand, availability and other safe dimensions. Search indexes are rebuildable projections and cannot broaden rights or disclosure.

### Offer and acquisition modes

Recovered LEX lineage includes bidding, direct purchase/licensing, lease/rental, transfer and sublicensing concepts. Each is represented as an explicit offer/transaction type rather than one generic checkout mutation.

### Eligibility and transaction state

Buyer/licensee identity, credentials, jurisdiction and policy are checked before agreement/fulfillment. Transaction progression follows the governed LEX state-machine rather than provider checkout state alone.

### Agreement and entitlement handoff

When exact terms are accepted and required conditions reconcile, the engine hands the result to the Agreement & Entitlement Layer and DLA lifecycle. A marketplace order ID never becomes the canonical license record.

### Economic handoff

Prices, fees, royalties and commissions reference versioned economic schedules. Payment/escrow events are normalized, idempotently processed and reconciled before creating final institutional economic state.

### Remedies

Refunds, disputes, rights complaints, transaction holds, seller/licensor suspension and listing takedowns route into cases/remedies. The current projection can be disabled without deleting historical listing/transaction evidence.

## Listing states

`DRAFT`, `ELIGIBILITY_REVIEW`, `ACTIVE`, `HOLD`, `SUSPENDED`, `ALLOCATED`, `EXPIRED`, `REVOKED`, `SUPERSEDED`.

A listing can remain visible as historical evidence while no longer being transactable.

## Search/discovery boundary

Discovery data is a projection. Exact contract terms, restricted masters, confidential pricing, personal identity and private deal data remain protected. Public search exposes only fields approved by listing/disclosure policy.

## Provider adapters

Marketplace UI, payment rail, search engine, object store, email system and external commerce providers are replaceable. Adapters translate provider events/IDs into canonical LEX objects and DAIL evidence.

## Abuse and security controls

- reject listing without valid source-right/DLA chain;
- prevent seller spoofing and cross-tenant listing;
- enforce scope/territory/term boundaries;
- protect unpublished/restricted assets;
- prevent duplicate transaction effects through idempotency;
- revalidate state at execution/fulfillment;
- hold stale listings after rights/policy change;
- preserve audit/correction history;
- apply rate/abuse controls to discovery and acquisition endpoints.

## Metrics

Operational metrics can include active listings, eligible-to-held transitions, search-to-offer conversion, transaction completion, dispute/refund rate, provider failure and settlement mismatch. Metrics are analytics/evidence, not rights truth or certification.

## Tests

Test valid listing, source-right revocation after listing, ineligible buyer, overscope offer, duplicate payment event, private listing disclosure, stale index after revocation, provider replacement, refund/dispute and cross-tenant seller spoofing.
