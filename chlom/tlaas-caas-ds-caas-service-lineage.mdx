# TLaaS, CaaS and dS-CaaS — Service Lineage

CHLOM's service-layer terminology evolved alongside its protocol architecture. Several labels describe related ideas—exposing licensing, trust and compliance capabilities as reusable services—but they are not automatically synonyms.

## TLaaS — Tokenized Licensing as a Service

The Technical Blueprint / Building & Understanding source family uses **TLaaS = Tokenized Licensing as a Service**. The concept packages tokenized or programmable licensing functions so external applications can issue/manage licenses without rebuilding the entire CHLOM stack.

Historical capabilities include license templates, issuance, renewal/revocation, transfer restrictions, payment/royalty rules, identity/eligibility checks, APIs/SDKs and optional on-chain representation.

Current interpretation keeps the reusable licensing-service goal while treating tokenization as optional.

## TLaaS — Trust Layer as a Service

The Grand Prospectus uses **TLaaS = Trust Layer as a Service** for a broader integration layer exposing CHLOM identity, verification, compliance, licensing and evidence capabilities through APIs, SDKs, plug-ins and service workflows.

This is wider than tokenized licensing. It resembles a provider-independent trust/control layer for applications.

## Why the conflict matters

If both concepts share one machine acronym without effective-period/source context, an integration could mistakenly treat a narrow licensing service as the entire trust layer or vice versa.

Current records should preserve both historical expansions until a founder adjudication/ADR chooses a canonical relationship—such as predecessor/successor, parent/child or separate namespaced services.

## CaaS — Compliance-as-a-Service

Current CrownThrive/CHLOM material uses **Compliance-as-a-Service (CaaS)** for reusable compliance/governance capabilities. A CaaS surface may expose policy evaluation, eligibility, permission checks, evidence receipts, holds, appeals, audit state and governed correction behavior.

CaaS is not a claim that CrownThrive replaces attorneys, regulators, accountants, auditors or other required qualified professionals.

## dS-CaaS

Current institutional documentation preserves **dS-CaaS** as a distinct governance/runtime framework label. Recovery material has associated it with decentralized/smart compliance-service concepts, but the exact canonical expansion and relationship to ordinary CaaS must remain source/ADR controlled if conflicting records exist.

Therefore agents must not silently replace `dS-CaaS` with `CaaS`, nor manufacture a definitive expansion solely from string similarity.

## Modern service architecture

Historical service concepts map into composable CrownThrive services rather than a monolithic protocol endpoint:

```text
Identity
+ Authorization
+ Policy/Rules
+ Evidence/DAIL
+ Rights/DLA
+ Approvals
+ Licensing/Entitlements
+ Revenue/Remedies
+ API/MCP adapters
= governed reusable service layer
```

## Provider independence

A service contract should preserve stable CrownThrive meaning across database, cloud, model, payment, identity or chain-provider changes. Provider success does not become institutional authority automatically.

## API/MCP exposure

Modern service exposure requires explicit tool identity, authentication, authorization, side-effect class, evidence, versioning, test/sandbox behavior, rate/request budgets, rollback and provider-exit controls. Historical descriptions of an API do not prove a current endpoint exists.

## Commercialization boundary

CHLOM service packs may eventually become licensed APIs, SDKs, MCP capabilities, policy packs, assessments or managed governance services. Exact price, SLA, certification, legal representation, production entitlement and protected-IP distribution remain independently governed.
