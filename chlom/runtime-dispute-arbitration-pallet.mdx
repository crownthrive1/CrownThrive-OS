## Source basis

The recovered Help Center names a distinct **Dispute & Arbitration Pallet** and a corresponding `DAP Whitepaper`. It separately names the **Dispute Resolution Engine & Protocol (DREP)**, meaning the pallet and engine should not be collapsed into one record. The Grand Prospectus and historical papers describe disputes, arbitration, appeal windows, guardianship/pause, evidence, decisions and remedies as first-class CHLOM functions.

This article preserves DAP as the stateful runtime-domain record; DREP is the orchestration/process engine that can operate over it.

## Historical purpose

DAP represented the durable dispute state machine:

```text
CLAIM / DISPUTE
      |
      v
CASE CREATED
      |
EVIDENCE + PARTIES + SCOPE
      |
      v
JURISDICTION / AUTHORITY CHECK
      |
      +---- unresolved ----> HOLD
      |
      v
REVIEW / ARBITRATION
      |
      v
DECISION
      |
      +--> REMEDY
      +--> APPEAL WINDOW
      +--> RESTORATION / CORRECTION
      |
      v
DAIL LINEAGE
```

## Non-purpose

DAP does not itself create:

- governmental judicial authority;
- mandatory arbitration outside an adopted agreement/policy;
- legal conclusions by AI;
- unilateral confiscation authority;
- irreversible deletion of disputed evidence;
- production deployment merely because the historical pallet was designed.

## Authority model

Reviewer/arbitrator assignment must resolve:

- scope/jurisdiction under the adopted policy/agreement;
- conflict-of-interest status;
- role/delegation validity;
- required quorum or professional qualification;
- decision ceiling;
- recusal and replacement rules.

```text
EXPERTISE != AUTHORITY
ROLE != DELEGATION
REVIEWER != ARBITRATOR AUTOMATICALLY
AI != FINAL D3/PROFESSIONAL DECISION AUTHORITY
```

## HOLD semantics

HOLD is not a loss or final ruling. It is a safety/control state when the dispute cannot validly advance.

Common reasons:

- evidence incomplete;
- rights/chain-of-title unresolved;
- reviewer conflict;
- required professional input pending;
- authority/jurisdiction unclear;
- provider evidence mismatch;
- appeal or timelock active;
- security incident affecting evidence integrity.

## Remedies

A remedy must be separately authorized and scoped. Examples include:

- restore prior license/entitlement state;
- suspend or revoke within existing authority;
- correct allocation/settlement records;
- issue refund/credit only through economic authority;
- require re-verification;
- correct public/private records;
- compensate/rollback a reversible provider action;
- route to external professional/legal process.

## Appeals

An appeal is a new review path, not mutation of the old ruling.

```text
DECISION v1
    |
appeal filed
    v
APPEAL RECORD
    |
new reviewer/body
    v
AFFIRM / MODIFY / REVERSE / REMAND
    |
    v
DECISION v2 + explicit supersession relationship
```

## Evidence preservation

Original submissions and decisions remain preserved. Corrections should use append-only relationships:

```text
original evidence
  -> challenge
  -> corrected/superseding evidence
```

The system should not rewrite history to make the final decision appear inevitable.

## Provider disputes

Processor, marketplace, payment, hosting or identity-provider dispute outcomes are evidence. They do not automatically settle institution-wide CHLOM rights, entitlement or governance state.

## Pseudocode pattern

```text
resolve_dispute(d):
  assert current_policy(d.policy_version)
  authority = resolve_decision_authority(d)
  if authority unresolved: HOLD
  if conflict(authority, d): HOLD
  evidence = resolve_evidence(d)
  if evidence insufficient: HOLD
  proposed = evaluate_under_policy(evidence, d)
  require_human_or_professional_gate_if_needed(proposed)
  decision = issue_signed_decision(proposed, authority)
  create_remedy_candidates(decision)
  append_DAIL(decision)
```

This is a public-safe process pattern, not protected adjudication logic.

## Security/threat model

Threats include:

- evidence tampering;
- biased or conflicted reviewer;
- forged authority/delegation;
- hidden evidence deletion;
- abuse of emergency pause as final punishment;
- replaying an old decision against a new version/object;
- leaking restricted evidence;
- AI overreach;
- provider outcome promoted as sovereign ruling;
- appeal suppression.

## Tests

1. valid dispute \+ authorized reviewer \+ complete evidence -\> decision path;
2. reviewer conflict -\> HOLD and reassign;
3. missing authority -\> HOLD;
4. disputed evidence integrity -\> evidence challenge state;
5. decision with unauthorized remedy -\> remedy rejected;
6. appeal -\> new decision lineage, old decision retained;
7. provider refund -\> no automatic rights ruling;
8. restricted evidence -\> redacted/public-safe projection only;
9. professional/legal decision required -\> human/professional gate;
10. correction -\> explicit supersession, never silent overwrite.

## Current mapping

Modern CHLOM can implement DAP through a case/dispute service backed by canonical records, Evidence Vault references, DAIL events, role/authority services, temporal controls and separate remedy executors. A blockchain runtime pallet is not required for the semantics to remain useful.

## Restricted companion

May include private evidence, protected reviewer mappings, conflict checks, legal/professional records, internal fraud signals, provider dispute payloads, sealed settlement schedules and incident evidence.
