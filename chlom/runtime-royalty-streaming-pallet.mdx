## Historical purpose

The Royalty Streaming Pallet extended static split allocation into **continuous or event-driven royalty accrual/distribution**, allowing CHLOM to model value flowing over time or as qualifying usage events accumulated.

```text
QUALIFYING USE / REVENUE EVENTS
          |
          v
ROYALTY SCHEDULE
          |
          v
ACCRUAL STREAM
          |
          v
CLAIMABLE / PAYABLE OBLIGATIONS
          |
          v
DISTRIBUTION / SETTLEMENT
```

## Streaming is an accounting/distribution pattern

Historical `streaming` can mean continuously updating accruals or periodic micro-distributions. It does not mean a current live token stream or guaranteed real-time payout.

```text
continuous calculation concept
      !=
live payment rail
```

## Accrual

```text
new qualifying event
      |
calculate incremental obligation
      |
append accrual entry
      |
update claimable projection
```

Accrual entries should be immutable/correctable rather than overwriting a single balance.

## Time-based model

A generic time-based stream can be represented as:

```text
accrued(t) = integral(rate(t), t0..t)
```

The actual rate function, units and basis must come from an authorized schedule. This equation is a model, not a current royalty promise.

## Event-based model

```text
accrued_total = sum(royalty(event_i, schedule_version_i))
```

Each event remains tied to its applicable rights/agreement/schedule version.

## Pause/revocation

A rights dispute or revoked license can pause future accrual/distribution according to governing agreement without deleting prior lawful accruals.

## Micro-payment boundary

Historical blockchain/token designs could perform very small automated transfers. Current provider/accounting systems may batch obligations for fees, minimum transfer amounts or operational efficiency. `streaming` semantics can therefore be implemented as a ledger stream with periodic settlement.

## Failure cases

### Duplicate usage event

Idempotency prevents duplicate accrual.

### Schedule changes mid-stream

New events use the effective schedule version; historical accrual remains tied to prior versions.

### Provider cannot economically send micro-payments

Accrual remains ledgered and can batch under authorized rules; do not call unpaid accrual settled.

### Rights disputed

Pause future distribution/activation according to policy; preserve prior evidence.

## Security/economic threats

- duplicate accrual;
- rate/schedule substitution;
- beneficiary alias drift;
- payout fragmentation/fee leakage;
- premature distribution during dispute;
- provider callbacks misclassified as settlement.
