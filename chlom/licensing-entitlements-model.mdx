# CHLOM licensing and entitlements model

> **State:** Phase 2 / 2.99 public-safe doctrine. This page defines a candidate institutional model. It does not grant rights, approve legal terms, set a price, certify fulfillment, enable checkout, issue an entitlement, or open Phase 3.

CHLOM treats **rights evidence, license authority, license terms, payment, entitlement, delivery, and remedy state as separate governed facts**. A file, product page, provider object, payment event, archive hash, entitlement row, or download link can prove only the fact within its own evidence scope.

This separation prevents a technical or commercial observation from silently becoming a rights grant.

## Core distinctions

| Concept | Meaning | Does not prove |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Asset or work identity | The stable institutional subject being governed | Ownership, authorship, licensability, or current deployment |
| Rights state | The evidence-backed status of ownership, permission, restriction, or unresolved rights | That an offer may be sold |
| DLA | The versioned Dynamic Licensing Asset that defines a permission package and lifecycle | Payment, delivery, or acceptance by a specific licensee |
| Offer | A proposed commercial presentation of an exact licensed scope | Rights clearance, price authority, or checkout availability |
| License terms | The exact effective-dated permission, restriction, remedy, and support artifact | That a customer accepted those terms |
| Payment evidence | Evidence that a provider or governed ledger observed a financial event | Ownership, license authority, entitlement, or fulfillment |
| Entitlement | A bounded access or use authorization issued under an accepted license | Transfer of ownership or rights outside its scope |
| Delivery | Evidence that the entitled subject received or accessed the governed release | Continuing license validity or rights beyond the entitlement |
| Remedy state | Refund, dispute, suspension, revocation, expiration, correction, or restoration state | Erasure of the historical transaction or evidence trail |

An entitlement is therefore **not the asset itself**. It is a governed relationship between a subject, an exact license, an exact release or service scope, and a current lifecycle state.

## Dynamic Licensing Asset

A DLA is the machine-readable licensing object for a governed permission package. At minimum, it should identify:

- the stable asset, work, service, framework, or capability ID;
- the exact release, edition, package, interface, or service scope;
- the rights controller or authorized licensing steward;
- the proposed or accepted licensee subject;
- the rights granted and expressly reserved;
- permitted use, users, seats, environments, channels, territories, term, and usage limits;
- adaptation, redistribution, sublicensing, source-access, support, update, and attribution conditions;
- applicable license and policy versions;
- evidence and chain-of-title references;
- required approvals and specialist reviews;
- economic-schedule references where separately authorized;
- suspension, expiration, revocation, cure, appeal, and restoration rules;
- predecessor, supersession, correction, and rollback references.

A DLA must preserve unresolved rights as unresolved. Missing permission is not converted into permission by a product manifest, payment configuration, founder enthusiasm, technical feasibility, or prior possession of a file.

## Governed lifecycle

The public-safe candidate lifecycle is:

```text
concept
  -> candidate
  -> packaged
  -> rights_cleared
  -> pricing_authorized
  -> fulfillment_certified
  -> checkout_staged
  -> live
```

Each transition requires its own evidence and authority. A later state must not be inferred from completion of an earlier one.

A license or entitlement may then move through a separate lifecycle such as:

```text
proposed
  -> offered
  -> accepted
  -> issued
  -> active
  -> expired | suspended | revoked | canceled | disputed
  -> restored or superseded when separately authorized
```

The exact vocabulary is versioned by the applicable contract. A held, blocked, unknown, deferred, or not-reviewed gate remains not-pass.

## Bind to an exact immutable release

Every order, license, entitlement, and delivery must bind to one immutable release. The binding should include:

- stable SKU or offer ID;
- exact asset and release version;
- content or package digest;
- manifest digest where applicable;
- license and terms versions;
- authorized amount, currency, or credit denomination;
- fulfillment and delivery policy version;
- source and predecessor lineage;
- applicable acceptance, review, and certification evidence.

“Latest” is not a lawful release selector for a completed transaction. A newer edition does not silently replace the bytes, terms, rights scope, price, support promise, or accessibility evidence that governed an earlier order.

When a product byte, version, term, policy, rights conclusion, source observation, reviewer authority, or custody record changes materially, dependent certification must be reopened or superseded.

## License acceptance and attestation

A governed license-acceptance record should bind:

1. the licensee's verified institutional or application identity;
2. the exact offer and immutable release;
3. the exact license and terms versions;
4. the permitted scope and important restrictions;
5. the acceptance method and timestamp;
6. the evidence or receipt reference;
7. the resulting entitlement ID;
8. correction, withdrawal, refund, dispute, expiration, and revocation relationships.

A typed name can be recorded as a typed-name attestation when the applicable authority accepts that method. It is not cryptographic nonrepudiation. A Fingerprint ID can identify the exact canonical record version but does not prove identity control, consent, authorship, ownership, legal sufficiency, or rights.

DID, Verifiable Credential, key-backed signature, or external-anchor evidence remains separately governed and cannot manufacture authority absent the underlying rights and acceptance record.

## Payment is not the license

Payment infrastructure records money movement and payment outcomes. It does not decide who owns an asset, whether CrownThrive may license it, which terms govern, or whether an entitlement should remain active after a remedy event.

For automated fulfillment, Stripe's current first-party guidance requires a server-side fulfillment path that can run more than once safely, uses webhooks rather than relying only on a landing-page redirect, verifies payment state, and records fulfillment. Stripe also advises verifying webhook signatures, handling duplicate events, limiting subscribed event types, and processing complex work asynchronously:

- [Stripe Checkout fulfillment](https://docs.stripe.com/checkout/fulfillment?payment-ui=stripe-hosted)
- [Stripe webhook security and duplicate-event guidance](https://docs.stripe.com/webhooks)

Those provider requirements are technical inputs to CrownThrive's own governed commerce contract. They are not a substitute for rights, license, tax, accessibility, identity, security, or fulfillment certification.

## Crown Credits boundary

Where Crown Credits are used, the governed model remains:

- Stripe is funding infrastructure only;
- Supabase Auth is the wallet-identity authority;
- the CrownThrive application ledger is the balance authority;
- paid credits are first-party, closed-loop, nontransferable, noncash, non-crypto, and non-interest-bearing;
- credit funding does not itself create product rights;
- redemption requires an exact offer and release binding;
- an entitlement and license are issued only after the applicable governed predicates pass;
- fixed-duration access purchased with credits is not silently converted into a recurring subscription.

A credit balance, top-up object, provider receipt, checkout redirect, synthetic row, or public catalog card is therefore not proof of a paid license or active entitlement.

## Fulfillment and protected delivery

Before fulfillment is certified, evidence should show that:

- the delivered bytes match the exact certified release digest;
- the private object or governed service scope exists;
- raw-body provider-signature verification and replay/idempotency controls pass where a webhook is used;
- the exact SKU, offer, provider price, amount, currency, and release reconcile;
- a successful paid transaction issues the intended order and entitlement exactly once;
- duplicate or concurrent events do not duplicate orders, ledger effects, licenses, or entitlements;
- delayed success and failure outcomes are handled;
- delivery access is bounded, least-privilege, and short-lived where appropriate;
- anonymous and cross-buyer access fail closed;
- refund, dispute, cancellation, expiration, and revocation behavior is proven;
- rollback, recovery, and canonical readback evidence exists.

A generated archive, storage object, public URL, signed URL, successful redirect, provider dashboard row, or passing structural validator is insufficient by itself.

## Refunds, disputes, reversals, and corrections

Money and entitlement state can diverge unless remedy behavior is explicit.

Refund, dispute, cancellation, expiration, charge reversal, or rights correction events should create append-only compensating records. The governed response may include:

- hold or suspension;
- entitlement revocation;
- credit-ledger compensation;
- delivery-link invalidation;
- support or appeal routing;
- restoration after a successful dispute outcome or corrected evidence;
- supersession by a replacement license or release.

Earlier events remain part of DAIL lineage. A remedy does not erase what occurred; it changes the current governed conclusion and records the downstream effects.

## License classes do not collapse into one another

Personal-use, team, institutional, commercial, developer, implementation, source-access, support, certification, OEM, and white-label rights are distinct license classes.

Possessing one class does not imply another. In particular:

- personal use does not imply redistribution, resale, adaptation, public performance, source access, or sublicensing;
- access to documentation does not imply access to restricted implementation;
- an API credential does not imply a software license or sovereign authority;
- certification does not imply ownership;
- a framework package does not imply operational certification or a sovereign vote;
- OEM or white-label rights remain disabled until separately governed;
- third-party code, documentation, screenshots, packages, or other material remains `THIRD_PARTY_LICENSED` or `RIGHTS_REVIEW` until reuse rights are known.

## Public-safe projection

Public documentation may explain:

- lifecycle and state semantics;
- license and entitlement field meanings;
- immutable release binding;
- evidence-scope and correction rules;
- public-safe license classes and restrictions;
- provider-independent interfaces;
- public digests and version identifiers;
- why held or unknown states fail closed.

Public documentation must not expose:

- customer or licensee identity;
- private evidence bodies or chain-of-title material;
- provider account IDs, private storage paths, or signed delivery URLs;
- credentials, webhook secrets, private keys, or recovery material;
- protected authority-routing, replay-defense, or signing implementation;
- proprietary pricing, scoring, calibration, or comparative analysis;
- restricted contracts or unresolved third-party material.

## Current CrownThrive boundary

The CrownThrive Developer Marketplace remains a release/readiness surface while its offers are held. Candidate packages and public release entries do not establish rights clearance, fulfillment certification, tax or legal approval, checkout availability, or entitlement issuance.

Until the exact governed activation predicates pass, public calls to action may point to the release/readiness register only. They must not present held products as purchasable, certified, legally approved, tax-classified, or available for protected delivery.

## What this model does not establish

This document does not itself establish:

- ownership, authorship, chain of title, or licensability;
- counsel, tax, accessibility, security, or payment approval;
- a binding license or customer acceptance;
- a live offer, exact price, Product, Price, Payment Link, Checkout Session, or charge;
- a paid order, entitlement, license, delivery, refund, or revocation;
- a framework certification, sovereign vote, or customer automation;
- DID control, Verifiable Credential authority, or cryptographic nonrepudiation;
- production deployment or Phase 3 entry.

Those conclusions require their own current evidence, authority, and exact-artifact review.
