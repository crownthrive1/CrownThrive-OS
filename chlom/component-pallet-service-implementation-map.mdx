# CHLOM Component, Pallet and Service Implementation Map

CHLOM has two related but distinct implementation vocabularies:

1. **historical protocol/runtime pallets**—Substrate/blockchain modules described in early papers; and
2. **modern CrownThrive capability pallets**—deployable bundles of business capability, roles, policies, data contracts, integrations and success measures.

Phase 3 starts cloud-first and maps later decentralized components behind stable institutional contracts.

## Build doctrine

- make CHLOM useful before decentralized;
- make it auditable before public;
- make it legally governable before autonomous;
- no public private-evidence exposure;
- no silent overwrite;
- no undocumented split/override;
- no autonomous legal determination;
- no forced wallet/token/chain/DAO dependency;
- no unsupported active listing;
- no partner integration without security, incident, export and exit terms.

## Twelve capability pallets

The modern build pack also organizes the ecosystem into capability pallets:

1. Identity & Access;
2. Governance & Compliance;
3. Rights & Licensing;
4. Media & IP;
5. Growth, Advertising & Measurement;
6. Commerce, Booking & Loyalty;
7. Community & Events;
8. Education & Impact;
9. MM Suites / Place-Based Operations;
10. Institutional Operations;
11. Capital, Revenue & Settlement;
12. Sustainability & Impact Evidence.

A capability pallet is a deployable package that may contain multiple services, portals, policies, integrations and KPIs. It is not automatically blockchain code.

### Pallet source template and packaging rule

New capability pallets should start from `developers/templates/chlom-capability-pallet-template.v1.yaml`. The template makes the current cloud/software meaning explicit while leaving room for a later decentralized/runtime adapter. Each pallet records stable identity/version, side-effect class, authority prerequisites, schemas/APIs/MCP/events/policies, evidence/tests/evals, security/rights review, rollback, third-party notices and independent commercialization state.

Agent packs may consume one or more capability pallets. The first automation-derived candidates are Evidence Reconciliation, Phase Gate, API/MCP Certification, Credential Continuity, Webhook Integrity, Vendor Engine Watch and Documentation Impact pallets. Their reusable source pattern is documented in [Agent Templates & Pallets](/automation/agent-templates-pallets-and-commercialization).

A historical **Substrate/runtime pallet** and a modern **CHLOM capability pallet** are never interchangeable labels. If a future implementation maps a capability pallet to blockchain/runtime code, that mapping uses the separate `developers/templates/chlom-runtime-adapter-template.v1.yaml` contract. The runtime adapter is research-gated, defaults to `production_deployed=false`, cannot replace the institutional/cloud core by implication, and has an earliest activation phase of Phase 9 after threat-model, legal/privacy/security, key-custody/recovery, rollback, interoperability and explicit activation gates pass.

A runtime-adapter template therefore does not prove a CrownThrive chain, token, wallet, DAO, smart contract, public network, money-transmission authority or compliant offering exists. The capability pallet and runtime adapter receive independent stable IDs, versions, tests, third-party-license records, recovery plans and deployment evidence.

Commercial packaging is separate from technical readiness. A pallet or runtime adapter may be technically specified yet remain `pricing_status: not_authorized` and `checkout_enabled: false` until its package license, third-party notices, entitlement/fulfillment and approved Stripe price exist.

## Required implementation states

`historical_source → recovered → reconciled → decomposed → specified → prototype → test → controlled_pilot → production → maintained → superseded`

Each state requires evidence. A component may be `production` in cloud form while its decentralized adapter remains `research`.

## Interface composition

Recovered portals, dashboards and widgets should be composed from shared components—identity, authority, asset, rights graph, DLA/license, evidence timeline, policy decision, approval, entitlement, case, split statement, webhook health and status—rather than implemented as hundreds of disconnected screens.

## Test families

- state-machine transition tests;
- cross-tenant isolation;
- authorization/relationship checks;
- stale/conflicting-source rejection;
- idempotency and replay;
- evidence integrity and restricted-data handling;
- append-only corrections;
- agreement/license immutability;
- revenue arithmetic and reconciliation;
- case/hold/remedy propagation;
- webhook signature/retry;
- backup/restore and rollback;
- public/developer/private visibility;
- human approval and emergency pause.

## Phase sequence

- **3.0:** registry, schemas, states, components, papers, environments and test fixtures;
- **3A:** DAIL, approvals, actors/authority, evidence and audit;
- **3B:** rights, DLA, rules, roles and eligibility;
- **3C:** revenue, entitlements, cases and remedies;
- **3D:** identity, API/MCP federation, SDKs and platform adapters;
- **3E:** LEX and advanced/privacy/decentralized laboratory;
- **4:** real ecosystem cohorts and MM Suites phygital proof;
- **5:** external licensing, developer economy and selective advanced activation.
