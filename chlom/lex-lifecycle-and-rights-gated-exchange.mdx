# LEX Lifecycle — Rights-Gated Licensing Exchange

LEX is CHLOM's licensing-exchange concept. Historical papers envision a decentralized marketplace for licensing, leasing, sublicensing, transferring and in some cases selling or fractionalizing eligible rights. Current CHLOM preserves the marketplace concept but makes **rights eligibility and institutional authorization prerequisites to commerce**.

## Historical role

LEX is intended to reduce slow negotiations, unclear ownership, opaque licensing terms, weak royalty reporting and fragmented marketplaces by connecting verified rights inventory with eligible licensees under explicit terms.

Historical capabilities include fixed-price licenses, auctions, lease/subscription structures, sublicensing, secondary transfers, fractional licensing concepts, programmable royalties and settlement.

## Current lifecycle

```text
asset/source registered
→ rights and chain-of-title reviewed
→ licensable scope determined
→ DLA/permission package defined
→ offer/listing candidate
→ policy/eligibility review
→ listing active
→ participant qualified
→ agreement/acceptance
→ payment evidence
→ entitlement/fulfillment
→ revenue/obligation reconciliation
→ monitoring
→ renewal / transfer / termination / dispute / revocation
```

Each stage is separate. A listing or payment cannot collapse the other stages into a single `sold` state.

## Listing eligibility

Before a listing can become transactably active, CHLOM should know what asset is involved, who controls the relevant rights, exactly which rights may be offered, the license class/version, restrictions, territory/term, economic schedule, required approvals, participant eligibility rules and remedy/termination behavior.

If those facts are missing or disputed, LEX should HOLD rather than infer licensability.

## DLA relationship

DLA is the permission package; LEX is the discovery/transaction surface.

```text
rights authority
→ DLA
→ eligible offer
→ LEX
```

LEX cannot manufacture DLA authority and cannot create ownership by accepting payment.

## Payment is not entitlement

A payment-provider event is evidence that a provider processed a financial action. It does not alone prove rights clearance, license acceptance, fulfillment or entitlement. Current CHLOM therefore separates payment, agreement, DLA, entitlement, delivery and settlement.

## ThriveEvergreen relationship

Where LEX activates CrownThrive economic state, the modern commerce/economic authority applies. Effective SKU/offer/pricing/settlement behavior must reconcile through the current institutional commerce rules rather than inheriting historical token-market assumptions.

## Historical token and smart-contract models

Earlier sources contemplated atomic on-chain settlement and tokenized license representations. Those remain future adapters. A modern LEX can operate through conventional APIs, databases, payment rails and signed agreements while preserving the same rights/evidence semantics.

## Royalties and secondary activity

Historical LEX concepts include persistent royalty participation and secondary licensing economics. Current royalties, commissions or resale participation require an adopted agreement and versioned economic schedule. Historical examples are not active rates.

## Disputes and remedies

A LEX transaction may enter HOLD, suspension, termination, refund/dispute, appeal or restoration workflows. Source-era DAL/adjudication concepts map to modern case/remedy services, with DAIL preserving event lineage.

## Current readiness boundary

A public LEX should not be claimed production-ready until rights packages, participant roles, policies, DLA lifecycle, agreement acceptance, payment/entitlement/fulfillment, settlement, refunds/disputes, privacy/security, accessibility/support, audit evidence and provider-exit behavior are tested.
