Early CHLOM papers explored a tokenized network economy. Those designs are important intellectual history, but they must not be confused with current production economics or live securities/crypto claims.

## CHLOM Coin — historical utility token

The Genesis Volume, AI-driven whitepaper, Technical Blueprint and Building & Understanding paper describe **CHLOM Coin** as the network utility/fuel asset.

Historical uses include:

- transaction/gas fees;
- license issuance/renewal payments;
- LEX marketplace settlement;
- validator/nominator staking;
- oracle/developer/community rewards;
- treasury funding;
- incentives and grants.

Illustrative designs included large initial supply, ongoing staking emissions, fee burns, slashing and treasury reserves.

These examples are **design models**, not current token issuance terms.

## CHM — historical governance/compliance token

The same source family describes **CHM** as the governance and compliance-stake token.

Historical uses include:

- proposal and referendum voting;
- conviction/time-lock voting;
- issuer or oracle compliance bonds;
- possible validator alignment;
- governance-participation incentives;
- special network privileges.

The design intentionally separated day-to-day network utility from governance influence.

## Illustrative tokenomics must remain illustrative

Historical papers contain examples such as:

- hypothetical billion-unit CHLOM Coin supply;
- hypothetical 100-million CHM cap;
- percentage allocations to development, partners, community, treasury, public distribution and staking pools;
- example inflation around several percent annually;
- example fee-burn percentages;
- possible treasury/growth allocations.

These values were scenario inputs. They must not be published as active economics without a later adopted economic decision.

## Historical staking/slashing

The token architecture proposed economic assurance through:

- validator stake;
- nominator stake;
- oracle/issuer bonds;
- slashing for malicious or false behavior;
- possible governance locking.

Modern CHLOM may preserve **assurance bonds** or deposits without requiring a public cryptocurrency. The business invariant is accountable collateral/assurance, not necessarily one specific token implementation.

## Historical treasury relationship

Token fees and penalties were expected to fund the Smart Treasury, including:

- protocol operations;
- grants and development;
- security incentives;
- reserves/insurance;
- community programs;
- staking rewards;
- ecosystem growth.

Modern economic implementation must instead resolve through explicit institutional schedules, adopted accounting/tax treatment, ThriveEvergreen where applicable, and provider reconciliation.

## Governance boundary

Historical CHM token voting does not override current CrownThrive governance. Current authority is established by the accepted institutional constitution, reserved human authority, delegated roles, evidence-bound reviews and machine gates.

A token balance cannot become a current CrownThrive corporate or legal vote merely because early CHLOM papers contemplated that model.

## Legal and regulatory activation gate

Before any public token, cryptocurrency, transferable economic instrument or token-based sale could become current, the program requires appropriate review including:

- legal classification;
- securities analysis;
- money-transmission/BSA-AML implications;
- sanctions controls;
- tax/accounting design;
- jurisdictional restrictions;
- custody/key management;
- smart-contract/bridge security;
- consumer disclosures;
- market-integrity controls;
- incident/recovery testing.

Historical design language is not a legal conclusion.

## Current economic architecture

Modern CHLOM separates:

- product/offer identity;
- price/economic schedule;
- payment-provider objects;
- payment evidence;
- license/DLA state;
- entitlement;
- revenue allocation;
- rewards/credits;
- commissions/royalties;
- settlement;
- treasury/impact obligations.

This allows CHLOM to deliver much of the historical economic functionality with conventional payment and ledger systems before any tokenization decision.

## Why preserve this research

Historical tokenomics captures valuable design questions:

- How should network security be incentivized?
- How should governance participation be separated from utility?
- How should bad actors post assurance?
- How should fees fund shared infrastructure?
- How should penalties and rewards remain auditable?
- How can economics align users, operators and stewards?

Those questions remain valid even if the final implementation is not a public token economy.
