Early CHLOM papers explored a tokenized network economy. Those designs are important intellectual history, but they must not be confused with current production economics or live securities/crypto claims.

## CHLOM Coin — historical utility token

The Genesis Volume, AI-driven whitepaper, Technical Blueprint and Building & Understanding paper describe **CHLOM Coin** as the network utility/fuel asset.

Historical uses include transaction/gas fees, license issuance/renewal payments, LEX marketplace settlement, validator/nominator staking, oracle/developer/community rewards, treasury funding and incentives.

Illustrative designs include large initial supply, ongoing staking emissions, fee burns, slashing and treasury reserves. These examples are **design models**, not current token issuance terms.

## CHM — historical governance/compliance token

**CHM** is the governance/compliance-stake token in the same source family. Historical uses include proposal/referendum voting, conviction/time-lock voting, issuer/oracle compliance bonds, possible validator alignment and governance participation incentives.

The design intentionally separates day-to-day network utility from governance influence.

## Illustrative economics remain illustrative

Historical sources contain examples such as hypothetical supply caps, allocations to development/partners/community/treasury/public distribution/staking, modest annual inflation, fee burns and treasury/growth allocations.

None of those values is an active economic schedule without a later adopted decision.

## Historical staking/slashing

The token architecture proposes economic assurance through validator stake, nominator stake, oracle/issuer bonds and slashing for malicious or false behavior.

Modern CHLOM may preserve **assurance bonds** or deposits without requiring a public cryptocurrency. The invariant is accountable collateral/assurance, not one specific token implementation.

## Treasury relationship

Historical token fees and penalties were expected to fund operations, grants/development, security incentives, reserves/insurance, community programs, staking rewards and growth.

Modern economic implementation resolves through explicit institutional schedules, adopted accounting/tax treatment, PentaGreen where applicable and provider reconciliation.

## Governance boundary

Historical CHM token voting does not override current CrownThrive governance. Current authority is established by the accepted institutional constitution, reserved human authority, delegated roles, evidence-bound reviews and machine gates.

A token balance cannot become a current CrownThrive corporate/legal vote merely because early CHLOM papers contemplated that model.

## Legal/regulatory activation gate

Before any public token, cryptocurrency, transferable economic instrument or token sale becomes current, appropriate review includes legal classification, securities analysis, money-transmission/BSA-AML implications, sanctions, tax/accounting, jurisdiction, custody/key management, smart-contract/bridge security, consumer disclosures, market integrity and incident/recovery testing.

Historical design language is not a legal conclusion.

## Current economic architecture

Modern CHLOM separates product/offer identity, price/economic schedule, payment-provider objects, payment evidence, license/DLA state, entitlement, revenue allocation, rewards/credits, commissions/royalties, settlement and treasury/impact obligations.

This allows much of the historical economic functionality to be implemented conventionally before any tokenization decision.
