## CHLOM Rules, Roles, and Authority Model

CrownThrive is operating in **Phase 3: Production + Convergence**. CHLOM remains the governing metaprotocol for authority, policy, evidence, rights, compliance, and bounded execution, while the Penta family provides the institutional operating environment through which that authority is organized and exercised.

The controlling rule is simple:

> **Penta systems may exercise authority that already exists. They may never manufacture authority.**

CHLOM therefore treats **identity, organizational role, capability, permission, decision authority, review authority, execution authority, and evidence as separate facts**. A person, agent, repository, workflow, API client, model, provider adapter, or framework may possess one of those properties without automatically possessing the others.

Technical capability is not institutional permission. A successful workflow is not approval. A provider credential is not CrownThrive authority. A vote is not execution permission unless a current governing instrument gives that vote an effect. A title is not a blanket grant.

## Current institutional authority chain

The primary Phase 3 workforce chain is:

```text
PentaBoard™
    ↓ directives
PentaDirectors™
    ↓ policies / SOPs / SLAs
PentaManagers™
    ↓ contracts / task orders
PentaCohorts™
    ↓ coordinated assignments
PentaWorkers™ / agents / bounded software executors
```

Two cross-cutting control families operate across that chain:

- **PentaLegal™** — legal holds, legal advisories, legal-routing controls, and legal-governance boundaries. It does not create judicial, governmental, attorney-client, or filing authority merely because software recorded a legal state.
- **PentaHR™** — workforce lifecycle, role changes, grievances, conduct, policy acknowledgement, accommodation routing, and bounded workforce administration. It does not independently create compensation, termination, rights, or reserved-human authority outside an applicable instrument.

Other Penta systems provide specialized operating functions — for example PentaCredentials™, PentaBuild™, PentaCertify™, PentaNurture™, PentaSuite™, PentaRFA™, PentaTriage™, PentaHealth™, PentaBenefits™, PentaPay™, PentaCost™, PentaNotes™, PentaAccelerator™, PentaFactory™, PentaDocs™, PentaFabric™, and related systems — but specialized function does not elevate a system above the authority chain.

## Role-to-instrument boundaries

Phase 3 separates organizational level from the instrument that level may issue.

| Authority body | Primary instrument | Typical effect | May not do |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **PentaBoard™** | Directive | Establish institution-level direction within existing corporate/founder/governance authority | Convert a directive into powers CrownThrive does not possess |
| **PentaDirectors™** | Policy, SOP, SLA | Translate directives into operating rules, standards, controls, and service expectations | Bind equal/higher authority outside a lawful delegation |
| **PentaManagers™** | Contract, task order | Assign bounded work, deliverables, resources, TTLs, evidence, and performance conditions | Self-create budget, rights, provider-write, or D3 authority |
| **PentaCohorts™** | Governed work coordination | Organize assigned workers/agents around a mission or contract | Issue higher governance instruments merely because members agree |
| **PentaWorkers™ / agents** | Execution receipt, work product, recommendation, evidence | Perform the exact authorized work and report the result | Originate authority, self-certify consequential work, or silently broaden scope |

PentaLegal and PentaHR are cross-cutting bounded controls rather than alternative ladders around this hierarchy.

## Core distinctions

| Concept | Meaning | Does not prove |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Institutional identity | The stable subject being governed | Current permission, role, rights, or deployment |
| Role | The responsibility assigned to a subject | Permission outside the role's registered scope |
| Capability | What a subject or tool is technically able to do | Authorization to do it |
| Credential binding | A provider authentication path exists | CrownThrive authority to invoke every provider operation |
| Permission | A bounded allowance under current policy | Approval of the resulting decision or artifact |
| Review | Independent evaluation of evidence or a proposed action | Execution authority unless separately granted |
| Vote or disposition | A governed decision contribution where a current instrument recognizes it | A new authority class or permission escalation |
| Execution | Carrying out an approved action | Authority to originate, approve, or certify the same action |
| Certification | A governed conclusion within a defined certification scope | General legal, commercial, security, rights, financial, or sovereign authority |
| Provider readback | Proof that an external system returned the expected result | A general license to perform unrelated provider writes |

A repository identity, OIDC identity, API/MCP tool, workflow, model, transport identity, subagent, provider connector, cookie, receipt, heartbeat, or message bus is therefore **not an authority source merely because it can authenticate or communicate**.

## Authority records are explicit and scoped

A governed authority record should identify, at minimum:

- the governed subject and stable institutional identifier;
- the authority source or accepted governing instrument;
- the issuer and issuer role;
- the exact scope in which authority applies;
- the permitted action or operation family;
- the autonomy and decision class where applicable;
- a contract, task order, policy, SOP, SLA, directive, or other authority reference;
- required independent reviewers or specialists;
- effective, TTL, expiration, suspension, and lockout conditions where applicable;
- evidence and exact-version references supporting the current state;
- escalation, appeal, revocation, correction, supersession, and rollback conditions.

Authority outside those bounds remains absent or unresolved. It is not inferred from convenience, historical practice, tool access, model confidence, provider capability, organizational seniority, or an unrelated prior approval.

## Autonomy and decision classes

CrownThrive continues to separate machine autonomy from decision consequence.

### Autonomy classes

- **A0 — Observe:** inspect, measure, compare, and report without changing governed state.
- **A1 — Prepare:** draft, classify, reconcile, simulate, and prepare bounded work for review.
- **A2 — Reversible:** perform specifically authorized reversible actions with validation, evidence, and rollback.
- **A3 — Controlled:** perform materially consequential actions only through the required independent controls, evidence, approvals, and recovery path.
- **A4 — Reserved:** actions held for an applicable human or other expressly recognized authority.

### Decision classes

- **D0 — Deterministic / low consequence:** mechanical, evidence-backed actions with no material interpretation or authority change.
- **D1 — Bounded / reversible:** constrained actions whose effects are limited, testable, and reversible.
- **D2 — Material / governed:** actions or artifacts requiring current authority, independent review, applicable specialist evidence, exact-version binding, and the accepted governance path.
- **D3 — Reserved human:** decisions that current CrownThrive governance reserves to a human or other expressly recognized authority. No agent quorum, Penta subsystem, provider connector, model output, or technical PASS substitutes for D3.

These labels describe the control burden for an action. They do not themselves grant permission.

## PentaRFA and PentaSuite agent authority

An agent is not created as a free-standing sovereign actor.

The governed lifecycle is:

```text
need identified
  ↓
PentaRFA™ request
  ↓
adjudication: grant / deny / conditional acceptance / restructure
  ↓
PentaSuite™ materialization
  ↓
lease + TTL + exact scope + manager/contract binding
  ↓
execution + heartbeat + evidence
  ↓
renew / remediate / restrict / expire / rollback / appeal
```

A PentaSuite lease is a bounded execution envelope, not inherited sovereign authority. Conditional acceptance may impose remediation, reduced scope, a shorter TTL, or other restrictions. Expiration, repeated violations, or unremediated conditions may produce a lockout or rollback according to the governing contract. Appeals remain available where the applicable instrument provides them.

No agent may use its own lifecycle machinery to approve its own consequential authority expansion.

## Separation of duties

Material work should separate the following functions whenever current policy requires independence.

| Function | Primary responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Originator / preparer | Produces the proposal, draft, analysis, request, or bounded implementation |
| Manager / contract issuer | Defines authorized scope, deliverables, TTL, evidence, and escalation conditions |
| Evidence auditor | Confirms source quality, freshness, contradiction state, lineage, and evidence scope |
| Domain specialist | Reviews material security, rights, legal, financial, privacy, cultural, accessibility, technical, or other domain implications |
| Certifier / gatekeeper | Determines whether the exact artifact satisfies the applicable governed gate |
| Executor | Performs the approved action through the permitted interface |
| Verifier / rollback owner | Confirms readback, expected result, failure behavior, and recovery path |
| Appeals / review body | Reconsiders a restricted or adverse disposition where a current instrument permits appeal |

The originator does not satisfy its own required independent approval. PentaBuild does not self-certify a consequential build. PentaCredentials does not convert a credential into authority. PentaCertify proves only the exact scope it tested. PentaNurture may detect drift and route repair, but it may not waive the gate that detected the drift.

## PentaNotes, voting, feedback, and ramifications

PentaNotes™ may collect feedback, proposals, breakdowns, wins, risks, process gaps, incident observations, commendations, and other governed workforce signals. Voting may record support, opposition, abstention, or a need for more evidence.

Those records are **deliberative evidence** unless a current instrument explicitly gives them a decision effect.

A note or vote does not autonomously:

- fire or suspend a worker;
- revoke a lease or contract;
- create a pay hold;
- create a cost hold;
- reduce rights;
- grant provider-write authority;
- move money;
- create legal effect;
- create a new governance power.

Positive ramifications such as recognition or accelerator nomination may be recorded within policy. Restrictive ramifications require the applicable authority instrument and, where policy requires, an appeal path.

## PentaCost, PentaPay, and economic authority

Economic recordkeeping is deliberately separated from external money movement.

- **PentaCost™** may maintain governed budgets, soft limits, hard limits, commitments, spend records, releases, refunds, and cost evidence. Hard caps fail closed.
- **PentaPay™** may maintain compensation eligibility, approval state, holds, accruals, reimbursements, credits, fees, stipends, milestone entries, and external payment receipts.

Neither system creates money-movement authority merely by recording an amount. Self-approval is prohibited where the beneficiary and approver are the same governed subject. External payment requires the separately authorized financial/provider rail and its readback evidence.

## PentaHealth and PentaHR boundaries

PentaHealth™ in the workforce control plane means **operational agent/workforce health** — runtime status, workload, availability, heartbeat, capacity, error rate, and safety-state observations. It is not a diagnostic or medical authority layer.

PentaHR™ governs workforce administration within current policy. A case, recommendation, performance record, or grievance does not automatically create a punitive result. Consequential workforce action still requires the applicable instrument, evidence, independence, appeal path where required, and reserved-human involvement where policy places the matter above autonomous authority.

## Framework, provider, and federation authority

The following principles apply across PentaFabric™, PentaMesh™, PentaFederation™, Framework Factory, provider adapters, child repositories, and other interoperable surfaces:

1. **Provider capability is not CrownThrive authority.**
2. **Credential presence is not provider certification.**
3. **Adapter compilation is not live-provider proof.**
4. **Read certification does not grant unrelated write authority.**
5. **A side-effecting operation requires evidence for that exact operation when the contract requires readback.**
6. **A child repository cannot self-activate or self-certify merely because its CI passes.**
7. **Delegation cannot broaden authority.**
8. **Research, candidate, draft, controlled-test, and historical states do not silently become production.**
9. **A deny or blocking disposition cannot be converted into approval by adding more non-authoritative participants.**
10. **Federation transports evidence and bounded requests; it does not manufacture parent authority.**

## Legacy governance records

Earlier CrownThrive records used predecessor governance structures, including A/B/C/D/S sovereign-voter terminology and Phase-2.99 promotion gates. Those records remain valid historical evidence for the decisions and experiments they actually describe.

They are **not the primary current workforce chain** unless a current, unsuperseded instrument expressly incorporates them for a defined purpose. Historical voter records, old phase labels, stale exact-head approvals, superseded quorum models, and archived task packets must not be treated as automatic authority for current Phase-3 execution.

Where a predecessor control remains useful, CrownThrive carries the invariant forward into the Penta environment rather than pretending the old transport state is still current.

## Delegation and temporary authority

Delegated authority should answer six questions:

1. **Who** receives the delegation?
2. **What** exact action or decision class is covered?
3. **Where** does it apply — repository, service, framework, platform, provider operation, corridor, or record family?
4. **Which instrument** authorizes it?
5. **When** does it begin and end, including TTL or revocation trigger?
6. **How** is the result independently verified and corrected if wrong?

Delegation must not silently survive a material version change, identity collision, expired lease, authority-policy change, superseding directive, revoked credential binding, failed certification, lockout, or rollback event when the underlying authorization no longer applies.

## Exact-version decision binding

Material reviews, contracts, certifications, directives, policies, approvals, votes, and execution receipts should bind to the exact artifact or governed state they evaluated.

If that artifact or state changes materially, earlier evidence may become stale. It must not be silently reused as approval for a new version.

This supports three core properties:

- reviewers know exactly what they reviewed;
- corrections preserve lineage rather than erasing history;
- PentaMerge/PentaCloser and other automated lifecycle systems cannot combine stale evidence into a false current-state PASS.

## Corrections, supersession, closure, and revocation

CrownThrive preserves historical evidence while keeping current state clean.

When authority or transport state changes, the system should append a correction, supersession, expiration, closure, revocation, replacement, or rollback record rather than rewrite history.

This is why a stale PR can be closed without deleting its branch: the engineering evidence remains recoverable while the obsolete transport stops masquerading as current work.

A correction should identify the affected subject and scope, the prior state, the stronger/newer authority source, the effective point of change, downstream effects, and any required reopen or rollback action.

## Public-safe authority projection

Public documentation may explain:

- the Penta organizational hierarchy and instrument boundaries;
- authority and autonomy classes;
- separation-of-duties principles;
- public-safe review/certification interfaces;
- reasons for fail-closed behavior;
- public-safe version identifiers and digests;
- provider/federation relationships at the contract level;
- supersession and archive semantics.

Public documentation must not expose raw credentials, protected calibration, private evaluation corpora, private evidence bodies, restricted runtime/security internals, private key-management design, customer/partner records, protected provider mappings, or other material classified as restricted.

## What this model does not establish

This document does not itself establish:

- a person's legal authority to bind an entity;
- employment, agency, fiduciary, or professional status;
- ownership, authorship, chain of title, or licensing rights;
- identity control from a DID, Fingerprint ID, account, repository, cookie, or credential alone;
- a new corporate board power;
- production deployment or operational certification;
- a legal filing or government status;
- a new voter or democratic authority;
- a live commercial offer, price, entitlement, certification mark, or checkout state;
- external money movement;
- cryptographic nonrepudiation from a typed-name attestation.

Those conclusions require their own current authority and evidence.
