# CHLOM Rules, Roles, and Authority Model

CHLOM treats **identity, role, capability, permission, decision authority, review authority, and execution authority as separate facts**. A person, agent, repository, workflow, API client, or framework can possess one of those properties without automatically possessing the others.

This separation is a core institutional control. It prevents technical access, automation, organizational titles, model confidence, repository existence, or successful execution from silently becoming authority.

## Core distinctions

| Concept | Meaning | Does not prove |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Institutional identity | The stable subject being governed | Current permission, role, rights, or deployment |
| Role | The responsibility assigned to a subject | Permission outside the role's registered scope |
| Capability | What a subject or tool is technically able to do | Authorization to do it |
| Permission | A bounded allowance under current policy | Approval of the resulting decision or artifact |
| Review | Independent evaluation of evidence or a proposed action | Execution authority unless separately granted |
| Vote or disposition | A governed decision contribution where the constitution recognizes it | A new authority class or permission escalation |
| Execution | Carrying out an approved action | Authority to originate, approve, or certify the same action |
| Certification | A governed conclusion within a defined certification scope | General legal, commercial, security, or sovereign authority |

A transport identity, repository identity, OIDC identity, MCP tool, subagent, script, or model process is therefore **not a sovereign voter merely because it can authenticate or communicate**.

## Authority is explicit and scoped

CHLOM authority records should identify, at minimum:

- the governed subject and stable institutional ID;
- the authority source or accepted policy basis;
- the exact scope in which the authority applies;
- the autonomy class and decision class where applicable;
- required independent reviewers or specialists;
- effective and expiration conditions where authority is temporary;
- evidence or record references supporting the current state;
- escalation, revocation, correction, and rollback conditions.

Authority outside those bounds remains absent or unresolved. It is not inferred from convenience, historical practice, tool access, organizational seniority, or a prior unrelated approval.

## Autonomy and decision classes

CrownThrive's accepted operating model separates increasing levels of machine autonomy from increasing decision consequence.

### Autonomy classes

- **A0 — Observe:** inspect, measure, compare, and report without changing governed state.
- **A1 — Prepare:** draft, classify, reconcile, and prepare bounded work for review.
- **A2 — Reversible:** perform specifically authorized reversible actions with validation and rollback.
- **A3 — Controlled:** perform materially consequential actions only through the required independent controls, evidence, approvals, and rollback path.
- **A4 — Reserved:** actions held for the applicable human or separately recognized authority.

### Decision classes

- **D0 — Deterministic / low risk:** mechanical, evidence-backed actions with no material interpretation or authority change.
- **D1 — Bounded / reversible:** constrained actions whose effects are limited, testable, and reversible.
- **D2 — Material / governed:** actions or artifacts requiring independent review, applicable specialist evidence, exact-version binding, and the accepted governance path.
- **D3 — Reserved human:** decisions that the current constitution reserves to a human or other expressly recognized authority. No agent quorum substitutes for D3.

The labels describe governance requirements; they do not by themselves grant permission.

## Separation of duties

Material work should separate the functions below whenever the accepted policy requires independence.

| Function | Primary responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Originator / preparer | Produces the proposal, draft, analysis, or bounded implementation |
| Evidence auditor | Confirms source quality, freshness, contradiction state, and evidence scope |
| Domain specialist | Reviews material security, rights, legal, financial, privacy, cultural, accessibility, or technical implications within the specialist's scope |
| Gatekeeper / certifier | Determines whether the exact artifact satisfies the applicable governed gate |
| Executor | Performs the approved action through the permitted interface |
| Verifier / rollback owner | Confirms the result, readback, failure behavior, and recovery path |

The originator does not satisfy its own required independent approval. A successful build, passing test, green CI run, published preview, transport receipt, or model recommendation may be evidence, but it is not automatically constitutional acceptance.

## Agent and framework authority

CrownThrive currently uses governed sovereign and specialist roles while also operating many non-voting agents, subagents, framework candidates, repository identities, and transport identities.

The following principles apply:

1. **Framework and subagent identities remain non-voting until separately accepted under the current constitution.**
2. **A child repository cannot self-activate or self-certify.** Parent certification and the applicable federation evidence remain separate requirements.
3. **Repository, OIDC, API, MCP, workflow, heartbeat, message, ACK, or reference-chain evidence proves only the scoped technical behavior it actually demonstrates.**
4. **Delegation cannot broaden authority.** A delegated child receives no more authority than the delegating parent can lawfully and institutionally grant within the specific task.
5. **Research status creates no production or sovereign authority.** Research candidates remain research candidates until deliberately promoted through the governed path.
6. **A deny or blocking disposition cannot be converted into approval by adding more non-authoritative participants.**

Under the current Framework Factory sequence, source research and bounded scaffolding may proceed ahead of sovereign activation, but activation remains dependent on the accepted predecessor and child-certification requirements defined by the governing program records.

## Delegation and temporary authority

Delegated authority should be narrow enough to answer five questions:

1. **Who** is receiving the delegation?
2. **What** exact action or decision class is covered?
3. **Where** does the authority apply — repository, service, framework, corridor, platform, or record family?
4. **When** does it begin and end, or what event revokes it?
5. **How** is the result independently verified and corrected if wrong?

Delegation must not silently survive a material version change, identity collision, authority-policy change, expired condition, superseding decision, or rollback event when the underlying authorization no longer applies.

## Exact-version decision binding

Material reviews, votes, certifications, and approvals should bind to the exact artifact or governed record version they evaluated. If the artifact changes materially, earlier review evidence may become stale and must not be silently reused as approval for the new version.

This supports three important properties:

- reviewers know precisely what they approved or blocked;
- later corrections can preserve the original decision and show what changed;
- automated promotion cannot combine unrelated or stale evidence into a false quorum.

## Corrections, supersession, and revocation

Authority records are correctable institutional evidence. When authority changes, CHLOM should preserve the earlier state and append the correction, supersession, expiration, revocation, or replacement record rather than rewriting history.

A correction should identify the affected subject and scope, the prior state, the stronger or newer authority source, the effective point of change, downstream effects, and any required reopen or rollback action.

This is consistent with CHLOM's broader evidence model: a historical record can remain true as history while no longer governing current action.

## Public-safe authority projection

Public documentation may explain:

- authority classes and high-level lifecycle;
- role separation and separation-of-duties principles;
- review and certification interfaces;
- public-safe decision thresholds already approved for disclosure;
- reasons for fail-closed behavior;
- public-safe digests and version identifiers;
- framework and federation relationships at the contract level.

Public documentation must not expose protected calibration, private evaluation corpora, credentials, private evidence bodies, restricted runtime or security internals, private key-management design, customer or partner records, or other material classified as restricted or protected under the CrownThrive IP-disclosure gate.

## What this model does not establish

This document does not itself establish:

- a person's legal authority to bind an entity;
- employment, agency, fiduciary, or professional status;
- ownership, authorship, chain of title, or licensing rights;
- identity control from a DID, Fingerprint ID, repository, or account alone;
- production deployment or operational certification;
- a new sovereign voter;
- Phase 3 entry;
- a live commercial offer, price, entitlement, certification mark, or checkout state;
- cryptographic nonrepudiation from a typed-name attestation.

Those conclusions require their own current authority and evidence.
