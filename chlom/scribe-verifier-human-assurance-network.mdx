## Why a human assurance layer exists

CHLOM automates what can be deterministic, but many institutional questions remain context-heavy:

- Was the evidence authentic and complete?
- Does a document actually support the claimed right?
- Is an anomaly a true violation or a false positive?
- Does a professional credential apply in the relevant jurisdiction?
- Is a dispute suitable for mediation, arbitration or another authority?
- Is an AI explanation adequate?
- Is a policy translation faithful to the underlying source?
- Does a public attestation disclose too much?
- Is a proposed exception safe and authorized?

The human assurance network supplies qualified review without turning every reviewer into an all-powerful governor.

## Scribe versus verifier

A useful modern distinction is:

```text
SCRIBE
  organizes / explains / annotates / synthesizes evidence

VERIFIER
  checks a bounded claim against defined evidence and criteria

DECISION AUTHORITY
  makes or approves the institutional decision when authorized
```

One person may hold more than one role only when separation-of-duties policy permits it.

## Scribe functions

A Scribe may perform tasks such as:

- normalize source documents into a case/evidence index;
- map terms to controlled vocabulary;
- identify missing evidence;
- construct timelines;
- link source records to DAIL events;
- summarize competing claims without deciding them;
- prepare public-safe versus restricted views;
- identify policy/version references;
- prepare draft attestation metadata;
- prepare decision packets for authorized reviewers;
- maintain contradiction and correction trails.

A Scribe should not silently convert a summary into truth.

## Verifier functions

A verifier receives a **bounded verification question**.

Examples:

```text
"Does evidence package E-17 prove training completion?"
"Is credential C-4 current and issued by an approved issuer?"
"Does child DLA-22 remain within parent DLA-8?"
"Does policy pack P-7 require quarterly audit for this class?"
"Does the submitted Merkle proof match checkpoint R-19?"
```

The verifier should answer only the scoped question and record the basis.

## Competency and authority

Expertise is not self-declared authority.

```text
self-description
      != verified competency
      != assigned reviewer role
      != institutional decision authority
```

A person can be a subject-matter expert without having authority to issue a professional license, legal opinion, financial approval or corporate decision.

## Reviewer registry

A modern Scribe/verifier registry may include:

- stable identity;
- organization affiliation;
- verified credentials;
- domain competencies;
- jurisdictions;
- authorized review classes;
- confidentiality clearance;
- conflict-of-interest status;
- training requirements;
- active/suspended status;
- review history;
- quality metrics;
- appeal/overturn history;
- effective period.

Sensitive employment or personal data should not be publicly projected.

## Conflict-of-interest gate

```text
candidate verifier
      |
      v
conflict check
  +---+---+
  |       |
clear   conflict
  |       |
  v       v
assign   disqualify / escalate
```

Conflicts may include financial interest, direct involvement, close relationship, prior advocacy, ownership interest or another material impairment of independence.

## Multi-verifier quorum

Some claims may require more than one verifier.

```text
V1 ---- PASS ---+
                 |
V2 ---- PASS ---+--> quorum satisfied
                 |
V3 ---- HOLD ---+
```

Quorum logic must define whether unanimity, majority, specialist concurrence or independent approval is required.

A majority cannot override a hard authority requirement.

## Cognitive Scribe concept

The historical phrase **Cognitive Scribe Network** suggests a human-plus-computational assistance model. Current CHLOM can interpret this conservatively as AI-assisted evidence organization and reviewer support.

```text
AI may:
- summarize
- classify
- compare
- retrieve
- detect contradictions
- draft structured receipts

AI may not:
- invent evidence
- fabricate credentials
- self-assign authority
- hide conflicting sources
- convert uncertainty to PASS
- become final authority merely through confidence
```

## Relationship to oracles

Oracles provide attributable external observations. Scribes/verifiers evaluate or contextualize claims that cannot be accepted automatically.

```text
ORACLE: "registry returned status X at time T"
SCRIBE: organizes evidence/context
VERIFIER: determines whether X satisfies bounded criteria
AUTHORITY: decides institutional effect
```

## Relationship to attestations

A verifier may issue or authorize an attestation only within scope.

A Scribe-prepared draft attestation is not effective until the required issuer/verifier authority acts.

## Relationship to arbitration

Arbitrators are not merely generic verifiers. Arbitration involves a dispute-resolution authority and procedural rights.

A verifier may supply findings to a case, while the arbitrator/panel makes the disposition under the governing process.

## Quality and accountability

Reviewer quality can be evaluated using metrics such as:

- turnaround time;
- evidence completeness;
- policy adherence;
- agreement with later verified outcomes;
- appeal/overturn rate;
- conflict disclosures;
- missed-expiration errors;
- documentation quality;
- confidentiality incidents.

Metrics support governance but should not become an opaque reputation score that automatically determines authority.

## Evidence privacy

Scribes and verifiers receive the minimum information required for their role.

```text
PUBLIC reviewer
   -> public evidence only

INTERNAL reviewer
   -> internal scoped evidence

RESTRICTED specialist
   -> least-privilege restricted package

SEALED material
   -> separately authorized process
```

## Separation of duties

For consequential decisions, preferred separation is:

```text
originator != scribe != verifier != approver != executor
```

Not every workflow requires five different humans, but no one role should silently inherit all authority.

## Appeals and corrections

A later verifier or appeal panel may disagree with an earlier verification.

```text
V1 result PASS
     |
new evidence
     |
     v
V2 result DENY
     |
     v
correction / case
     |
     v
current state updated

V1 remains historical
```

## Failure states

| Condition | Disposition |
| --- | --- |
| verifier identity unresolved | HOLD |
| competency missing | HOLD |
| conflict of interest | reassign |
| evidence incomplete | HOLD |
| criteria version missing | HOLD |
| evidence contradicts claim | DENY or case |
| specialist required but unavailable | HOLD |
| AI output used without attribution/version | HOLD consequential decision |
| reviewer authority expired | DENY effective verification |

## Required test vectors

1. qualified verifier, no conflict;
2. verifier credential expired;
3. conflict-of-interest detected after assignment;
4. two-verifier quorum with one HOLD;
5. AI summary contradicts source;
6. restricted evidence leakage attempt;
7. verifier tries to self-approve authority expansion;
8. overturned verification;
9. duplicate review submission;
10. cross-jurisdiction competency mismatch;
11. Scribe draft mistakenly treated as final attestation;
12. reviewer suspended while case is open.

## Core invariants

```text
expertise != authority
scribe != verifier
verifier != final decision authority
AI assistance != human verification
majority vote != professional authority
review history != permission to self-escalate
summary != source evidence
```

## Current implementation boundary

A bounded human assurance network can operate through conventional identity, workflow, evidence and case-management services today. Token staking, decentralized reviewer markets or protocol-native reputation remain historical/research possibilities unless separately adopted.
