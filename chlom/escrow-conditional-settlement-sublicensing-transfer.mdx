# Escrow, Conditional Settlement, Sublicensing and Transfer

The Technical Blueprint describes LEX transfer flows in which payment is escrowed, license eligibility/compliance is checked, the license token is transferred, and payment is released only if the transfer succeeds. It also explicitly contemplates rental, sublicensing and transfer restrictions.

The current CHLOM implementation model preserves the **conditionality invariant** without requiring smart-contract escrow or tokenized licenses.

## Historical LEX escrow flow

The historical source effectively models:

```text
seller lists license
      |
      v
buyer initiates purchase
      |
      v
payment escrowed
      |
      v
compliance pre-check
      |
      +--> buyer eligible?
      +--> license active?
      +--> transfer permitted?
      +--> sanctions/policy clear?
      +--> royalty obligations resolvable?
      |
      v
license transfer succeeds
      |
      v
payment released
```

If compliance or transfer fails, the historical model aborts the trade and does not release payment.

## Escrow as a state, not necessarily a product

The institutional meaning of escrow is **conditional control of value pending fulfillment of defined prerequisites**.

It can be implemented through:

- smart-contract escrow;
- payment authorization/capture separation;
- marketplace hold;
- reserve ledger;
- third-party escrow provider;
- invoice/deposit workflow;
- other legally appropriate rails.

The provider may change; the conditions should not.

## Sublicensing

Sublicensing creates a new permission downstream from an existing license. It cannot grant rights the parent license does not contain.

```text
ORIGINAL RIGHTS
      |
      v
MASTER / PARENT DLA
      |
      +--> sublicensing allowed?
      |
     NO -----> DENY
      |
     YES
      |
      v
child DLA scope <= parent scope
      |
      v
sub-licensee eligibility
      |
      v
child entitlement
```

## Scope conservation invariant

```text
CHILD RIGHTS <= PARENT RIGHTS <= SOURCE RIGHTS
```

A sublicensing system must not expand territory, media, duration, exclusivity, modification rights, transfer rights or economic authority beyond the parent license unless an independent upstream right authorizes the expansion.

## Transfer versus sublicense

These should be distinct operations.

```text
TRANSFER
current holder -> successor holder
rights object may move according to transfer rules

SUBLICENSE
parent holder retains parent rights
child permission granted within parent scope
```

A transfer may terminate or reduce the former holder's entitlement, while a sublicense may preserve it.

## Transfer pre-check

Before transfer/sublicense, CHLOM should resolve:

- source rights allow the operation;
- parent DLA is active and not held/revoked;
- transfer/sublicense cap is not exceeded;
- destination participant is eligible;
- jurisdiction/territory restrictions are satisfied;
- required credentials are current;
- economic/royalty rules are resolvable;
- no active case/hold blocks the operation;
- agreement/assent requirements are satisfied.

## Atomicity versus compensation

Historical smart-contract design emphasizes atomic settlement: either all conditions succeed or nothing changes.

Conventional providers may not offer perfect atomicity across systems. Modern CHLOM therefore needs compensation logic.

```text
Step A succeeds: payment captured
Step B fails: entitlement cannot issue
        |
        v
compensating action required
        |
        +--> refund
        +--> void capture
        +--> credit/reversal
        +--> case if provider failure
```

## DAIL event chain

A transfer or sublicense should produce linked events:

```text
offer accepted
 -> payment authorized
 -> conditions evaluated
 -> transfer/sublicense approved
 -> DLA/entitlement updated
 -> fulfillment confirmed
 -> settlement released
 -> allocation reconciled
```

Failures, refunds and corrections append their own events.

## Case/remedy integration

A disputed transfer may require temporary HOLD on settlement or entitlement. Remedies can include cancellation, refund, reversal, license restoration, reissue, partial correction or escalation depending on the governing agreement and authority.

## Failure conditions

HOLD or DENY when:

- parent rights cannot be proven;
- license is expired/revoked/held;
- child scope would exceed parent scope;
- participant eligibility is stale or unresolved;
- payment and entitlement states diverge without compensation plan;
- duplicate execution cannot be excluded;
- provider cannot reverse a failed partial transaction;
- active dispute blocks settlement.
