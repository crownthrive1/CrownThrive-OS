# Building and Understanding the CHLOM Metaprotocol — Historical Build Guide

The **Building and Understanding the CHLOM Metaprotocol** paper is a developer-oriented bridge between the master whitepaper and the deeper Technical Blueprint. It explains the architecture component by component and then outlines how a development team could construct the system.

## Historical component set

The source presents CHLOM as an integrated metaprotocol containing AI-powered compliance and fraud detection, a Decentralized Licensing Authority, LEX, decentralized identity/credentials, a zero-knowledge proof layer, Substrate blockchain infrastructure, on-chain governance, a dual-token economy and Smart Treasury automation.

Its central idea is that **compliance should happen as a property of the system**, not only as after-the-fact paperwork.

## AI compliance and security

The source describes continuous regulatory checks, fraud/anomaly detection, contract analysis, compliance reporting and machine-learning oracles. Modern CHLOM preserves those capability goals while requiring explicit model/provider/version/evaluation/authority records and preventing AI from manufacturing legal or institutional authority.

## Historical DLA and LEX

DLA means **Decentralized Licensing Authority** in this source. Together with LEX it supports license issuance, revocation, transfer, leasing, sublicensing, fractional rights and atomic settlement.

Current DLA means **Dynamic Licensing Asset**, the governed license object; issuer/steward authority is separated.

## DID/SBT and privacy

The source describes DIDs, soulbound credentials, revocation registries and ZK-based verified anonymity. A participant can prove eligibility or credential status without exposing unnecessary underlying identity data.

Current implementation preserves selective disclosure and least-data principles whether or not the final representation is an SBT.

## Historical blockchain design

The paper proposes NPoS/Proof-of-Stake, high throughput, compliance pre-check hooks, modular pallets, off-chain workers, EVM/WASM possibilities and interoperability.

The current cloud-first architecture is a sequencing change: stable institutional contracts are built first so a future chain adapter cannot redefine business meaning.

## Dual-token research

Historical design uses **CHLOM Coin** for utility/payments/fees/marketplace/staking and **CHM** for governance, regulatory/issuer bonds and higher-responsibility roles. Illustrative supply, inflation, burns and distributions remain research until explicitly adopted.

## Historical developer sequence

1. establish the Substrate base;
2. implement identity/SBT support;
3. implement DLA;
4. implement LEX;
5. implement rules and pre-check hooks;
6. implement oracles;
7. integrate governance;
8. support dual tokens;
9. build treasury automation;
10. integrate off-chain AI;
11. integrate ZK proofs;
12. test, audit and phase the launch.

Modern Phase 3 decomposes the same requirements into cloud-first services before decentralized adapters are promoted.

## Smart Treasury

The paper describes programmatic treasury policies, compliance-aware allocation, automated reporting and possible AI-assisted yield optimization. Current implementation must separate recommendation from execution and provider evidence from institutional economic truth.

## Current interpretation

This is a **historical engineering explanation**, not a current deployment report. Its concepts may be decomposed into modern services while source-era assumptions remain visible.
