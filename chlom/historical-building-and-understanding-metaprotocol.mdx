# Building and Understanding the CHLOM Metaprotocol — Historical Build Guide

The **Building and Understanding the CHLOM Metaprotocol** paper is a developer-oriented explanatory source that bridges the master whitepaper and deeper Technical Blueprint. It explains CHLOM component by component and outlines how a team could build it from the ground up.

## Historical component set

The source presents CHLOM as an integrated metaprotocol composed of:

- AI-powered compliance and fraud detection;
- Decentralized Licensing Authority;
- CHLOM License Exchange;
- decentralized identity and credentials;
- zero-knowledge proof layer;
- Substrate blockchain infrastructure;
- on-chain governance;
- dual-token economy;
- Smart Treasury and automation.

Its core operating thesis is that **compliance should happen as a property of the system**, not only as separate after-the-fact paperwork.

## AI-driven compliance

The paper describes a 24/7 AI compliance layer handling regulatory checks, fraud/anomaly detection, smart-contract analysis, automated reporting and machine-learning oracles.

Modern CHLOM preserves these capability goals but requires model/provider/version/evaluation/authority records and prohibits AI from creating its own legal or institutional authority.

## Historical DLA and LEX

DLA is the historical **Decentralized Licensing Authority** and LEX is the controlled license marketplace. Together they support issuance, revocation, transfer, leasing, sublicensing, fractional rights and atomic settlement.

Current DLA uses **Dynamic Licensing Asset** for the license object. LEX retains its exchange role as a gated target service.

## DID/SBT and privacy

The paper describes self-sovereign DIDs, soulbound credentials, revocation registries and ZK-based verified anonymity. A participant can prove eligibility or credential status without revealing unnecessary underlying identity data.

Current implementation should preserve selective disclosure and least-data principles whether or not the eventual credential representation is an SBT.

## Historical blockchain design

The source proposes NPoS/Proof-of-Stake, high throughput, compliance pre-check hooks, modular pallets, off-chain workers, EVM/WASM possibilities and interoperability.

The current cloud-first architecture is a sequencing change, not a repudiation of the source: stable institutional contracts are built first so a later chain adapter cannot redefine business meaning.

## Dual-token research

The paper uses **CHLOM Coin** for utility/payments/fees/marketplace/staking and **CHM** for governance, regulatory/issuer bonds and high-responsibility roles.

Illustrative supply, distribution, inflation, burn and staking mechanics are historical research until separately adopted.

## Developer build sequence

The source recommends:

1. establish the Substrate base;
2. implement identity/SBT support;
3. implement DLA;
4. implement LEX;
5. implement compliance rules and pre-check hooks;
6. implement oracles;
7. integrate governance;
8. support dual tokens;
9. build treasury automation;
10. integrate off-chain AI;
11. integrate ZK proofs;
12. test, audit and phase the launch.

Modern Phase 3 decomposes the same requirements into cloud-first services before decentralized adapters are promoted.

## Smart Treasury

The source describes programmatic treasury policies, compliance-aware allocation, automated reporting and possible AI-assisted yield optimization.

Current implementation must distinguish recommendation from execution and provider evidence from institutional economic truth.
