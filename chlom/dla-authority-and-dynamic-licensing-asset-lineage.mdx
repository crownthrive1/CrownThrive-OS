# DLA Lineage — Licensing Authority to Dynamic Licensing Asset

DLA is one of the most consequential CHLOM acronym changes because early sources used the term for an **authority**, while current CHLOM reserves it for a **license object**. Wave 2 preserves both meanings and separates their institutional functions.

## Historical DLA — Decentralized Licensing Authority

The AI-driven whitepaper, Genesis Volume, Technical Blueprint, Building & Understanding paper and Grand Prospectus use **DLA = Decentralized Licensing Authority**.

The historical DLA is the trust and issuance authority for governed licenses. Its source-era responsibilities include verifying issuer/licensor eligibility; evaluating identity or credential prerequisites; issuing, renewing, suspending and revoking licenses; recording license class, scope, duration and restrictions; connecting licenses to compliance rules; integrating AI/risk/oracle inputs; supporting LEX listings and transfers; enforcing transfer, sublicensing and usage constraints; recording audit or adjudication outcomes; and coordinating governance overrides or emergency restrictions.

Historical sources sometimes represent this authority as a pallet, protocol module or decentralized network function. Those representations are design lineage, not evidence that a live decentralized licensing authority is currently operating.

## Current DLA — Dynamic Licensing Asset

Current machine-readable CHLOM uses **DLA = Dynamic Licensing Asset**.

A Dynamic Licensing Asset is the governed permission package describing what rights are actually granted, to whom, for what use, under which restrictions, for what period, and with what economic/remedy obligations.

A DLA may reference source asset/work/brand/property; rights controller or licensor; licensee/authorized organization; license class and version; rights granted and reserved; territory/jurisdiction; media/channel/use context; effective date and expiration; usage limits; sublicensing/transfer/modification rules; economic schedule references; royalty/commission/split obligations; evidence and chain-of-title references; approvals; entitlement/fulfillment relationships; and status/hold/remedy state.

```text
+-----------+  +-----------+
| Entitlement|  | LEX Offer |
+-----+-----+  +-----+-----+
      |              |
      +------+-------+

        +---------+

        +---------+
```

The authority that can approve a license is not the license itself. Separating the two removes an ambiguity in early CHLOM language and supports least privilege, auditability and provider independence.

## DLA lifecycle

```text
candidate
   |
   v
rights_review
   |
   v
policy_review
   |
   v
approval_pending
   |
   +---- DENY ---------> closed / rejected
   |
   +---- HOLD ---------> unresolved / evidence_needed
   |
   v
approved
   |
   v
active
   |
   +----> suspended
   +----> expired
   +----> revoked
   +----> terminated
             |
             v
     appeal / correction / restoration
```

Exact lifecycle names may vary by service version, but unresolved rights or missing authority cannot silently resolve to active.

## Rights-before-license rule

```text
asset exists
    != owner verified
    != rights controlled
    != rights transferable
    != license approved
    != entitlement active
```

The rights/chain-of-title layer remains authoritative for what can be licensed. DLA expresses the permission decision after those prerequisites are satisfied.

## Relationship to LEX

LEX may discover or transact an eligible licensing opportunity, but the exchange cannot bypass DLA and rights controls.

```text
rights inventory
      |
      v
licensing authority / policy
      |
      v
DLA permission package
      |
      v
eligible LEX offer/listing
      |
      v
agreement / payment
      |
      v
entitlement / fulfillment
      |
      v
DAIL + revenue + remedy records
```

A catalog listing, payment-provider object or marketplace purchase cannot substitute for a valid DLA.

## Historical tokenized-license concepts

Early papers sometimes contemplated tokenized license representations, NFTs or on-chain license objects. Current CHLOM treats tokenization as an optional future adapter. The durable business object is the DLA permission package; a token or chain record may later prove or transport selected state but does not redefine the rights.

## Evidence and correction

A material DLA change should preserve version/effective-period lineage. Revocation, scope reduction, renewal, correction or restoration appends a new state/evidence event rather than erasing the earlier license history. DAIL should record or reference the relevant lifecycle events and correction chain.

## Modern implementation mapping

Historical DLA capabilities decompose into Rights & Chain-of-Title Service, Licensing Stewardship / Issuer Authority, Dynamic Licensing Asset Service, Policy/Eligibility Service, Approval/Authority Service, Entitlement Service, Revenue/Obligation Service, Case/Remedy Service, DAIL Event & Evidence Service, and optional LEX/cryptographic adapters.
