## Non-purpose

Smart Tax does not:

- make CHLOM a tax authority;
- infer tax rates from historical examples;
- replace a tax professional/accounting system;
- determine residency/nexus from weak signals;
- file/remit without explicit authority;
- treat provider tax estimates as institutional truth;
- activate historical yield-to-pay-tax designs automatically.

## Accrual versus payment

```text
CALCULATED TAX
  !=
ACCRUED LIABILITY
  !=
WITHHELD/RESERVED AMOUNT
  !=
REMITTED AMOUNT
  !=
FILED/ACCEPTED RETURN
```

Each state has distinct evidence.

## Withholding/reserve

If authorized, the system can mark or reserve an amount against a liability. It must not silently remove funds from another party's entitlement without valid authority.

```text
gross obligation
 -> authorized withholding
 -> net payable
 + tax reserve/liability record
```

## Jurisdiction detection

Jurisdiction can depend on facts such as entity formation, residence, place of service/sale, customer location, nexus or other legally defined criteria. Automated signals can propose candidates, but ambiguous/high-impact classification should route to professional review.

```text
IP/device location != legal tax jurisdiction automatically
```

## Historical Smart Yield relationship

Historical concepts suggested allocating funds to yield strategies so earnings could offset taxes/insurance. Current boundary:

```text
tax obligation != investment strategy
```

Any investment/yield behavior requires separate Smart Treasury/DeFi/Yield authority and cannot delay/jeopardize tax obligations.

## Failure modes

- jurisdiction unresolved -\> HOLD;
- rule source missing/stale -\> HOLD;
- professional review required but missing -\> HOLD;
- currency mismatch -\> convert using adopted evidence or reject;
- provider estimate differs from professional/canonical rule -\> reconcile/escalate;
- remittance provider fails -\> liability remains open;
- duplicate event -\> idempotent protection;
- correction after filing -\> adjustment/amended-process path.

## Threat model

- wrong jurisdiction;
- stale rates/rules;
- provider estimate promoted as tax advice;
- manipulation of tax basis;
- unauthorized withholding;
- duplicate remittance;
- missed due date;
- currency/rounding errors;
- exposure of sensitive tax data;
- investment/yield logic compromising required liability reserves.

## Test vectors

1. source-backed rule \+ reviewed tax profile -\> calculation candidate;
2. unknown jurisdiction -\> HOLD;
3. provider tax estimate only -\> not canonical tax conclusion;
4. duplicate transaction -\> no duplicate accrual;
5. authorized withholding -\> separate withheld/liability records;
6. remittance fails -\> liability remains unsettled;
7. rule effective date changes -\> exact version per period;
8. historical tax formula/example -\> not current rate;
9. correction -\> explicit adjustment;
10. public projection -\> no private taxpayer identifiers/details.

## Current mapping

Current CHLOM can use tax-event records, professional-reviewed rule packs, P-11 accrual/withholding objects, scheduler reminders and reconciliation. Actual tax calculation, filing and remittance must remain under appropriate professional/legal/accounting authority.

## Restricted companion

Contains taxpayer/entity data, jurisdictions, tax classifications, private transactions, rates/rules where restricted, professional advice, filing/remittance records, provider accounts and correction evidence.
