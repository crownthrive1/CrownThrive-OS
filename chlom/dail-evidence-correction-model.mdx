# DAIL evidence and correction model

> **State:** Phase 2 / 2.99 public-safe doctrine. This page documents evidence and correction semantics only. It does not open Phase 3, activate a cryptographic identity system, certify an asset, establish legal sufficiency or expose restricted evidence.

DAIL — the **Digital Authority and Integrity Layer** — is CHLOM's evidence and integrity surface. It records why a governed decision occurred, which authority and evidence supported it, what state changed, and how later corrections relate to the earlier record.

DAIL is not defined by a blockchain. CrownThrive can maintain the evidence model with conventional governed records; cryptographic proofs or external anchors are optional later adapters and do not replace institutional authority.

## Core event record

A DAIL event should identify, at minimum:

- the actor or institutional subject;
- the authority reference;
- the governed object;
- the action or decision;
- the stated reason;
- the evidence references;
- the event time;
- the prior-state reference where applicable;
- the resulting state;
- correction, reversal or dispute linkage where applicable.

These fields establish traceability. They do not independently prove that an actor owns an object, holds legal authority or possesses every right required for a later action.

## Evidence has scope

Evidence must be interpreted within what it actually proves.

A public page proves what was represented publicly; it does not prove that a backend capability worked. A payment record can establish a transaction observation; it does not necessarily establish a license. A historical source can establish provenance or prior design intent without proving current deployment or present rights.

A deterministic Fingerprint ID identifies the integrity of a canonicalized record version. It does not establish ownership, authorship, certification, identity control, legal validity or deployment.

Unknown evidence stays explicit rather than being converted into a positive claim.

## Append corrections instead of rewriting history

Material corrections create new records linked to what they correct or supersede.

A correction should preserve:

1. the original statement, event or state;
2. the stronger or newer evidence;
3. the reason for correction;
4. the affected downstream state;
5. the corrected conclusion or state;
6. effective and recorded timestamps;
7. the relationship to the earlier record.

Reversals and disputes follow the same principle. Earlier evidence remains part of the lineage even when its conclusion is no longer current.

## Decision lifecycle

A governed decision may move through states such as:

`observed -> evaluated -> allowed | denied | blocked | escalated`

Later events may record transitions such as:

- `allowed -> corrected`
- `allowed -> reversed`
- `blocked -> resolved`
- `any state -> disputed`

The exact vocabulary is governed and versioned by the applicable contract. Public documentation can explain lifecycle semantics without publishing private policy calibration.

## Relationship to DLA and LEX

**DLA** identifies the licensing or authority requirements that govern a request.

**LEX** represents configured predicates, evidence requirements, allowed or denied actions, escalation conditions and expected outputs.

**DAIL** records the evidence-backed decision and subsequent lineage.

None of these layers substitutes for law, contracts, filings, professional review, identity proof, payment infrastructure or rights evidence outside the layer's defined authority.

## Fingerprints and later proof adapters

A DAIL record can reference a versioned Fingerprint ID so another governed process can identify exactly which canonical record version is being cited.

Future DID/VC or key-backed signatures, proof anchors and public-chain adapters remain separately governed. They can strengthen verification but do not manufacture CrownThrive authority.

## Public and protected boundaries

Public-safe DAIL documentation may explain:

- lifecycle and state semantics;
- record-field semantics;
- evidence-scope principles;
- correction and supersession rules;
- versioning;
- proof-interface concepts;
- public digests.

Public documentation must not reproduce:

- private evidence bodies;
- customer or partner records;
- credentials or signing material;
- raw security findings;
- proprietary policy or economic calibration;
- restricted runtime DDL;
- protected key-management design;
- private DAIL event data.

## What a DAIL record does not prove

A DAIL record alone does not prove:

- ownership;
- authorship;
- rights clearance;
- identity control;
- legal sufficiency;
- production deployment;
- payment settlement;
- certification;
- commercial availability.

Those conclusions require their own governed evidence and authority.
