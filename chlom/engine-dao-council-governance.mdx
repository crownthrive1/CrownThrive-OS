## Non-purpose

The engine does not imply:

- a current tokenholder DAO;
- one-token-one-vote as current CrownThrive governance;
- that a vote can override law, rights or contractual limits;
- self-manufactured authority;
- irreversible hidden emergency power;
- automatic execution of every approved recommendation.

## Quorum/approval math

The engine can support weighted or role-based rules. Let:

```text
E = eligible decision weight/count
P = participating weight/count
Y = approving weight/count
```

Then profiles may define:

```text
quorum = P / E
approval = Y / P
```

Historical examples such as 15% quorum, simple majority or 2/3 supermajority remain historical examples unless adopted in a current governance profile.

## Bicameral / dual-governance lineage

Historical CHLOM includes a Dual DAO/bicameral concept combining broad stakeholder/token voting with curated council oversight. Current mapping can preserve the **two-lane governance principle** without tokenization:

```text
participatory/stakeholder lane
          +
qualified/reserved oversight lane
          |
     adopted decision rule
```

## Council review

Council review can:

- approve within bounded scope;
- veto/hold where charter allows;
- require additional evidence;
- trigger emergency protection;
- route constitutional/reserved questions upward.

It cannot expand its own charter.

## Technical Committee handoff

Technical changes can require a separate technical review before governance decision or execution.

```text
governance authority
   + technical safety evidence
   + security/release evidence
```

## Emergency governance

Emergency measures require pre-delegated scope, evidence, time bounds, minimum approvals, automatic review/sunset and restoration/compensation path.

## Enactment

Approval does not equal execution. The engine produces an execution envelope containing:

- approved proposal/version;
- exact target/change;
- authority evidence;
- effective time/timelock;
- rollback/compensation reference;
- idempotency/correlation ID.

Execution adapters then apply and read back the change.

## Configuration/model/oracle changes

A governance decision may authorize changes to:

- parameters;
- policies;
- provider/source allowlists;
- model versions;
- oracle profiles;
- budgets;
- technical releases.

Each downstream domain still validates compatibility and scope.

## Failure modes

- wrong governance profile -\> HOLD;
- quorum unavailable -\> proposal remains unresolved;
- authority expired during timelock -\> revalidate/HOLD;
- target version changed -\> require compatibility/reapproval;
- provider execution fails -\> governance decision remains but implementation not certified;
- emergency measure expires -\> automatic sunset/review;
- proposal self-expands decision authority -\> reserved/constitutional gate.

## Threat model

- governance capture;
- Sybil or identity fraud;
- vote replay;
- quorum denominator manipulation;
- conflicted council members;
- hidden proposal mutation after approval;
- emergency power abuse;
- stale authority at execution;
- provider-side change without governance receipt;
- AI recommendation masquerading as decision.

## Test vectors

1. eligible proposal \+ correct profile \+ threshold met -\> approved;
2. proposal changes after votes -\> prior approvals invalid for new version;
3. quorum not met -\> not approved;
4. emergency action without preauthorized scope -\> reject/hold;
5. timelock executes after target changed -\> HOLD revalidation;
6. technical change missing technical review -\> HOLD;
7. economic change missing economic authority -\> HOLD;
8. provider applies unapproved config -\> drift incident;
9. historical token vote data -\> not current governance authority;
10. approved action execution fails -\> not falsely marked implemented.
