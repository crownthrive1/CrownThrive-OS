# FORM-012 — Basis-Point Allocation Mathematics

## Formula identity

```yaml
formula_id: FORM-012
classification: derived_public_safe_model
origin: CHLOM royalty/split requirements and historical allocation lineage
adopted_schedule_values: false
protected_companion: VAULT-IP-004
```

## Public-safe expression

For distributable amount `D` and recipient share `bps_i` in basis points:

```text
allocation_i = D * bps_i / 10,000
```

A full-allocation schedule normally satisfies:

```text
Σ bps_i = 10,000
```

If the policy intentionally leaves an unallocated reserve:

```text
Σ bps_i <= 10,000
reserve_bps = 10,000 - Σ bps_i
```

## Why basis points

Integer basis points avoid many ambiguities of floating-point percentages, provide 0.01 percentage-point granularity, and make schedule validation straightforward. Higher-precision schemes may be used where adopted, but the denominator and units must be explicit.

## Distribution basis

The formula is incomplete unless `D` is defined. `D` may be gross receipt, net distributable, a royalty basis, or another contract-defined amount. CHLOM must never mix gross and net schedules silently.

## Schedule constraints

A schedule should identify recipient stable IDs, basis points, effective dates, source agreement/policy, floors/caps if any, reserve treatment, rounding mode and authority. Duplicate recipient aliases should resolve to stable identities before aggregation.

## Conservation

After rounding and holds, the governed economic process must reconcile every unit:

```text
distribution_basis = Σ allocations + reserve + deterministic_residue
```

Any unexplained difference is a reconciliation failure, not 'rounding noise.'

## Tests

Test exact 10,000-bps distribution, partial distribution, schedule over 10,000, zero share, one recipient, duplicate recipient alias, currency precision and rounding residue.

## Versioning

Schedule values are versioned separately from FORM-012. Updating a royalty percentage does not require changing the formula if the allocation semantics remain identical.
